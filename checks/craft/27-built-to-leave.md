# Craft review — ch. 27, Built to Leave

Reviewed 2026-08-28, first review of a first draft, against CONTEXT.md §2, §3, §4, §6d and the §6i
entry for this chapter (its four Prohibited rules plus the identity condition); every quotation
checked against `bh-ceo-2023.raw` and `bh-loss-2023.raw`. All the figures are exact and in the
right document: $2.2m / 996 / $67m, $129,000 before-tax for six months, $1.76m EBITDA for the prior
year, $867,223 FY21 profit, $1.8m FY22 before-tax loss, the $2.6m swing, 19% / 38% / 44%, one price
increase in six years, two unfiled reports. Before-tax profit, EBITDA and loss never blur; the
valuation is called "a number the company proposed" in Move 3 and Reading; the departure is never
said to cause the loss; nothing after April 2023 is used; the identity is used for identity and
nothing else — all four §6i rules hold. What does not hold is the chronology around the reading of
the books, and it is the chapter's central claim. Hyde's own words in the loss article are that
"towards the end of last year" — 2022, while Norris was still CEO — "the company had appointed a new
head of finance and new external accountants." So "the manager's first act was to find out what he
had been handed" (Move 2), "the reading half was what the person he hired then had to do" (Move 3)
and "the test … the new manager ran in his first weeks" (Move 3) all attribute to the February 2023
arrival a read that the record dates to late 2022 under the founders (finding 1). The chapter also
leaves unused the one dated figure that would carry its argument without any narrative at all: the
July 2022 shareholder update's projected loss of $327,000 for the year that was filed nine months
later at $1.8 million (finding 2) — Reading lists the July and October updates as a source and the
text never touches them. One quotation is the reporter's reported speech in Norris's mouth (finding
3). Nothing below needs a new fact; every fix is a sentence already in the two articles.

## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, on the right square, who has told the story from the manager's chair when the record puts the read in the founders' last months.

Move 1 is the reader's: "one day you will hand this business to someone — a buyer, a manager, your
own children, or whoever is standing there when you are ill — and on that day the business will be
worth exactly what a stranger can run and read." That is the chapter in one sentence and the rule
passes §6d — *a business is built to leave when someone who did not build it can run it from what
is written and read it from what is filed; decide which of the two things you are building and do
the stranger's read now, while the stranger is imaginary.* The "two things to build" section applies
§2 rather than re-arguing it, and "Decide which you are building and write it down" leaves the
decision with the reader. Monday 1, 2 and 4 are failable on the day. The case is the right one and
the seven-week structure — a handover done as the experienced people advise, then the filing — is
exactly what §6i asked the chapter to show.

The problem is the story's chair. From "Thirteen months later" onward the chapter is told as
*founder leaves → manager arrives → manager opens the books → finds absorbed costs and unread
accounts*, and Move 3 builds the transferable test on it ("hand the books to someone who does not
know the business, and see what they find … the one the new manager ran in his first weeks"). The
articles say something different and, for this book, better: in July 2022 the company itself told
shareholders it was heading for a loss ($327,000 projected); in October 2022 it told them it was
"a tough year" and named the costs; at the end of 2022 it appointed a new head of finance and
external accountants; Norris left in February 2023; the year was filed in March at $1.8 million.
The stranger's read was commissioned under the founders and came back after they had gone — which
is the practice-kept-as-an-asset discovery §2 describes, with dates, and it needs no "first act."
The chapter's one-sentence chronology claim, "the loss was in the books before Norris left, whoever
was reading them," is true and unsupported on the page; the July update supports it.

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "is the cleanest statement of the Part IV question
this book has"; "What he did about it is the part worth copying"; "generous and exact"; "Norris did
the running half as well as it can be done"; "Both are right, and between them they mark the line"
(finding 7). (2) apparatus: "it is a different company in every respect that matters here —
different trade, different scale, different sources, reported by a named trade journalist rather
than told by the founder"; "and the chapter does not"; "this chapter does not describe"; "is not
sourced here and is not used" (findings 6, 7). (3) flinch: none. (4) counting and self-announcing:
"The valuation is the warning." (finding 4). (5) seams: "What the record does show is the thing this
chapter is about" (finding 7). (6) source accounting inside the argument: the after-April-2023
silence is said in Move 2's last paragraph, Move 3's limits paragraph, the owed block and Reading's
Note (finding 6). (7) n/a.

Continuity. Ch. 23 after its craft pass holds the founders "still involved at key intervals" and
quotes "we don't have a lot to do" with Norris's own "Unlikely" attached; ch. 27's "handed their
tasks to an admin and found they did not have a lot to do" drops both qualifiers and names "his
partner" where ch. 23 names McClafferty (finding 8). "Since its second page" — the two-answers
sentence is on the index (Part IV's intro: "two honest answers, and only one of them is a problem"),
which is the book's first page, and in ch. 16's close and ch. 17's boundaries; "second page" is
right about nothing (finding 8). "WP Curve was sold in 2016" is sourced only as ch. 10's Reading
aside ("acquired in 2016"); the dannorris site row is registered for identity alone, and the
chapter's "His own site puts the sequence in its title" uses the arrow for sequence — a hair over
the one fact, and the fix is to lean on ch. 10 (finding 8). Ch. 22's three figures, ch. 24's four
agreements, ch. 18's handoff documents and ch. 23's hours count are all real and the Monday 1
pointers are accurate. Ch. 16's sentence and ch. 28's stopping are real, but the chapter's sorting —
practice → ch. 28, asset → items 1–3 — gives the practice no exit but stopping, which is the slide
§2 warns against (finding 5).

Word count: about 2,320 against a 2,000 floor. Findings 1 and 2 add roughly 90 words; findings 6
and 7 remove roughly 110. Net flat.

## 1. The stranger's read is dated to the manager's "first act" and "first weeks"; the record dates it to late 2022, under the founders

**Quote** — Move 2: *"And on the books themselves, with a new head of finance and new external
accountants: "I don't know if the guys had complete oversight …""* — *"handed by its founder to a
manager in the way the experienced people said to do it, and the manager's first act was to find
out what he had been handed: costs absorbed for years without a price change, and accounts nobody
had complete oversight of."* — Move 3: *"Norris did the running half as well as it can be done: he
saw that the business had outgrown him, hired the person, took the advice not to hang around, and
left. The reading half was what the person he hired then had to do, and the answer was in figures
nobody inside had been looking at as an outsider would"* — *"it is the one the new manager ran in
his first weeks: hand the books to someone who does not know the business, and see what they find."*

**Lens** — (b) chronology inside the sources; (c) is "the reading half was what the person he hired
then had to do" earned.

**What's wrong** — The loss article: "He said that towards the end of last year the company had
appointed a new head of finance and new external accountants which had given a clearer insight into
the business' accounts." The article is April 2023; "last year" is 2022; Hyde's appointment was
announced in the first week of February 2023. The read was commissioned while Norris was CEO, and
"the guys" whose oversight Hyde doubts are the people who commissioned it. "First act" and "first
weeks" are the chapter's narrative, not the article's, and they carry the whole of Move 3's test.
Also: Norris did not "hire the person" alone — "the founders and our key investors agreed we wanted
some experienced management," and the appointment is the board's in the company's email — and "he
saw that the business had outgrown him" compresses an October 2022 email in which he "intended to
stay" and a February 2023 hindsight. The honest sequence is stronger for the chapter: the founders
brought in the outside readers themselves, and what the readers found arrived after the founder had
gone. That is §2's discovery with dates on it.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"And on the books themselves. Towards the end of 2022, while Norris was still
chief executive, the company had appointed a new head of finance and new external accountants, which
Hyde said "had given a clearer insight into the business' accounts": "I don't know if the guys had
complete oversight through that [prior] period. The business had grown pretty enormously.""* —
*"A business was priced by 996 people at sixty-seven million dollars, handed by its founder to a
manager in the way the experienced people said to do it, and read — by a head of finance and
accountants the founders had hired in their last months — as it had not been read before: costs
absorbed through six years with one price change, and accounts nobody had complete oversight of."*
— Move 3: *"Norris did the running half: the founders and their main investors agreed the business
needed experienced management, the board hired the person, he took the advice not to hang around,
and he left. The reading half was begun in the same months, by people who did not know the
business, and its answer arrived after he had gone — in figures nobody inside had been reading as
an outsider would"* — *"it is the one Black Hops ran in the founders' last months: hand the books to
someone who does not know the business, and see what they find."*

## 2. The $327,000 projection is the chapter's missing figure, and "the loss was in the books before Norris left" is true and unsupported

**Quote** — Move 2: *"the costs that rose rose for every brewer, and the loss was in the books
before Norris left, whoever was reading them."* — Reading: *"… and the July and October 2022
shareholder updates."*

**Lens** — (b) figures kept distinct, chronology sourced; §4 numbers or nothing.

**What's wrong** — Reading claims the July and October 2022 updates as a source and the text never
uses them. The CEO article: "In a July 2022 update to shareholders, the company projected it faced a
loss of $327,000 for the previous year" — the same FY22 year filed nine months later at a before-tax
loss of $1.8 million. That is the chapter's argument as a pair of dated numbers from one company
about one year: what the founders' books said in July and what the outsiders' books said in March.
It also makes "in the books before Norris left" a sourced sentence instead of an inference. "The
costs that rose rose for every brewer" has no source; Hyde's own comparison is that "the bigger
companies, and the smaller brands have really pushed ahead and shifted their price points, and we
just have not done that," which is the point the chapter needs (others repriced) rather than the one
it asserts (everyone's costs rose). The two articles also give two figures for the six months before
the raise — "a before-tax profit of $129,000" (April) and "a profit of almost $200,000" (February) —
and the chapter uses one without saying the other exists; a clause in Reading keeps the figures
distinct as the Note promises. (The $327,000 and "almost $200,000" are both already in the
registered source; the Note's "kept distinct" then becomes literally true.)

**Rewrite** — Move 2, the last paragraph: *"Nothing on that record says the departure caused the
loss: the prior year was, in the manager's letter, "a Covid year in Australia" of subsidies and
people at home; the costs were the industry's, and the manager's own comparison is that other
brewers "shifted their price points, and we just have not done that"; and the loss was in the books
before Norris left, whoever was reading them — in July 2022 the company had told shareholders it
projected "a loss of $327,000" for the year that was filed the following March at $1.8 million."* —
Reading, Source 1: add *"and the July 2022 projected loss of $327,000 and the "almost $200,000"
six-month profit as that article gives it."* — Reading, Note: after "kept distinct here" add *"; the
two articles give the pre-raise six-month profit as $129,000 before tax and "almost $200,000", and
the chapter uses the before-tax figure."*

## 3. "In hindsight, Black Hops had outgrown him and staying would create confusion" is the reporter's sentence in Norris's quotation marks

**Quote** — Move 2: *"Norris's account of why, given to the reporter by email, is the cleanest
statement of the Part IV question this book has: "in hindsight, Black Hops had outgrown him and
staying would create confusion." In his own words: "Black Hops started as a fun idea …""*

**Lens** — (b) quotation as it stands in the source; mode 1.

**What's wrong** — The article: "This week he told Brews News that, in hindsight, Black Hops had
outgrown him and staying would create confusion." Reported speech — "him," not "me" — in
Kirkegaard's voice. The chapter puts it in quotation marks as Norris's "account," then says "In his
own words" for the next sentence, which half-admits the first was not. The sources.tsv row already
quotes it as paraphrase ("that in hindsight the company had 'outgrown him'"). "The cleanest
statement of the Part IV question this book has" is the verdict before the evidence; the reporter's
sentence does not need it.

**Rewrite** — *"Norris's account of why was given to the reporter by email, and the reporter's
summary of it is that, in hindsight, Black Hops had outgrown him and staying would create confusion.
In his own words: "Black Hops started as a fun idea at the pub …""*

## 4. "The valuation is the warning" is announced, and the warning it then gives — "a story about three blokes" — is not in the record; the warning that is in the record goes unused

**Quote** — Move 3: *"The valuation is the warning. Sixty-seven million dollars was a number the
company proposed and a market of 996 people accepted on the strength of six months of profit and a
story about three blokes — and the story was the part that could not be transferred. The new manager
said at once that the tagline would change; Norris himself said "our story has been about making
great beer" … Both are right, and between them they mark the line: the beer transfers, the founders'
story does not, and a price built on the second is a price for something the buyer does not
receive."*

**Lens** — (c) argued or asserted; (b) inferences inside the sources; §6i "never present the $67m
as worth"; mode 4.

**What's wrong** — Three things. (i) Nothing in either article says the investors priced the story.
The offer documents the article describes carry figures: six months' profit, an EBITDA, "navigated
the brewery valley of death." "A story about three blokes" as a component of the price is the
chapter's. (ii) "Said at once" and "would change" overstate: Hyde "told Brews News that this would
change in discussions with the remaining Black Hops team," said "'Gold Coast' is something that's got
to be still within that tagline," and added "I think the company has wanted to change it for quite
some time" — the company was already moving off the story before the transfer, which cuts against
"the part that could not be transferred." (iii) The warning that *is* on the record is sharper and
already quoted elsewhere in the chapter: the price was set on the company's own six-month figures,
by a company that had not filed two annual reports, on a platform that "hosted the 2022 crowdfunding
campaign despite Black Hops not having filed its required financials." That is a valuation nobody
outside had read the books behind — the chapter's own thesis, and rule-compliant, because it says
what the number was a price *for*, not what the business was worth. Keep the tagline as what it is
in the article: a thing the departures made a problem for, which the new manager said would change
in discussion.

**Rewrite** — *"Sixty-seven million dollars was a number the company proposed and 996 people accepted
in a day, on the strength of six months of the company's own figures, from a company whose last two
annual reports were not on file with the regulator and whose platform hosted the raise anyway. Nobody
outside had read the books the price rested on, and that is the warning: a price is a claim about
what a stranger would receive, and it is only as good as what a stranger could check. The departures
also left the company with a line it could no longer say — "Beer, made by 3 Blokes on the Gold
Coast" — and Hyde said the tagline would change in discussion with the team, adding that "the company
has wanted to change it for quite some time"; Norris's own view was that "for our customers, our
story has been about making great beer." The beer transfers; the three blokes do not; and at the
reader's size the same line runs through the relationship with the customers — the part of chapter
seventeen's table that was you, not the business — a buyer, a manager or a successor gets only what
was moved off it."*

## 5. The practice is given no exit but chapter twenty-eight, which decides for the reader

**Quote** — Move 3: *"a business that is good work and a good living for as long as its owner does
the work, and that ends when they stop … the practice needs chapter sixteen's number and chapter
twenty-eight's exit; the asset needs the stranger's read, now, while the stranger is imaginary."* —
Monday 4: *"If it is the practice, chapter twenty-eight is the plan; if it is the asset, items 1 to 3
are the work."*

**Lens** — (d) applies §2 without deciding for the reader; §4 "two legitimate answers … neither is
inferior."

**What's wrong** — The section opens well ("None of this says you must build the asset") and then
sorts: asset → handover, price history, stranger's read; practice → ch. 28, stopping. But Move 1 has
just said every reason you might leave "arrives unannounced," and the person "standing there when
you are ill" arrives for a practice too. A practice is handed over — to a locum, a colleague, a
family member, a buyer of the client list — as often as it is wound up, and Monday 1's page is the
thing that hand needs. "Ends when they stop" is the "you own a job" slide in kinder words. The
decision the reader should be left with is which one, not whether the practice gets a plan.

**Rewrite** — Move 3: *"a business that is good work and a good living for as long as its owner
does the work, and that is handed on, if it is handed on at all, as a set of relationships and a
client list rather than as a machine. The mistake is not knowing which you have … Decide which you
are building and write it down, because the decision changes what you do this year: the practice
needs chapter sixteen's number, and its handover page is shorter and about people; the asset needs
the stranger's read, now, while the stranger is imaginary; and chapter twenty-eight is open to both."*
— Monday 4: *"If it is the practice, item 1 is still due and is mostly names; if it is the asset,
items 1 to 3 are the work, and they are due before anyone asks for them."*

## 9. Monday 3's "businesslike friend" and "what it is worth"; Monday 5 is a rule, not a Monday

**Quote** — Monday 3: *"Give the monthly page and the last two years' accounts to your accountant or
to a businesslike friend, with one instruction: tell me what this business earns and what it is
worth, without asking me anything. Write down every question they had to ask."* — Monday 5: *"If you
leave, leave. When the day comes — a manager, a buyer, a successor — take Norris's advice as he took
it: set the date, hand over the whole thing, and do not stay in the background. But only after items
1 to 3 exist …"*

**Lens** — (e) concrete enough to fail at; §4 no hedging.

**What's wrong** — "A businesslike friend" is a category the reader can satisfy with anyone;
"what it is worth" asks a friend for an appraisal the chapter has just spent Move 3 saying a price
is not. The failable version names the person by what they have never seen (your business) and what
they must produce (three numbers, and a list of questions). Monday 5 has no "this week" in it — it
is Move 3's advice restated with "if," and its only action ("set the date") is conditional on a day
that may be years off. Either give it a this-week action (write the sentence now, dated) or fold it
into Monday 4. "As he took it" also imports finding 1's compression; the advice was given to Norris
by others, and he passed it on.

**Rewrite** — Monday 3: *"Run the stranger's read. Give the monthly page and the last two years'
accounts to one person who has run a business and has never seen yours — your accountant, or the
owner of the shop two doors down — with one instruction: write down what this business earns, what
it costs to run, and what you could not tell from the page, without asking me anything. Their third
list is the handover's missing lines; each one is a fact that lives only in your head."* — Monday 5:
*"Write the leaving sentence and date it. "When I hand this over, I hand over the whole thing on
[date], and I do not stay in the background." The advice Norris was given and passed on. It is only
safe to keep once items 1 to 3 exist, because a clean departure hands the stranger the business
exactly as it is, and that is the day they find out what it is."*

## Overall note

The rule is the right one, the opening is the reader's, the figures are exact and kept apart, and
the four §6i prohibitions hold — the chapter never says the departure caused the loss, never calls
the $67m a worth, never blurs profit with EBITDA with loss, and never reaches for 2024. What needs
doing is to give the story back its dates. The read of the books that the chapter hands to the new
manager as his "first act" was, by his own words, commissioned at the end of 2022 under the
founders; the loss the chapter says was "in the books before Norris left" was projected to
shareholders in July 2022 at $327,000 and filed at $1.8 million; and both sentences are already in
the registered sources (findings 1, 2). Put them in and the chapter's thesis — the founders kept an
asset's books as a practice keeps them, and found out when outsiders read them — is shown rather
than narrated, and Move 3's test loses its invented "first weeks." Then the reporter's sentence comes
out of Norris's quotation marks (finding 3), the valuation's warning is argued from the unfiled
reports instead of asserted from the tagline (finding 4), the practice is given a handover and not
only an ending (finding 5), the after-April silence is said once (finding 6), the verdicts and the
ledger come off the stage (finding 7), the pointers to chapter twenty-three and the index are made
true (finding 8), Monday 3 names a person and Monday 5 becomes something to write this week
(finding 9), and four words the articles do not contain are put back (finding 10). Nothing needs a
new fact.
