# Craft review — Chapter 05, "Leaving Well"

Reviewed 2026-08-26. Scope: prose craft only; facts and figures assumed correct.
Constraint honoured throughout: the narrator stays invisible; all rewrites keep the
constitution's voice rules (second person for the reader, third for the case, no hedging
in Monday, numbers stay).

The short version: this chapter is close. Move 1 and Move 2 are the best sustained prose
in Part I — "the gap between those two facts has started to feel like a personal failing"
and "four years of evenings will produce four years of evenings, not a business" are a
person writing, not a machine. The findings below are mostly places where the book's own
machinery — the template, the debt register, the section furniture — becomes audible.

---

## Findings (ranked by impact)

### 1. The consultancy reader gets pushed to third person at their worst moment

**Quote:** "Told to leave on repeat paid revenue covering their floor, those readers get
a bar they can only clear by first doing the thing it is meant to authorise."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** This subsection exists for exactly one reader — the one whose business
cannot accumulate — and at the moment the chapter hands them the worse news, it steps
back and discusses "those readers" in the third person. The surrounding prose is already
in second person ("anything needing you to answer a phone at eleven on a Tuesday"; "You
cannot bill hours you do not have"), so this one sentence is the only place the chapter
looks away from the person it is talking to.

**Rewrite:** "Told to leave on repeat paid revenue covering your floor, you get a bar you
can only clear by first doing the thing it is meant to authorise."

### 2. The climax of the case is stepped on by the next line of bookkeeping

**Quote:** "He went full time on 1 April 2010. That year the business took $42,589.90 net
of refunds and returned $25,904.66."

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** The leaving is the event the whole case has been walking toward, and it
gets one flat sentence before the accounting resumes. Worse, the 2010 numbers are left
inert when they are the proof of the chapter's own thesis — that he left when it was
boring and the business did not need him to be excited. Make the numbers carry that beat
instead of just sitting after it.

**Rewrite:** "He went full time on 1 April 2010, and the business barely noticed:
$42,589.90 net of refunds that year, $25,904.66 returned." (Same facts; the "barely
noticed" is licensed by the later boundary — he worked *less* after leaving because the
product was mature.)

### 3. The counter-case's second paragraph is the debt register talking, not the book

**Quote:** "What he did not have was the evidence standard set out above. He went full
time with no customers at all; revenue began months afterwards. The six-month threshold
is what this case tests, not the recommendation to leave only after repeated paid revenue
covers your floor."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear / warmth without slack.

**What's wrong:** The scoping is required — the register says Gymdesk counters only the
threshold advice, and that honesty must survive — but the third sentence is written in
the register's own admin word order ("is what this case tests, not the recommendation
to"). The reader can hear the filing system. Say the same true thing in the book's voice:
lead with the fact, let the classification follow from it.

**Rewrite:** "What he did not have was the evidence standard set out above: no customers
at all on the day he went full time, and no revenue for months after. So this tests the
threshold, not the advice to leave only once repeated paid revenue covers your floor."

### 4. "The next block in this chapter" — a pointer to the furniture, and it points at the wrong thing

**Quote:** "Which is exactly why the threshold has to be written down while you are calm —
the subject of <a>chapter four</a> — and why the next block in this chapter matters more
than anything above it."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (template seams).

**What's wrong:** "Block" is the book's production vocabulary (`.counter`, `.owed`)
surfacing in the prose, and the pointer is ambiguous besides: the next thing on the page
is the Dutch law panel, which is not about thresholds at all. The reader is being pointed
at the chapter's own div structure instead of at the thing itself. If the counter-case
was meant, name it; if Monday item four was meant, name that.

**Rewrite:** "Which is exactly why the threshold has to be written down while you are
calm — the subject of chapter four, and of the fourth item on Monday's list." (Shorter;
if the intended referent was the counter-case: "…and why the counter-case at the end of
this chapter matters more than anything above it.")

### 5. Four consecutive sections open by counting their own contents

**Quote:** "Two things in that case matter more than the headline." (Also: "Three parts,
replacing the three above rather than supplementing them." / "Three, and the third is the
one that will actually happen to you." / "Three questions have statutory answers in the
Netherlands…")

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** Any one of these is fine, and "Three, and the third is the one that
will actually happen to you" is genuinely good. But four section-openings in a row that
announce an inventory is the template becoming audible — the chapter numbering its
furniture before showing it. Vary the weakest one; the other three then read as choices
rather than a tic.

**Rewrite (for the first, the weakest):** "The headline is the four years. Two quieter
details matter more." ("The first is…" / "The second is…" still follow cleanly.)

### 6. The book congratulates itself on its own construction

**Quote:** "That case gets its own answer further down, rather than a promise that some
later chapter will handle it."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** The second clause is the invisible narrator turning to camera — the
book announcing a virtue it is about to perform anyway. A writer who trusts the reader
just delivers the answer further down; the announcement is the machine explaining its
compliance.

**Rewrite:** "That case gets its own answer further down." (Cut the last eleven words;
nothing is lost — the answer arrives two paragraphs later and speaks for itself.)

### 7. Move 3 opens with the template's own label for the move

**Quote:** "The transferable content is not \"wait four years.\""

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** "Transferable content" is the constitution's name for what this section
is supposed to contain ("the transferable principle"). Opening the section by naming its
slot is the seam showing — a person who loves this subject says what carries over, not
what category of prose they are now producing.

**Rewrite:** "What transfers is not \"wait four years.\""

### 8. The chapter's wittiest image seats the reader at McKenzie's desk

**Quote:** "work done in January still sells in June while you are asleep at your desk in
Nagoya."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair (person discipline).

**What's wrong:** The joke is good — earnings arriving while the owner naps through the
salaryman afternoon — but the Nagoya desk belongs to the case, and the book's person rule
says the reader never sits in it. Give the image back to McKenzie and the wit survives
with the rule intact; it may even sharpen, because it becomes a fact rather than a
fancy.

**Rewrite:** "McKenzie's January work was still selling in June while he slept at his
desk in Nagoya."

### 9. A bar nobody would decline to clear

**Quote:** "Two published annual cycles is a high bar and most readers will not want to
clear it."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** Diction slip: every reader would love to clear this bar; what they will
not accept is the wait it implies. As written the sentence says the opposite of what the
ear expects and stalls the reader for a beat at the head of an important list.

**Rewrite:** "Two published annual cycles is a high bar, and most readers will not wait
for it."

---

## Overall note

The single change that would most improve this chapter: warm up the caseless stretch.
"When the business cannot accumulate" is the chapter's coldest four hundred words, and it
sits exactly where its most anxious reader lives — the consultant or agency owner who has
just been told the main standard is circular for them. The honesty is right ("No case in
these pages supports this version" must stay), but honesty is not the same as bareness.
The proof that texture works without a case is already in the section: "signed in March,
worked in May, invoiced in June, paid in August, and only if nobody is slow" is the best
sentence in it, precisely because it has watched an invoice age. Keep that reader in
"you" the whole way (finding 1), and give the other two bullets one sentence each of that
same lived grain — what a rung-two commitment from a buyer who knows you are still
employed actually sounds like; what the written consequence looks like on the page. No
new cases, no added claims, no narrator: just the same generosity the employed-path
reader gets everywhere else in the chapter.
