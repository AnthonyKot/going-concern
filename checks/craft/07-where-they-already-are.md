# Craft review — Chapter 07, "Where They Already Are"

Reviewed 2026-08-26. Scope: prose craft only; facts and figures assumed correct.
Constraint honoured throughout: the narrator stays invisible; all rewrites keep the
constitution's voice rules (second person for the reader, third for the case, no hedging
in Monday, numbers stay, counter-case discipline stays).

The short version: the reasoning is the best in the book so far — "Organic search is not
ungatekept; it is gatekept more politely", "That is not a channel he built but a fact
about the world he found", "clinics that have not yet decided their no-show problem is a
software problem are not searching for anything" are a person writing. Two things hold
the chapter back: it argues itself out of its central vocabulary and then quietly reverts
to it, and Move 2 — the load-bearing move — is a quotation file rather than a told story.

---

## Findings (ranked by impact)

### 1. The chapter corrects "owned versus rented," then hands the reader the corrected-away words

**Quote:** "His words, not this book's, and the ownership language needs correcting.
Owning an article is not owning its rank … The distinction that holds is narrower —
whether effort spent this month still produces customers in six months without further
spending. Accumulating against contemporaneous." — then, in Boundaries: "What survives is
owned versus rented, which is a property of channels rather than of that particular
channel." — and in Monday: "**Sort your current channels into owned and rented.** …
Anything where the answer is "nothing" is rented, which is fine, as long as you know."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** The correction paragraph is one of the chapter's finest moves — it takes
the case's own slogan and shows it doesn't survive inspection. Then Boundaries and Monday
both revert to the exact framing the chapter just called wrong. A writer who owned this
distinction would not lose it two sections later; as it stands, the chapter's sharpest
piece of thinking is overruled by its own action list. (Same shape as the pilot's top
finding: a Monday list contradicting the chapter's own method.)

**Rewrite:** Propagate the corrected pair. Boundaries: "What survives is accumulating
against contemporaneous — a property of channels, not of that particular channel."
Monday 2: "**Sort your current channels by what accumulates.** For each, answer one
question: if I stop this month, what do I still have in March? Anything where the answer
is "nothing" is contemporaneous, which is fine, as long as you know." Zero new facts; the
Monday item's own March test already defines the corrected terms.

### 2. Move 2 gives the harvest before the bet, in the source's formatting

**Quote:** "He published the numbers each December. For 2009: "Visits: 546,000 … Unique
Visitors: 470,000 … Page Views: 1.6 million" and "Traffic sources of note: Google (48%),
AdWords (20%)". For 2010: "Visits: 777,000 (up from 546k) …""

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** The case opens on two paragraphs of spliced analytics labels — numbers
the figures block directly below already carries — and only afterwards reveals the
mechanism ("the freelancer-produced content -> SEO engine that made my year in 2008").
Result before cause means the reader never passes through the moment of not-knowing: a
man commissioning $3,000 of freelance articles about bingo in 2008, with no way to know
they would ever rank. Everything needed for the story is on the page; it is told in the
order of an annual report, and the verbatim "Visits:" / "Unique Visitors:" capitalisation
is a summarizer's seam — the source's dashboard pasted in, context and all ("Binghoo
(11%)" is unglossed; either ellipt it from the quote or gloss it, `pending fact-check`).

**Rewrite:** Reorder: intro paragraph (Hello World, 90/90) → the 2008 engine and the
mini-sites — the bet — → the December numbers as the payoff, written in the book's voice:
"He published the numbers each December: 546,000 visits in 2009, 777,000 the year after,
Google carrying just under half both years and paid ads a steady fifth." Keep verbatim
quotes only where they carry his voice (the 24-cents line, "made my year in 2008", the
10x); let the figures block hold the rest. Cuts words overall; zero new facts.

### 3. CSS classes are talking inside the four questions

**Quote:** "The block below failed this question and could not work its way out." — and,
in question three: "a launch does not, which is chapter six's Product Hunt block."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music — the template seam showing.

**What's wrong:** The four questions are the chapter's transferable core, and twice inside
them the prose points at the book's furniture instead of at a business. "Block" is the
stylesheet's word (`.limit`, `.counter`), not a writer's. Pointing is fine — "the case
below ran three on precisely that arrangement" in Move 1 works because it points at a
case — but a block is a thing only the book's builder can see.

**Rewrite:** "Appointment Reminder, below, failed this question and could not work its
way out." And: "a launch does not, which chapter six's Product Hunt launch already
showed." Same length; naming the second product early spoils nothing and warms the list.

### 4. The chapter narrates its own construction at the head of a strong paragraph

**Quote:** "Question four is answered shallowly on purpose."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** This is the debt register's admin voice surfacing — the book describing
how it built the chapter rather than telling the reader what to do. The paragraph it
opens is good (the month-one number versus chapter fourteen's arithmetic, "disqualify the
obviously ruinous"), which by the ranking rule makes the false note at its head a major
one, not a minor one.

**Rewrite:** "Answer question four shallowly, on purpose." One word-class change turns
the self-description into method; the rest of the paragraph ("Do not settle question four
now") is already in that register and follows without a seam.

### 5. Monday 3 sets a review date the chapter has taught the reader nothing to read at

**Quote:** "**Pick the two you will practise for six months.** Write the date six months
out. Practised channels cannot be evaluated at week three, and deciding the review date
now is what stops you quitting in week three."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** By the chapter's own account the early signal is "indistinguishable
from noise," Appointment Reminder shows four months of an unbuilt channel proves nothing,
and the owed block defines proper practice as "a year or more." So the reader arrives at
the six-month date with no instruction for what the review can actually decide — the item
is a date without a question, which fails "concrete enough to fail at."

**Rewrite:** "**Pick the two you will practise for six months.** Write the date six
months out; deciding it now is what stops you quitting in week three. At the date, judge
whether you got better, not whether it paid: the skill shows before the returns do."
(Cuts "Practised channels cannot be evaluated at week three, and" to pay for the added
sentence; the skill-before-returns claim is Move 1's "bad at first, better with
repetition" plus "take months to produce anything" — no new facts.)

### 6. A subjectless fragment stumbles into one of the chapter's best lines

**Quote:** "Better than cutting what you are not committed to: the question is not
whether a channel earns its money but whether it earns the hours you must keep feeding
it."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "Better than cutting" has no subject — the reader must backtrack a
sentence to work out that the autopilot arrangement is what's better — and the stumble
sits directly in front of the money-versus-hours line, which is among the most quotable
sentences in the chapter. A ramp should be invisible here.

**Rewrite:** "That arrangement beats cutting what you are not committed to: the question
is not whether a channel earns its money but whether it earns the hours you must keep
feeding it."

### 7. The figures block shows a collapse the prose never explains

**Quote:** "Trial downloads · 56,000 → 21,000" (figures block) — while the prose says only
"His online trials went from seventeen thousand to seventy-two thousand while the rate
fell by a quarter, and sales rose".

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** Downloads falling by nearly two thirds looks like a business in trouble,
and the explanation — the trial moved from a download to the browser — appears nowhere in
the chapter's prose, only in the Reading list ("the funnel shift from downloads to online
trials"). A detail's context left behind; the reader is left to invent a story for a
number the book put in front of them.

**Rewrite:** In the rate section: "Trials went from a download to the browser, and from
seventeen thousand to seventy-two thousand, while the rate fell by a quarter — and sales
rose". To pay for the added clause, trim "The lesson generalises past analytics and is
the most portable thing here" to "The lesson generalises past analytics." — the trailing
clause is the book grading its own content anyway.

### 8. The book surfaces as a speaking entity

**Quote:** "His words, not this book's, and the ownership language needs correcting."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack — apparatus in prose.

**What's wrong:** "This book" steps on stage as a character with opinions, the closest the
chapter comes to a narrator admission. The attribution work is already done — the
previous paragraph opens "He states the distinction himself."

**Rewrite:** "The framing is his, and the ownership language needs correcting." Shorter,
same attribution, no entity.

### 9. One arrow in the figures block means something different from the other six

**Quote:** "Content spend → sales · $3,000 → $30,000"

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** Six rows use "→" to mean 2009-to-2010; this row uses it to mean
spend-becomes-sales. A reader scanning the block reads it as content spend rising tenfold
in a year before the caption corrects them.

**Rewrite:** Relabel the row: key "$3,000 of writing", value "$30,000 of sales".

### 10. An earthquake arrives without a name

**Quote:** "he writes, listing burnout, consulting work and an earthquake"

**Lens:** Warmth without slack.

**What's wrong:** The list is the block's most generous gesture toward the founder, and
the reader halts on its last word — whose earthquake, where? The deadpan works only if
the reader isn't derailed wondering whether it's a metaphor.

**Rewrite:** "listing burnout, consulting work and the Tōhoku earthquake" — `pending
fact-check` (that the March 2011 earthquake, in the Japan he lived in, is the one meant;
this is the review's only fix requiring a fact not on the page besides the optional
Binghoo gloss in finding 2).

---

## Overall note

The single most improving change is structural: re-tell Move 2 as bet-then-harvest in the
book's own voice. Put the 2008 engine — $3,000 of freelance writing on bingo topics,
commissioned before anyone knew it would rank — ahead of the December numbers, and
translate the pasted analytics labels into prose, keeping verbatim quotes only for the
lines that carry McKenzie's voice. One reordering fixes the missing moment of
not-knowing, the summarizer's seams (the "Visits:" formatting, Binghoo), the duplication
with the figures block, and makes the limit block land harder — because the reader will
have felt how long the 2008 bet took to pay before being told the second product needed
customers sooner than that.
