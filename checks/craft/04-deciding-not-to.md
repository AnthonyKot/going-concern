# Craft review — Chapter 04, "Deciding Not To"

Reviewed 2026-08-26. Craft only; all figures assumed correct. Findings ranked by impact.

The short version: this is the most carefully argued chapter in Part I, and its care is
audible — the lede is perfect, "Expect to want to move it. That wanting is data" earns its
place in any book on this subject, and the Monday list's crossed-out-list action (item 3)
is the best single instruction in the book so far. The weaknesses are all one weakness
wearing different clothes: the chapter keeps showing the reader its filing system. It counts
its points before making them, points at its own layout blocks, and once lets its revision
vocabulary leak into the prose. The rigor is real; the seams make it read as process rather
than conviction.

---

## 1. The chapter's most careful paragraph narrates its own layout

**Quote:**
> That the menu has to be wider than *stop* is demonstrated by the block below, where the
> last option on the list is the one that saved a business. That you should decide *which*
> one now, rather than in the room on the day, is a different claim and a smaller one. The
> block below does not prove it — the man in it improvised, and it worked. What the block
> does show is the alternative in operation

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (template seams).

**What's wrong:** "The block below" three times, plus "the man in it" for someone the chapter
is about to name anyway. This paragraph carries the chapter's hardest-won distinction — four
external review passes, per the register — and it reads like the margin notes from those
passes left in place. A person who loves this subject points at the case; a machine following
the rules points at the div.

**Rewrite** (same facts, slightly shorter):

> That the menu has to be wider than *stop*, Gymdesk demonstrates below — the last option on
> the list is the one that saved the business. That you should decide *which* one now, rather
> than in the room on the day, is a different claim and a smaller one. Gymdesk does not prove
> it: Galperin improvised, and it worked. What his case does show is the alternative in
> operation

## 2. Move 1 steps back to "people" at exactly the moment it should say "you"

**Quote:**
> The hard part is not the discipline. It is knowing what to put in it — because the thing
> that kills people here is not weakness, it is that by month twenty they have accumulated a
> genuinely impressive pile of evidence that means nothing.

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** Every sentence in Move 1 up to this point is aimed at the reader — their
evenings, their sense of themselves as a person who finishes things. Then, at the section's
sharpest moment, the prose flinches to third person, and the reader is invited to file
themselves under the exceptions. ("Kills" also brushes the banned war register, so one fix
covers both.)

**Rewrite** (shorter):

> The hard part is not the discipline. It is knowing what to put in it — because what undoes
> you here is not weakness, it is that by month twenty you will have accumulated a genuinely
> impressive pile of evidence that means nothing.

## 3. The family money — the case's highest-stakes fact — is filed under "Along the way"

**Quote:**
> Along the way she drained her savings, converted to a Delaware corporation, and between
> 2023 and early 2024 raised $265,000 from family, friends and physicians.

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** This is the one place the reader could feel that it was still, five years
in, genuinely undecided — she believed enough to take money from people who knew her, and the
arithmetic that ended it had still never been run. "Along the way" is the diction of
logistics; it flattens the beat that makes "Then she did the arithmetic" land as a hinge
rather than a next item.

**Rewrite** (adds seven words; pay for it by cutting ", in her own post-mortem," from the
paragraph above — the retrospective framing is already carried by the clarifications
paragraph):

> She drained her savings, converted to a Delaware corporation, and between 2023 and early
> 2024 — five years in, the arithmetic still unrun — raised $265,000 from family, friends
> and physicians.

## 4. "The substitution" arrives with a definite article and no antecedent

**Quote:**
> Draelos identifies the substitution herself, and her prescription is one sentence long:
> *"ask them to buy it before you build it."*

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (template seams).

**What's wrong:** The chapter never uses the word "substitution" before this — the concept
(milestones standing in for evidence) is built beautifully, but the *term* belongs to the
book's working vocabulary, not to anything the reader has been given. It reads like a note
from the drafting process. And the sentence is sitting on a better word: she is an MD, and
"prescription" is already there waiting for its pair.

**Rewrite** (shorter):

> Draelos makes the diagnosis herself, and her prescription is one sentence long: *"ask them
> to buy it before you build it."*

---

## 5. Three of the chapter's most careful paragraphs open with the same counting tic

**Quote:**
> Two clarifications before drawing anything from this.
> — then —
> Two claims live in that sentence and they are not equally supported, so take them apart.
> — then —
> Two, and they are serious enough that the rest of the chapter should be read through them.

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** Once is a voice; three times in one chapter is a template. Each of these
announces an enumeration the reader is about to get anyway, so the announcement is the
throat-clear the constitution bans from openings, relocated to mid-chapter.

**Rewrite:** Cut the first outright — open the paragraph directly on "Cydoc is not purely a
self-funded business of the kind this book is otherwise about…"; the two items follow
immediately and count themselves. The other two openings then read as deliberate rather than
habitual, and can stand.

## 6. The inventory paragraph slips into summarizer's diction

**Quote:**
> A HIPAA-compliant platform on web, Android and iOS, deployed in multiple practices across
> multiple specialties. A study run with **eighteen medical students**, not practising
> doctors, showing the system *"saved 11 minutes per visit"* — with real customers later
> reporting similar savings of around ten.

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** "Multiple… multiple" is the vague quantifier of someone paraphrasing a
source, and "similar savings of around ten" drops its unit — ten what? A practitioner keeps
the units on and does not say "multiple" twice in nine words.

**Rewrite** (same length):

> A HIPAA-compliant platform on web, Android and iOS, deployed in working practices across
> several specialties. A study run with **eighteen medical students**, not practising
> doctors, showing the system *"saved 11 minutes per visit"* — savings real customers later
> put at about ten minutes.

## 7. Monday item 1 makes the reader recount

**Quote:**
> **Write one sentence with a number, a date, and a consequence.** The template: *if fewer
> than [N] strangers have paid me [amount] by [date], I will [specific action].* If you
> cannot fill all four slots, you do not have a rule, and the missing slot is the one you
> are avoiding.

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** The bold lead counts three, the template shows four brackets, and the
follow-up counts four. It parses — [N] and [amount] are both "a number" — but this is the
one sentence in the chapter the reader is told to execute exactly, in a chapter about
writing rules precise enough to honour. A hiccup here costs more than anywhere else.

**Rewrite** (shorter — stop counting):

> **Write one sentence with a number, a date, and a consequence.** The template: *if fewer
> than [N] strangers have paid me [amount] by [date], I will [specific action].* If you
> cannot fill every slot, you do not have a rule, and the missing slot is the one you are
> avoiding.

---

## Overall note

One pass through the chapter deleting every reference to its own machinery — "the block
below," "the man in it," "two clarifications before," "the substitution" — would do more than
any local fix. The chapter's epistemic care is its greatest asset: it separates what Gymdesk
proves from what it merely shows, it weighs its own evidence, it names its boundaries. But
that care currently arrives half as argument and half as visible apparatus, and the apparatus
is what makes a scrupulous chapter read like a compliant one. Point every self-reference at
the case, the person, or the reader instead, and the same sentences — almost unchanged —
read as a person who has thought hard about stopping, rather than a process that ran
correctly. Findings 1, 4, and 5 all fall out of this single pass.
