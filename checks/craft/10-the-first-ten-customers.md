# Craft review — ch. 10, The First Ten Customers

Reviewed against CONTEXT.md §3 (template), §4 (voice), §6c (ch. 10 → 11 handoff), §6d (caveat
placement). Facts assumed correct throughout; no proposed fix moves a caveat out of the reader's
path in a way §6d forbids, and none touches the §6c boundary — the two-readings section keeps both
readings in every rewrite below.

Sweep result for the five known failure modes: #2 (apparatus surfacing) is this chapter's dominant
infection — six instances, two of them hard rule violations (findings 2 and 3). #3 (second-person
flinch) appears three times, twice at sharp moments (findings 6, 7, 10). #1 (verdict-first) is
mostly under control inside the paragraphs but shows in the centre section's opener (finding 4).
#4 (self-counting openers) appears once, inside finding 1's quote. #5 (summarizer's seams) is
largely absent — figures are glossed, the vague quantifier ("tens of thousands") is explicitly
priced as indefinite. The chapter-specific findings are 1, 5, and 8.

---

## 1. The launch beat is smothered by two paragraphs of source accounting

**Quote:** "That first week is the chapter's title, and one division makes it concrete. If Informly
held steady, the new $476 at $69 a month is **about seven customers in seven days**. That division
is this book's, not a figure Norris published, and it assumes the older business stayed flat across
the week — which is likely and unstated in the source." — followed immediately by — "Two figures in
this chapter need reconciling before they are used. The $952 is a total reached *within a week* of
a 26 June launch — so early July — while the ladder below records June closing at $907. The sources
give the launch date and the interval but not the measurement dates, so treat the sequence as this
book's reading: June ends at $907, and the first seven days of trading finish a few days later at
$952."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (and story craft). Move 2 is the load-bearing part of the chapter by
constitution, and this is its best beat — a year of grinding to $476, then doubling in a week.

**What's wrong:** Two sentences after that beat lands, the chapter stops the story for ~130 words
of audit: an attribution, an assumption, a reconciliation, measurement dates. This is §6d's exact
target — sourcing mechanics in the argument's path — and the Reading list already carries the
division's full mechanics verbatim ("it holds only if Informly's revenue was flat across that
week"). Two other apparatus notes ride along: "That first week is the chapter's title" (the chapter
narrating itself — and the title says ten, the week gave seven) and "Two figures in this chapter
need reconciling" (a section opener counting its own contents).

**Rewrite** (both paragraphs → one, ~65 words; the measurement-dates point moves to the Reading
list's existing arithmetic note):

> One division makes that week concrete. If Informly held steady, the new $476 at $69 a month is
> **about seven customers in seven days** — this book's division, not a figure Norris published.
> The week straddles a month's end: the ladder below closes June at $907, and the seventh day of
> trading lands a few days later at $952.

Reading note gains one clause: "…Norris publishes the two totals, not the customer count, and the
launch date and interval rather than measurement dates."

## 2. Drafting history confessed in the chapter's most delicate section

**Quote:** "The December cancellations are retention evidence. They also contain acquisition
evidence, and an earlier draft of this chapter denied the first in order to claim the second, which
was wrong: customers leaving inside a month is exactly what failing to hold people looks like."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair (apparatus surfacing; narrator admission).

**What's wrong:** "An earlier draft of this chapter… which was wrong" is the book's revision history
in the reader's path — §6d keeps that in CONTEXT.md, and the invisible-narrator rule forbids the
confession. The correction §6c recorded is real and must survive; the confession of having needed it
must not. The reader should get the rule, not the changelog.

**Rewrite** (same facts, both readings kept per §6c, shorter):

> The December cancellations are retention evidence — customers leaving inside a month is exactly
> what failing to hold people looks like. The same cancellations are also acquisition evidence, and
> neither reading cancels the other.

## 3. The narrator surfaces as "I"

**Quote:** "Anyone wanting WP Curve's own revenue will not find it here, and neither did I."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack (narrator invisibility).

**What's wrong:** The only first person in the chapter, and a direct violation of the standing
constraint. The line is trying to be warm — an honest shrug — but the warmth has to come from the
plainness of the admission, not from a narrator stepping onstage to make it.

**Rewrite:**

> Anyone wanting WP Curve's own revenue will not find it here — the reports never separate it.

## 4. The centre section opens by pricing its own case

**Quote:** "This is the chapter's centre, and what makes WP Curve worth the space is that the same
class of discovery arrived through two different instruments, four months apart."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear (apparatus surfacing).

**What's wrong:** "The chapter's centre" and "worth the space" are the case budget talking —
CONTEXT.md's selection vocabulary in the reader's ear, at the head of the most important section.
The same tic appears in Move 2: "It is a service business of the least glamorous kind, which is why
it is here" — cut "which is why it is here" and let the next clause do the justifying. A practitioner
just says the thing the machinery was built to notice.

**Rewrite:**

> The same discovery arrived twice, four months apart, through two instruments that cannot
> substitute for each other.

## 5. The chapter misses its own best collision: the thesis died in the middle of the best month

**Quote:** "Then, in October: *'from the 50-odd web agencies Dan and I have spoken to'*, even after
reshaping the offering to suit them, nothing. So they stopped: *'completely remove our agency
offering and only offer our services to business owners.'*"

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** Move 2 has already told the reader that October signed 59 customers against a
previous best of 13 and jumped MRR to $6,196. Move 3 never connects the two facts: they abandoned
their intended buyer *during their best month ever*. That collision is the whole reason the
discipline is hard — the totals were screaming yes while the sorted conversations said no — and both
facts are already on the page, three paragraphs apart, unlinked.

**Rewrite** (add one sentence after "So they stopped: …"; the ~25 words are paid for by the cuts in
findings 1 and 4):

> They stopped in the middle of their best month — the same October that signed 59 customers of the
> other kind. Nothing in the totals said anything was wrong; the fifty conversations did.

## 6. Flinch to "someone" at the centre section's point of application

**Quote:** "Which is why the fourth question — *did delivery reveal the wrong customer?* — cannot be
answered in advance and has to be asked deliberately once real delivery records exist, by someone
looking for a pattern in who is unhappy rather than counting how many."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** The centre section's entire payoff lands on the reader's desk — and at exactly
that moment the sentence hands the job to "someone." This is failure mode 3 in its purest form: the
passive-plus-third construction at the sharpest line.

**Rewrite:**

> Which is why the fourth question — *did delivery reveal the wrong customer?* — cannot be answered
> in advance. You ask it deliberately, once real delivery records exist, looking for a pattern in
> who is unhappy rather than counting how many.

## 7. "The reader's test"

**Quote:** "The reader's test is correspondingly wider."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** Third person for the reader ("the reader's"), plus a connective from a journal
article ("correspondingly"), at the pivot where the case is handed over for application.

**Rewrite:**

> Your test is wider.

## 8. Monday has six actions; the template caps it at five — and the first two are one action

**Quote:** "1. **Carry the previous decisions into one row per customer.** … 2. **Add what delivery
actually required.** …"

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (template compliance; the seam shows as administrative sprawl).

**What's wrong:** §3 says three to five numbered actions; this list has six, and it reads long
because items 1 and 2 are the same action — build the row — split by which chapters the columns come
from, which is the operating record's internal bookkeeping showing through. Merging them restores
the cap without losing a column, and item 3 (the §6c-mandated ch. 11 columns) is untouched.

**Rewrite** (items 1 and 2 → one item, all columns kept):

> **Build one row per customer.** Carry in the segment and refusals from chapter six, the
> acquisition mechanism from seven, the ask and objection from eight, the price and promised scope
> from nine. Add **why they bought now** — the trigger, not the reason they liked it — any
> **discount or concession** granted to close, and what delivery actually required: actual hours,
> work outside scope, rework, failures and promises missed, and one plain flag: **was delivery
> completed**. Blank cells are missing instrumentation, not zeroes.

## 9. The customer-count paragraph stitches five quotes into one breath

**Quote:** "And the line that makes the scale of that honest: *'Our previous best month of customer
signup was 13.'* By the end of November, 155. By New Year, *'We started December with 155 customers
and welcomed the new year with 178 customers'*, at *'a final MRR of $9,812'* — *'we fell $188
short'* of ten thousand — which they put at *'an annual revenue run rate of $117,744.'*"

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "Makes the scale of that honest" is syntax under strain, "By the end of November,
155" is immediately repeated inside the next quote ("started December with 155 customers"), and the
closing sentence carries four quotations on two em-dashes. The best paragraph of raw evidence in the
chapter, delivered out of breath.

**Rewrite** (all numbers kept, one duplicate cut, one sentence split):

> And the line that gives those figures their scale: *"Our previous best month of customer signup
> was 13."* By New Year, *"We started December with 155 customers and welcomed the new year with 178
> customers"*, at *"a final MRR of $9,812"* — *"we fell $188 short"* of ten thousand. They put that
> at *"an annual revenue run rate of $117,744."*

## 10. "A reader without it" — the flinch lands on the reader most likely to be reading

**Quote:** "That is a real asset, it predates the business, and a reader without it should expect
the same process to run slower rather than to fail."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack.

**What's wrong:** This Boundaries note exists precisely for the reader who has no mentors, no
audience, no blogger emailing a list within a day of a phone call — which is nearly every reader of
this book. At the one moment Boundaries speaks directly to their situation, it steps back to "a
reader." Second person here is not decoration; it is the difference between a disclosure and a
reassurance.

**Rewrite:**

> That is a real asset, it predates the business, and if you are starting without one, expect the
> same process to run slower rather than to fail.

## Overall note

The single change: get the audit voice out of the story's path. This chapter's distinctive damage is
not thin material or verdict-first telling — the case is strong and mostly well-sequenced — it is
that the book's back office keeps walking through the front room: two paragraphs of reconciliation
on top of the launch beat, a drafting confession inside the two-readings section, a first-person
shrug in Boundaries, the case budget pricing WP Curve's "space" aloud. Findings 1–4 are one repair
executed four times: say the thing once, plainly, and let Boundaries and Reading — the rooms this
book already built for the accounting — hold the accounting. Done together they also fund the one
addition worth making (finding 5), which turns the centre section's discipline from asserted to
felt: the totals said yes, the conversations said no, and they believed the conversations.
