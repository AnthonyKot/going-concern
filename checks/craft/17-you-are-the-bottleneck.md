## 1. "Set the two together" — and then the paragraph only looks at one of them

**Quote:** "Set the two together and the distinction is visible. Here is a business that ran for
eleven years with one person on it, and which accumulated underneath itself a piece of work whose
difficulty turned on knowing code written a decade earlier — deferred for years because it was
never this week's problem, then done alone on a system that was serving customers throughout."

**Lens:** Story craft (and the practitioner's ear).

**What's wrong:** The chapter promises a collision and delivers an exhibit label. McKenzie's
method is *build time assets* — code that saves time later — and his definition of time debt is
"anything that you do which will commit you to doing unavoidable work in the future." Cegłowski's
2020 job was core code "dated back to 2009–2010" that had to be moved, alone, on a live system.
Those are the same years, and on McKenzie's own definition the same object is both: the asset of
2010 is the unavoidable work of 2020. That is the bridge between the two cases, it is the
chapter's thesis in one image, and it is nowhere in Move 2 — Move 3 gestures at it a page later
("the case above is built on that insight… the third category arriving by a route that felt like
progress") after the reader has left the cases behind. No new fact is needed; the quotations are
three paragraphs apart.

**Rewrite:** "Set the two together. McKenzie's method is to build time assets — code that keeps
working after the hours are spent — and his own definition of time debt is anything that commits
you to unavoidable work later. Pinboard's core code was written in 2009 and 2010, the years that
post was describing. By 2020 the same object was both: an asset that had run a business for a
decade, and a piece of unavoidable work that could only be done by someone who knew it, done alone
on a system that was serving customers throughout. Nothing in the intervening years' hours would
have shown it, because it was never this week's problem." (Replaces the first two sentences of
the quoted paragraph; the "Be precise" sentences that follow are handled in finding 2, so the
paragraph gets shorter overall.)

## 2. The climax is followed by three consecutive paragraphs of audit

**Quote:** "Be precise about what that shows. The record identifies no second operator and the
owner's knowledge of the old code plainly mattered; it does not tell us whether another engineer
could have done the same job given documentation and time. What it demonstrates is concentration
that was never measured — not irreplaceability, which is a stronger claim and would need evidence
nobody collected." — then — "One caution about the previous chapter, since this one is built on
the difference between quantities: ch. 16's flat line was a revenue series. It says nothing about
hours worked, and it must not be read backwards into a claim about workload. What can be said is
narrower — a business publicly documented as running with one person for eleven years, whose
revenue record stayed comparatively stable for long stretches while a technical dependency
accumulated underneath it." — then — "What neither case establishes. Neither owner ran the
experiment…"

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (and scar tissue).

**What's wrong:** The mariachi line is the best sentence in the chapter and the reader gets
roughly 230 words of defence immediately after it: the irreplaceability correction, the
revenue-for-hours correction, and the shared-limit paragraph, each written to satisfy a reviewer
who has already been satisfied. The three say one thing between them — *the records describe
work, not absence, so dependence is inferred* — and the Reading note already says it. §6d allows
one principal caveat in the argument; "What neither case establishes" is that caveat, and it
changes how the reader applies the advice, so it stays. The other two are cuts to one clause
each. Keep every corrected fact: "concentration, not irreplaceability" survives as four words
inside the reordered paragraph of finding 1; "revenue is not hours" survives as one sentence at
the Pinboard entry (finding 4), which is where a reader reaching back to ch. 16 actually needs it.

**Rewrite:** Delete the "Be precise" paragraph and the "One caution" paragraph. Append to the
finding-1 paragraph: "The record names no second operator and does not say whether another
engineer could have done it with documentation and time — concentration that was never measured,
not irreplaceability." Then trim the survivor: "**What neither case establishes.** Neither owner
ran the experiment this chapter is about: being unavailable. No figure in either record measures
what six weeks of illness would have done; both are inferred from the owner's own description of
the work. Nor does the comparison show McKenzie's business was more fragile than it looked — his
evidence answers a question about hours, and the question about dependence was never asked of
it." (Net cut of about 150 words from Move 2.)

## 3. The ordering section argues with its own reviewer instead of with the reader

**Quote:** "What follows is a default, not a sequence. The ranking rule above governs: shortest
tolerable delay first, item by item, from your own table. Categories are for making sure you looked
everywhere, and they routinely disagree with the default […] Where the default and your table
disagree, the table is right. // With that said, this is the order most businesses find:" — and
in Monday 1: "Twenty minutes, and the ranked list, not the categories, is what you work from."

**Lens:** Practitioner's ear (and scar tissue).

**What's wrong:** The eighth correction was right — the table governs — and the fix has been
stated four times: the section heading, the opening sentence, "the table is right", and a clause
smuggled into Monday. "The order most businesses find" is the one false note: the register admits
this ordering has no case behind it, and a practitioner would not claim to know what most
businesses find. The disagreements list (urgent support, deployment procedure, regulatory call) is
the good part — it teaches the reader how categories mislead — and it is buried between two
restatements of the rule.

**Rewrite:** Heading stays. Open: "The ranking rule governs: shortest tolerable delay first, from
your own table. The four categories are for making sure you looked everywhere, and they will
disagree with it — urgent support is recurring work with a one-hour tolerance, a deployment
procedure is operational knowledge that stops the business at once, a safety or regulatory call
outranks any password, and some access is recoverable in a week. Where they disagree, the table
is right. With a blank table, look in this order:" Cut "What follows is a default, not a
sequence" and "most businesses find". Monday 1 ends: "Twenty minutes, and the ranked list is what
you work from."

## 4. Pinboard enters verdict-first, and the quote is announced before it lands

**Quote:** "For what a concentrated dependence looks like, take a long-running one-person
business we have already counted. Chapter sixteen used Pinboard's eleven years of revenue; the
same owner, in the same series of posts, describes what maintaining it alone eventually involved.
[…] He describes the operation itself in one line: 'Doing this on a live system is like performing
kidney transplants on a playing mariachi band.'"

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** The reader is told what Pinboard will show before seeing anything, so the
migration arrives as an illustration rather than as a surprise. The moment that could have gone
either way — ten years of putting it off, a codebase that could no longer be run on a laptop, then
a decision to do it now, alone, on a live system — is on the page but in the wrong order: the
deferral is described *after* the quote, in the next paragraph. "He describes the operation itself
in one line:" is throat-clearing before the chapter's best line. This is also where the
revenue-is-not-hours caution belongs, in one sentence, because this is the sentence that reaches
back to ch. 16.

**Rewrite:** "Chapter sixteen counted Pinboard's revenue for eleven years, and a revenue line
says nothing about hours; this chapter needs a different row of the same record. By 2020 much of
the site's core code 'dated back to 2009–2010', and things had reached the point where its owner
could not 'run a bug-for-bug identical copy of the site on a modern laptop'. It had never been
this week's problem. He spent the pandemic 'moving Pinboard onto modern versions of PHP and
MySQL' — alone, on a system serving customers throughout. 'Doing this on a live system is like
performing kidney transplants on a playing mariachi band.'" (Same quotations, reordered; the
"deferred for years because it was never this week's problem" clause moves up from the next
paragraph and is cut there.)

## 5. The McKenzie verdict introduces itself, and "genuinely" is a flinch

**Quote:** "This is a genuinely good answer to the wrong question, and it is worth being precise
about what it demonstrates. It shows that a business can be built whose recurring work fits
inside five hours a week — the first of the four categories, handled superbly. It does not show
that the other three were handled, and the post does not claim they were."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "A good answer to the wrong question" is the chapter's key on McKenzie and it is
strong; "genuinely" apologises for it, and "it is worth being precise about what it demonstrates"
tells the reader a careful paragraph is coming instead of being one. The same tic ("Be precise
about what that shows") recurs at the Pinboard pivot — finding 2 removes that one; this is the
other.

**Rewrite:** "It is a good answer to the wrong question. A business was built whose recurring work
fits inside five hours a week — the first of the four categories, handled superbly. Whether the
other three were handled, the post does not say, and does not claim to." (Rest of the paragraph
unchanged.)

## 6. The sharpest line in Move 1 steps back to "a business"

**Quote:** "A business can be excellent at the first and hollow underneath in the other three.
That combination is the subject of this chapter, because it is both the most common shape and the
one that reads, from the inside, as success."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** The paragraph before it is the chapter's best second-person writing — "it runs
on *your* five hours, and it stops for a fortnight when you are in hospital." Then, at the line
that names the reader's likely condition, the prose retreats to "a business" and "the subject of
this chapter". "Reads, from the inside, as success" is the observation that makes the reader feel
seen; it should be aimed at them.

**Rewrite:** "You can be excellent at the first and hollow in the other three, and from the inside
that reads as success. It is the most common shape there is, and it is the one this chapter is
about."

## 9. Boundaries asserts the counter-case the owed block says has not been found

**Quote:** "Some businesses have been made worse by an owner who systematised the very thing
customers were paying for."

**Lens:** Practitioner's ear (summarizer's seam).

**What's wrong:** "Some businesses have been" is the vague quantifier standing in for a case, and
the case is precisely the one the `.owed` block admits is missing. The book's rule is numbers or
nothing; here the honest form is the conditional aimed at the reader, which is also the sharper
sentence.

**Rewrite:** "Systematise the thing customers were paying for and you have removed the product,
not the bottleneck."

## 10. The owed block's last line is the chapter's real close, and "honestly" gets in its way

**Quote:** "The shape is findable in principle, because the businesses that survive it publish
and the ones that regret it sometimes do too. What makes it hard is that 'worse' here has to be
measured against a counterfactual owner who did nothing, and no business runs both arms of that
experiment. Until it is found, note the asymmetry honestly: this chapter recommends work whose
benefit shows up only in an emergency that may not arrive, and whose cost is paid immediately and
in full."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack.

**What's wrong:** The final sentence is the truest thing in the chapter about what it is asking of
the reader, and it is the last thing they read. "Honestly" is the narrator grading their own
candour; "findable in principle… sometimes do too" is the register's admin voice, and §6d asks
for one compact paragraph.

**Rewrite:** "What is owed is an owner who ran this diagnosis properly — found the real
dependencies rather than the annoying ones, wrote the rules, moved the credentials, documented the
knowledge, handed the recurring work over — and whose business got measurably worse for it:
customers who had been buying the owner's judgement and left when they met the rule, or a
delegated decision that cost more than every hour it saved. It is hard to find because 'worse'
has to be measured against an owner who did nothing, and no business runs both arms of that
experiment. Until it is found, the asymmetry stands: this chapter recommends work whose benefit
shows up only in an emergency that may not arrive, and whose cost is paid immediately and in
full."

## Overall note

Make the two cases argue. Right now Move 2 is two exhibits with a label between them and ~230
words of audit after the second; the chapter's thesis — that the hours never show the dependence
— is asserted in Move 1 and defended in Move 2, but never *demonstrated* by the cases against each
other. The demonstration is already on the page in pieces: McKenzie's 2010 method builds time
assets and defines time debt as work you have committed yourself to later; Pinboard's 2020 job is
code from 2009–2010 that had become exactly that. Put those two quotations in one paragraph
(finding 1), let the migration arrive before its verdict (finding 4), and cut the audit down to
the single principal caveat (finding 2). Move 2 loses about 150 words and gains the one moment
where the reader sees, rather than is told, that a time asset and an operational dependence can be
the same object ten years apart. Everything else in this report is trimming; that is the change.
