# Craft review — ch. 25, What Protects You

Reviewed 2026-08-28, first review of a first draft, against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §6d, the §6i entry for
this chapter and the Studio Neat and Cydoc case ledgers; every quotation checked against the "A Word
on Patents" section of `sn-howto.raw`, and `./verify.sh --strict 25` run (13 verified, 0 drifted).
All thirteen quotations are exact. Of the §6i entry's three rules, all three hold in the letter: the
patent material is carried as "two non-lawyers' reading of one jurisdiction" at the point the
quotations land; the brand bet is called "a belief, stated at the time" and never a result; Cydoc is
named as a different sum. But the Cydoc sentence holds the rule by inventing the contrast — "the
stakes, the buyers and the money were all of another order" is not what ch. 04 says about a business
with four customers paying thirty dollars a month (finding 3). Above that sit two facts the chapter
adds to its sources: the Glif "took off in January 2011" (ch. 03 says October 2010; January 2011 is
the archive date of the campaign page) and "no outside money" (nowhere), and a chronology — decision
first, imitations after — that the post does not establish (findings 1, 2). Nothing below needs a new
fact; every fix is a cut, a re-pointing, or words already in the source or in chapter three.

## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person with the right case and the right rule, who has read the post for the sequence it ought to show and the neighbouring chapters for the instruments they ought to hold.

Move 1 is right: "Something is working, and now you are afraid of losing it" is the reader's square,
the three fears (the competitor, the supplier, the trained person with the list) are all at the
reader's size, and "which is a real thing to buy, as long as you know that is what you bought" is the
chapter's honesty stated before the case. The rule passes §6d in one sentence — *an instrument
protects you only if it changes what happens on the day of the event it was bought for; if it gives you
the right to begin a process, what you bought is standing, and the protection is the process, priced
separately* — and the Decision block says it in the same terms. Move 2 is told mostly forwards, the
non-lawyer caveat lands where the post puts it (before the understanding, not after), and the closing
paragraph of Move 2 is the best sentence in the chapter: "a business at the reader's scale faced the
exact event a legal protection exists to prevent, had declined the protection on a stated reading of
what it would do on that day, and put its money into something else." Monday 1 and 4 are failable and
are the chapter.

The chapter's problem is one problem in three places, and the shape is ch. 23's: it reads its sources
for what they ought to show. First, the case is introduced with a date and a funding fact ch. 03 does
not contain and one of which ch. 03 contradicts (finding 1). Second, the post's paragraph order —
"We decided to pass" then "To put things in perspective, since launching the Glif…" — is read as a
chronology: "Then the thing they had been afraid of happened", "the response of people who had already
decided". The post is retrospective throughout; it does not say whether the imitations were on Amazon
before or after they stopped worrying about a patent, and "freaked out" is dated to the blow-up, which
is the same period "since launching the Glif" covers (finding 2). Third, "being the one the customer
chooses again" is presented as "the thing this book has been building since chapter eleven" and as
what Studio Neat "leaned on instead" — but the post's sentence is about a buyer choosing the original
over the copy, not about repeat; ch. 11's subject is whether the relationship persists, with the
warning that renewal is "evidence worth investigating, not a verdict"; and ch. 22's concentration
figure is "the share of the money that comes from the top handful", which does not say whether the
handful chose you again. The instruments are the right ones; the pointers to them are wrong (finding
4). The chapter's framework survives all three fixes, because the post does show what it needs: a fear,
a reading of what the instrument does, a decision, an event met without it, and a stated belief in
place of a result.

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "The sentence this chapter is built on is the correction";
"is not a shrug" — said of a sentence that follows "shrug our shoulders" (finding 7). (2) apparatus:
"the question this chapter asks of each one"; "The chapter uses the decision and the reasoning. It
cannot borrow an outcome the source does not report" — the ledger's instruction to the author, on the
page (finding 7). (3) flinch: none found; the reader is in the room throughout. (4) counting and
self-announcing: "Which sets the boundary."; "Two limits on the case, stated as the case states them"
(findings 5, 7). (5) seams: "which is what they were paying to avoid" (they paid nothing); "the answer
was no" for "Our guess is no" (finding 9). (6) source accounting inside the argument: the
belief-not-result caveat appears four times — Move 2's close, Move 3's "Two limits", Reading's Note
and the owed block — and the US-law caveat twice, in Move 2 where the source carries it and again in
"Two limits" (finding 5). (7) n/a.

Word count: 2,065 against a 3,000 ceiling. Findings 1–4 and 6 are roughly word-neutral; findings 5
and 7 remove about 120; finding 8 adds about 30.

## 1. "Took off in January 2011" contradicts chapter three, and "no outside money" is sourced nowhere

**Quote** — Move 2: *"Two people, no outside money, a small physical product that took off in January
2011."*

**Lens** — continuity with ch. 03/09; (b) every word the chapter adds to its source.

**What's wrong** — Ch. 03: "The campaign opened on 3 October 2010 … He linked to them the next
morning … '1 hour and 25 minutes after the post went live, we surpassed our funding goal' … By the
close on 2 November … They started shipping 'in early December, 2010'." The only "January 2011" in
ch. 03 is its Reading list — "the two campaign pages as they stood at close, via the Internet Archive:
Glif (January 2011)" — the snapshot date, not the event. A reader who goes back to ch. 03 finds the
chapter's case beginning three months before this chapter says it did. "No outside money" is in
neither ch. 03, ch. 09, the ledger nor the post; the ledger's word is "Two designers with an existing
audience", the post's is "a small company of two people", and the Kickstarter money is, by ch. 03's
own account, five thousand strangers' money paid in advance — which is the opposite of the point "no
outside money" is meant to make, if it is meant to make one. "Took off" for "blow up" is fair. Say
what ch. 03 says, in ch. 03's numbers.

**Rewrite** — *"Two people and a small moulded clamp that reached its funding goal eighty-five minutes
after one link in October 2010 and shipped in December."*

## 2. The post does not order the decision and the imitations, and the chapter's "Then" does

**Quote** — Move 2: *"Then the thing they had been afraid of happened. "To put things in perspective,
since launching the Glif, there have been a few direct imitations…" They link to them. And their
response is the response of people who had already decided what a patent would and would not have
done: "There is not much we can do…""* — Move 2, close: *"had declined the protection on a stated
reading of what it would do on that day"* (order-ambiguous, but read after "Then" it says
before).

**Lens** — (b) a story told forwards, inferences inside the source.

**What's wrong** — The post's sequence is a sequence of paragraphs, not of events. "I remember when
… the Glif started to blow up, and I was freaked out we didn't have a patent" dates the fear to
October 2010. "Ultimately, we have realized … We decided to pass" is undated. "Since launching the
Glif, there have been a few direct imitations" covers the same stretch of time as the fear. Nothing
says the imitations appeared after the decision, and nothing says the founders had "already decided"
when they did; it is at least as consistent with the post that watching two copies appear on Amazon
is part of how they "realized" what a patent would have done. The chapter needs neither reading. Its
claim — the exact event met without the instrument, and the reasoning stated — is true in either
order. Tell it as the post tells it: a retrospective in which fear, understanding, decision and
imitations are all on the page, and the counterfactual is run with the copies already in view. "They
link to them" is right (two Amazon listings, one on amazon.co.uk); "They then run the counterfactual
themselves" and "what they were counting on instead" are fair. "Their response" should become "what
they say about it", which is what it is.

**Rewrite** — *"And the thing they had been afraid of is on the same page, not as a fear but as a
fact: "To put things in perspective, since launching the Glif, there have been a few direct imitations
that have cropped up, out of Germany and Taiwan." They link to both — Amazon listings. The post does
not say whether the copies appeared before or after they stopped wanting a patent, and it does not
need to, because what they say about them is the reading they had just given: "There is not much we
can do, other than shrug our shoulders and carry on." They then run the counterfactual themselves,
with the copies in view: …"* — Move 2, close: *"a business at the reader's scale met the exact event
a legal protection exists to prevent, had read — before or while it happened — what the protection
would do on that day, declined it, and put its money into something else."*

## 3. The Cydoc contrast is invented: ch. 04's Cydoc had four customers at thirty dollars a month

**Quote** — Move 3: *"Chapter four's Cydoc held patents, in healthcare software, where the stakes, the
buyers and the money were all of another order; two decisions at two scales are not in tension, and
the difference is the sum, not the principle."*

**Lens** — §6i rule 3 (never generalise to "patents are never worth it"; name the difference from
Cydoc); continuity with ch. 04.

**What's wrong** — Ch. 04's Cydoc: a solo founder on "my $35,000 annual MD/PhD stipend", $265,000
from family, friends and physicians, four paying customers, "Contribution/doctor ~$30/mo", small
practices that "wanted to pay fewer than $100 per month". The money was not of another order; it was
of a smaller one than Studio Neat's $137,417 campaign. What ch. 04 says about the two patents is
specific and is the opposite of a different calculus: they sit in the list of milestones — "Patents. A
peer-reviewable clinical result. Deployments in real practices…" — that "satisfied" a badly written
stopping rule, and "A patent is evidence of novelty, not of demand." The ledger's own words are
"healthcare software, higher stakes, different economics", meaning the confounders it names —
integration cost, a regulated buyer with a long cycle — not a bigger business. So the honest contrast
is not scale of money; it is that ch. 04 asked what a patent proves and this chapter asks what it
does, and the two chapters' Cydoc and Glif can both hold patents or not without disagreeing. The
sentence "the difference is the sum, not the principle" is right and should stay; the sum should be
described with ch. 04's facts.

**Rewrite** — *"Chapter four's Cydoc held two United States patents, in healthcare software sold to
regulated buyers behind a four-thousand-dollar integration, and chapter four's point about them was
that a patent is evidence of novelty and not of demand — a different question, asked of a different
business, and neither chapter says whether the other's decision was wrong. Two decisions at two sums
are not in tension; the difference is the sum, not the principle."*

## 4. "Being chosen again" is put into the post and into ch. 11, and the ch. 22 pointer names the wrong figure

**Quote** — Move 3: *"The thing they leaned on instead is the thing this book has been building since
chapter eleven: being the one the customer chooses again."* — Move 3: *"Being chosen again protects the
business only where the customers who chose it are the ones paying for it — the concentration figure
from chapter twenty-two says whether they are."* — Monday 3: *"From chapter eleven's columns and
chapter twenty-two's concentration figure: which customers would a copy actually take — the ones who
chose you again, or the ones who never did"*. — Decision block: *"being the one the customers choose
again"*.

**Lens** — (d) is "being chosen again" earned from the brand sentence; continuity with ch. 11 and 22;
(e) Monday 3.

**What's wrong** — Three separate slips. (i) The post: "As long as our brand remains strong, we think
enough customers will choose the real deal versus the knockoff." That is a buyer at the point of
purchase choosing the original over the copy; it says nothing about the same customer coming back.
"Chooses again" is the book's gloss and reads as the founders'. (ii) Ch. 11: its subject is "whether
the relationship persists, and why"; its Monday adds the column "did they renew, reorder or expand",
which ch. 22 calls "the renew-and-reorder column"; and it warns that "Renewal is evidence worth
investigating, not a verdict" — people "renew out of inertia or the cost of switching". "The thing
this book has been building since chapter eleven" is a stronger claim than ch. 11 makes for itself.
(iii) Ch. 22: "Concentration is the figure you can state and least like to: the share of the money
that comes from the top handful." It names the top customers; it does not say whether they are
returning ones. The figure that does is ch. 22's first — "new customers this week against returning
ones, from the record chapter ten started" — and ch. 11's column. So the boundary sentence and Monday
3 both send the reader to a figure that cannot answer their question. The chapter's own argument —
that a copy takes the customers who were never choosing you — is good and is the book's; it needs
the post's word (brand, meaning the buyer chooses the original) and the book's instrument (the
returning figure and the renew-and-reorder column) kept apart, and the concentration figure used for
what it is: the names, and how much of the money they are.

**Rewrite** — Move 3, opening of the section: *"What they leaned on instead they call brand, and they
say what they mean by it: enough customers choosing the real deal over the copy. At this size that
choice leaves a record, and the book has been keeping it since chapter eleven — the customers who
came back, in the renew-and-reorder column, against the ones who paid once."* — Boundary: *"Being
chosen protects the business only where the customers who chose it are the ones paying for it. Chapter
twenty-two's first figure — returning against new — says how many are; its concentration figure says
who the largest are by name and how much of the money they hold, and whether those names are in the
renew-and-reorder column is the question."* — Monday 3: *"From chapter eleven's renew-and-reorder
column and chapter twenty-two's returning-against-new figure, with the concentration figure's names
beside them: which customers would a copy actually take — the ones who came back, or the ones who
never did — and what their money was last year."* — Decision block: *"being the one the customers
choose, and come back to"*.

## 5. The belief-not-result caveat is carried four times and the US-law caveat twice; "Two limits" duplicates Move 2 and Reading

**Quote** — Move 2, close: *"That is a belief, stated at the time, and the post does not measure it.
Nothing in it reports what the imitations did to sales, whether the brand held, or what a customer who
bought the copy would have paid for the original."* — Move 3: *"Two limits on the case, stated as the
case states them. The reading of patents is two founders' understanding of American law, offered with
their own instruction to ask an expert, and a reader in the Netherlands or anywhere in Europe faces a
different regime — registered designs, trade marks, the routes and costs of each — that this chapter
does not describe and the source does not cover. And the bet they made is reported as a belief, not a
result: the post says the brand would hold, and does not come back to say whether it did. The chapter
uses the decision and the reasoning. It cannot borrow an outcome the source does not report."* —
Reading, Note: *"The patent material is their own understanding of the American system … The
imitations are named and linked but not dated, and no later post reporting whether the brand held was
found."* — Owed: *"it is the bet placed, with no report of how it paid."*

**Lens** — Mode 6 / §6d (one principal caveat in the argument); mode 4.

**What's wrong** — §6i requires both caveats and the chapter honours both — then honours them again.
The US-law limit is already carried in Move 2 at the point the quotations land ("they stay there, as
two non-lawyers' reading of one jurisdiction") and in the Note. The belief-not-result limit is carried
in Move 2's closing paragraph, which is the right place and the right length, and in the Note. "Two
limits on the case, stated as the case states them" restates both, announces itself as a count, and
ends on the ledger's instruction to the author ("The chapter uses the decision and the reasoning. It
cannot borrow an outcome the source does not report"). The one thing in the paragraph that changes
how the reader applies the advice — that a European reader's instruments are different ones — is
already Monday 5's "someone who practises in your jurisdiction", and can be one sentence there. The
owed block's clause is doing classification, which is what owed is for; keep it.

**Rewrite** — Move 3: cut the "Two limits" paragraph and replace with one sentence at the end of the
preceding paragraph: *"…rather than what you may argue about afterwards. A reader in the Netherlands or
anywhere in Europe holds different instruments from the two the post names — registered designs, trade
marks, the routes and costs of each — and which of them changes the outcome on the day is Monday's
last item, not this chapter's to say."* Keep Move 2's closing paragraph and the Note as they are.

## 6. The NDA and exclusivity sentences state law the book cannot source, and the ch. 24 "first kind" claim is half true by ch. 24's own account

**Quote** — Move 3: *"The same test applies to the others the fear reaches for. A non-disclosure
agreement does nothing on the day the disclosure happens; it gives you a claim afterwards. An
exclusivity clause does not stop the supplier selling elsewhere; it gives you grounds. Each is worth
exactly the process behind it, and the process has a price the instrument's price does not include."*
— Move 3: *"the agreements that are yours to write — who owns the work, what your terms say — are
protection of the first kind, because they change what is true on the day rather than what you may
argue about afterwards."*

**Lens** — (c) the extension of the test to instruments the source says nothing about, marked as the
book's reasoning or as findings; §8 domain-competence (no ground box, no statement of law in any
jurisdiction); continuity with ch. 24.

**What's wrong** — "Does nothing on the day the disclosure happens; it gives you a claim afterwards"
is a statement about what a non-disclosure agreement does in law, in every jurisdiction, and the
chapter has no source for it; the same for "does not stop the supplier selling elsewhere". Both are
plausible and both are the kind of sentence §8 says a reader can act on and be harmed by — a signed
agreement can also be the ground for stopping something before it happens, and whether it is depends
on the jurisdiction and the clause, which is exactly what the chapter says of patents. The fix is not
to drop the extension — it is the chapter's transferable move — but to state it as the test applied
and not as the answer: the reader asks of the NDA what the founders asked of the patent, and takes the
question to Monday 5. On the ch. 24 sentence: ownership is protection of the first kind by ch. 24's
account ("the sentence that makes them yours either exists or it does not"); "what your terms say" is
not, by the same chapter — a limit on liability is "a limit on nothing" if not delivered, and thirty
copied pages "would be set aside as onerous the moment they were tested". Terms are argued on the day
of the dispute; that is ch. 24's whole principle. Keep ownership in the first kind and say terms are
the second, read in advance.

**Rewrite** — Move 3: *"The same question goes to the others the fear reaches for, and the post does
not answer it for them; you do, instrument by instrument. On the morning the person you trained opens
the list at a competitor's desk, what does the non-disclosure agreement do — stop the opening, or give
you a claim about it? On the day the supplier ships your design to someone bigger, what does the
exclusivity clause do? Where the honest answer is "a claim", what you bought is standing, and the
process behind it has a price the instrument's price does not include. Which answer is honest is a
question for someone who practises where you do, and it is the question Monday's last item puts to
them."* — Move 3, ch. 24: *"And the paper from chapter twenty-four is not made redundant by any of
this. The sentence that makes the work yours is protection of the first kind: it changes what is true
on the day. Your terms are the second kind, and chapter twenty-four's order — written, delivered,
read once as the customer would — is what decides whether the claim they give you is worth
anything."*

## 7. Verdict-first, apparatus on stage and self-announcing turns

**Quote** — Move 2: *"The sentence this chapter is built on is the correction:"* — Move 1: *"What none
of the sellers of those things will tell you is the question this chapter asks of each one:"* — Move
3: *"Which sets the boundary."* — Move 3: *""We'd rather just spend that time designing more great
products" is not a shrug. It is a decision about where the protection actually lives."* — Move 3
(with finding 5): *"The chapter uses the decision and the reasoning. It cannot borrow an outcome the
source does not report."*

**Lens** — Mode 1 / mode 2 / mode 4.

**What's wrong** — "The sentence this chapter is built on" grades the quotation before the reader
has it; the quotation carries itself. "The question this chapter asks" is the chapter describing its
outline, and "none of the sellers … will tell you" is a claim about sellers with no source. "Which
sets the boundary" is a stage direction. "Is not a shrug" is a verdict against the post's own word —
the sentence before it is "shrug our shoulders and carry on" — and the two sentences are in the same
breath in the post, so the post has both; the chapter can say so rather than overrule it. The ledger
instruction is finding 5's cut.

**Rewrite** — *"Then the correction, which is the sentence the rest of this chapter applies:"* → or
simply *"Then the correction:"* — Move 1: *"The question to ask of each one, and the one the sellers
are not paid to raise: on the day the thing you fear actually happens, what does this instrument do?"*
— *"Which sets the boundary. Being chosen…"* → *"Being chosen…"* (finding 4's rewrite). — *"They shrug
in one sentence and in the next say where the time goes instead: "We'd rather just spend that time
designing more great products." Read together, that is a decision about where the protection actually
lives."*

## 8. Monday 2 needs Monday 5's lawyer to complete, and Monday 5 is ch. 24's hour bought twice

**Quote** — Monday 2: *"If the line contains sue, claim or enforce, write the lawyer's estimate for
that process next to the instrument's price, and add them."* — Monday 5: *"Ask an expert once, with
items 1 to 3 on paper. Studio Neat's own instruction. Take the event, the instruments and the two
prices to someone who practises in your jurisdiction…"* — ch. 24, Monday 5: *"Buy one hour. Take items
1 to 4 and the two agreements that carry the most money to a lawyer, and ask one question: what have I
agreed to that I do not know about?"*

**Lens** — (e) concrete enough to fail at, doable this week; Monday lists chain rather than repeat
(§6d coherence pass, item 4).

**What's wrong** — Monday 2 asks for "the lawyer's estimate" on the day the reader writes the line,
and the lawyer arrives at item 5; as written, item 2 cannot be finished until item 5 is, and item 5
needs "items 1 to 3 on paper". The reader can do item 2 this week with her own figure — or with the
word *unknown*, which is the finding — and let item 5 replace it. And ch. 24, one chapter back, has
already sent the reader to buy one hour with a lawyer and a question; a second hour for a second
question is a repeat where the lists should chain. The two questions fit in one hour, and saying so
makes both chapters' Mondays cheaper. "Ask an expert once" and "including the ones you have not heard
of" are both doable and stay.

**Rewrite** — Monday 2: *"…write what you think that process would cost next to the instrument's price
— or the word unknown — and add them. That total is what the protection costs; the first figure alone is
what the comfort costs. Item 5 replaces your figure with a real one."* — Monday 5: *"Ask an expert
once, with items 1 to 3 on paper — in the hour chapter twenty-four told you to buy, if you have not
bought it yet. Studio Neat's own instruction. Take the event, the instruments and the two prices to
someone who practises in your jurisdiction, and ask which instrument would change the outcome on the
day — including the ones you have not heard of. Then decide, and write down which price you paid."*

## 9. Small glosses the post does not bear, and the Decision block's unqualified compression

**Quote** — Move 3: *"would they sink the time and money into going after the imitators, and the answer
was no — and once the answer is no, the instrument's value is the comfort it gave before the event,
which is what they were paying to avoid."* — Move 2: *"Their other points are about the American
system as they understood it"*. — Decision: *"the one kind of protection at this scale that cannot be
copied — being the one the customers choose again."*

**Lens** — (b) every word the chapter adds; §4 provocations are not laws.

**What's wrong** — The post says "Our guess is no", and "the answer was no" hardens a guess into a
finding — the same slip the chapter is careful about two paragraphs earlier. "Which is what they were
paying to avoid" has no referent: they paid nothing, and the comfort was what a patent would have
bought. "Their other points" implies the force-field correction is not about the American system; the
whole section is, and the chapter's own position (the lesson portable, the mechanics quarantined) is
better said as that. The Decision block's "the one kind of protection at this scale that cannot be
copied" is a compression the chapter itself qualifies in the owed block (the brand may not hold; the
thing they chose you for may be in the object after all), and the Decision block is the one place a
reader will quote from; "cannot be copied" should be "has to be earned rather than bought", which is
the claim the chapter actually makes.

**Rewrite** — *"…would they sink the time and money into going after the imitators, and their guess was
no — and once the answer is no, what the instrument would have bought is the comfort of the months
before the event, at a patent's price."* — *"The rest of what they learned is the American system as
they understood it — that the cheaper kind…"* — Decision: *"…whether the money is better put into the
one kind of protection at this scale that has to be earned rather than bought — being the one the
customers choose, and come back to."*

## 10. Chapter twenty-four's last sentence is this chapter's first premise, and Move 1 does not pick it up

**Quote** — Move 1: *"Every one of those is possible, and the market for things that make the feeling
go away is large — registrations, agreements, clauses, a lawyer's letter kept in a drawer."* — ch. 24,
Move 3: *"the agreements you signed by clicking are the only ones in the business that were drafted by
a lawyer, which is a reason to read them, not a reason to feel protected by them."*

**Lens** — continuity with the preceding chapter; (a) Move 1 on the reader's position.

**What's wrong** — Ch. 24 ends its principle on the distinction this chapter is about — a thing that
is legally real and a thing that makes you feel protected — and this chapter's Move 1 arrives at the
same distinction from the other side without saying it has been here before. One clause in the lede or
Move 1 joins the two chapters and costs nothing. Nothing else in the handoff is wrong: "the paper from
chapter twenty-four" is the right description, and ch. 24's four agreements are not re-listed.

**Rewrite** — Move 1: *"Every one of those is possible, and the market for things that make the feeling
go away is large — registrations, agreements, clauses, a lawyer's letter kept in a drawer. Chapter
twenty-four ended on the agreements you clicked: a reason to read them, not a reason to feel protected
by them. This chapter asks the same of the ones you would buy on purpose."*

## Overall note

The rule is right, the case is the right section of the right source, and the extension from one
instrument to the rest — what does it do on the day, and what does the process behind it cost — is
worth keeping as the chapter's own. What needs doing is to stop reading the sources for the sequence
and the instruments they ought to hold. Ch. 03 dates the Glif to October 2010 and says nothing about
outside money (finding 1); the post lists fear, understanding, decision and imitations without
ordering the last two, and the chapter's claim is as strong in either order (finding 2); ch. 04's Cydoc
was a smaller business than Studio Neat, not a larger one, and its patents were the wrong currency for
a stopping rule, which is a different question from this chapter's and not a contradiction (finding
3). The brand sentence is about a buyer choosing the original over a copy; the book's instrument for
"chosen again" is ch. 11's renew-and-reorder column and ch. 22's returning figure, and the
concentration figure supplies the names and the shares, not the answer (finding 4). Then the usual
pass: the two caveats said once each where the source carries them and not again in a counted
paragraph (finding 5), the NDA and exclusivity sentences turned from findings into the test applied
and handed to Monday 5's expert (finding 6), the verdicts and stage directions cut (finding 7), Monday
2 made finishable this week and Monday 5 chained to ch. 24's hour (finding 8), the small glosses
trimmed to the post's words and the Decision block's "cannot be copied" qualified (finding 9), and ch.
24's closing sentence picked up (finding 10). Nothing here needs a new fact; every replacement uses
words already in the post, in ch. 03, ch. 04, ch. 11, ch. 22 or ch. 24.
