## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, with the right case and the right rule, who has read the post for what it ought to show rather than for what it shows.

Move 1 is the best opening in Part IV–V: "You already know the failure from the inside, because you
are the reserve. When someone is off, you cover" is the reader's square exactly, and "a reserve you
cannot stop paying for is the risk chapter one warned you about wearing a safer name" plants the
boundary before the case. The rule passes §6d in one sentence — *slack is capacity paid for and not
sold, and it is reserve rather than waste only if you can say what it is held against, how much there
is, and what would make you spend it* — and the Decision block says it in the same terms. Monday 1
and 2 are failable and are the chapter. The owed block holds the counter-case definition and says
why the case is hard to find. The ratio sentence does what §6i asked.

The chapter's problem is one problem in three places. It needs WP Curve's month to show reserve
being *built*, and the post shows a business being *filled*. Norris hires "at our current growth
rate … every 2 weeks" — to keep up with two customers a day, not ahead of them — and in the comments
under the same post says "we are growing so quickly we don't want to grow faster, so we have shelved
most things designed to increase growth." That is a business with no slack in people, hiring at the
pace of demand. The chapter reads it as "hires landing every two weeks, ahead of any one of them
being needed on the day they started" (Move 2), then two sections later says "a business adding two
customers a day will use every hand it hires on the day it hires them" (Move 3) — which is the post's
version, and which contradicts the first (finding 1). Second place: the hire "made end to end without
either founder" was, in the sentence the chapter itself quotes, made with the founders "still
involved at key intervals" and "overseeing the process at key points" (finding 2). Third: the Happy %
fall is introduced with the correct sentence — Norris "does not connect the drop to the growth. The
chapter cannot connect them either" — and then connected, in the next sentence ("the quality of what
the capacity produced falling") and again in Move 3 ("a team stretched thin produces the fix that
'wasn't quite correct the first time'"), where "stretched thin" appears in no version of the post
(finding 3). The chapter's framework survives all three fixes, because the post does show two of the

an owner's reserve that is filled the moment it appears — Alex's bracketed aside, "[Alex: I think Dan
will come up with something...]", which the chapter cut and which is the best evidence in the post for
"reserve is spent silently" (finding 5).

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "the people section is where the month's real event is";

## 1. "Hiring ahead of need" is not in the post, the post says the opposite, and Move 3 agrees with the post against Move 2

**Quote** — Move 2: *"Read that as a month in which reserve appeared in three places at once. People:
hires landing every two weeks, ahead of any one of them being needed on the day they started."* —
Move 3: *"In people: the next hire made at the point where the current team is nearly full, not after
it has been over-full for a quarter — Norris's every-two-weeks is the pace of a business that hires
ahead of the day it cannot cope."* — Move 3: *"Hiring ahead of need is the same act at a different
scale"* — Move 3: *"It is consumed by growth, because a business adding two customers a day will use
every hand it hires on the day it hires them."*

**Lens** — (b) inference inside the source; §6i rule 4; (d) the framework earned from the source.

**What's wrong** — The post's sentence is "At our current growth rate we are hiring every 2 weeks, so
it's important to have someone other than me looking after this process." That is hiring *at* the
rate of growth: 57 net customers in a month on an unlimited-fixes promise, and a hire a fortnight to
serve them. Nothing says a hire landed before it was needed, and the post's own frame — "if we can
deal with a few key challenges" — plus Norris's reply in the comments ("we are growing so quickly we
don't want to grow faster … we have shelved most things designed to increase growth for now") describe
a business at capacity, throttling demand because it cannot add hands faster. The chapter's Move 3
knows this: "will use every hand it hires on the day it hires them" is exactly right, and it is the
sentence that makes "ahead of any one of them being needed on the day they started" false inside the
same chapter. The consequence runs through the argument: the people-leg of the three places is not
shown by the case; the Webvan boundary paragraph defends "hiring ahead of need" on WP Curve's behalf
when WP Curve did not do it; and Monday 3's "made at eighty percent of full" has no case behind it.
The honest version is stronger for the chapter's thesis, not weaker: the month shows a business with
*no* people reserve, hiring to stay level, whose owners then discover that every hour they freed was
refilled — which is what the second half of the chapter is about. The people reserve the chapter
wants is one unit beyond that pace, and it is the book's prescription, not Norris's practice.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"Two of the three places this chapter will name are on the page. Process: a
hire run by the admin from a written document, with the founders at the key points and not in
between. The owners' week: two people who, by their own account, did not have a lot to do. The third
place is not there. People were being added at the rate the customers were — "at our current growth
rate we are hiring every 2 weeks" — and under the post Norris tells a commenter that "we are growing
so quickly we don't want to grow faster, so we have shelved most things designed to increase growth."
A hire a fortnight was the business staying level, not getting ahead."* — Move 3, the people
sentence: *"In people: the next hire made while the current team still has hours unsold, not after it
has been over-full for a quarter. WP Curve's month is the pace without the margin — a hire a fortnight
to stay level with two customers a day — and the reserve is the one unit beyond that pace, which is
this book's prescription and not something Norris reports doing."* — Move 3: *"It is consumed by
growth, because a business adding two customers a day will use every hand it hires on the day it hires
them — which is what WP Curve's fortnightly hire was."* (Move 3's boundary paragraph: see finding 4.)

## 4. The Webvan boundary is defended in terms ch. 01 does not use, when ch. 01 already states the distinction the reader can apply

**Quote** — Move 3: *"The boundary is chapter one, and it is a hard one. Webvan built its capacity
ahead of demand and died of the fixed cost. Hiring ahead of need is the same act at a different scale,
and the difference has to be stated, not felt: WP Curve added one person at a time, on revenue that
was already recurring and had grown for eleven of twelve months, in a unit — one developer, one
fortnight's pace — that could be stopped the month the growth stopped. A warehouse cannot be
stopped."* — Move 1: *"Chapter one told you to keep every cost variable until demand was proved"*.

**Lens** — (c) is the Webvan boundary a real distinction the reader can apply; continuity with ch. 01; §6i rule 4.

**What's wrong** — Three things. (i) Ch. 01 is not against fixed capacity; its rule is *order*: "The
trap is not fixed costs. The trap is taking them on in the wrong order," and "Contractors cost more
per hour than employees and can be stopped" is its own stoppability test. So the boundary is not a
place where ch. 23 has to argue its way past ch. 01 — it is ch. 01's second half applied. Saying "the
same act at a different scale" concedes a contradiction that does not exist and then uses "scale",
which is the word the brief warns against, as the escape. (ii) Ch. 01's account of Webvan is more
exact than "built its capacity ahead of demand and died of the fixed cost": it says the Bechtel option
was real and *was* stopped — "By late 2000 it had no plans to build any more" — and that the fatal act
was "committing to a facility whose economics required eight thousand orders a day before a single
customer had placed one." So "A warehouse cannot be stopped" is half wrong by ch. 01's own reading:
the unbuilt ones were; the standing one was not. The distinction the reader can apply is the one ch.
01 gives — a cost whose breakeven volume you can state, taken on after the demand is shown, in a unit
you can put down — and it should be said in those words. (iii) "keep every cost variable" overstates
ch. 01, which says "as much as possible" and then says where nothing converts the advice hardens.
"Had grown for eleven of twelve months" misreads "exceeded 15% in 11 out of our 12 months in
business" — the post is stronger (more than fifteen percent in eleven of twelve), and the chapter's
gloss implies a month of no growth the post does not report; the revenue is also company-wide, which
the sentence should carry since it is the ground for the hire. With finding 1 applied, the paragraph
also has to stop claiming WP Curve hired ahead.

**Rewrite** — Move 1: *"Chapter one told you to keep as much of the cost base variable as you could
until demand was proved"*. — Move 3: *"The boundary is chapter one, and it is the same rule from the
other side. Webvan committed to a building whose economics needed eight thousand orders a day before
the first customer had ordered; the warehouses it had not yet built it could and did stop, and the one
standing it could not. Chapter one's line was never against fixed cost — it was against taking it on
in the wrong order — and a reserve is a fixed cost taken on in the right one: after the revenue
repeats, in a unit you can put down. WP Curve's fortnightly hire passes that test whether or not it
was a reserve — one person at a time, on a company-wide revenue line that had grown by more than
fifteen percent in eleven of its twelve months, in a unit that could be stopped the month the growth
stopped. The reserve this chapter asks for is one such unit more than the pace needs, bought the
same way."* Keep the bold rule and the ratio sentence as they are.

## Overall note

The rule is right, the case is the right company at the right month, and the three-place framework is
worth keeping — it is the chapter's own and should be presented as such, which finding 1's rewrite
