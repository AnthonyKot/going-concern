# Craft review — ch. 15, Paying Yourself

Reviewed 2026-08-26 against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §5b, §6d, §6f and the two review rounds recorded
after §6f. Dutch legal and tax content is out of scope and no finding below touches a quoted string,
a figure, or the box. Every rewrite uses facts already on the page.

## Does the quality drop here? Partly, and it is localised.

The argument prose is not weaker than ch. 11–14 — some of it is sharper ("a cost that is never
entered is a cost that never fails a test"; "it stops being legitimate when the date is never
written down, because then it cannot arrive"; "looks like the third from the inside and like the
first from the outside"; "one owner is handed the discipline whether they want it or not"). The
drop is in two places: **the first two paragraphs of Move 2**, which are coy about the jurisdiction
and clear their throat about structure, and **the test section of Move 3**, where the six
post-publication corrections have left their defensive framing in the reader's path. The chapter
reads like two hands — one that wrote the aphorisms, one that patched the reviews — and the second
hand is audible exactly where the reader most needs the first: at the instrument, at the stopping
rule, and at the one paragraph where they should recognise their own week.

Sweep of the known failure modes: (1) verdict-first — not applicable, no company case, and Move 2
correctly builds from the regulated owner to the sharper unregulated one; (2) apparatus in prose —
present, six instances, all listed below; (3) second-person flinch — twice, both at sharp moments;
(4) counting openings — two; (5) summarizer seams — one stiff corrected sentence in Move 2;
(6) source accounting in the argument — clean, the Reading list holds it, one stray pointer to
"the project's own notes".

## Findings, ranked by impact

### 1. The chapter's instrument arrives wrapped in a reviewer's anxiety

**Quote:** "Put that total into a management restatement — a copy of last year's profit and loss
with one extra line for the cost of your own labour — and recompute. A copy, and only ever a copy:
the box below is there because for a sole trader this charge is not deductible in the statutory
calculation, and a shadow document is exactly where such a cost belongs. Never the filed accounts.
One of three things is then true, and all three are useful:"

**Lens:** Warmth without slack / practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** This is the test the whole chapter exists to hand over, and it says "copy" three
times ("a copy", "only ever a copy", "never the filed accounts") and then points at the box to
justify itself. That is correction #2 from the second review still visible as a scar — the sentence
exists to satisfy the reviewer, not the reader. A practitioner would say it once, plainly, and move
on; Monday item 3 already repeats the warning where it belongs.

**Rewrite:** "Put that total into a management restatement — a copy of last year's profit and loss
with one extra line for the cost of your own labour — and recompute. A copy, not the filed accounts:
for a sole trader this charge is not a deductible cost, and a shadow document is where it belongs.
One of three things is then true, and all three are useful:"

(Cuts 28 words. The box's authority is untouched; the reader reaches it on the next screen anyway.)

### 2. The climax delivered as an audit note

**Quote:** "Then over the period you measured the business did not cover the market cost of your
labour, and nothing you have found closes the gap. Raise the price, cut the work, redesign the model
— or this is chapter four's stopping rule arriving with a number attached rather than a feeling.
Note the limit: one restatement of one year says the gap was not closed then, not that the business
never worked."

**Lens:** Story craft / rhythm.

**What's wrong:** "Arriving with a number attached rather than a feeling" is the line the reader
has been dreading since Move 1, and it should end the answer. Instead it is followed by "Note the
limit", which is correction #5 narrating itself. The limit is already carried, twice, in the same
answer ("over the period you measured"; "nothing you have found") — the third statement is
duplicate, and its register ("Note the limit") is the debt register's, not the book's.

**Rewrite:** cut the final sentence. The answer ends on "…rather than a feeling."

### 3. The chapter's best paragraph flinches to third person at the moment of recognition

**Quote:** "costed at the hours a competent replacement would need rather than the hours you spend,
which cuts both ways; […] The result is usually uncomfortable in a specific way: the founder
discovers they are doing forty hours of skilled work and twenty hours of work they could hire for a
fraction, and that the second twenty is what has been crowding out the first."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** The four-step method is addressed to "you"; then, at the exact line where this
reader would see their own week, the prose steps back to "the founder… they". This is the sentence
that earns the whole section, and it is delivered to someone else. "Which cuts both ways" is
correction #3's fingerprint — a clause defending the method against a reviewer's objection, not
explaining it to the reader.

**Rewrite:** "costed at the hours a competent replacement would need rather than the hours you
spend, in either direction; […] The result is usually uncomfortable in a specific way: you discover
you are doing forty hours of skilled work and twenty hours you could hire for a fraction, and that
the second twenty has been crowding out the first."

### 4. Move 2's strongest line, then the apparatus steps on it

**Quote:** "So one owner is handed the discipline whether they want it or not, and the other has to
supply it themselves. The figures, articles and wording are quarantined in the box below; nothing
above depends on them."

**Lens:** Rhythm / apparatus surfacing.

**What's wrong:** The first sentence survived two reviews untouched because it is the chapter in
one line. The second is stage management — "quarantined", "the box below" — and it takes the
last word of Move 2 away from the reader and gives it to the book's machinery. The box carries its
own scope statement and checked line; §6d says that is where the mechanics live.

**Rewrite:** cut the second sentence. Move 2 ends on "…supply it themselves."

### 5. The mortgage moment, addressed to "owners"; then a scar the reader cannot decode

**Quote:** "Owners routinely leave it unpaid, or take money whenever the account looks healthy, so
the household absorbs the volatility and nobody measures how much." […] "Do not let one word do all
three jobs."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair / warmth without slack.

**What's wrong:** Two problems in one section. First, the sentence about the household absorbing
the volatility is the one place this chapter touches the reader's actual fear — the mortgage, the
uneven months — and it flinches to "owners" and "the household". Second, "Do not let one word do
all three jobs" is correction #4 talking to itself: the reader never saw the word ("owed") that was
doing three jobs, so the warning has no referent on the page.

**Rewrite:** "You will leave it unpaid, or take money whenever the account looks healthy, and your
household absorbs the volatility without anyone measuring how much." And cut "Do not let one word do
all three jobs." — the paragraph before it already names the three things (imputed cost, cash
received, debt) and closes cleanly on "there the imputed cost stays a management adjustment."

### 6. Move 2 opens coy, then clears its throat about structure

**Quote:** "There is a jurisdiction that refuses to let this stay invisible, and its refusal is
instructive precisely because management is not what it is for." […] "The structure is worth seeing
before the details, because the shape is what transfers and the details do not. Which side of the
fork you start on turns on legal form and on your ownership-and-work relationship with the entity —
not on how much you choose to take out."

**Lens:** Practitioner's ear / rhythm.

**What's wrong:** "There is a jurisdiction that" withholds a name the box gives two screens later
and the flag gives in the register — it reads as the prose protecting itself, not as suspense.
"Instructive precisely because" is lecture-hall register. The second paragraph announces that
structure matters before showing any, and "ownership-and-work relationship with the entity" is the
corrected sentence from review one ("longer and duller") still wearing its admin clothes. The legal
clauses must stay; only the framing around them is the finding.

**Rewrite:** "The Netherlands refuses to let this stay invisible — for some owners — and the refusal
is worth watching because it has nothing to do with management. The rule is not a viability test;
nothing in it asks whether the business works. […] Which side of the fork you start on turns on
legal form and on whether you work for a company you hold a substantial interest in — not on how
much you choose to take out."

(Cuts the "structure is worth seeing" sentence entirely. The clause set — legal form, working for
the company, substantial interest, not the amount drawn — is unchanged; the sentence about size and
sector that follows stays as written. Fact pipeline to reread the rephrased clause against the box,
per the review-one rule.)

### 7. Monday item 1 contradicts its own heading

**Quote:** "List the jobs, not the hours. Write down every distinct role you perform in the
business and roughly what share of your week each takes."

**Lens:** Practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** The heading says not the hours; the instruction then asks for hours. The reader
following it literally stalls on the first item. The method in Move 3 does want the reader's hours
— as the thing to be *replaced* by a competent replacement's hours in item 2 — so the fix is to say
what the hours are for, not to remove them.

**Rewrite:** "List the jobs before the hours. Write down every distinct role you perform in the
business, then roughly what share of your week each takes — your hours for now; the replacement's
come next."

### 8. Move 1 counts its own contents

**Quote:** "Two different things are being confused when this happens, and they need separating
first:"

**Lens:** Rhythm.

**What's wrong:** Passive, and an announcement of a list rather than the first thing in it. The
previous paragraph ended on the chapter's first good line ("a cost that is never entered is a cost
that never fails a test"); this one deflates it before the list can restore the pressure.

**Rewrite:** "The residual hides two things that are not the same:"

### 9. Two stray pointers at the book's own bookkeeping

**Quote:** "Note what is not owed — an owner who drew a full market wage the business could not
afford is no counter-case, because this chapter never asks anyone to draw it." […] "This chapter was
written under a stricter process than the rest of the book, recorded on the method page's terms in
the project's own notes: every Dutch claim was sourced…"

**Lens:** Apparatus surfacing.

**What's wrong:** "What is not owed" is the debt register's vocabulary; the sentence does real work
(it draws the line correction #6 needed) but says it in the ledger's voice. "Recorded on the method
page's terms in the project's own notes" points the reader at a file they cannot open. The Reading
list is the right home for the process note (§6d), so the note stays; the pointer goes.

**Rewrite:** "An owner who drew a full market wage the business could not afford would not count:
this chapter never asks anyone to draw it." And: "This chapter was written under a stricter process
than the rest of the book: every Dutch claim was sourced and checked before the prose was written,
and nothing in the box is derived, computed or combined."

### 10. Monday item 6 closes on a phrase that does not quite mean anything

**Quote:** "The answer is defensible when right and expensive when wrong, and it is not one to
settle from a book."

**Lens:** Practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** "Defensible when right" is a near-tautology that reads as a balanced antithesis
without being one; the reader who parses it loses the item's last beat, which is the book handing
the question to a professional — the one place §5b requires warmth to be unambiguous.

**Rewrite:** "Getting it wrong is expensive and getting it right is not, and it is not a question to
settle from a book."

## Overall note

The single most improving change is not a rewrite but a **scar-tissue pass**: remove the six
sentences that exist because a reviewer once objected — "a copy, and only ever a copy… the box below
is there because", "Note the limit", "which cuts both ways", "Do not let one word do all three jobs",
"the figures, articles and wording are quarantined in the box below", "the structure is worth seeing
before the details" — and let the corrected *facts* stand without their defensive framing. Every
correction from both review rounds survives intact; what goes is the sound of the chapter arguing
with its own history. Roughly ninety words out, zero new facts, and the chapter's two hands become
one. Do that first; findings 3, 5 and 7 (the second-person flinches and the Monday contradiction) are
the next twenty minutes.
