Reviewed against CONTEXT.md §3 (template), §4 (voice), §6c (ch. 10 → 11 handoff), §6d (caveat
placement) and the Turing ledger entry (§6, Permitted/Prohibited). Facts assumed correct. No
rewrite below asserts a cause for the 2020 fall, treats the three reports as a series, or moves a
caveat out of the reader's path; the ch. 10 boundary sentences are kept in every proposal.

Sweep for the six known failure modes: #6 (source accounting in the argument's path) and #5
(details pasted in with their context left behind) are this chapter's infection — Move 2 is in
places a pdftotext transcript with the questionnaire's questions still attached (findings 2, 4),
and the quantifier-discipline pass recorded in CONTEXT.md §3 has left its scaffolding standing in

but present at every move boundary (findings 5, 10). #1 (verdict-first) is largely under control —
the comparison block does put the good delivery numbers before the employment fall. #3
(second-person flinch) is essentially absent; #4 (self-counting openers) appears once ("A word on
what renewal and referral prove, since this chapter is about to lean on them"). The
chapter-specific findings are 1, 3 and 6.

## 1. The climax is delivered, then delivered again as an audit of itself

**Quote:** "By every measure of what the school itself did, 2020 was at least as good a year as
2019." … [one paragraph later] … "By the report's measures closest to delivery, 2020 was at least
as strong as 2019 — which is a claim about those measures, not a verdict on the teaching. A
business watching only them would have recorded a good year."

**Lens:** Story craft / rhythm.

**What's wrong:** This section is the chapter — delivery held still, outcome collapsed. The first
sentence lands it; the "And employment in-field fell…" paragraph is the best in the chapter. Then
the third paragraph restates the first sentence with the qualification bolted on, which is the
§3 quantifier fix ("the delivery was excellent" → hedged) visible as a repair rather than absorbed
into the prose. The reader hears the punchline twice, the second time with a lawyer present.

**Rewrite:** Put the qualification into the first statement once, and cut the restatement:

> "…Median pay for those who got work: $75,000 both years, unchanged. By every measure the report
> has of what the school itself did, 2020 was at least as good a year as 2019."

Then leave the "And employment in-field fell…" paragraph exactly as it is, and open the third
paragraph at "A business watching only those measures would have recorded a good year. But what
the customer was buying was not the delivery…" — deleting "By the report's measures closest to
delivery, 2020 was at least as strong as 2019 — which is a claim about those measures, not a
verdict on the teaching." Net cut, ~30 words, nothing conceded.

## 2. Move 2 quotes the questionnaire, not the school

**Quote:** "The median was *"What median pay rate do graduates earn? $75,000"*, and the commonest
title *"73.1% Software Engineer"*." Also: "*"How many students graduate within 100% of published
program length (on-time)? 67.95%"*", "*"Graduates Included in Report: 67"*: *"How many students
graduated on-time? 60.2%"*".

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear / summarizer's seams.

**What's wrong:** The figures arrive with the PDF's form-field questions still attached, so a
sentence like "The median was 'What median pay rate do graduates earn? $75,000'" is
ungrammatical on the page. This is the verification pipeline's need (string-match against
pdftotext, as the Reading note admits) surfacing as prose. The chapter's argument is that a
standard written by someone else forces categories into the open; the reader should feel those
categories arrive one at a time, in the seller's reluctant order, not read a form.

**Rewrite** (2019 paragraph; the 2016 paragraph takes the same treatment):

> Three years later the format is tighter and harder. Back End Engineering, sixty-seven
> graduates in the report. On time: *"60.2%"*. Within 150% of the published length: *"79.5%"*.
> At 180 days, *"80.6% Employed In-Field"*, *"9.0% Still Seeking a Job In-Field"* — and *"7.5%
> Could Not Contact"*. Median pay *"$75,000"*; the commonest title, *"Software Engineer"*.

Keeps every quoted figure inside quotation marks, drops the question text. **Constraint to
check, not a fact:** if the claims script matches the full question string rather than the
figure, the match targets need updating alongside — flag to the fact pipeline before applying.

## 3. A Monday action points at the wrong column, and another at the wrong chapter

**Quote:** "Fill the last column from the population that paid. Include everyone, including the
ones who never started. The gap between paid and used is the number you are least likely to have
and most likely to need." And: "Their answers belong in chapter eight's reasons column, which is
now the same document."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** Action 2 lists five columns in order — use, outcome, renew, refer, reason for
leaving. "The last column" is therefore *reason for leaving*, but the sentence that follows is
about the paid → used gap, which is the *first* column. A reader doing this on Monday opens the
spreadsheet and cannot tell which column they are filling. Action 5 then sends them to "chapter
eight's reasons column" — a cross-reference to a document the reader built three chapters ago,
when the column they need (reason in their words) is the one they added in action 2 of this
list. Both are repoints, zero new facts.

**Rewrite:**

> 3. Fill the first column from the population that paid — everyone, including the ones who never
> started. The gap between paid and used is the number you are least likely to have and most
> likely to need.

> 5. Ask the five most recent leavers one question. "What were you hoping would be different?"
> Past tense, no defence, no offer. Their answer, in their words, is the fifth column.

## 4. The 2020 figures are given twice — once as a comparison, once as a transcript

**Quote:** "The 2020 figures: *"How many students graduated on-time? 61.4%"*, *"How many students
graduated within 150% of program length? 87.5%"*, *"63.1% Employed In-Field"* — of which only
*"47.7% Full-Time Employee"* and *"13.8% Full-Time Apprentice / Contractor"* — with *"26.2% Still
Seeking a Job In-Field"* and *"7.7% Could Not Contact"*."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** The comparison block immediately above already gives every one of these
figures except the full-time / apprentice split, in the right dramatic order (delivery up, pay
flat, then the fall). The paragraph after it replays the block as a list, so Move 2 ends on
its own echo instead of on the fall. The only new information is the split inside 63.1%, which
is worth one sentence.

**Rewrite:** Replace the paragraph with:

> And inside that 63.1%, only *"47.7% Full-Time Employee"*; the rest, *"13.8% Full-Time
> Apprentice / Contractor"*.

Move 2 then ends on "Could not contact 7.5% → 7.7%" in the block and one sentence that makes
the good number smaller. Cut of ~50 words.

## 5. The situation opens with the book's table of contents instead of the reader's bank account

**Quote:** "Part II ended with a sale you could make again. Part III asks whether the money works,
and it begins here rather than with the accounts because many of the numbers later in this part
depend on whether customers use, complete, stay, return or refer — and all of them are easier to
read once those populations have been separated." Followed by: "A word on what renewal and
referral prove, since this chapter is about to lean on them."

**Lens:** Rhythm / reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** §3 says Move 1 opens on the reader's actual position, no throat-clearing. This
one opens by explaining why the chapter is where it is in the book, then announces a caveat
before the argument the caveat qualifies has been made. The chapter's best paragraph — "Payment
is an event that arrives in your bank and announces itself…" — is sitting third. The renewal
paragraph is true and should stay, but it belongs after the three tests have been named, because
"stayed the course" is the test it qualifies.

**Rewrite:** Cut the first paragraph entirely (the boundary paragraph at the end of Move 1
already says what ch. 10 owned and what the rest of Part III owns — the handoff survives
intact). Open on "The asymmetry is structural" with its first two words removed:

> Payment is an event that arrives in your bank and announces itself. The outcome is a state in
> someone else's life…

Then the "What follows is one paying population followed through three successive tests"
paragraph; then the renewal paragraph with its opener replaced:

> Renewal and referral sit inside the second test, and neither settles the third. People refer
> before they have received anything, renew out of inertia…

Then the boundary paragraph. Cut ~70 words; every claim kept.

## 6. "The period is what it is" — the chapter winks where it should just say the year

**Quote:** "What caused it is not in the report and will not be asserted here. The period is what
it is, and a labour market is an obvious candidate, but the document does not say so, no
comparison with other schools is offered, and one programme in one city cannot carry that claim."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack / practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** "Will not be asserted here" is the ledger's *Prohibited* line speaking in the
reader's path, and "the period is what it is" is coy about a year every reader can name. The
ledger forbids saying the pandemic *caused* the fall; it does not forbid saying it was 2020. Coyness
reads as machinery; naming the year and refusing the inference reads as a person being careful.

**Rewrite:**

> The report does not say what caused it. It was 2020, and the labour market is the obvious
> candidate — but the document offers no comparison with other schools, and one programme in one
> city cannot carry that claim. What the case supports is narrower and enough: delivery and
> outcome are separate variables.

Same facts, same refusal, ~15 words shorter.

## 7. The ninety-day section trips over its own correction

**Quote:** "A school measuring only at ninety days would miss outcomes that had not arrived yet.
Measuring much later risks counting results increasingly shaped by other causes — though not none
of its own, and late measurement also catches delayed failures the early number misses."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "Though not none of its own" is a double negative bolted on to prevent an
absolute — the §3 quantifier fix again, left as scaffolding. The first sentence also repeats what
the previous paragraph just said ("Eleven points of the final result had not arrived yet"). The
paragraph's real content is one balanced sentence.

**Rewrite:**

> Measure at ninety days and you miss what has not arrived. Measure at a year and you are
> counting what the world did as much as what you did — and catching the late failures too.
> Neither number is wrong; they answer different questions. Which is why the instruction is to
> define the outcome with a date attached and then keep the date fixed…

## 8. "Four populations" in the epigraph, "Three populations" in the heading

**Quote:** Epigraph: "Paying, using, finishing and getting the result are four different
populations". Section heading: "Three populations, not one". Move 1: "one paying population
followed through three successive tests".

**Lens:** Reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** The count changes three times before the argument starts. It is reconcilable
(four populations, three gaps) but the reader has to do the reconciling, at exactly the moment
the chapter is teaching them to count carefully. The section is in fact about counting from the
first population, not about how many there are.

**Rewrite:** Heading → "Count from the population that paid". The section's last sentences
already say this ("Count from the population that paid, not the population that finished.
Everything else is arithmetic performed on survivors."), so the heading now names what the
section proves. No other change.

## 9. A broken sentence inside the chapter's central paragraph

**Quote:** "Not necessarily someone the purchase failed — the report cannot say whether they
finished later, got the job anyway, or left for reasons of their own. What it does say is that
they cannot be silently removed from the population that paid. The whole discipline of this
chapter is in that gap."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack.

**What's wrong:** "Someone the purchase failed" is missing its preposition, in the paragraph that
carries the chapter's rule; and "the whole discipline of this chapter" is the book describing
itself. The generosity toward the non-finishers is right and should stay — it is the warmest
move in the section.

**Rewrite:**

> Not necessarily someone the purchase failed *for* — the report cannot say whether they finished
> later, got the job anyway, or left for reasons of their own. What it can say is that they were
> there, and cannot be quietly removed from the population that paid. Count from the population
> that paid, not the population that finished. Everything else is arithmetic performed on
> survivors.

Deletes "The whole discipline of this chapter is in that gap."

## Overall note

Rewrite Move 2 as prose that carries the figures instead of a transcript that carries the form.
The chapter's own thesis is that a standard written by someone other than the seller forces
categories into the open one at a time — paid, finished, employed, hired by us, could not reach —
and the reader should be walked through them in that order, feeling each one make the previous
number smaller, with the 2020 employment fall as the last thing they learn. The structure for
this already exists on the page (the comparison block is correctly ordered); what stands in its
way is the questionnaire text pasted in with the figures, the replayed 2020 list, and the climax
restated as an audit. All three are cuts and repoints; none needs a new fact.
