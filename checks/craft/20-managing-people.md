§6i entry for this chapter; every Turnbull quotation checked against `groove-fire-2015.raw` and

## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, with a good ear, who keeps a US frame in the room the Dutch box then has to argue with.

Move 1 is the best opening in Part IV: "Three months in, you know" puts the reader on the exact
square, and "The month is not a plan" is the sentence a reader keeps. The rule survives the §6d
test in one sentence — *the date at which you must say yes or no goes in before the doubt, not
after it* — and Move 3's boundary paragraph qualifies it in the same breath, with the ch. 18
hand-back ("this gap is the transfer happening, not failing") placed exactly where a reader would
otherwise misuse the rule. Monday is concrete and failable, and Monday 5's third sentence ("only
allowed if you write down what will be different about the month") is the whole chapter in one
action. The chapter does not repeat ch. 19: the trial-as-task point is referred to, not re-argued,
and the box hands Groove's trial back to ch. 19's box in one clause. The Dutch box is close to the
ch. 19 model — statute quoted, cap dated as stale by construction, lawyer named, checked line dated
— and it clears both §6i traps in its own text: "it is not a fine for doing it wrong" is the
transitievergoeding sentence the entry asked for.

Two problems carry the weight. First, the chapter says things about Groove the post does not:
that the team was "remote", that Turnbull worked "in a country where an employer can part with
someone at the end of a short trial on his own judgment", that the failure he owns "is later than
the hiring" (the post says the opposite — "I should've been better at spotting the poor fit before
I hired them"), that "nearly 40%" is "close to half", and that the three practices are "each a
correction of something he had done wrong" when only one is. The country sentence is the §6i trap
in its exact form — the US at-will practice, unsourced, stated as the case's ground — and the box
then opens by describing the case as "a freedom you do not have" *and* three paragraphs later says
the case's trial is a task contract that ch. 19's box governs. Both cannot be true, and the second
is the one the chapter's own reading supports. Second, the box's route sentence points the
reader's case at the wrong door: it names UWV permission under art. 671a, which the statute
confines to grounds a and b (economic, long-term illness); ground d — the chapter's ground — goes
to the kantonrechter under 671b, which is not among the articles checked, and "a court" appears in
the box unsourced.

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "What he built in response is a calendar, not a
temperament" precedes the trial quotation; "The number he starts with is not a boast" precedes the
number (finding 4). (2) apparatus: "a founder's, not a filing" in Move 2; "it is what the case
below actually describes" in Move 1 (findings 4, 8). (3) flinch: none — second person holds
through every Move and Monday. (4) counting and self-announcing: "Three costs, and the third…",
"Inside the trial, three practices, each…", "One word in his account needs handling", "He is
explicit about what it is and is not" (findings 3, 6, 7). (5) summariser's seams: "remote",
"skilled", "close to half", "later than the hiring", "worst at", "What got easier" (findings 1, 3,
4). (6) source accounting inside the argument: the Move 2 closing paragraph is Reading's Note
restated sentence for sentence — "no count is given beyond 'a handful'", "no figure follows the
forcing function" — and it is the paragraph carrying the country claim (finding 1). (7) not
applicable.

Word count: 2,938 rendered against a 3,000 ceiling. The cuts in findings 1, 3, 4 and 7 remove
roughly 120 words; finding 2's addition puts about 40 back.

## 1. The Move 2 closing paragraph gives Groove a country and a legal regime the post does not, and duplicates Reading

**Quote** — Move 2, last paragraph: *"The post is one side of a two-sided event. No one he let go
is quoted; no count is given beyond "a handful"; no figure follows the forcing function to show that
milestones were hit faster once it existed. And his team was remote, skilled, and in a country
where an employer can part with someone at the end of a short trial on his own judgment. What
transfers is the shape — a date set at the start, feedback during, other people's judgment before
the decision — and the shape is what the rest of this chapter takes."*

**Lens** — Story craft (inference inside the source) / §6d / the §6i trap.

**What's wrong** — The post names no country, and nothing on the page does either. "Remote" is
not in it: the nearest thing is "communicate with each other on Slack all day", which is not the
same claim. "On his own judgment" contradicts the sentence the chapter quotes two paragraphs
earlier ("I trust my team's … judgment even more than my own"). And the whole "in a country where
an employer can…" clause is the US-at-will frame §6i says must not leak into the argument — here it
is placed as the case's ground, unsourced, one paragraph before the box. The three evidence-class
sentences before it are Reading's Note, which already carries them per §6d. What the paragraph
needs to do is hand the reader the transferable shape, and that is its last sentence.

**Rewrite** — *"What transfers is the shape — a date set at the start, feedback during, other
people's judgment before the decision — and the shape is what the rest of this chapter takes.
What the trial ends is, in his words, "a paid consulting gig"; what ending an employment takes is
a different question, and on Dutch ground it is the box's."* (Cuts about 55 words; Reading keeps
the evidence-class note.)

## 2. The box sends the chapter's own case to the UWV, and the court route is unsourced

**Quote** — Box: *"and the exceptions that follow include permission from the UWV under article
671a and termination during the trial period. Outside those, the employee's written agreement or
a court is the route."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b: statute quoted, nothing overstated).

**What's wrong** — Art. 671a lid 1 in the consulted text: *"De werkgever die voornemens is de
arbeidsovereenkomst op te zeggen op grond van artikel 669, lid 3, onderdeel a of b, verzoekt
hiervoor schriftelijk toestemming aan het Uitvoeringsinstituut werknemersverzekeringen"* — UWV
permission is the route for grounds a and b only. Ground d, the one the box says "covers the case
in this chapter", is the kantonrechter's: art. 671b lid 1 — *"De kantonrechter kan op verzoek van
de werkgever de arbeidsovereenkomst ontbinden: a. op grond van artikel 669, lid 3, onderdelen c
tot en met i"*. As written, a reader with a performance case is pointed at the UWV, and the court
— the door that is actually theirs — appears as "a court", with no article behind it and 671b
absent from the checked line. This is the one finding that changes what the reader does.

**Rewrite** — `pending fact-check` (671a lid 1 and 671b lid 1 need claim rows; same registered
source): *"and the exceptions that follow include termination during the trial period and
permission "als bedoeld in artikel 671a" — which article 671a confines to grounds a and b of
article 669. For the ground this chapter is about, the route is the court: article 7:671b, "De
kantonrechter kan op verzoek van de werkgever de arbeidsovereenkomst ontbinden: a. op grond van
artikel 669, lid 3, onderdelen c tot en met i". Outside those, the employee's written agreement is
what article 671 names."* Add 671a and 671b to the checked line and to the Reading source line.
Zero-fact fallback if the claim rows wait: cut "permission from the UWV under article 671a and"
and "or a court", leaving *"the exceptions that follow include termination during the trial period;
outside them, the employee's written agreement is what the article names, and the other routes are
what the lawyer is for."*

## 3. "Later than the hiring" — the post says the opposite; and "each a correction" is true of one practice in three

**Quote** — Move 2: *"The failure he owns is later than the hiring, and it is a failure of timing.
"My own hesitance to spot poor fits and wait too long" had cost, in his list:"* — Move 2: *"Inside
the trial, three practices, each a correction of something he had done wrong."*

**Lens** — Story craft / counting opener (modes 4 and 5).

**What's wrong** — Turnbull: "I've always felt like the fact that they didn't work out because of a
cultural fit, versus a skill one, is even more my fault; that I should've been better at spotting
the poor fit before I hired them." He owns a spotting failure *and* a timing failure, and the
quoted clause itself says "hesitance to spot poor fits". The chapter's reading — that the cost is in
the delay — is right and is what the costs list shows; it does not need the false "later than the
hiring" to get there. Of the three practices, only the feedback one is framed as a correction
("Early on, I used to wait…"); team feedback and asking the employee are given as practice, not
penance. And the post has four steps, not three — the omitted one (knowing the person's goals) is a
fair cut, but "three practices" reads as the post's count.

**Rewrite** — *"The failure he owns is not one of skill. "My own hesitance to spot poor fits and
wait too long" had cost, in his list:"* — *"Inside the trial, he describes what he does now, starting
with the thing he had got wrong. On feedback:"*

## 4. "Close to half", "worst at", "not a boast", "a founder's, not a filing"

**Quote** — Move 2, first paragraph: *"Alex Turnbull wrote a post about the part of the job he was
worst at. The number he starts with is not a boast: "In our three years … nearly 40% of the team we
launched with." By his own account, then — a founder's, not a filing — close to half the people he
started with had gone within three years, and he had let them go."*

**Lens** — Numbers or nothing (§4) / apparatus (modes 2, 5).

**What's wrong** — "Nearly 40%" is not "close to half"; the §6i entry flagged the figure as round
and self-reported precisely so the draft would not move it. "He had let them go" merges two
sentences the post keeps apart — "let a handful of employees [go]" and "turned over nearly 40%" —
and turnover is not all dismissal. "Worst at" is not in the post ("something I've always had trouble
with", "still struggle with"). "Not a boast" is a verdict placed before the reader has the number.
"A founder's, not a filing" is Reading's Note in the argument. The quotation carries everything the
paragraph needs; "by his own account" is the §5 attribution and should stay.

**Rewrite** — *"Alex Turnbull wrote a post about the part of the job he says he has always had
trouble with. The figure he starts with is his own: "But still, on a small team, a few employees is
a lot. We've turned over nearly 40% of the team we launched with." By his own account, that is the
cost side of three years of hiring, and the post is about the part of it he calls his own."*
(This also drops the source's "employees ago" typo — see finding 9.)

## 5. The box's first paragraph calls the case "a freedom you do not have" and its fourth says the case is a task contract

**Quote** — Box: *"If you are, read this before the conversation, not after it, because the case
above describes a freedom you do not have."* — Box: *"Groove's two-to-four-week paid trial is not
this — it is a contract for a task before any job exists, and chapter nineteen's box applies to it."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b) / the §6i trap in the other direction.

**What's wrong** — Ending a "paid consulting gig" on its date is not a freedom Dutch law withholds;
the box's own fourth paragraph says so. What the Dutch reader lacks is the freedom to treat a
self-set date as the end of an *employment*, which is the reader's actual case in Move 1 (three
months in, past any proeftijd). The opening sentence should say that, not describe the US case as
the thing the reader cannot have. The fourth paragraph is right and stays.

**Rewrite** — *"If you are, read this before the conversation, not after it, because the date this
chapter tells you to set is a date for a decision, not for a dismissal, and here the two are
different acts."*

## 6. "Told in good time, and given a real chance to improve" — the statute has a third condition, and Turnbull's notes-to-self are not notice

**Quote** — Box: *"— "mits de werkgever de werknemer hiervan tijdig in kennis heeft gesteld en hem in
voldoende mate in de gelegenheid heeft gesteld zijn functioneren te verbeteren". Told in good
time, and given a real chance to improve. The notes Turnbull keeps for himself are, here, close to
a precondition of the ground existing."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b: nothing understated either).

**What's wrong** — Art. 669 lid 3 onderdeel d continues: *"en de ongeschiktheid niet het gevolg is
van onvoldoende zorg van de werkgever voor scholing van de werknemer of voor de
arbeidsomstandigheden van de werknemer"* — the unsuitability must not be the employer's own doing
through lack of training or working conditions. For a first employer who has just read ch. 18 on
handing work over badly, that is the condition most likely to bite, and the box stops one clause
short of it. Second, Turnbull's notes are "for your own benefit" — the statute's precondition is that
the *employee* was told (*in kennis gesteld*); a private log is evidence that feedback happened, not
the feedback. The gloss as written lets a reader think the notebook is the compliance.

**Rewrite** — `pending fact-check` for the third clause (same article, needs a claim row): extend the
quotation through *"…zijn functioneren te verbeteren en de ongeschiktheid niet het gevolg is van
onvoldoende zorg van de werkgever voor scholing van de werknemer of voor de arbeidsomstandigheden
van de werknemer"* and gloss: *"Told in good time, given a real chance to improve, and not left
untrained. Feedback given as it arises, and a record that it was given, are here close to a
precondition of the ground existing; notes kept only for yourself are not notice to the person."*
Zero-fact fallback: keep the quotation as is and change only the last sentence to the one above.

## 7. The "fit" paragraph is earned and placed right; its opening announces it and its last clause states Dutch law loosely

**Quote** — Move 3: *"One word in his account needs handling. Fit — cultural, chemistry — is a real
thing and a dangerous one, because it is the word under which an owner's discomfort with someone
unlike them gets recorded as a finding. The defence is the same as for every other judgment in this
book: if you cannot write down what is not working in terms of the work — what was asked, what was
done, what it cost — you do not yet have a fit problem. You have a feeling, and a feeling is not
grounds, in your notes or, as the box says, in law."*

**Lens** — Self-announcing climax (mode 4) / §5b leak / is the addition earned.

**What's wrong** — The paragraph is the chapter's, not Turnbull's — his whole frame is "cultural
fit" and a sports analogy — and the chapter is right to push back, because Monday 1 rests on it and
the box's ground d is written in terms of "de bedongen arbeid". So it is earned, and sitting between
the judgment paragraph and the box is the right place: it is the hinge from "whose judgment" to
"what the law will accept as a judgment". Two faults. "One word in his account needs handling" is
the narrator clearing his throat before the point. And "a feeling is not grounds … in law" is a
statement about Dutch law outside the box that the box does not support — art. 669 lid 3 has a
ground g, a disturbed working relationship, which the box rightly leaves out of scope; "in law" is
therefore both unsourced and, on its face, wrong. Tie the sentence to the box's ground, not to law.

**Rewrite** — *"Fit — cultural, chemistry, Turnbull's own word for it — is a real thing and a
dangerous one, because it is the word under which an owner's discomfort with someone unlike them
gets recorded as a finding. The defence is the one this book gives every judgment: if you cannot
write down what is not working in terms of the work — what was asked, what was done, what it cost —
you do not yet have a fit problem. You have a feeling, and a feeling is not a finding in your
notes, and it is not the ground the box quotes."*

## 8. Two loose statements of Dutch law outside the box, and one in the decision

**Quote** — Decision box: *"when it will be later, costlier, and on Dutch ground possibly not yours to
set at all."* — Move 3, boundary paragraph: *"And in the Netherlands the date is only partly yours:
the statutory trial period is short and has to be written into the contract, and after it the
calendar belongs to the law, in the box below."* — Monday 5: *"On the date, say one of two
sentences. Yes, and here is what permanent looks like, or no, and here is how we part."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b quarantine) / the practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong** — "Possibly not yours to set at all" overshoots: the decision date is always the
owner's to set; what is not theirs is what the date can end. Move 3 paraphrases art. 652 outside the
box ("has to be written into the contract") and adds "the calendar belongs to the law", which is
the chapter's phrase, not the statute's. Monday 5 is the one place a Dutch reader with an employee
past the proeftijd acts, and "no, and here is how we part" is exactly the sentence the box says
cannot be said unilaterally; Monday 2 sends them to the lawyer before the *announcement* of the
date, but Monday 5 does not send them anywhere before the *no*.

**Rewrite** — Decision: *"when it will be later, costlier, and on Dutch ground a decision about a
conversation the law has already shaped."* Move 3: *"And on Dutch ground the date is only partly
yours: what a trial period can be, and what ending an employment after one takes, are in the box
below."* Monday 5, add one sentence: *"On Dutch ground, the second sentence is the one you have
already put in front of the lawyer, in the words they gave you."*

## 9. Small seams: "What got easier", "He is explicit", "what the case below actually describes", and a source typo carried verbatim

**Quote** — Move 2: *"What got easier was being unable to avoid it."* — Move 2: *"He is explicit about
what it is and is not:"* — Move 1: *"That is a different thing to build than a management style, and
it is what the case below actually describes."* — Move 2: *""In our three years, we've only let a
handful of employees ago.""*

**Lens** — Rhythm / apparatus (modes 2, 4, 5).

**What's wrong** — The post says nothing got easier; it says the conversation "doesn't get any
easier" and the trial makes it "inescapable". "What got easier" invents a consolation. "He is
explicit about" announces a quotation that speaks for itself. "What the case below actually
describes" is the chapter pointing at its own structure. And "employees ago" is the post's typo for
"go", reproduced inside quotation marks without a [sic]; finding 4's rewrite starts the quotation
one sentence later and removes the problem.

**Rewrite** — *"What changed was that it could no longer be avoided."* — cut "He is explicit about
what it is and is not:" and run the quotation on from the previous sentence with a colon. — Move 1:
*"That is a different thing to build than a management style."* — quotation: as in finding 4.

## 10. Reading's Note says nothing from the people let go appears; the post relays them

**Quote** — Reading, Note: *"nothing from the people let go appears in the post"*.

**Lens** — Sourcing (§5) — the Note has to be exactly right because it is where the caveat lives.

**What's wrong** — Turnbull: "I've heard this feedback later on from employees I've let go; quitting
is hard and uncomfortable. Getting fired forces you out the door and on to your next chapter." And
the "several people … didn't think there was a fit" passage the chapter quotes is also the
employees' side, in his words. Nothing is quoted from them; something from them appears, relayed by
the person who let them go, which is a weaker thing than nothing and should be said as what it is.

**Rewrite** — *"the people let go appear only as he relays them — several who said the fit was not
there, and some who told him later it had been for the best — and no figure shows the forcing
function producing the faster milestones he says the delay had cost."*

## Overall note

The chapter's rule is right, its case is right, and its Monday is the most failable in the part.
What needs doing is the same as for ch. 19: take the chapter's hands off the post — "remote", the
country sentence, "later than the hiring", "close to half", "each a correction", "what got easier",
"worst at" — and Move 2 stands on Turnbull's own sentences, which are strong enough: nearly 40%,
"more than qualified", the three costs, the 2-4 week trial as "a paid consulting gig", the forcing
function, "this helped nobody", "even more than my own". Then fix the box's one door (finding 2),
which is the single change that alters what a Dutch reader does, and take the two sentences that
describe Groove's freedom out of the Dutch reader's path (findings 1 and 5) so the box is not
arguing with the chapter. Findings 2 and 6 are the two `pending fact-check` items; both are extra
sentences from the same consolidated statute already in the ledger, and both have a zero-fact
fallback if the claim rows have to wait.
