# Craft review — ch. 13, Profit Is Not Cash

Reviewed against CONTEXT.md §3 (template), §4 (voice), the Instacart second-use ledger entry, and
§6d (caveat placement). Facts assumed correct throughout. No rewrite below adds a fact; no rewrite
moves a caveat out of the reader's path in a way §6d forbids; the three-quantities rule from the
ledger (profit, operating cash, change in balance) is respected in every rewrite and is in fact the
subject of finding 1.

Sweep result for the six known failure modes: #6 (source accounting in the argument's path) is the
dominant infection — Move 2 opens with a paragraph of reconciliation arithmetic before the fact the
chapter itself calls "the fact worth carrying" (finding 4). #3 (second-person flinch) appears at
the two sharpest moments of the chapter's best section, where the owner becomes "he" (finding 2),
and in Move 1's corollary (finding 7). #2 (apparatus surfacing) shows in the Move 1 opener, which
describes the book instead of the reader (finding 5), and in Boundaries' "same class of debt"
(finding 9). #4 (self-counting openers) appears once, lightly (finding 10). #1 (verdict-first) is
mild here because an S-1 has no protagonist — which is itself the chapter-specific problem
(finding 3). #5 (summarizer's seams) is largely absent; every figure is glossed.

The chapter-specific findings are 1, 3 and 6. The chapter is, on the whole, one of the stronger
ones: the March/May paragraph, "meets its own success as a liquidity event", and "Timing of
collection. Timing of payment." are the real thing. The damage is concentrated where it costs most.

## 1. Monday 3 reconciles across one bridge when the chapter has just built two

**Quote:** "Reconcile last month's profit to last month's cash movement. Receivables, payables and
stock, money held for the tax authority, then non-cash entries. If the four do not close the gap,
something is happening that you have not found yet."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** The chapter's central teaching is "Two bridges, not one" — profit to operating
cash, then operating cash to the change in the balance — and the ledger's own prohibition is that
operating cash is never the bank balance. This action asks the reader to close the gap between
profit and "cash movement" (which a reader with a bank statement will read as the balance) using
only first-bridge items. Followed exactly, it fails on any month the reader bought a laptop or
paid themselves, and the closing line then tells them "something is happening that you have not
found yet" — when what is happening is the second bridge the chapter told them about. A Monday
action contradicting the chapter's method is the most expensive kind of finding.

**Rewrite:** "Reconcile last month's profit to last month's change in the bank balance, in two
steps. First bridge: receivables, payables and stock, money held for the tax authority, non-cash
entries. Second bridge: equipment, loans drawn or repaid, and what you took out. If the two
bridges do not close the gap, something is happening that you have not found yet."

## 2. The chapter's best paragraph hands the reader's payroll to "he"

**Quote:** "Which is why businesses fail while growing, and why the failure surprises the owner:
every indicator he was taught to watch improved right up to the week the payroll did not clear."
— and, twelve lines later — "An owner can read every statement he has and still never see the
operating cycle as one demand on cash, because no line adds it up for him."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** These are the two sharpest sentences in the chapter — the payroll that does not
clear, the number no line adds up — and at both the reader is moved out of the chair and a
third-person owner put in it. Everything around them is second person ("Now win the year you have
been working towards", "The extra €36,000 has to come from somewhere"). The flinch is exactly
where the reader should feel it was written about them.

**Rewrite:** "Which is why businesses fail while growing, and why it surprises you: every
indicator you were taught to watch improves right up to the week the payroll does not clear." And:
"You can read every statement you have and still never see the operating cycle as one demand on
cash, because no line adds it up for you."

## 3. The reader is in Instacart's filing and the chapter does not say where

**Quote:** "The clearest published demonstration is in a document the reader will never file." —
"Two causes of the gap appear in the filing, and they are the two the reader has as well." —
"Timing of collection. Timing of payment. That is the whole subject, and a business with eleven
customers has it in exactly the form a business with millions does."

**Lens:** Story craft (and the reader in the chair).

**What's wrong:** Three times Move 2 talks about "the reader" in the third person, and each time
it is apologising for the scale mismatch rather than using it. But the mismatch is the story. The
filing's own phrase — "timing of vendor payments" — is, read from the other end, some supplier's
receivable: a business the reader's size, being paid on a large company's schedule. That is why
the Dutch box's thirty-day cap for a large debtor and an SME creditor exists, and why Monday 5
sends the reader to check it. The chapter has every piece of this on the page and never turns
the filing round. Without that turn, Move 2 is a document read aloud; with it, the reader has a
seat in the case — on the wrong side of it.

**Rewrite:** Opening sentence: "The clearest published demonstration is in a document you will
never file." Middle: "Two causes of the gap appear in the filing, and you have both." Closing
beat, replacing the last quoted paragraph at the same length: "Timing of collection. Timing of
payment. Somebody is on the other end of that second phrase — a supplier whose receivable is
Instacart's 'timing', and who is very likely your size. That is the whole subject, and a
business with eleven customers has it in exactly the form a business with millions does: you are
just more often the vendor being timed." (No new fact: the vendor quotation is already on the
page; that vendors are smaller than Instacart is the Boundaries' own claim.)

## 4. Move 2 opens with the reconciliation and saves the surprise for paragraph three

**Quote:** "For the full year 2022, 'net cash provided by operating activities was $277 million,
which consisted of net income of $428 million, adjusted for certain non-cash items of $275
million' and 'net cash inflows from changes in operating assets and liabilities of $124 million'.
Watch the signs, because they are the lesson: net income of $428m, less $275m of net non-cash
adjustments, plus $124m from movements in operating assets and liabilities, gives $277m."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (failure mode 6).

**What's wrong:** The first thing the reader meets in the case is a sum being checked — four
figures, two signs, "gives $277m" — and the chapter then says "Now the six months to June 2022,
which is the fact worth carrying." If it is the fact worth carrying, it should be carried first:
a loss of $74m and $99m of cash from operations is the moment of not-knowing the whole chapter
turns on, and it lands flat after a paragraph of arithmetic. The signs are not the lesson; the
lesson is that the two numbers can point opposite ways. The full-year anatomy belongs second, as
the explanation, and in one sentence rather than a worked reconciliation — the Reading list
already names the three comparisons.

**Rewrite:** Reorder the three periods: H1 2022 (the loss that generated cash) first, opened
"Start with the six months to June 2022, because it is the one to carry"; then H1 2023 (identical
figures, coincidence); then full-year 2022 as the anatomy, with the arithmetic compressed to:
"For the full year 2022 the filing shows the parts: net income of $428m, less $275m of non-cash
items, plus $124m of movements in operating assets and liabilities, gives operating cash of
$277m. The non-cash item that dominates is 'a deferred tax benefit of $373 million' — a gain that
raised profit without raising cash, and so has to come back out." Cut "Watch the signs, because
they are the lesson" and the duplicated quotation of the same three figures. The figure table
already displays the ordering; it can follow the new order or stay as is.

## 5. Move 1 opens by describing the book

**Quote:** "The previous chapter named three statements and asked you to look at available cash
every week. This one explains why that instruction was separate from the profit figure, and why
the two numbers can tell opposite stories about the same month."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (failure mode 2).

**What's wrong:** The template says Move 1 opens "on the reader's actual position, not on
history" — and this opens on the previous chapter's history and a synopsis of "this one". The
reader's actual position is already on the page, four paragraphs down: March, the invoice, May.
That paragraph is the best opening in the section and is buried under two definitions.

**Rewrite:** Open Move 1 on the mechanism paragraph as it stands — "You do the work in March,
invoice at the end of March, and are paid in May…" — followed by the three definitions, and let
the reference to chapter twelve survive as one clause inside the first definition list's lead-in:
"Three numbers get mistaken for each other, which is where most of the confusion lives — and why
the previous chapter's weekly cash figure was given separately from profit:". Cut the current
first paragraph entirely.

## 6. "Three things get called 'cash'" — nobody calls profit cash

**Quote:** "Three things get called 'cash' and they are not the same, which is where most of the
confusion lives:"

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** The first of the three is profit, which no owner calls cash; the false note is
in the lead sentence of the chapter's defining list. What owners actually conflate is "how the
money is doing" — "we made forty this month" said of a profit figure, with the bank in mind.

**Rewrite:** "Three numbers get mistaken for one another when an owner says 'we made money this
month', and they are not the same:" (If finding 5 is taken, this line becomes the list's lead-in
there.)

---

## 7. The corollary "catches people" — not you

**Quote:** "Which produces the corollary that catches people who are doing well: growth magnifies
the cash cycle, in whichever direction that cycle already runs."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** This is the chapter's rule in one sentence, and the person it catches is
"people". The reader is, by the book's premise, the person doing well.

**Rewrite:** "Which produces the corollary that catches you precisely when things are going well:
growth magnifies the cash cycle, in whichever direction that cycle already runs."

## 8. "That is the whole trap in one movement" reuses the chapter's best cadence

**Quote:** "That is the whole trap in one movement. The extra €36,000 has to come from somewhere:
retained cash you had, an overdraft, a supplier who agrees to wait, or work you turn down."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "That is the whole subject" closes Move 2 and earns its weight. Repeating the
"That is the whole X" shape one section later, as a paragraph opener announcing the paragraph,
spends it. The paragraph is stronger starting on the money.

**Rewrite:** Cut the first sentence. Begin: "The extra €36,000 has to come from somewhere: …"

## 9. Boundaries speaks in the debt register's voice

**Quote:** "That absence is the same class of debt as the previous chapter's weekly numbers."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack (failure mode 2).

**What's wrong:** "Class of debt" is the register's admin vocabulary, and the sentence does not
change how the reader applies anything — the preceding sentence already states the limitation
that does ("no small business in this book is shown measuring its own cash gap and acting on
it"). The Reading note already carries the debt and points to the method page, which is where
§6d puts it.

**Rewrite:** Cut the sentence. The boundary ends on "…acting on it."

## 10. "Four levers." counts its contents, and collections are put "on the list"

**Quote:** "Four levers. Invoice as soon as the contract and the work allow…" — "Collections
belong on the list too, with a caveat: chasing is a real lever and worth doing properly, but it
cannot repair a model whose ordinary terms require more cash than the business can fund. Fix the
terms, then chase."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (failure mode 4).

**What's wrong:** A section opener announcing its own count, then a follow-on paragraph
negotiating with "the list". The final sentence — "Fix the terms, then chase." — is the right
close for the section and is the only thing the collections paragraph needs.

**Rewrite:** Drop "Four levers." and start on "Invoice as soon as the contract and the work
allow…". Replace the collections paragraph with: "Chasing is a real lever and worth doing
properly, but it cannot repair a model whose ordinary terms require more cash than the business
can fund. Fix the terms, then chase."

## Overall note

Turn the scale mismatch from an apology into the spine. Right now the chapter says three times
that the reader is not Instacart and then, in Boundaries, that the bargaining power does not
transfer. But the filing's "timing of vendor payments" is the reader's receivable seen from the
paying end, and the Dutch box's thirty-day cap for large debtors against SME creditors is the
law's answer to exactly that line. Finding 3 makes the turn in Move 2; findings 4 and 5 clear the
path to it by leading with the loss-that-generated-cash and the March/May month rather than a
reconciliation and a synopsis; finding 1 then makes Monday obey the chapter's own two bridges.
Taken together, the reader stops watching a large company's arithmetic and starts reading the
document as the party on the other side of it.
