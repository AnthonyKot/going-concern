# Craft review — ch. 18, Writing It Down

Reviewed 2026-08-26 against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §6d, §6j and the three-correction review recorded
under §6j. Every rewrite below uses facts already on the page; none touches a quoted string. One
finding (no. 5) removes a figure that §6j records as already withdrawn — that is a cut, not a
fact change.

## Does it read like a person or a machine? Mostly a person — with a patch visibly sewn on.

Move 1 is the best situation opening in Part IV: the deploy flag nobody remembers adding, the
supplier who will actually bend, "the answer lived nowhere except the exchange that just
happened." The reader is in the chair from the first line. Move 3's "the same shape twice" test
is a real rule a reader can say in one sentence, and "describe but not install" is the kind of
phrase a practitioner keeps. The Monday list is concrete enough to fail at.

The damage is concentrated in Move 2, and it has one cause: the three corrections were added as
two new paragraphs (5 and 6) without removing the paragraphs they corrected (2 and 3). The case
now tells the same moment twice — once in the withdrawn draft form, with the derived ratio still
attached, then again in the corrected form with a sentence of review-room framing announcing that
the chapter is about to be careful. A reader feels the seam even if they cannot name it. The
second-largest problem is that the Monday list hands the document down founder-to-hire, which is
the one thing the case says Galperin's mechanism does *not* do.

Sweep of the known modes: (1) verdict-first — yes, para 2 announces "the paradox this chapter
exists to explain" before the quote that is the paradox; (2) apparatus in prose — "this book has
already used", "how this chapter would overstate its own case", "the handoff test above";
(3) flinch to third person — clean, second person holds throughout; (4) counting openings —
"Two things in that account"; (5) summarizer seams — the surviving "three to four times" (twice);
(6) source accounting in the argument — Move 3's "that is a design claim and it is his"
sentence, already covered in Move 2 and Reading; (7) scar tissue — the whole of Move 2 paras 5–6's
framing, "by his own account" five times in one Move, the evidence-class caveat stated three times.

## 1. Move 2 tells the exposure twice, and the first telling still carries the withdrawn ratio

**Quote** — para 2: *"The gap showed up first as a timing problem, on tasks he had underestimated:
"Tasks that would take 15-20 minutes of my day as a side activity, required hours for a dedicated
person to complete." Three to four times the duration, on the same task, in the hands of someone
hired specifically to take it off him."* — and para 5: *"Two things in that account are easy to run
together, and running them together is how this chapter would overstate its own case. The first is
what hiring exposed, before any document existed. He gives its shape precisely: …"*

**Lens** — Story craft / Rhythm (template and review seams)

**What's wrong** — The correction was appended, not applied. Paragraph 2 still delivers the
exposure with the derived ratio §6j says was removed; paragraph 5 then re-delivers the same
exposure with the better quotation, introduced by a sentence that belongs in CONTEXT.md, not in
the reader's path. The reader passes through the moment of not-knowing twice, and the second time
they are told the chapter is guarding itself. The good line — "The work moved and he did not" —
arrives after the reader has already been given the answer once.

**Rewrite** — Cut "Three to four times the duration … take it off him." entirely. Cut the first
sentence of para 5 ("Two things in that account … overstate its own case.") and the words "The
first is what hiring exposed, before any document existed." Splice the couple-of-minutes
comparison directly into para 2, so the story runs once, in order:

> The gap showed up first as time, on tasks he had underestimated: "Tasks that would take 15-20
> minutes of my day as a side activity, required hours for a dedicated person to complete." He
> gives one of them its exact shape. A support process that would "take me a couple of minutes to
> go through and respond" can, for a rep who is not a developer and has no view of the development
> queue, "take close to an hour, and require my participation at each one of those decision
> points." Read the second clause twice. The work moved and he did not.

Then para 3 (the intuition quotation) follows as his diagnosis of *why*, para 4 as what he built,
and para 6 — trimmed to begin "The programme came after — his response to that finding, not a
measurement of it." — as the honest state. Net: shorter by about sixty words, one telling, source
order kept.

## 2. Monday hands the document down; the case says the document is written by the people using it

**Quote** — Move 2, para 4: *"The document is not written once by the founder and handed down. It
is written by the people using it, on the case in front of them, and it grows only where it is
actually needed."* — against Monday 2 and 4: *"Pick the top procedure item and write it as steps"*
… *"Record the gap the same day, in two numbers."*

**Lens** — The reader in the chair / Story craft

**What's wrong** — Move 2 singles out one mechanism as "the one piece described precisely enough
to judge as a mechanism": reps write the entry, on the ticket in front of them. The Monday list
then has the reader write the document alone, hand it over, and record the gap themselves — the
founder-writes-and-hands-down shape the case explicitly contrasts. The case's real find is left on
the table at the one point where the reader acts on it.

**Rewrite** — Monday 2 stays (the first version has to exist). Add one sentence to Monday 4, and
cut its last sentence to pay for it:

> Record the gap the same day, in two numbers. Time taken against your own baseline, and whether
> the outcome was correct. Then have the person who used it write the corrections in — the step
> they got wrong, the question they had to ask you — before you touch it. The document is not
> finished until the numbers exist and someone other than you has edited it.

(Cut: "before that it is a draft, however complete it reads." — already said in Move 3.)

## 3. Move 1 and Move 3 make the chapter's sharpest point twice in the same words

**Quote** — Move 1: *"A document takes an afternoon, produces something to point at, and asks
nothing of anyone else. Handing a task over for real means watching someone else do it worse than
you would, live, on a case that might notice"* — and Move 3: *"Writing at your own desk, alone,
costs you nothing socially and asks nothing of anyone else; watching someone else use what you
wrote, on a live case, where they might get it visibly wrong, costs both."*

**Lens** — Rhythm and music

**What's wrong** — Same contrast, same "asks nothing of anyone else", same "live", forty
paragraphs apart. The second occurrence reads as the template refilling a slot the situation
already filled, and it dulls the one idea in the chapter that ought to land once and hard.

**Rewrite** — Keep Move 1's version (it is the better one). In Move 3, open the "comfortable
avoidance" section on what is new there — the unfalsifiable stopping condition — and go straight
to the defence:

> *I'll delegate once it's properly documented* is not a plan. It is a stopping condition with no
> way to fail it: a document can always be made more complete, and completeness was never the
> thing that mattered. The mechanical defence: a document does not count as written down until …

Cut the section's first sentence ("This chapter's whole subject is dangerous in a specific way …
actually avoiding.") and the "Writing at your own desk … costs both." sentence.

## 4. The paradox introduces itself before the reader has met it

**Quote** — *"What happened instead, by his own account, is the paradox this chapter exists to
explain."*

**Lens** — Story craft

**What's wrong** — Climax announced before it is earned. The reader is told there is a paradox,
told the chapter exists for it, and only then shown it. Told the other way round, "more employees
produced more work reaching him" lands as a surprise the reader shares with Galperin.

**Rewrite** —

> What happened instead: more employees produced more work reaching him, not less, because the
> questions that were trivial for him were not trivial for anyone he hired to take them.

## 5. The counter-case block still cites the withdrawn ratio

**Quote** — *"Galperin's account is not that case: the three-to-four-times slowdown is the cost of
a document doing exactly what documents do"*

**Lens** — The practitioner's ear (summarizer's seam)

**What's wrong** — §6j records this figure as derived, unregistered and replaced. It survives here
and in Move 2 para 2 (finding 1). A number the chapter has elsewhere declined to claim, restated
as settled in the closing block, is the kind of thing an owner who has read the source notices.

**Rewrite** — *"Galperin's account is not that case: the slowdown he reports is the cost of a
document doing exactly what documents do"*

## 6. "By his own account" five times in one Move

**Quote** — *"and by his own account he ran it alone"* / *"What happened instead, by his own
account"* / *"By his own account, the reason was not"* / *"He built the structure anyway, for the
part that was actually procedure. By his own account:"* / Boundaries: *"in the same stretch of
years, by his own account"*

**Lens** — Warmth without slack

**What's wrong** — §6j asks for the attribution; it does not ask for it per paragraph. Repeated at
this density it stops being attribution and becomes the register's voice — a reviewer clearing
their throat every time the source speaks. The quotation marks already do the work, and Reading
says it once, properly.

**Rewrite** — Keep the first ("by his own account he ran it alone at the start") and the one in
Boundaries. Cut the other three: *"What happened instead: …"* (finding 4); *"The reason was not
that the people he hired were incapable."*; *"He built the structure anyway, for the part that was
actually procedure: "I started also documenting …"*

## 7. One wrong word in the chapter's best paragraph

**Quote** — *"watching someone else do it worse than you would, live, on a case that might notice"*

**Lens** — The practitioner's ear

**What's wrong** — A case does not notice; a customer does. The rest of the paragraph is the
truest thing in the chapter, and this is the one phrase in it that sounds written rather than
watched.

**Rewrite** — *"watching someone else do it worse than you would, live, in front of a customer who
might notice"*

## 8. The evidence caveat is delivered three times, and its Boundaries version points at nothing

**Quote** — Boundaries, para 2: *"The evidence here is one founder's felt account, not a
measurement. There is no controlled comparison … none of the employees' side of the story. The
growth to more than ten FTE and the documentation programme happened in the same stretch of years,
by his own account, and that is all the source establishes — not that the second produced the
first. Score your own attempt at the handoff test above; a founder's own report of what worked is
the weakest evidence class this book uses, and it is what a business that files with nobody has to
offer."*

**Lens** — Warmth without slack / §6d

**What's wrong** — Move 2 already closes on the principal caveat ("a diagnosis with numbers
attached, and a remedy whose result is not yet in") and Reading holds the evidence-class note.
This is the third statement. "Score your own attempt" is an instruction with no object — score it
against what? — and it is the one sentence here that could turn the caveat toward the reader
instead of at the source. The last clause ("what a business that files with nobody has to offer")
is the warmest line in the section and should survive.

**Rewrite** — Keep the growth-and-programme adjacency sentence (it changes how the reader applies
the advice — §6d says that stays). Replace the rest with:

> Everything above is one founder's felt account: no comparison against undocumented handoffs, no
> figure for how much his own load eased, none of the employees' side. It is what a business that
> files with nobody has to offer. Run the handoff test yourself and the two numbers it produces
> will be better evidence than anything in this chapter.

## 10. Chapter seventeen is recapped three times; the third telling of Galperin is introduced as reuse

**Quote** — Move 3 opening: *"Chapter seventeen split what depends on you into four kinds and
assigned two of them a remedy that has nothing to do with documentation: credentials fail
mechanically … in the room."* — and Move 2 opening: *"Eran Galperin is the founder this book has

**Lens** — Rhythm / The reader in the chair

**What's wrong** — The chapter's first paragraph, Move 3's first paragraph and Boundaries each

case on its third appearance should be introduced as the part the earlier chapters could not
reach, not as a book admitting to reusing a source — "this book has already used" is the

**Rewrite** — Move 3: cut the first paragraph down to its last clause, so the section opens

you have never had to explain it out loud."* Move 2:

> Eran Galperin has been in this book twice: chapter four for his stopping rule, chapter five for
> his move to full time. This is the part of his story those chapters had no use for — what
> happened once Gymdesk outgrew him.

## Overall note

Rebuild Move 2 as a single telling in source order — expectation, exposure (both quotations,
ending on "require my participation at each one of those decision points"), his diagnosis, what he
built, the honest state — and remove every trace of the first draft's version, including both
"three to four times" and the "two things are easy to run together" framing. The correction
recorded in §6j was right; it has not yet been made in the chapter, only added to it. Once Move 2
tells the story once, the Monday list can be turned to face the case's own mechanism (finding 2),
and the chapter's closing — the owed block's "costs nothing today and everything on the one day it
is actually needed" — already works as an ending for a reader today; the pointers to ch. 19, 20
and 23 in Boundaries are fine as forward references, since each is one clause and carries no
weight.
