## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, on the right square, who has written a third stopping rule where the book has two and has read one KVK section as an obligation where the page offers a choice.

Move 1 is the reader's: "It has not failed. That is what makes it hard" is the best first
sentence in Part V, and "whether going on is a decision or the absence of one" is the chapter in
nine words. The case is right and the long quotation is placed where it should be — after the
September 2014 position, before the sale — and the chapter does not put a threshold into the
founders' hands: "It took us a while to come to terms with this reality" is allowed to be what it
is. Monday 3 ("You are not selling. You are finding out what the decision would cost to delay")
and Monday 5's "a third thing … only allowed with a new number and a new date written down, and
once" are failable and are ch. 16's "'not enough evidence yet' is an allowed verdict once" carried
forward exactly. The box keeps the fork first, quotes rather than paraphrases, names the jurist and
the accountant, dates itself, and its closing paragraph ("This box quotes the procedure; it does not
say what your baten are…") is the §5b posture in one sentence.

Two problems carry the weight. First, the rule. The reader arrives here holding ch. 4's sentence
(money threshold, date, consequence from a menu) and ch. 16's (throw-off, run length, date, "raise
prices / cut the offer / take contract work / stop"). Ch. 28 says it is "chapter four's rule with
the consequence changed" and then states a new instrument — "if the last big move has not moved
[the figure] by [the date], we have the talk" — whose trigger is a move, not a run, and whose
consequence is a conversation, not a decision. Ch. 16's sentence, whose fourth consequence is
*stop* and which the ch. 26 review asked to be the gate into this chapter, appears only as a
boundary ("a practice that covers sixteen's number is not in this chapter"). So the book ends with
three rules, and the close's "a number, a date, and a conversation" has quietly replaced ch. 4's
third component. The honest version is smaller: ch. 16's sentence is the rule; ch. 28's contribution
is that each of its first three consequences is a move that gets a date, and when the menu is used
up the consequence left is the one this chapter is for (finding 1). Second, the box. The KVK page's
"Wat als er wel baten zijn?" section says a turboliquidatie *can also* be used by an active
rechtspersoon if it first sells everything and pays creditors as far as possible; its earlier
section says that if baten remain at dissolution, turboliquidatie is not possible and "de
wettelijke vereffeningsprocedure" applies. The box merges these into one obligation — "they must
be realised first … before the dissolution" — and attaches the "veel schulden" quotation to a
condition ("where debts exceed assets") the page states separately. A reader with assets is told
one route where the Chamber describes two (finding 2). A vof reader is on neither side of the
box's fork, and the page says so in one sentence the box does not quote (finding 3).

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "Then the date that should have been a stopping rule
and was not" precedes the September 2014 quotation; "What follows is the sentence this chapter
exists for" precedes the long quotation; "Five years, ended on purpose, late, and in order" closes
Move 2 (finding 6). (2) apparatus: "the reason he is in this chapter for the last time"; "the part
almost never written up"; "No figure is given for what the investors got back, and none is invented
here" (Reading already carries it); "the account is unusual in giving it" (finding 8). (3) flinch:
none — second person holds through Move 1, Move 3 and Monday. (4) counting and self-announcing:
"five times, from five backgrounds" (finding 5); "What follows is the sentence…" (finding 6).
(5) seams: "two engineers", "most of a year", "an SEO agency", "got them into an accelerator",
"ten-hour days", "throughout" (finding 5); "Binpress's order is the general one" (finding 8).
(6) source accounting inside the argument: Reading's Note carries sixth-appearance, evidence class,
better-resourced position, no recovery figure, "almost $1M" not a filing, dateModified; Move 2
repeats the recovery figure and Move 3 does not repeat the rest — one duplication (finding 8).
(7) n/a.

Continuity. Ch. 4's three components are metric-in-money, date, consequence-from-a-menu; ch. 28's
"when the number was small and the consequence was going back to a salary" makes the menu's last
item ch. 4's consequence (finding 7). Ch. 16's sentence is bypassed (finding 1). Ch. 20's forcing

## 1. The end-rule is a third instrument; ch. 16's sentence is the rule and this chapter is its last consequence

**Quote** — Move 3: *"This is chapter twenty's forcing function pointed at the business instead of
at a person, and chapter four's rule with the consequence changed. At the start the consequence
was a salary; at the end it is the procedure in the next section, and the rule reads: if the last
big move has not moved [the figure] by [the date], we have the talk that month. The figure is one
of chapter twenty-two's three, chosen before the move."* — Move 3: *"Chapter sixteen's Pinboard did
not grow for years and paid its owner; a practice that covers sixteen's number is not in this
chapter."* — Move 3: *"Chapter twenty-six's shock that does not lift ends up here too, by the same
test."* — Monday 1: *"One sentence: if [the last big move] has not moved [one of chapter
twenty-two's figures] to [a number] by [a date], we have the talk that month."* — Close: *"the
answer is the same instrument: a number, a date, and a conversation you have agreed in advance to
have."*

**Lens** — (c) one rule, coherent with ch. 4 and ch. 16; §6g ("ch. 28 owns ending"); the ch. 26
review's finding 9.

**What's wrong** — Ch. 16 already gave the reader the sentence for a business she owns: *"This
business must throw off €X in six consecutive months / four consecutive quarters, by date; if it
has not, I will raise prices / cut the offer / take contract work / stop"* — with its run length,
its "a miss is not an instruction to stop", its once-a-year recount and its "'not enough evidence
yet' … once". The ch. 26 review routed the shock that does not lift through that sentence and made
ch. 28 open "only if that consequence is the last of its four." Ch. 28 does not pick that up; it
frames itself as ch. 4 with a different consequence, states a rule whose trigger is a *move* rather
than a run, and makes the consequence "the talk" — a conversation, where ch. 4 insisted the third
component be an action chosen in advance. The reader now holds three sentences and cannot say which
decides. The three instruments are reconcilable and the reconciliation is stronger than any of
them: ch. 16's first three consequences *are* the big moves, each of them needs the date ch. 20
taught, and when the last has had its run the consequence left in ch. 16's sentence is stop —
which is what this chapter is for. Binpress supports exactly that shape: the pivot was the last
move on the menu, and it had no date. The close then keeps ch. 4's "number, date, consequence"
rather than swapping in "conversation."

**Rewrite** — Move 3: *"This is chapter twenty's forcing function pointed at the business instead
of at a person, and it is not a new rule. The sentence you wrote in chapter sixteen already ends
in four consequences — raise prices, cut the offer, take contract work, stop — and the first three
are the big moves. What this chapter adds is that each of them gets a date of its own: if [the
move] has not moved [the figure] by [the date], the next consequence on the list is the one you
take, and when the list is used up, the one left is stop, and the procedure in the next section is
how it is carried out. The figure is sixteen's throw-off or one of chapter twenty-two's three,
chosen before the move."* — Move 3, boundary: *"Chapter sixteen's Pinboard did not grow for years
and paid its owner; a business whose sentence is still being met is not in this chapter, and a
single miss is not an instruction to open it."* — Move 3: *"Chapter twenty-six's shock that does
not lift arrives here the same way, through sixteen's sentence, and only when its last consequence
is the one left."* — Monday 1: *"Take the sentence from chapter sixteen and give its next
consequence a date. One line: if [the move] has not moved [sixteen's throw-off or one of chapter
twenty-two's figures] to [a number] by [a date], we take the next consequence on the list that
month. Date it, sign it with whoever owns the business with you, and put it where the weekly
numbers are read. When the list has one item left, this chapter is the month."* — Close: *"the
answer is the same instrument: a number, a date, and a consequence you chose while you could still
choose."*

## 2. The box turns the Chamber's "can also" into "must", drops the statutory liquidation route, and pins "veel schulden" to a condition the page states elsewhere

**Quote** — Box: *"Where there are assets, they must be realised first and the creditors paid as
far as they go, in their legal order, before the dissolution; where debts exceed assets, the
Chamber's own guidance is to consider bankruptcy instead, with a curator: "Zijn er veel schulden?
Dan kun je overwegen om geen turboliquidatie te doen, en in plaats daarvan een faillissement aan te
vragen.""*

**Lens** — (d) official guidance quoted not paraphrased, nothing derived; §6i's "never suggest
turboliquidatie as a way to avoid creditors" and "baten is broader than owners assume."

**What's wrong** — The page says two different things about assets, in two sections, and the box
has merged them. First section: *"Zijn er wel baten op het moment van ontbinding? Dan is een
turboliquidatie niet mogelijk en wordt je bedrijf niet direct beëindigd. Je moet dan de wettelijke
vereffeningsprocedure volgen. De stappen van deze procedure staan in de wet."* — assets at
dissolution means no turboliquidatie and the statutory liquidation instead. "Wat als er wel baten
zijn?": *"Maar de turboliquidatie kun je ook gebruiken voor actieve rechtspersonen die nog baten
hebben. De aanwezige baten moet je dan vóór de ontbinding eerst 'vereffenen'. Dit betekent dat je
alle bezittingen verkoopt. En je schuldeisers zoveel mogelijk afbetaalt, zodat er geen baten meer
overblijven. Hierbij houd je rekening met de belangen en rangorde van schuldeisers."* — an option
("kun je ook"), whose "moet" is conditional on choosing it. The box's "they must be realised first
… before the dissolution" presents the option as the only path and never mentions the statutory
procedure, which for a bv with assets is the default. "As far as they go, in their legal order" is a
fair gloss of "zoveel mogelijk … rangorde" once the sentence is placed as an option. Second, "where
debts exceed assets" is the page's *"Als er meer schulden dan bezittingen zijn, vragen de
schuldeisers óf de bestuurder een faillissement aan"* (a statement of what happens, in the first
section); the quotation the box attaches to it is from "Wat als er nog schulden zijn?", whose
condition is "veel schulden" and whose first sentence — *"Een turboliquidatie kan ook als niet alle
schulden zijn afbetaald"* — the box omits. Omitting it is the cautious side of §6i, but the gloss
should match the sentence it quotes. Both corrections are the page's own words.

**Rewrite** — `pending fact-check` (three new spans from a registered source need claim rows):
*"Where there are assets at the moment of dissolution the fast route is closed — "Dan is een
turboliquidatie niet mogelijk … Je moet dan de wettelijke vereffeningsprocedure volgen" — and the
Chamber describes one way to reach it: sell everything and pay the creditors first, "zoveel
mogelijk … Hierbij houd je rekening met de belangen en rangorde van schuldeisers", so that no baten
remain when the resolution is passed. Where the debts are many, its guidance is to consider
bankruptcy instead, with a curator: "Zijn er veel schulden? …""* Zero-fact fallback: *"Where there
are assets, the Chamber describes two paths — the statutory liquidation procedure, or selling
everything and paying the creditors as far as they go, in their order, so that nothing remains at
the moment of dissolution — and which is yours is the jurist's question; where the debts are many,
its guidance is to consider bankruptcy instead, with a curator: "Zijn er veel schulden? …""*

## 3. A vof is on neither side of the fork; the creditor-notification duty and the misuse penalties are the page's warning and the box carries only half of it; the law is "tijdelijk"

**Quote** — Box: *"the formal end has two routes, and which one is yours turns on legal form"*;
*"Trading in your own name — an eenmanszaak"* / *"A bv or other legal person"*. — Box: *"And the
route carries a filing duty since the transparency law of November 2023: "Binnen veertien dagen na
het ontbindingsbesluit moet het bestuur verschillende (financiële) documenten indienen bij ons.""*
— Box, close: *"what you personally answer for if the route is misused."*

**Lens** — (d) fork first, scope read from the source (the ch. 20 review's finding 2); §6i's
"the box must carry that warning alongside the mechanism, not the mechanism alone"; §5b dating.

**What's wrong** — Three things. (i) The turboliquidatie page: *"De turboliquidatie kun je niet
gebruiken voor het beëindigen van een eenmanszaak of vennootschap onder firma (vof)."* A vof is not
an eenmanszaak and not a legal person, so a partnership reader — common at this book's scale — reads
the box and finds no door. One clause closes it. (ii) The fourteen-day duty is quoted; the sentence
after it is the mechanism that protects creditors and the box does not carry it: *"Nadat alles is
ingediend, stelt het bestuur schuldeisers schriftelijk op de hoogte van de turboliquidatie."* And
the page's "Risico's" and "Persoonlijk aansprakelijk" sections — creditors can ask the court to
re-liquidate, a director who ranked creditors wrongly or hid baten can be held personally liable,
the OM can seek a bestuursverbod of up to five years — are what §6i said must sit beside the
mechanism. The box gestures at them ("what you personally answer for") without a source sentence.
(iii) The page calls the law *"de tijdelijke wet Transparantie turboliquidatie"* and adds *"De
nieuwe wet is tijdelijk, de kans bestaat dat er een permanente wet komt"* — a reader in two years
needs "temporary" more than "November 2023", and the checked line's "Wet transparantie
turboliquidatie" drops the word.

**Rewrite** — `pending fact-check` for (i) and (ii) (new spans, same source): after the fork
sentence add *"A partnership is on neither side: "De turboliquidatie kun je niet gebruiken voor het
beëindigen van een eenmanszaak of vennootschap onder firma (vof)", and a vof's end is the jurist's
question from the start."* After the fourteen-day quotation add *"and, once filed, the creditors
are told: "Nadat alles is ingediend, stelt het bestuur schuldeisers schriftelijk op de hoogte van
de turboliquidatie." Misuse is the Chamber's own warning — a creditor can ask the court to
re-liquidate, a director who paid some creditors ahead of others or hid baten can be held
personally liable, and a bestuursverbod of up to five years is possible."* For (iii), zero-fact:
*"since the temporary transparency law in force from 15 November 2023"* and in the checked line
*"the tijdelijke Wet transparantie turboliquidatie"*. Zero-fact fallback for (i): *"A vof is on
neither side of this box; ask before the date."*

## 7. The mirror of chapter four is bent: the salary was one item on the menu, not the rule's consequence

**Quote** — Move 1: *"The stopping rule chapter four gave you was for the start, when the number was
small and the consequence was going back to a salary."* — Move 3: *"chapter four's rule with the
consequence changed. At the start the consequence was a salary"*.

**Lens** — continuity with ch. 4 (three components; the consequence chosen from a menu).

**What's wrong** — Ch. 4's third component is "what failing it means", and its whole argument is

was small and every consequence on its menu was cheap — a salary to go back to, a domain to keep."*
— Move 3: covered by finding 1's rewrite (which removes "chapter four's rule with the consequence

## 10. The book close: it earns its place and it overclaims in one clause; Part I's close carried a limitation and this one carries none

**Quote** — Close: *"Each one was answered by a business that published enough to be checked, and
each answer came with the case that would have proved it wrong — found, or owed and said so."* —
*"the answer is the same instrument: a number, a date, and a conversation you have agreed in
advance to have."*

**Lens** — §1's part-closing rule (counted separately, closes a part not a chapter); §4 voice; the
ch. 5 close as the form.

**What's wrong** — The close earns its place: 140 words, no sale, the last question folded back

evidence base is skewed to software founders who publish; the method page says so) is the thing a
