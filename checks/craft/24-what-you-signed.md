# Craft review — ch. 24, What You Signed

Reviewed 2026-08-28, first review of a first draft, against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §5b, §6d, §6f and the
§6i entry for this chapter; every Dutch quotation checked against `auteurswet-2026.raw` (BWBR0001886,
consolidated 2026-01-01) and `bw6-2026.raw` (BWBR0005289). All seven statutory strings are exact
(art. 1; art. 7; art. 2 lid 3 first sentence; art. 2 lid 3 scope clause; art. 233 in full; art. 234
lid 1 first clause; the on-request clause). No quotation is wrong. Three glosses are wider than the
text they gloss, and one of them is the risk the §6i entry handed forward. There is no case, by
finding, and the chapter says so four times. No rewrite below adds a fact; two items would need a
claim row and are marked `pending fact-check`, both extra sentences from sources already registered.

## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, with a good ear, whose portable argument keeps stepping over the line into the box.

Move 1 is right: "Nothing has gone wrong. That is the situation" puts the reader on the square
where this chapter is actually read, and the second paragraph — writing it down "feels like the
moment the business stops being a relationship and starts being a contract" — is the objection a
reader would raise, answered before they raise it. The four-agreements map is the book's own, is
coherent, and is ordered by a stated rule (permanent, recurring, unchangeable, largest) that Move 3
states in one sentence and that a reader could repeat. The thresholds for the hour are rules of
thumb and the Reading Note says so. Monday is the most failable list in Part V: item 2 ("find the
sentence that makes it yours … while they still answer your emails") and item 3 ("move your terms
from the invoice to the quote") are actions with a date on them. The box quotes rather than
paraphrases, names two professionals, and hands the employee/contractor line to ch. 19's box, which
is consistent — ch. 19's box defines employment by "in dienst van de andere partij" (7:610) and
art. 7 Auteurswet turns on "in dienst van een ander", so the two boxes are drawing the same line
from two statutes, and the hand-off is exact.

Two problems carry the weight. First, the portable argument states Dutch law outside the box.
Move 2's opening promises the default "described here in the shape it takes anywhere" and the next
sentence says "in the Netherlands the answer turns on one fact", followed by plain-English
statements of arts. 7 and 2 lid 3 and of arts. 233/234 — and the customers one is wrong in
mechanism: terms that were not handed over do not "bind nobody"; under art. 232 the counterparty
is bound even if it did not know the content, and 233/234 make the clauses *vernietigbaar*. The box
says "voidable"; the paragraph outside it says "bind … only if". Second, the box glosses art. 2
lid 3's scope clause as applying to "everyone who is not an employee" — the freelancer, "the
agency" — when the statute confines that sentence to a transfer "door de maker of door een
natuurlijke persoon die het auteursrecht als erfgenaam of legataris van de maker heeft verkregen",
and lid 5 switches it off for makers under arts. 7 and 8. The writing requirement is general; the
"only as far as the writing goes" rule is not, and the §6i entry's one flagged risk was precisely
that the chapter would treat a default as wider than its text.

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "the cheapest in the book to read and the most
expensive to skip" precedes any reason (finding 7). (2) apparatus: "That is a statement about the
evidence, not a choice of style"; "the search for one that did turned up only law firms writing to
be found"; Reading Note's "the search recorded in the project's notes" (finding 5). (3) flinch:
none; second person holds through every Move and Monday. (4) counting and self-announcing: "Take
the four agreements in the order they get expensive"; "That is the whole principle, and everything
else in this chapter is the order in which it bites"; "The failure mode runs both ways" (finding 7).
(5) seams: "the only agreements in your business drafted by a professional" appears in Move 2 and
again in Move 3, and contradicts Move 1's lease (finding 8). (6) source accounting inside the
argument: the whole first paragraph of Move 2, and Monday 5's "That page is the chapter's case"
(finding 5). (7) not applicable.

The `.owed` title "What this chapter owes" is earned: ch. 12, the no-case precedent, uses the same
title for the same reason (two debts of different kinds), and ch. 15 does not because it owes only
the counter-case. Keep it. What is not earned is its length — 200 words where §6d asks for one
compact paragraph — and the first of its two debts is the same sentence as Move 2's opening and
Reading's Note (finding 5).

Word count: 2,525 rendered. The cuts in findings 1, 5, 7 and 8 remove roughly 150 words; findings
2 and 4's `pending` additions put about 60 back.

## 1. Move 2 states Dutch law outside the box, contradicts its own "anywhere", and gets the customers mechanism wrong

**Quote** — Move 2, makers: *"Whether you own it is a separate question, and in the Netherlands the
answer turns on one fact. Work made by an employee, in the course of employment, is by default the
employer's. Work made by anyone else — the freelancer, the agency, the friend who did it cheap —
stays the maker's unless a transfer was agreed in writing, and then covers only what the writing
expressly says. The invoice is not that writing. Paying it transfers nothing."* — Move 2,
customers: *"They bind the customer only if the customer had a real chance to read them before or
at the moment the deal was made — handed over then, not linked later — and even then a term that is
unreasonably onerous can be set aside. A link in the footer of the invoice, sent after the work was
ordered, is the commonest way for a business to have terms that bind nobody."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b quarantine) / §6f "the box quotes and navigates".

**What's wrong** — The paragraph before these promises the shape "anywhere" and the next sentence
says "in the Netherlands". Both paragraphs are then unquoted glosses of the box, placed where a
non-Dutch reader is told the shape holds. Three of the glosses are loose in ways the box is not.
"In the course of employment" is wider than art. 7's condition (finding 3). "Covers only what the
writing expressly says" is applied to "the agency", which lid 5 excludes (finding 2). And "bind
the customer only if … had a real chance to read them" / "terms that bind nobody" inverts the
mechanism: art. 232 — *"Een wederpartij is ook dan aan de algemene voorwaarden gebonden als bij het
sluiten van de overeenkomst de gebruiker begreep of moest begrijpen dat zij de inhoud daarvan niet
kende"* — the customer is bound; arts. 233–234 make the clause *vernietigbaar*, which is what the
box correctly says. A reader who has read the box and then Move 2 gets two different rules. The
recommendation is not to move these sentences into the box (the box already quotes what they
gloss) but to make Move 2 portable, as it says it is, and let the two signposts do the pointing.
This is a zero-fact fix and the one that most changes what the chapter claims.

**Rewrite** — makers: *"Whether you own it is a separate question, and the answer is not in the
invoice. Everywhere this book knows of, the default starts with the person who made the work, and
the routes by which it becomes the business's are few, specific, and written; which routes, and
what the writing has to say, are jurisdiction's — the box quotes the Dutch ones. What is portable is
the negative: paying for the work is not one of the routes. Paying the invoice transfers nothing."*
— customers: *"They protect you only to the extent the customer can be held to them, and every
jurisdiction has a version of the same test: did the customer have a chance to see them before the
deal, or only after? On Dutch ground the box quotes the test and what fails it. A link in the footer
of the invoice, sent after the work was ordered, is the commonest way for a business to have terms
it cannot hold anyone to."*

## 2. The box applies art. 2 lid 3's scope clause to "everyone who is not an employee"; the statute confines it to a transfer by the maker, and lid 5 switches it off for arts. 7 and 8

**Quote** — Box: *"For everyone who is not an employee, article 2 lid 3 governs: "De overeenkomst
op grond waarvan het auteursrecht geheel of gedeeltelijk wordt overgedragen of waarin een
exclusieve licentie wordt verleend, wordt schriftelijk aangegaan." In writing — and only as far as
the writing goes: the transfer "omvat alleen die bevoegdheden die uitdrukkelijk in de overeenkomst
staan vermeld of die uit de aard en de strekking ervan noodzakelijkerwijs voortvloeien.""*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b: nothing overstated) / the §6i risk.

**What's wrong** — The first sentence of lid 3 (writing) is general and the gloss is right. The
second sentence is not general. In the consulted text it reads: *"De overdracht of de verlening van
een exclusieve licentie door de maker of door een natuurlijke persoon die het auteursrecht als
erfgenaam of legataris van de maker heeft verkregen, omvat alleen die bevoegdheden die
uitdrukkelijk in de overeenkomst staan vermeld…"* — the chapter's quotation starts after "door de
maker of door een natuurlijke persoon…", which is the clause that limits it. And lid 5: *"Het derde
lid, tweede volzin, en het vierde lid zijn niet van toepassing op een maker als bedoeld in artikel
7 en 8."* So the "only as far as the writing goes" rule protects a natural-person maker — the
freelancer, the friend — and does not apply where the transferor is an employer-maker (art. 7) or
a legal person deemed maker (art. 8), which is what "the agency" in Move 2 usually is. As written,
the box tells a reader that a transfer from an agency-bv is confined to what the writing expressly
says; the statute says the opposite for that transferor. Also worth noting, not for the box: the
same lid's third sentence requires *"een daartoe bestemde akte"* for delivery, which is stronger
than "in writing" and is another reason the invoice is not the instrument.

**Rewrite** — `pending fact-check` (lid 3 second sentence in full, and lid 5, need claim rows;
same registered source): *"For everyone who is not an employee, article 2 lid 3 governs: "De
overeenkomst op grond waarvan het auteursrecht geheel of gedeeltelijk wordt overgedragen of waarin
een exclusieve licentie wordt verleend, wordt schriftelijk aangegaan." In writing. And when the
person transferring is the one who made it — the freelancer, the friend — the transfer reaches only
as far as the writing goes: "De overdracht of de verlening van een exclusieve licentie door de
maker … omvat alleen die bevoegdheden die uitdrukkelijk in de overeenkomst staan vermeld of die uit
de aard en de strekking ervan noodzakelijkerwijs voortvloeien." Lid 5 takes that second sentence
away where the transferor is itself a maker under articles 7 or 8 — an agency transferring its
employees' work is the jurist's question, not this box's."* Zero-fact fallback: keep the quotation
as is and change the gloss to *"In writing — and, for a transfer by the person who made it, only
as far as the writing goes:"*, and in Move 2 (finding 1's rewrite already does this) drop "the
agency" from the list of makers to whom the scope rule is said to apply.

## 3. "Work made in employment" is wider than art. 7's condition, and the article's own exception is not glossed

**Quote** — Box: *"Article 7 moves that default to the employer for work made in employment:"* —
Move 2: *"Work made by an employee, in the course of employment, is by default the employer's."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b) / faithful gloss.

**What's wrong** — Art. 7 as quoted: *"Indien de arbeid, in dienst van een ander verricht, bestaat
in het vervaardigen van bepaalde werken…"* — the condition is that the work the employee is
employed to do *consists in* making such works, not that a work was made while employed. A
bookkeeper who designs the logo in an afternoon is not obviously inside it; a developer employed
to write code is. The gloss "in the course of employment" is a different jurisdiction's phrase and
a wider one. And the article's "tenzij tusschen partijen anders is overeengekomen" — the default
yields to agreement — is quoted but not glossed, so a reader does not learn that the employment
contract can move it back. Both fixes are inside the quotation already on the page.

**Rewrite** — Box: *"Article 7 moves that default to the employer where making such works is what
the employee is employed to do, unless the contract says otherwise:"* — and after the quotation, in
place of the current "In dienst van een ander — in another's employ — is the condition": *"Two
conditions, then: in another's employ, and employed to make this kind of thing; and a contract can
undo the default. In dienst van een ander is the line, and a contractor is not in your employ;
chapter nineteen's box is where that line is drawn."* Move 2's version is replaced by finding 1.

## 4. The box's "Whether your terms bind" does not say whom the section reaches, and the chapter's "bind nobody" outside it is the wrong verb

**Quote** — Box: *"Whether your terms bind. Book 6 of the Civil Code, article 233: …"* — Box: *"—
handed over before or at the conclusion of the contract — with the further routes the article
lists after that clause, and with the consequence that if they were not handed over then, the
terms are also voidable where the user does not send them "op verzoek van de wederpartij onverwijld
op zijn kosten"."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b: scope) / ch. 20 finding 2's standard.

**What's wrong** — The on-request gloss is right: art. 234 lid 1 second sentence reads *"Indien de
voorwaarden niet voor of bij het sluiten van de overeenkomst aan de wederpartij ter hand zijn
gesteld, zijn de bedingen tevens vernietigbaar indien de gebruiker de voorwaarden niet op verzoek
van de wederpartij onverwijld op zijn kosten aan haar toezendt"* — "also voidable" is exactly
*tevens vernietigbaar*. "The further routes" is a fair pointer to the 230c route, the deposit-and-
inspection route "indien dit redelijkerwijs niet mogelijk is", and lid 2's electronic route, and
Monday 3's "attach them to whatever the customer sees before they say yes … keep the dated email" is
consistent with lid 2. What the box does not say is who can invoke any of this. Art. 247 lid 2:
*"Op overeenkomsten tussen partijen die handelen in de uitoefening van een beroep of bedrijf en die
niet beide in Nederland gevestigd zijn, is deze afdeling niet van toepassing"* — for a business
customer abroad, none of 233/234 applies. Art. 235 lid 1: the grounds cannot be invoked by a
large company (a rechtspersoon that has published accounts, or fifty or more employees); lid 3:
nor by *"een partij die meermalen dezelfde of nagenoeg dezelfde algemene voorwaarden in haar
overeenkomsten gebruikt"*. The omissions cut in the reader's favour (they make the reader more
careful, not less), so this is a scope note rather than a routing error — but the box's heading
promises "whether your terms bind" and the section it quotes answers that for consumers and Dutch
counterparties only, and the reader whose customers are businesses abroad is given a test that
does not apply to them. One sentence fixes it.

**Rewrite** — `pending fact-check` (247 lid 2 and 235 lid 1 need claim rows; same registered
source): after the on-request sentence add *"Whom this section reaches is its own question — art.
247 makes it inapplicable between businesses "die niet beide in Nederland gevestigd zijn", and art.
235 takes the grounds away from large counterparties — and that is one of the things to put in front
of the jurist with your customer list."* Zero-fact fallback: in the closing paragraph, change
*"whether a limit in your terms would hold"* to *"whether a limit in your terms would hold, and
which of your customers this section even reaches"*.

## 5. The absence of a case is said four times, and twice with the apparatus showing

**Quote** — Move 2, first paragraph: *"There is no company in this chapter. That is a statement
about the evidence, not a choice of style: a small self-funded business that lost money to a clause
it never wrote does not publish the fact, and the search for one that did turned up only law firms
writing to be found. What stands in the case's place is the thing that actually happens when nothing
is written — the default — shown for one jurisdiction in the box below and described here in the
shape it takes anywhere."* — Monday 5: *"That page is the chapter's case, and it is yours."* —
`.owed`, first half (about 95 words, "Two things, and the first is the case itself … dressing a

search recorded in the project's notes found no small self-funded business with a primary document
showing a cost that followed from an unwritten or undelivered agreement."*

**Lens** — §6d (one principal caveat, said once) / apparatus (modes 2, 6).

**What's wrong** — Ch. 15, the model, opens Move 2 on the regime ("The Netherlands refuses to let
this stay invisible") and never apologises for the missing company; ch. 12 says it once, in the
`.owed`. Ch. 24 says it in Move 2 with a sentence about evidence classes, again in Monday 5 as a
flourish, again at length in `.owed`, and again in Reading with a pointer to "the project's notes"
— a file the reader does not have. "That is a statement about the evidence, not a choice of style"
and "writing to be found" are the case-hunt entry speaking. The right distribution per §6d: Move 2
says it in one sentence and moves to the default; `.owed` carries the specification of what the
case would be (that is what `.owed` is for) in one compact paragraph together with the
counter-case; Reading keeps the sourcing fact in one clause without the notes pointer; Monday 5
loses the flourish.

**Rewrite** — Move 2 opening: *"There is no company in this chapter, because the business that
lost money to a clause it never wrote does not publish the fact. What stands in the case's place is
what happens when nothing is written — the default — quoted for one jurisdiction in the box below,
and described here in the shape it takes anywhere."* — Monday 5, last sentence: *"Write the answer
down and date it."* — `.owed`, whole block: *"Two things. The case: a small self-funded business
with a dated agreement — a client contract, a standard-terms page, a contractor's quote, a policy —
and a cost that followed from what it did or did not say; none was found with a primary document
rather than a law firm's retelling. And the counter-case: a business that wrote all four agreements
down, delivered its terms in time, bought the hour — and was worse for it, because the written
agreement was what the customer walked away from, or doing it right cost more than every loss it
prevented. Until either arrives, the argument rests on what the statute does by default, which is
at least a thing the reader can check."* (Cuts about 80 words.) — Reading Note: *"No company case:
none was found with a primary document showing a cost that followed from an unwritten or
undelivered agreement. The four-agreements map and the ordering in Move 3 are this book's; the
thresholds for buying a professional's hour are rules of thumb, not law. Nothing in this chapter on
liability or insurance is sourced to a statute, and the box says so."*

## 6. "The legal form you chose in chapter twelve" points at a chapter that does not choose one, and the sentence quietly states law

**Quote** — Move 2, the world: *"Somebody pays. Whether it is you, your business, or an insurer
depends on the legal form you chose in chapter twelve, on what your terms say and whether they
bind, and on a policy you may or may not hold."*

**Lens** — Continuity / §5b (law stated outside the box).

**What's wrong** — Ch. 12 is about reading the three sets of accounts; its only legal-form content
is that *filing* turns on form ("De rechtsvorm van uw bedrijf bepaalt of u een jaarrekening moet
deponeren"). Nobody chooses a form there, and ch. 15 — which also points back — does it correctly
("another legal-form fork of the kind chapter twelve found in the filing rules"). More important,
"whether it is you [or] your business … depends on the legal form" is a statement about limited
liability, unsourced, outside the box, on the one subject the box explicitly declines to quote. The
chapter is entitled to say that form is one of the things the answer turns on; it should not
imply what it turns into. Frame it as the question to carry, not the rule.

**Rewrite** — *"Somebody pays. Who — you, the business, an insurer — is a question with three parts:
your legal form, which is the fork chapter twelve met in the filing rules and the notaris's subject
rather than this book's; what your terms say and whether they can be held to; and a policy you may
or may not hold."*

## 7. Verdict-first opener, counting opener, and the self-announcing climax

**Quote** — Move 1: *"Nothing has gone wrong. That is the situation, and it is why this chapter is
the cheapest in the book to read and the most expensive to skip."* — Move 2: *"Take the four
agreements in the order they get expensive."* — Move 3: *"That is the whole principle, and
everything else in this chapter is the order in which it bites."* — Move 3: *"The failure mode runs
both ways."*

**Lens** — Modes 1 and 4.

**What's wrong** — "Cheapest to read and most expensive to skip" is a blurb for the chapter placed
inside it, before the reader has a reason to believe either half. "Take the four agreements in the
order they get expensive" is the narrator announcing the structure — and the order is then argued
properly in Move 3's last paragraph, which is where it belongs. "That is the whole principle, and
everything else… is the order in which it bites" is the chapter grading itself; the principle is
already on the page in the two sentences before. "The failure mode runs both ways" announces a
contrast that the next two sentences make on their own ("One is… The other is…").

**Rewrite** — Move 1: *"Nothing has gone wrong. That is the situation, and it is the only time this
chapter is cheap."* — Move 2: cut the sentence and open the paragraph on *"The people who make
things."* — Move 3: cut "That is the whole principle, and everything else in this chapter is the
order in which it bites." and run straight to *"Writing the agreement down does not make the
relationship a contract."* — Move 3: cut "The failure mode runs both ways." and open on *"One
failure is the handshake business…"*.

## 8. "The only agreements drafted by a professional" is said twice and contradicts the lease; "set aside as onerous" is the box's test used loosely outside it

**Quote** — Move 2, suppliers: *"It can tell you that they are the only agreements in your business
drafted by a professional, and that the professional was not yours."* — Move 3: *"And the
agreements you signed by clicking are the only ones in the business that were drafted by a lawyer,
which is a reason to read them, not a reason to feel protected by them."* — Move 3: *"half of which
would be set aside as onerous the moment they were tested"*.

**Lens** — Seams (mode 5) / §5b leak / the practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong** — The chapter's own first sentence has the reader signing "a lease, a bank
mandate", both of which were drafted by someone's lawyer, so "the only agreements … drafted by a
professional" is false on the chapter's own terms; and the sentence is made twice, once per Move.
The observation that survives is the good one — those terms were drafted for the other side. "Set
aside as onerous" is art. 233 onder a's language ("onredelijk bezwarend") applied, as a prediction
with a fraction attached ("half"), to a template of any jurisdiction; it is the box's test used as
a universal outside it. The paragraph's point — that borrowed big-company terms cost goodwill and
would not survive testing — does not need the fraction or the statutory verb.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"It can tell you that every one of them was drafted by a professional, and
that the professional was not yours."* — Move 3: *"And the agreements you signed by clicking were
drafted by a lawyer for the other side, which is a reason to read them, not a reason to feel
protected by them."* — Move 3: *"most of which would not survive being tested and all of which cost
a customer's goodwill to present"*.

## 9. Monday 2's Dutch clause and Monday 4's "legal form" are the right amount of law; one word in Monday 2 overreaches

**Quote** — Monday 2: *"If there is no sentence, ask the person who made it for one this week,
while they still answer your emails — on Dutch ground, in writing, and naming what is transferred."*

**Lens** — §5b / Monday as failable action.

**What's wrong** — This is the acceptable form: signposted "on Dutch ground", and it restates only
what the box quotes (schriftelijk; uitdrukkelijk vermeld). It stays. One caution follows from
finding 2: "naming what is transferred" is the right instruction for a freelancer's transfer, and
harmless for an agency's; keep it. What overreaches is "ask the person who made it for one" — a
sentence in an email is not the *akte* lid 3's third sentence requires for delivery, and the box
does not quote that sentence. Since the chapter cannot state it without a claim row, the Monday
should not imply that an emailed sentence closes the question.

**Rewrite** — *"If there is no sentence, ask the person who made it for one this week, while they
still answer your emails — on Dutch ground, in writing, naming what is transferred, and on the
lawyer's paper if the work is one the business could not replace."* (The last clause is the
chapter's own threshold from Move 3, not a legal claim.)

## Overall note

The chapter's map is right and is the book's own; nothing in it smuggles a legal claim about a
jurisdiction except where Move 2 stops being portable and starts glossing the box in English — and
that is the one structural fix (finding 1). Inside the box every quotation is exact, and the two
glosses that overreach are both in the direction the §6i entry warned about: a default read wider
than its text. Finding 2 is the important one — the scope clause of art. 2 lid 3 belongs to
transfers by the maker, and lid 5 says so — because it is the sentence a reader would rely on with
an agency-built site. Finding 3 tightens art. 7 to what it says. Finding 4 is a scope note the box
can carry in one sentence. The no-case handling wants to be ch. 15's: said once, at the top of
Move 2, without the apparatus, and the `.owed` block keeps its ch. 12 title but not its length
(finding 5). The four `pending fact-check` clauses (lid 3 second sentence and lid 5; 247 lid 2 and
235 lid 1; the BW6 date) are all from the two sources already registered, and each has a zero-fact
fallback in the finding.
