# Craft review — ch. 21, Meetings That Aren't Theater

Reviewed 2026-08-28, first review of a first draft, against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §6d and the §6i
entry for this chapter; every Turnbull quotation checked against `groove-meetings-2016.raw`, and
ch. 20 and ch. 17 read for continuity. All fifteen quotations are exact. No rewrite below adds a
fact; the cuts remove words and one sequence the post does not contain. One optional addition is
marked `pending fact-check`.

## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, with a good ear — who has rearranged the case to fit the argument.

Move 1 is right about the reader: the tap on the shoulder as "a meeting with no start time, no end,
no agenda, and always the same attendee" is the chapter's best line and the whole argument in one
sentence, and "an interruption with a calendar invitation" is its equal in Move 3. The rule is
statable in one sentence — *the theatre is the unstructured meeting; an owner, a clock on every
segment and the same shape every time is what makes a fixed one cheaper than the interruptions* —
and the Friday call is read exactly right: the meeting everyone liked died because it was optional
in practice. The boundary (coordination, not decisions) is the kind §3 asks for, the `.owed` block
is honest about what two months and a feeling cannot show, and Monday 5 turns the chapter into a
test the reader can fail. The chapter does not re-introduce Groove as new, which the §6i ruling
asked for.

Two problems carry the weight. First, the story is not told in the post's order. The chapter has
the daily standup start, fail "at first", and then be rescued by a video; the post has the video
shown at the retreat and the standup put in place *after* it. On that invented sequence the chapter
then builds its cleanest sentence — "the same nine people, in the same meeting, went from
forty-five minutes to ten" — which the post does not say: it says Groove's "9-person 45-minute
meetings" looked amateur next to the video, and gives a ten-minute skeleton for Tuesday–Thursday.
Which meeting ran forty-five minutes, and what happened to it, is not on the page. Second, the
"two people at a counter … three minutes at opening" figure appears twice in Move 3 as if it were
something the case established, and the boundary paragraph then repeats it as "the claim" the
chapter makes — it is the book's own proposal and has to be labelled as one.

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "which died of its own design" before the Friday
quotation; "the one he did not want" before "scared me a bit" (finding 8, mild). (2) apparatus: "He
opens with a disclaimer this chapter keeps"; "The reason is the one from Move 1" — the chapter
naming its own moves; "— Turnbull's account of what it showed, not this book's —" (findings 6, 8).
(3) flinch: none; second person holds. (4) counting and self-announcing: "built right means three
things", "By his own account, four rhythms.", "What structure meant, concretely, was three
things.", "Three parts, and each one answers a way the meeting goes wrong." (finding 5).
(5) summariser's seams: "nine-person, fully remote", "deliberately", "where it could not be skipped
for work", "in order", "with what each cost" (finding 2). (6) source accounting in the argument:
the Move 2 parenthesis duplicates Reading; but §6i requires the two-layer attribution *in the
prose*, so the fix is to keep it in Move 2 as narrative and cut the half that is about this book
(finding 6). (7) not applicable.

Word count: 2,077 on chapter content against a 2,000 floor and 3,000 ceiling. The rewrites below
are roughly neutral; finding 9's continuity clause and finding 4's reframing add a sentence each.

Spelling: the book is British in its prose — `colour`, `behaviour`, `recognise`, `programme`,
`centre` across ch. 01–18, and ch. 04 already has "accountability theatre". The eight "theatre"s in
this chapter's prose are consistent with that. The display title "Meetings That Aren't Theater" is
American, and it is repeated in ch. 20's "Next chapter" link, the running head and the filename.
The prose should not change. The title is the author's call, and if it changes it must change in
ch. 20's nav, the index and CONTEXT §6i at the same time; the filename can stay as it is either
way. Flagged, not ranked.

## 1. The standup is made to fail first, and the video is moved to after it

**Quote** — Move 2: *"Fourth, the one he did not want. Daily standups, "which we had previously only
done via Slack posts", would now be live, ten in the morning, five days a week — a change that, in
his words, "scared me a bit", because a meeting every day looked like the worst interruption of
all. "But Kris, our business coach, pushed us to try it." And it did not work either, at first,
for a reason that only became visible when he saw someone else do it. / At a coaching retreat, the
coach showed him a video …"*

**Lens** — Story craft (told forwards, inferences inside the source).

**What's wrong** — The post's order is: "After our coaching retreat last year, we put into place a
new meeting rhythm" (the live standup); "At our retreat, Kris showed me a video". The video came at
the retreat, and the standup was adopted after it, on the coach's push — the post does not say the
live standup ran unstructured and failed before the video was seen. "And it did not work either, at
first" is not in the post, and "a reason that only became visible when he saw someone else do it"
puts the retreat after the standup. The one thing the post does say about a before-and-after is the
parenthesis "after figuring out the biggest key that was missing", which is not dated. The chapter
also drops the post's hedges — "nearly 20", "less than 7" — in "Twenty people in seven minutes".
Told in the post's order the story is simpler and still turns on the same hinge: the retreat gave
him both the standup and the reason it would not be forty-five minutes.

**Rewrite** — *"Fourth, the one he did not want. It came out of a coaching retreat. Daily standups,
"which we had previously only done via Slack posts", would now be live, at ten in the morning,
Monday to Friday — a change that, in his words, "scared me a bit", because a meeting every day
looked like the worst interruption of all. "But Kris, our business coach, pushed us to try it."
What made him willing was something the coach showed him at the same retreat: a video of another
company's daily standup, in which, as Turnbull describes it, "nearly 20 people participated and
shared updates, and the meeting still took less than 7 minutes. It made our 9-person 45-minute
meetings look amateur in comparison." Nearly twenty people in under seven minutes, against nine in
forty-five. The difference was not fewer people or fewer meetings. "That missing piece is
structure.""*

## 2. "The same nine people, in the same meeting, went from forty-five minutes to ten"

**Quote** — Move 2, last paragraph: *"What the post establishes is narrower and still the point: the
same nine people, in the same meeting, went from forty-five minutes to ten by adding structure, not
by removing the meeting."* — Move 3: *"A scheduled meeting with no structure is the same theatre
with a start time — the forty-five-minute version"* — Move 3: *"a standing meeting that starts
making decisions will grow back to forty-five minutes within a month."*

**Lens** — Story craft (figures load-bearing).

**What's wrong** — This is the sentence the chapter rests on and the post does not contain it. "Our
9-person 45-minute meetings" is a description of what Groove's meetings had been like — the Monday
call is separately called "an hour-long meeting" — not a measurement of the daily standup before
structure. The post never says which meeting took forty-five minutes or that any particular meeting
went to ten; it gives a ten-minute Tuesday–Thursday skeleton and a thirty-minute Monday. "The same
nine people" also carries "nine-person team", which the post does not say (see finding 3). What the
post does establish is a comparison and a design: their meetings ran forty-five minutes with nine
people; a structured one elsewhere ran under seven with nearly twenty; the skeleton they built has
ten minutes on the clock. That is enough for the argument and it is what Move 3's ratio sentence
already uses. "Grow back to forty-five minutes within a month" in the boundary paragraph is a
prediction with no source at all; it should lose the number.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"What the post establishes is narrower and still the point: meetings that had
run forty-five minutes with nine people were replaced by one with ten minutes on the clock, and the
thing that changed was the structure, not the number of meetings."* Move 3, first section: keep
"the forty-five-minute version" (it now refers to Groove's own description). Boundary: *"and a
standing meeting that starts making decisions will grow back toward the length it had before."*

## 3. Words the post does not contain — a list

**Quote** — *"Groove's nine-person, fully remote team"* — *"four rhythms … in order, with what each
cost"* — *"twenty are deliberately for weekends and personal things"* — *"It was given a fixed
twenty minutes inside a work meeting, where it could not be skipped for work."* — *"He opens with
a disclaimer this chapter keeps"* — *"A meeting with no work in it is the first one skipped when
there is work."*

**Lens** — Summariser's seams (mode 5).

**What's wrong** — Taken one at a time. *Nine-person team*: the post says "our 9-person 45-minute
meetings"; the team size is not stated, and Groove had other people who may not have been in the
meeting. *Fully remote*: the post says "our remote team" and "our distributed team"; "fully" is
added. *In order, with what each cost*: the four are numbered, not dated — "Friday Fun Day" began
"a few months ago" and the standup "after our coaching retreat last year", so the Friday call may
postdate the standup; the post gives pros and cons, not costs. *Deliberately*: the post says "This
is culture time", nothing about intent. *Where it could not be skipped for work*: the chapter's
inference, and a good one, but stated as the post's design reason; the post says only that the
culture time is inside the Monday meeting. *He opens with a disclaimer*: he opens with the Fried
quotation — Reading says so, and the two contradict each other on the page. *The first one skipped
when there is work*: this is the chapter's gloss and reads as Turnbull's; it is fine if it is
plainly the book's sentence, which one word does. Three things the prompt listed as suspect turn
out to be in the post: "poker tournament … voice call" ("a team tournament on Replay Poker (in
parallel with a voice call on Google Hangouts)"), "ten in the morning, five days a week" ("10AM
EST, Monday through Friday"), and the culture-time figure (20 minutes).

**Rewrite** — *"what Groove's remote team had tried in the way of meeting rhythms, with what he
liked and did not like about each"* — *"Third, a Friday call for culture only"* (cut "in order"
above; the numbering "First … Fourth" is the post's) — *"A few paragraphs in he gives a disclaimer
this chapter takes at its word"* — *"of which twenty are for weekends and personal things"* —
*"It was given a fixed twenty minutes inside a work meeting. That is the chapter's reading of why
it survived: put there, it could not be skipped for work."* — *"A meeting with no work in it, in
other words, is the first one skipped when there is work."*

## 4. "Two people at a counter … three minutes" is a proposal presented as a finding, twice

**Quote** — Move 3: *"For two people at a counter the whole thing is three minutes at opening,
standing up, and the numbers segment is chapter twelve's weekly cash figure said out loud — which
is where chapter twenty-two starts."* — Move 3, last paragraph: *"and so is this chapter beyond the
claim that a fixed three minutes at opening is cheaper than the day of being asked."*

**Lens** — The principle's boundaries (§3 Move 3) / Monday as test.

**What's wrong** — The chapter has no evidence about two people at a counter; the source is nine
people on a call. The first sentence states the three-minute version as if it followed from the
case, and the boundary paragraph then calls it "the claim" the chapter makes, which it cannot make
from this post. The honest form is the one the `.owed` block already uses — "Monday's fifth item is
the test this chapter offers in place of the case it does not have" — so the fix is to say in Move
3 that the counter version is the book's translation and Monday 5 is what tests it. That also
answers the §6i rule about minute counts: the three minutes are derived from the mechanism (one
minute a person plus the number), not copied from Groove.

**Rewrite** — First: *"For two people at a counter, this book's translation — and it is a
translation, not something the post shows — is three minutes at opening, standing up: a minute
each, and the numbers segment is chapter twelve's weekly cash figure said out loud, which is where
chapter twenty-two starts. Monday's fifth item is what tests it."* Last paragraph: *"but the post is
silent on a team in one room, and so is this chapter beyond a proposal: that a fixed three minutes
at opening costs less than a day of being asked, which the count in Monday's fifth item will
confirm or not."*

## 5. Four counting openers and one self-announcing climax

**Quote** — Lede: *"It is the opposite of it, if it is built right, and built right means three
things."* — Move 2: *"By his own account, four rhythms."* — Move 2: *"What structure meant,
concretely, was three things."* — Move 3: *"Three parts, and each one answers a way the meeting
goes wrong."*

**Lens** — Rhythm (mode 4).

**What's wrong** — Each announces the count and then delivers the list; the reader is told "three"
three times before Move 3 has named what the three are. The lists survive without the headcount:
Move 2's "An owner … A clock on every part … And the same skeleton every day" is already a list a
reader can count, and Move 3's bold run-ins do the same. The lede's version is the worst because it
promises a structure the chapter will announce twice more.

**Rewrite** — Lede: *"It is the opposite of it, if it is built right."* Move 2: *"He had tried four
by then."* Move 2: *"Structure, in the post, is concrete."* Move 3: *"Each part answers a way the
meeting goes wrong."*

## 6. The two-layer attribution is in the right place, but half of it is about this book

**Quote** — Move 2: *"At a coaching retreat, the coach showed him a video — Turnbull's account of
what it showed, not this book's — of another company's daily standup"* — Reading: *"The
nearly-twenty-people, under-seven-minutes figure is Turnbull's description of a video his coach
showed him of another company; it is not sourced to that company and this book has not verified
it."*

**Lens** — §6d / source accounting in the argument (mode 6).

**What's wrong** — §6i says the prose must attribute the figures two layers deep, so Move 2 is the
right place and the parenthesis is not a duplicate of Reading in principle. It is one in form: "not
this book's" is the book talking about its verification status inside the story, which is what
Reading is for. The attribution the story needs is "a video his coach showed him" plus "as Turnbull
describes it" — both layers, no apparatus. Finding 1's rewrite already contains this form; Reading
keeps the verification sentence unchanged. One further note: the post names the company in the
video (1-800-Got-Junk). The chapter says "another company", which is a choice — it keeps a
recognisable name out of a chapter whose case budget is already spent. Fine as is; if the author
wants the name, it is on the page of the registered source and needs no new row.

**Rewrite** — As in finding 1: *"something the coach showed him at the same retreat: a video of
another company's daily standup, in which, as Turnbull describes it, …"*

## 7. Monday 3 fixes Groove's seconds, and Monday 1 counts from memory

**Quote** — Monday 3: *"Per person, sixty seconds: what got done, what is next, what is blocked.
Then thirty seconds: this week's cash figure from chapter twelve, read aloud by you."* — Move 3:
*"sixty seconds a person for done, next, blocked; thirty seconds for the numbers"* — Monday 1:
*"Count last week's meetings, including the ones nobody called meetings. Every got a minute?, every
question at your desk, with a guess at minutes and the people involved. Multiply out."*

**Lens** — Monday (concrete enough to fail at) / §6i (minute counts not a template).

**What's wrong** — Move 3 says "Groove's minute counts are Groove's, and the post says so", and
Monday 3 then prescribes Groove's sixty and thirty seconds without saying where they came from. The
reader should get a number — §4 forbids "consider" — but the number should be labelled as a
starting figure the reader owns. Monday 1 asks for a retrospective guess; a guessed baseline is the
one thing Monday 5 cannot be failed against, because the second count will be a tally and the first
was not. The count should run forward for a week. "Multiply out" should say what by what.

**Rewrite** — Monday 1: *"For the next five working days, keep a tally. Every got a minute?, every
question at your desk: minutes, and how many people were in it. At the end of the week multiply
minutes by people and add it up. That is the cost of the rhythm you have now, and it is the number
item 5 is measured against."* Monday 3: *"Per person, a minute — Groove's figure, and as good a
starting number as any: what got done, what is next, what is blocked. Then half a minute: this
week's cash figure from chapter twelve, read aloud by you."* Monday 5: *"After four weeks of the
slot, tally again for five days."*

## 8. The chapter refers to its own moves, and two verdicts precede their quotations

**Quote** — Move 2: *"The reason is the one from Move 1, in a remote team's clothes."* — Move 2:
*"Third, a Friday call for culture only, a poker tournament over a voice call, which died of its
own design: "perhaps the biggest mistake …""* — Move 2: *"Fourth, the one he did not want."*

**Lens** — Apparatus (mode 2) / verdict-first (mode 1).

**What's wrong** — "Move 1" is a heading label, not something a reader carries as a name; no other
chapter refers to its moves in prose. The sentence wants the thing itself — the tap on the shoulder
— which the quotation that follows supplies. "Died of its own design" is the chapter's verdict on
the Friday call placed before the quotation that is the evidence for it, and it is repeated in Move
3 as the section's argument, where it belongs. "The one he did not want" is mild and can stay if
the quotation follows immediately, which finding 1's rewrite arranges.

**Rewrite** — *"The reason is the tap on the shoulder, in a remote team's clothes."* — *"Third, a
Friday call for culture only, a poker tournament over a voice call. It lasted a few months: "perhaps
the biggest mistake …""*

## 9. The throughline from ch. 20 is a pointer, not a feeling

**Quote** — Move 2 opening: *"A year after the post chapter twenty used, Alex Turnbull wrote up what
Groove's nine-person, fully remote team had tried in the way of meeting rhythms"* — Move 3: *"The
decisions have their own dates — chapter twenty put one in the calendar — and the daily meeting's
job is to notice that a decision is needed and send it there."*

**Lens** — Continuity (§6i ruling: no re-introduction; make the throughline felt).

**What's wrong** — The chapter obeys the first half of the ruling and not the second. "A year after
the post chapter twenty used" is a date by cross-reference; it tells the reader when, not why the
same man is here again. Ch. 20's Move 2 line is "What he built in response is a calendar, not a
temperament", and this chapter's case is the same move made a second time — a clock instead of his
nerve, now for the meeting rather than the firing — and Move 3's "an owner who is not you" is the
same idea as ch. 20's date being set "so that it is not you who decides". One clause at the opening
and none elsewhere makes the repeat read as continuity. The Move 3 pointer is fine. Also: "the
shape chapter seventeen named" — ch. 17 names the bottleneck ("what cannot move without" you); "the
place where the answers are kept" and "you as the room" are this chapter's own images, which is
fine, but the pointer should claim only what ch. 17 said.

**Rewrite** — Move 2 opening: *"Chapter twenty's Turnbull built a calendar so that a firing would
not depend on his nerve. A year after that post, in February 2016, he wrote up the same kind of
repair for the other thing that had been running on mood — what Groove's remote team had tried in
the way of meeting rhythms, with what he liked and did not like about each."* Move 1: *"because you
are the place where the answers are kept — chapter seventeen's bottleneck, seen from the inside."*

## 10. Move 1 tells the reader what they have sat through, and the lede's provocation is not qualified

**Quote** — Lede: *"sounds like the corporate theatre you left"* — Move 1: *"You have sat in those.
They ran an hour, they were for the benefit of whoever ran them, and they produced a feeling of
management and nothing else."* — Decision box: *"which is also a meeting rhythm, the most expensive
one there is"* — Move 3: *"got a minute?, is the most theatrical of all"*.

**Lens** — The reader in the chair (§3: open on the reader's actual position) / provocations are
not laws (§4).

**What's wrong** — §2's reader has a hardware shop or a two-person studio; nothing in the book says
they came from an office, and "the corporate theatre you left" and "You have sat in those" assign
them a past. The paragraph's real content — what a bad fixed meeting is — does not need the
biography; it needs the description, which is already there. "Most theatrical of all" is qualified
inside the chapter by the boundary paragraph and `.owed` ("for a business of two, the fixed rhythm
may cost more than the interruptions it replaces"), so it can stand. "The most expensive one there
is" in the decision box is not qualified anywhere and is contradicted by that same `.owed`
sentence. It should say what the chapter can defend: the one nobody has costed.

**Rewrite** — Lede: *"sounds like theatre — the fixed hour that exists because it is in the
calendar."* Move 1: *"You know the kind, or have heard about it: an hour long, for the benefit of
whoever ran it, producing a feeling of management and nothing else."* Decision box: *"which is also
a meeting rhythm — the one nobody has costed, and the one in which you are the agenda."*

## Overall note

The case is the right one and the reading of it is right; what needs doing is putting the post's
events back in the post's order and taking the chapter's hands off the numbers. Finding 1 restores
the sequence (retreat, video, standup) and finding 2 replaces the one sentence the argument cannot
have — "the same nine people, in the same meeting, went from forty-five minutes to ten" — with what
the post does say, which carries the ratio just as well. Finding 3's list removes every word the
post lacks: "nine-person", "fully", "in order", "with what each cost", "deliberately", "where it
could not be skipped", "He opens with". Findings 4 and 7 label the three-minute counter version and
the sixty-second segment as the book's own numbers, testable by Monday 5, which is what §6i's first
rule asks and what the `.owed` block already says. Finding 9's opening clause is the one addition
worth making, because it turns the second Groove chapter in a row from a coincidence into the same
builder making the same repair twice. Nothing here is `pending fact-check` except the optional
naming of 1-800-Got-Junk in finding 6, which is on the page of the registered source and would
need only a prose decision, not a new row.
