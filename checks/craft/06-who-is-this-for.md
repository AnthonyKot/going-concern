# Craft review — Chapter 06, "Who Is This For"

Reviewed 2026-08-26. Scope: prose craft only; facts and figures assumed correct.
Constraint honoured throughout: the narrator stays invisible; all rewrites keep the
constitution's voice rules (second person for the reader, third for the case, no hedging
in Monday, numbers stay, counter-case discipline stays).

The short version: Move 1 is the best opening in the book so far — "a coincidence you are
trying to have twice" and "a product shaped like its own support queue" are a person who
has watched this happen. The case is told in the right order, the honesty sections are
real, and the limit block earns its border. What damages the chapter is a single habit
repeated in its most load-bearing places: the chapter keeps narrating its own conscience —
its drafts, its obligations, its arithmetic — instead of simply having one. And the Monday
list, in one item, restores the exact error the chapter's central paragraph exists to
correct.

---

## Findings (ranked by impact)

### 1. Monday item 4 runs the arrow the chapter just reversed

**Quote:** "Trace the sentence into the accounts. Does your positioning force a pricing
model, a support cost, a market you can no longer sell to? If the answer is no in every
direction, it is decoration."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** "Force" is the verb of the draft's chronology error. Move 3 spends its
pivotal paragraph establishing that the statement did not create the choices — "The
positioning statement did not create these choices … What the sentence did was gather
four scattered decisions into one line and make them binding" — and then hands the reader
a Monday test written in the pre-correction direction. The chapter's own settled test is
the delete test ("A statement you could delete without changing a single downstream
choice is a slogan"); the Monday list should run that test, not the one the chapter
retired.

**Rewrite:** "**Trace the sentence into the accounts.** Delete it for a day: does any
price, any support promise, any refused market come loose? If nothing anywhere comes
loose, it is decoration." Zero new facts; same length.

### 2. The chapter narrates its own editorial conscience, four times, in load-bearing places

**Quote:** "Now the chronology, because it is easy to get backwards and this chapter got
it backwards in draft." / "Less than the curve suggests, and the chapter is obliged to
say so before the reader gets there first." / "Their own verdict is more generous than
that line, and the chapter has to carry both halves rather than quote the one that
suits:" / "Their reading of why the incumbent costs nothing, and it is theirs rather
than this book's:"

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** Drafting history and editorial obligation surfacing in prose — the
known apparatus failure, but here it clusters on the chapter's three best moments: the
corrected chronology, the "how much was positioning" concession, and the limit block's
both-halves quote. Each instance announces the honesty the sentence is about to perform,
which converts character into compliance. A person who knows the trade states the
correction; only the machine cites its revision log.

**Rewrite:** Four deletions, no replacements. "Now the chronology, because it is easy to
get backwards." / "Less than the curve suggests. Five things changed in March 2020…" /
"Their own verdict is more generous than that line: 'Product Hunt was worthwhile,' they
write, and also…" / "Their reading of why the incumbent costs nothing:" (the attribution
"Their reading" already does the distancing).

### 3. The chapter's most seen-making sentence flinches to third person

**Quote:** "Twenty-three percent a month is not stagnation, but at $178 MRR it adds
forty-one dollars, and forty-one dollars feels like nothing — so a founder compounding
healthily can read his own dashboard and conclude he is stuck."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** This is the best paragraph in the chapter, and at the exact moment the
insight leaves Uku's dashboard and touches the reader's future one, it retreats to a
generic "a founder … his" — neither the case (which would be Uku, by name) nor the
reader. Monday item 5 already says "You may be growing steadily and calling it stagnant,"
so the second-person version of this thought exists twenty lines later; the flinch is
only here.

**Rewrite:** "…and forty-one dollars feels like nothing — so you can be compounding
healthily and still read your own dashboard as proof that you are stuck."

### 4. The best section closes on the book selling something

**Quote:** "Some of what positioning bought here was arithmetic and some was the ability
to see it. Only the first is what this chapter is selling."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** The closing line of the chapter's strongest run swerves from the case's
money ledger into the book's own storefront — apparatus surfacing again, and a word
("selling") that grates against the book's nothing-sold stance. Small note, but it is the
last thing the reader hears before "Boundaries," in the paragraph the whole section was
building to.

**Rewrite:** "Only the first can be counted on." — stays inside the bought/paid-for
register the section has been running.

### 5. The climax of the case lands verdict-first

**Quote:** "The post was *'Why you should stop using Google Analytics on your website'*.
It reached the top of Hacker News."

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** The swing and the verdict arrive in the same breath. The reader never
stands in the moment where two people at $433 a month have just told the internet to stop
using Google and do not yet know whether anyone will care — and every fact needed for
that beat is already on the page (Marko joined March 16; March closed at $433).

**Rewrite:** "The post was *'Why you should stop using Google Analytics on your website'*
— two people at $433 a month, telling everyone to drop Google. It reached the top of
Hacker News." To pay for the clause, shorten the preceding quotation to "*'On April 8th,
I published my first Plausible blog post … we decided to pick a fight.'*" (the ellipsis
convention is already in use three paragraphs up).

### 6. First person surfaces in the Reading note

**Quote:** "The figures are theirs; the compounding is mine, and you can redo it from the
ladder above."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** "Mine" is the narrator standing up — the one thing the voice never
does, and low-rank placement here is only about reading impact: as a rule breach it must
be fixed regardless. It is also redundant; the same sentence has already said "this
book's arithmetic on their published figures."

**Rewrite:** "The growth rates — 23%, 45%, 37% a month — are this book's arithmetic on
their published figures, not their claim. You can redo it from the ladder above."

### 7. The list of first-weeks changes is introduced with instructions on how to feel about it

**Quote:** "What happened in the first few weeks is listed plainly, and the list matters
more than any single item on it:"

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** "Is listed plainly" describes the source document, not the events; and
"matters more than any single item" hands down the verdict of the "How much of this was
positioning?" section before the reader has seen a single item — telling instead of
showing, and defusing the honesty section's turn.

**Rewrite:** "In the first few weeks the two of them changed five things at once:" — the
confound becomes a fact of the story, and Move 3's "Five things changed in March 2020"
then lands as a callback instead of a correction.

### 8. Move 2 ends as a press kit

**Quote:** "…a *'completely independent, self-funded and bootstrapped team of four'*,
describing themselves as *'intentionally small, profitable and sustainable'*, with…"

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** The case closes by handing the microphone to the company's brochure —
two consecutive strings of self-description with no number between them. The Reading
section admits everything here is self-reported; ending the load-bearing move on the most
marketing-flavoured of the quotes is where the summarizer, not the witness, is audible.

**Rewrite:** Cut "describing themselves as *'intentionally small, profitable and
sustainable'*," — keep "team of four," the 7,000 subscribers, the 50,000 websites, and
the never-paid-to-advertise line, which are the checkable parts and the ch. 7 handoff.

### 9. The case opens with a quotation from a speaker the reader cannot place

**Quote:** "In December 2018 a developer called Uku wrote the first line of an analytics
tool. *'The marketing head at the company he worked for asked him to integrate Google
Analytics into their landing page.'*"

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** The quoted voice says "he" about Uku, and its author — Marko — is not
introduced for two more paragraphs, so the reader's first beat inside the case is a
stumble over who is talking. The sentence is flat reportage that gains nothing from
quotation marks, and paraphrasing it protects the genuine reveal ("This other person was
me") for the paragraph built to deliver it.

**Rewrite:** "In December 2018 a developer called Uku wrote the first line of an
analytics tool. The marketing head at the company he worked for had asked him to put
Google Analytics on their landing page. He did not want to."

### 10. A textbook cross-reference inside the Simple bullet

**Quote:** "That is a second lesson inside the chapter, not a contradiction of it; see
below."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "Inside the chapter" and "see below" are both the apparatus talking;
the paragraph being pointed to (the funnels-at-$83,637 renegotiation) arrives four
paragraphs later and does not need a signpost.

**Rewrite:** "That is a second lesson, not a contradiction of the first."

---

## Overall note

One pass through the chapter deleting every sentence in which the chapter refers to
itself — "got it backwards in draft," "is obliged to say so," "has to carry both halves,"
"what this chapter is selling," "theirs rather than this book's," "the compounding is
mine." The honesty underneath those sentences is the chapter's real distinction: the
confound section, the gentler arithmetic, the sourcing confession are things almost no
book in this genre does. Announced, they read as a machine demonstrating compliance with
a rulebook; performed silently, the same content reads as the character of the person
writing. (Independent of that pass, Monday item 4 must change in any event — it is the
one place the chapter contradicts its own corrected lesson.)
