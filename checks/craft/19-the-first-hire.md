# Craft review — ch. 19, The First Hire

Reviewed 2026-08-28, first review of a first draft, against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §5b, §6d and the
§6i entry for this chapter; every Galperin quotation checked against `galperin-2023-covid.raw` and
every Dutch quotation against `rijks-schijnzelfstandigheid-2026.raw`. All fourteen quotations are
exact (one typographic note in finding 9). No rewrite below adds a fact; two findings remove
inferences the source does not support, which is a cut, not a fact change. One optional addition is
marked `pending fact-check` because it would need a claim row even though it is on the page of the
already-registered source.

## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, and a careful one — who keeps stepping in front of the case.

This is the strongest first draft in Part IV. Move 1 puts the reader exactly where they are — the
platform contractor "on the shift so long that the customers know their name" — and "Hold the
arithmetic" is the right instruction at the right moment. Move 3's test is one a reader can say in
one sentence: *if the deliverable is late you bought a task; if the day does not run you have a job.*
"A job wearing a task's contract" and "most of a job's dependence without any of its commitment"
are lines a practitioner keeps. The Dutch box is the best in the book so far: statute quoted, the
presumption explained as a presumption and not a reclassification, enforcement quoted rather than
summarised, the assessment framework named as the thing it does not quote. The Monday list is
concrete enough to fail at.

Two problems carry most of the weight. First, the chapter says things about Galperin's contractors
the post does not: that the Upwork support was bought "by the hour" and "for years", that the four
end-2022 contractors were "still" there and there "on purpose", that the first employee had been "a
freelancer", and that he "regrets the order" of his two hires — the post regrets a timing, not an
order. Each is small; together they are the difference between a case and a case read the way the
chapter needs it read, and the Reading note's honesty about evidence class does not cover an
inference the source never made. Second, the statutory presumption — handled exactly right inside
the box — leaks out of it three times in looser form: in the lede, in the decision box, and most
seriously in Monday 2, where "the presumption in the box has [decided] too … accept that it has
been decided for you" is precisely the overshoot §6i warns against.

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: twice, mild — "And he did not experience the change as
a budget line" precedes the quotation that shows it, and "It failed because what he needed was not
an output" states the chapter's reading of the agencies before the reader has his (finding 7).
(2) apparatus: the Move 2 opener — "the same post chapter eighteen's source names as its
predecessor", "As before, the numbers are his own" — and "this chapter gives no figure because any
it gave would be wrong for someone" (findings 4, 8). (3) flinch: none; second person holds in
every Move. (4) counting and self-announcing: "Two questions come before that one", "Two things in
that sentence matter more than the role", "Note the order", "That last split is the quiet fact in
the post", "this is the part to read twice" (findings 2, 4, 6). (5) summariser's seams: "for
years" (twice), "on purpose", "a third of the people" (findings 1, 3). (6) source accounting in the
argument: "As before, the numbers are his own … not a filing" duplicates Reading; the "this case is
consistent with it and does not prove it" sentence duplicates Reading's last line (findings 4, 6).
(7) not applicable.

Word count: 3,254 rendered, against a 3,000 ceiling. The cuts in findings 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10 take
out roughly 260 words without removing a fact; the loaded-cost instruction is currently given three
times and the ch. 20 pointer three times.

## 1. The Upwork contractors are given a duration and a billing unit the post does not give them

**Quote** — Move 2: *"He had been buying that work by the hour for years — "We have engaged customer
service people through Upwork previously as contractors, and it just wasn't working out.""* — Move
3: *"Galperin's support hires ran that way for years, and "it just wasn't working out" is the whole
of what he says about what it cost."* — Move 3, Convert section: *"only that the support job
existed for years before anyone was paid to hold it."*

**Lens** — Story craft (the figures load-bearing; the inference inside the source).

**What's wrong** — The post says "previously". It gives no start date, no duration and no billing
arrangement for the Upwork contractors. "By the hour" is the reader's platform contractor from Move
1 being read back into the case; "for years" appears three times and is the single most
load-bearing unsourced word in the chapter, because the whole "job wearing a task's contract"
argument in Move 3 rests on the arrangement having run a long time. The honest version is shorter
and still does the work, because the regret sentence carries the duration by itself.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"He had been buying that work before, as contractors: "We have engaged
customer service people through Upwork previously as contractors, and it just wasn't working out.""*
Move 3: *"Galperin's support contractors ran that way for long enough that "I still regret not doing
this sooner" is his whole verdict on it."* Convert section: *"only that the support job existed
before anyone was paid to hold it."*

## 2. Monday 2 turns the presumption into a decision — the one overshoot §6i names

**Quote** — Monday 2: *"Weekly, or twenty-plus a month, for all three: you have already made the
decision in practice, and if you are in the Netherlands the presumption in the box has too. Decide
it on paper this week or accept that it has been decided for you."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b) / the practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong** — The box says, correctly, that a presumption "is not a reclassification — it can
be rebutted — but it moves the burden." Monday then tells the Dutch reader the presumption "has
[decided] too" and that the matter "has been decided for you." That is the presumption reported as
reclassification, in the one place the reader acts, and it also applies the statutory trigger
(three months, weekly or twenty hours) as if it were the book's own test for every reader. Two
further leaks of the same kind outside the box, both loose statements of law in the portable
argument: the lede's *"the second of them is answered by the state whether or not you ask it"* and
the decision box's *"the law reads the arrangement from what actually happens, not from what the two
of you called it."* Neither is sourced to anything; both are true-ish of many jurisdictions and are
exactly what §5b quarantines.

**Rewrite** — Monday 2: *"Count the hours you have bought from the same person over the last three
months. If it is every week, or more than twenty hours a month, for all three, you have made the
decision in practice, and on Dutch ground that is the threshold at which the box's presumption
starts running against you. Decide it on paper this week."* Lede: *"The question you think you are
asking is whether you can afford it. Whether this is a task or a job comes first, and in the
Netherlands the answer is not only yours to give."* Decision box, last clause: *"and because on
Dutch ground the law has a test of its own for the same question."*

## 3. "Still", "on purpose", and "a third" — three words the headcount split does not contain

**Quote** — Move 2: *"That last split is the quiet fact in the post. The task-or-job decision was not
made once, at the first hire, and then settled. Eighteen months later a third of the people doing
the work were still being bought as tasks, on purpose, next to the ones being paid as jobs. He does
not say which work went which way."* — Move 3: *"The four contractors still at Gymdesk at the end
of 2022 are not a mistake in the post and are not one here."*

**Lens** — Story craft / summariser's seam.

**What's wrong** — The post gives one figure: "8 employees and 4 contractors" at end-2022. It does
not say the four were the same people as earlier contractors ("still"), that keeping them as
contractors was deliberate ("on purpose"), or what they did. "A third" and "eighteen months" are
derived, and the chapter's own next sentence — "He does not say which work went which way" — is the
honest one. The inference the chapter wants (the line is drawn repeatedly, not once) survives with
all three words removed. "The quiet fact in the post" announces the point instead of making it.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"The task-or-job decision was not made once, at the first hire, and then
settled: at the end of 2022 the business was paying eight people as jobs and buying four as tasks,
side by side. He does not say which work went which way."* Move 3: *"The four contractors at
Gymdesk at the end of 2022 are not a mistake in the post and are not one here."*

## 4. Move 2 opens as ch. 18 opened, and carries its Reading note with it

**Quote** — *"Eran Galperin has been in this book three times, and this chapter uses the one stretch
of Gymdesk's history the earlier chapters had no use for: the two years in which a one-person
business took on its first employee, told in a post he wrote in May 2023 — the same post chapter
eighteen's source names as its predecessor. As before, the numbers are his own and are read here as
a founder's account, not a filing."*

**Lens** — Rhythm / apparatus (modes 2 and 6).

**What's wrong** — Ch. 18's Move 2 opens "Eran Galperin has been in this book twice … This is the
part of his story those chapters had no use for." The reader gets the same sentence two chapters
running with the number incremented. The predecessor-post relationship is already stated in Reading
("is the one chapter eighteen's source links as 'I wrote about the process'"), where §6d puts it,
and "As before, the numbers are his own" is the Reading note repeated in the argument's path. The
useful thing to say here is where the reader last saw him: ch. 18 showed Gymdesk a year *later*
than this, with ten people and a documentation programme. Told forwards, this chapter is how the
first of those people arrived.

**Rewrite** — *"Chapter eighteen met Eran Galperin a year further on than this, with more than ten
people at Gymdesk and a knowledge base for getting them unstuck. This is how the first of them
arrived, told in a post he wrote in May 2023 about the two years before that."* (Cuts about 45
words; the predecessor-post line stays in Reading.)

## 5. "Regrets the order" — the post regrets a timing

**Quote** — Move 3, Convert section: *"One thing this case cannot tell you is where to hire first.
Galperin put the first job on the growth channel and the second on his own bottleneck, and regrets
the order; a single account of one sequence, judged by the person who chose it, does not establish
that the reverse order is right — only that the support job existed for years before anyone was
paid to hold it. Chapter seventeen's ranking is still the rule this book gives; this case is
consistent with it and does not prove it."* — and Move 2: *"Note the order. The work that had been
eating his own day — support, the thing chapter seventeen would have ranked first — was the second
job he created, not the first, and it is the one he passes judgment on"*

**Lens** — Story craft / §6d (one principal caveat).

**What's wrong** — "I still regret not doing this sooner" is a regret about when the support hire
happened, not about which of the two hires came first; the post never sets them against each other.
Move 2 nudges the reader toward the order ("Note the order") and Move 3 then withdraws the nudge at
length, ending on a sentence that restates Reading's last line ("one person's judgment of one
sequence"). And "the thing chapter seventeen would have ranked first" contradicts ch. 17, whose
default order puts recurring work *last* and whose rule is that the absence table decides —
support ranks high only where its tolerable delay is short, which ch. 17 says it often is but does
not decree. This paragraph is the chapter's principal caveat and should stay; it should be two
sentences and make no claim about order-regret.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"The work that had been eating his own day — support — was the second job he
created, not the first, and it is the one he passes judgment on: "I still regret not doing this
sooner.""* Move 3: *"One thing this case cannot tell you is where to hire first. Galperin put the
first job on the growth channel and the second on the work that was eating his day, and regrets
only that the second came late; one sequence, judged by the person who chose it, says nothing about
whether the reverse would have been better. Chapter seventeen's table is still what sets the
order."*

## 6. The first employee is made a "freelancer", and the trial is made an interview's equal

**Quote** — Move 2: *"Two things in that sentence matter more than the role. The person was already
doing the work for him, as a freelancer, before the job existed. And he did not experience the
change as a budget line. By his own account:"* — Move 3: *"A freelancer who has done the work for
you for months has been on trial the whole time — you know the output, the speed, and what it is
like to have them in the business, and none of that came from an interview."*

**Lens** — Story craft / counting opener (mode 4).

**What's wrong** — The post says the hire was "a person who used to write for us". Not "freelancer",
not "for months", and nothing about what it was like to have them in the business. The
freelancer/employee contrast in the next sentence of the post is Galperin's general remark, not a
description of this person. The chapter's "Convert before you recruit" section and Monday 4 rest on
this hire having been a conversion, and "used to write for us" supports that — but only that. "Two
things in that sentence matter" counts the point before making it, and "he did not experience the
change as a budget line" is the verdict placed before the quotation that is the evidence for it.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"The person had written for the business before the job existed. And the
sentence he gives the moment is not about a budget: "This was a big decision …"* (quotation
unchanged). Move 3: *"Someone who has done the work for you for a while has been on trial the whole
time — you know the output and the speed, and none of that came from an interview."*

## 7. The agencies are given the chapter's diagnosis before the reader gets Galperin's

**Quote** — *"The task did not fail because the agencies were bad at tasks. It failed because what he
needed was not an output. It was someone inside the business, for as long as the business needed
them."*

**Lens** — Story craft (verdict-first).

**What's wrong** — Galperin's stated reason is that outsiders "were never going to understand our
audience like I did" and that he wanted a team "that would live and breath our product and
vertical." The chapter's task/job reading of that is fair, but it is the chapter's, and it is
asserted as what happened. One clause of attribution keeps the inference and gives the source its
own line. The chapter also omits that the post's *first* outsourcing — the UX designer contracted in
2019 for the relaunch — was a task bought well that worked, which would be the cleanest evidence on
the page for "both forms are legitimate". Optional, `pending fact-check` (source already
registered; would need a claim row).

**Rewrite** — *"His own reason is about the audience: outsiders "were never going to understand our
audience like I did." Read as a purchase, that is the task form failing on its own terms — what he
needed was not an output but someone inside the business, for as long as the business needed
them."*

## 8. The loaded-cost instruction is given three times and once as apparatus

**Quote** — Move 3: *"Take the cost from your accountant, not from the wage. What an employee costs
the business in a month is not the figure on the offer, and in every jurisdiction there are charges
above it that vary by form, sector and year; this chapter gives no figure because any it gave would
be wrong for someone. Ask for the loaded monthly cost, then check it against the lowest month in
chapter thirteen's weekly cash figure — the lowest, because that is the month the obligation will
still fall due."* — Box: *"and ask your accountant what the loaded cost of the employee form is
before you compare it with the contractor's invoice."* — Monday 3: *"Ask your accountant for the
loaded monthly cost of one employee at the wage you have in mind — the figure with every employer's
charge in it, not the offer. Set it against the lowest month of the last twelve in your weekly cash
figure."*

**Lens** — Warmth without slack / apparatus (mode 2).

**What's wrong** — Monday 3 is the version that should survive; it is the most concrete. Move 3's
paragraph says the same thing with "this chapter gives no figure because…" — the book explaining its
own policy — and the box repeats it a third time where the box's job is the lawyer. "The lowest
month … in the weekly cash figure" mixes units in both Move 3 and Monday.

**Rewrite** — Move 3, whole paragraph: *"The figure to check is not the wage but what the employee
costs the business in a month with every employer's charge in it, and the month to check it against
is the lowest one in the last year of chapter thirteen's weekly cash figure — the lowest, because
that is the month the obligation will still fall due."* Box: cut "and ask your accountant … the
contractor's invoice." Monday 3: *"Set it against the lowest four weeks of the last twelve months in
your weekly cash figure."* (Net cut about 50 words.)

## 9. The box decides the reader's case, and two small Dutch-ground notes

**Quote** — Box: *"The platform contractor on the same shift for a year is squarely inside it."* —
Box: *"and this is the part to read twice"* — Move 3: *"the state has a test of its own, in the box
below, and it does not consult the label."* — `.owed`: *"carries a liability the box above puts a
date on."*

**Lens** — Dutch ground (§5b: no advice, nothing derived).

**What's wrong** — Everything else in the box is quotation and gloss; "squarely inside it" is the
box applying art. 610a to a hypothetical, which is what it says one paragraph later is the lawyer's
job. "Read twice" repeats ch. 18's "Read the second clause twice" one chapter on. "Does not consult
the label" and "puts a date on" are loose paraphrases of law outside the box — the second, in the
owed block, is aimed at every reader, and the liability is Dutch. Typographic: the agencies
quotation has straight double quotes nested inside the outer quotation (`"growth"`); the source has
curly quotes, and a nested quotation should be single-quoted.

**Rewrite** — Box: cut "The platform contractor on the same shift for a year is squarely inside it."
and "and this is the part to read twice". Move 3: *"because on Dutch ground the state has a test of
its own, in the box below."* Owed: *"costs nothing on any given Monday and, on Dutch ground, carries
the liability the box above quotes."* Agencies quotation: *'growth'* in single quotes.

## 10. Two provocations left standing, and a metaphor that runs backwards

**Quote** — *"The first employee is the first cost that cannot be varied: it falls due monthly …"* —
*"A hire funded by the growth it is supposed to produce is a task priced as a job: if the growth
does not come, you are left with the obligation and not the output."* — *"Gymdesk made its first
hire with monthly revenue near twenty thousand dollars and eighteen months of it recurring through
a shutdown of its whole market."*

**Lens** — The practitioner's ear / §4 (provocations are not laws).

**What's wrong** — "The first cost that cannot be varied" is false for any reader with a lease, and
ch. 1 asked them to keep costs variable knowing some cannot be. The line wants one qualifying
clause. The "task priced as a job" figure is inverted: the error described — a fixed obligation
taken on for an uncertain output — is a *job* bought on a *task's* reasoning, which is also the
phrase the chapter uses correctly elsewhere ("a job wearing a task's contract"). And the Gymdesk
sentence is loose in a way a source-reader notices: $19.5k is the end-2020 figure, the hire is June
2021, and "eighteen months of it recurring" describes a series that churned for five months and then
grew 60% — not a level that recurred. The fact the chapter needs is that the hire was made on
revenue that had already survived the shutdown.

**Rewrite** — *"The first employee is, for most businesses, the first cost that cannot be varied and
has a person attached: it falls due monthly …"* — *"A hire funded by the growth it is supposed to
produce is a job bought on a task's reasoning: if the growth does not come, you are left with the
obligation and not the output."* — *"Gymdesk made its first hire in the middle of 2021, on revenue
that had ended the previous year near $19.5k a month after churning through a shutdown of its whole
market."*

## Overall note

The case is right and the box is right; what needs doing is taking the chapter's hands off the
case. Remove every word the post does not contain — "by the hour", "for years" (three times), "as
a freelancer", "still", "on purpose", "regrets the order", "would have ranked first" — and Move 2
becomes a story told forwards on Galperin's own sentences, which are strong enough to carry it: the
agency experiments, "used to write for us", the every-month sentence, "it just wasn't working out",
"I still regret not doing this sooner", eight and four. Then pull the presumption back inside the
box (finding 2), where the chapter already handles it perfectly, and let Monday 2 say only what is
true everywhere. The cuts in findings 4, 5, 8 and 9 bring the count under the ceiling without
losing a figure. Finding 7's UX-designer sentence is the one addition worth considering, because it
gives "both forms are legitimate" its only evidence in the chapter — but it is a new claim row, and
the chapter stands without it.
