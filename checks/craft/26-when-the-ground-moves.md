# Craft review — ch. 26, When the Ground Moves

Reviewed 2026-08-28, first review of a first draft, against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §6d, the §6i entry
for this chapter (its four rules) and the Gymdesk ledger; every Galperin quotation checked against
`galperin-2023-covid.raw`. Eleven of the twelve quotations are exact and in place. The twelfth —
gyms "opening again, or scaling up their operations" — is exact but taken from the 2022 paragraph
and placed in 2021 (finding 3). Of the four §6i rules, two hold outright: the 40% and the churn never
touch, and no churn rate is manufactured; the 60% and 2.5× carry "self-reported … not audited" in
Reading. The other two hold in Move 2's last paragraph and are then broken in Move 3: having said
the account contains no counterfactual, the chapter runs one of its own ("A business that had cut to
the March revenue in March would have had no hands to build the June features with") and argues the
timing rule from the year-end figure ("his year still ended up sixty percent") — which is the
features-caused-the-recovery inference in a different sentence. Underneath that is the same fault
the ch. 23 review found: the chapter reads the post for the thing it needs — a reserve held through a
shock — when the post shows a one-man business whose owner kept building. Nothing below needs a new
fact; the fixes are re-orderings of the post's own sentences, a re-pointing, three cuts, and one
piece of arithmetic the reader can already do from chapter thirteen.

## Does it read like a person or a machine? A person, on the right ground, who has borrowed ch. 23's reserve for a case that never had one.

Move 1 is exactly the reader's square: "The figures from chapter twenty-two moved in a week, and
not because of anything on your side of the counter … when you ask why the answer is the same from
all of them and has nothing to do with you." "Your revenue is the shadow of that change, a few weeks
behind it" is the chapter's best sentence, and "a response before a reading is a guess" is the rule
in seven words. The rule passes §6d in one sentence — *an external shock reaches you through what
your customers can no longer do; read it as their event, sell to them as they now are, offer the pause
before the cancellation, and count the cost when it is over, not when it hits* — and the index entry
says the same. Monday 1, 2, 3 and 5 are failable and are the chapter. The case is the right one and
the pre-shock paragraph is handled well: a business accelerating, the 40% quoted once and left alone.
The owed block holds the definition.

The problem is one problem in two places. First: the post says nothing about what Gymdesk cut, held
or spent. Ch. 19, from the same document, says the year "ended with him doing every job, as he had
for five years," and the post dates the first employee to June 2021 and calls 2020 "a one-man, owner
run business." So "What Gymdesk did … was not cut or wait" is the chapter's, and "no hands to build
the June features with" describes hands the business did not have — the hands were Galperin's own.
The timing paragraph in Move 3 leans ch. 23's reserve on a case that shows no reserve being held and
none being drawn, which is precisely the gap §6i said this chapter was positioned to fill and the
post does not fill (finding 1). The honest version is stronger: the reserve Gymdesk spent in 2020 was
its owner, which is what ch. 23 said is spent first in every small business. Second: the chapter
re-orders the post. Galperin writes "things were starting to stabilize around June" *before* he
mentions the features; the chapter puts the features first and the June sentence after, so the page
reads features → stabilisation, which is the causal arrow rule 1 forbids — and then glosses the list
as things a gym "could not operate without by summer," which for a vaccination check in summer 2020
is not possible (finding 2). Put the post's order back and the chapter's own caution does the work
without a caveat.

Sweep of the known modes. (1) verdict-first: "Every item on that list is something a gym did not
need in February and could not operate without by summer" (finding 2). (2) apparatus: "exactly as
Move 1 describes"; "none is invented here"; "this chapter did not find one"; "That is what the case
shows, and it is all it shows"; "the response that survives the account above"; "The limits are the
case's own"; "what this chapter takes from Gymdesk" (finding 10). (3) flinch: none — the reader is
in the room throughout. (4) counting and self-announcing: "The year's figure:"; "So: one dated,
figure-bearing report" (finding 10). (5) seams: "The reason the question comes before the cut is
timing" is fine; "The asking is the point" is fine; "So:" is the seam (finding 10). (6) source
accounting inside the argument: the counterfactual/hindsight/survivorship set is said four times —
Move 2's last paragraph, Move 3's limits paragraph, the owed block, Reading's Note — and §6d allows
one in the argument (finding 6). (7) n/a.

Continuity. Ch. 19's Move 2 already quotes "We had significant churn from March to around August"
and "having grown our MRR by 60% to around $19.5k," so ch. 26's "has a section those chapters did not
need" is wrong on its face (and §6i's "ch. 19 never touched" March–August 2020 is wrong in CONTEXT
too — worth correcting there so it does not propagate) (finding 8). Ch. 23 after its craft pass
holds reserve in *hours*, against three named shocks none of which is a demand shock, and hands "the
buffer … reserve in money" to "a later chapter" — so Monday 4's "Chapter twenty-three's count: how
many months does the business run" points at a count ch. 23 does not contain (finding 5). Ch. 2's
pointer imports "what they would pay to have back," which is the hypothetical ch. 2 spent Everpix
demonstrating is worthless (finding 4). Ch. 11's pointer is accurate: five columns, "if they left,
the reason in their words." Ch. 28's handoff skips ch. 16's stopping sentence (finding 9).

Word count: about 2,030 against a 2,000 floor. Findings 1, 2, 5 and 7 add roughly 140 words;
findings 6 and 10 remove roughly 120. Net slightly up.

## 1. "Not cut or wait," the "no hands" counterfactual, and "his year still ended up sixty percent" — a reserve the case does not show, argued from a result the chapter said it would not claim

**Quote** — Move 2: *"What Gymdesk did, by the same account, was not cut or wait. It built for gyms
as they now were"*. — Move 3: *"Chapter twenty-three's reserve exists for exactly this month, and
cutting it in the first week of a shock is spending the reserve on the shock's announcement rather
than its cost. Galperin's churn ran five months; his year still ended up sixty percent. A business
that had cut to the March revenue in March would have had no hands to build the June features
with."* — Owed: *"the chapter carries it by claiming only the order of the response and none of the
result."*

**Lens** — (b) inference inside the source; §6i rules 1 and 4; continuity with ch. 19 and ch. 23.

**What's wrong** — Three things, in order of weight. (i) The post does not say Gymdesk did not cut.
It says nothing about costs, staff or spending in 2020 at all. What the document does say — and
ch. 19 has already used it — is that the first full-time hire came in June 2021 and that only then
did the business begin "the transition from a one-man, owner run business to an actual company."
In 2020 the company was Galperin plus contractors. "By the same account" attributes to the post a
decision it does not record, and "no hands to build the June features with" imagines a payroll the
business did not have. (ii) The sentence "Galperin's churn ran five months; his year still ended up
sixty percent," placed as the reason the reserve should not be cut, is the result being used as the
argument — one paragraph after Move 2 said the account "does not test whether the first produced
the second." The owed block then says the chapter claims "none of the result." It claims it here.
"Five months" is also the chapter's arithmetic on "March to around August" (six months inclusive,
five exclusive, "around" either way), which rule 4 says not to do. (iii) Ch. 23's reserve is capacity
in hours held against three named shocks — a person off, an order doubling, the owner ill — and
ch. 23 explicitly sends reserve in money to "a later chapter." A demand shock draws on money, not
hours; "exists for exactly this month" is not what ch. 23 says. The chapter's timing rule is right
and is the book's; what the case can show for it is narrower and better: the only reserve on the
page is the owner, and the post shows him spending it — which is ch. 23's own claim that the owner's
reserve is "spent first." Say that, mark the timing rule as the book's, and drop the counterfactual.

**Rewrite** — Move 2: *"What the account says Gymdesk did next is build, and the post is silent on
everything else — there was, by the same document, almost nothing to cut: the first employee came in
June 2021, and until then this was "a one-man, owner run business." It built for gyms as they now
were: "We kept adapting…"* — Move 3, the timing paragraph: *"The reason the question comes before the
cut is timing. A shock arrives as an announcement and is paid for over months, and a cut made in the
first week is priced on the announcement. Chapter twenty-three held reserve in hours; a demand shock
draws on the other kind, money, and the buffer that chapter left to a later one is drawn on here. Gymdesk
had no reserve of either kind to hold in 2020 except its owner, and the post shows that one being
spent — the features were built by the same person who was answering the freeze requests. That is
the order the case shows, and the rule that the cut waits for the reading is this book's, not
his. It comes when the reading says what your customers can now do is less than the business needs,
for longer than the reserve lasts, and that is a number, not a feeling in week one."* — Owed: keep
*"claiming only the order of the response and none of the result"* — it becomes true once the Move 3
sentence goes.

## 2. The post's order is reversed around "stabilize around June," and the feature list is glossed as things a gym "could not operate without by summer"

**Quote** — Move 2: *"It built for gyms as they now were: "We kept adapting … and more." Every item
on that list is something a gym did not need in February and could not operate without by summer —
a booking system that enforces a headcount limit, a check at the door, a way to run a class for
people who cannot come to the room. "Yet, things were starting to stabilize around June", he writes
— before the year's figure, and before anyone could know whether June was the floor."* — Move 3:
*"capacity limits, checks, online classes were the shape of a gym in June 2020"*.

**Lens** — (b) a story told forwards, in the source's order; §6i rule 1.

**What's wrong** — In the post the sentences run: churn "from March to around August. Yet, things
were starting to stabilize around June, and it seemed like we would manage through this unexpected
turn of events." *Then*, a new paragraph: "We kept adapting to the new environment, and added many
features…" The founder reports stabilisation before he mentions the features, and does not date a
single feature. The chapter swaps them, so the reader meets the features and then the recovery, and
"before the year's figure" makes the June sentence sound like an interim result of the building.
That is the arrow rule 1 forbids, drawn by sequence rather than by a verb. The gloss compounds it:
"could not operate without by summer" and "the shape of a gym in June 2020" date the list to summer
2020, and a "COVID vaccination check" could not have been in use before vaccines existed — the
post itself puts vaccines in the 2021 paragraph. "Every item … could not operate without" is a
verdict, and it is the chapter's. The chapter also cut "and it seemed like we would manage through
this unexpected turn of events," which is the one place the founder records what he believed at the
time rather than in 2023 — and it is exactly the sentence the chapter's "before anyone could know
whether June was the floor" is reaching for. Put the post's order back and the correlation caveat
in the next paragraph gets shorter, because the account's own sequence no longer needs undoing.

**Rewrite** — Move 2, in the post's order: *"…We had significant churn from March to around
August." No rate is given for that churn. What the account gives is the span and the shape:
customers who could not open, asking either to pause or to leave. And, in the same paragraph, the
founder's view from inside it: "Yet, things were starting to stabilize around June, and it seemed
like we would manage through this unexpected turn of events." Only then does the account turn to what
the company built…"* — then finding 1's rewrite — then: *"…"online training features and more." A
booking system with a headcount limit, a check at the door, a class for people who cannot come to the
room: features for a gym under restriction, none of them dated in the account, and the vaccination
check cannot have been a 2020 feature at all."* — Move 3: *"capacity limits, checks, online classes
were the shape of a gym under restriction, and a vendor still selling the shape of a gym in February
2020 would have had nothing to sell — which is this book's reading of the list, not a claim the
account makes."*

## 3. "Opening again, or scaling up their operations" is 2022, placed in 2021

ended 2021 strongly, having grown our MRR by around x2.5 compared to 2020.""*

**Lens** — (b) quotation in its own place; §6i rule 1 (the confound for the 2.5×).

**What's wrong** — The post: "We ended 2021 strongly, having grown our MRR by around x2.5 compared to

scaling up their operations" against ch. 26; the span still verifies, but it should be attached to
the year it belongs to or replaced.)

**Rewrite** — *"The next year, with "most people … starting to get used to this new reality, and
gyms … following suit": "We ended 2021 strongly, having grown our MRR by around x2.5 compared to
2020." Vaccines, in his account, were "starting to become available" as that year closed, and gyms
"opening again, or scaling up their operations" is his description of 2022."*

## 4. The pointer to chapter two imports the question chapter two spent Everpix rejecting

**Quote** — Move 3: *"The asking is the same instrument as chapter two's, run again on ground that
has moved: what they did last week, what they are doing instead, what they would pay to have back."*
— Monday 1: *"ask five of them this week — chapter two's questions, on the changed ground."*

**Lens** — continuity with ch. 02; (e) Monday 1 concrete enough to fail at.

**What's wrong** — Ch. 2's five questions are all past tense and all about money already moving:
"When did you last hit this? What did you do instead? What did that cost you, in hours or in money?
Who else would have to agree? What do you pay for anything like this today?" — and its case is 1,288
Everpix respondents of whom 12.1% said they "would pay" and almost none did. "What they would pay to
have back" is that question. The first two of ch. 26's three are ch. 2's; the third contradicts it.
On changed ground the past-tense version exists and is sharper: what they are paying for now, this
week, to get by — the workaround with money in it, which ch. 2 calls the strongest evidence a
conversation produces. That is also the third column of Monday 1 ("what they now need") given a
figure.

**Rewrite** — Move 3: *"The asking is the same instrument as chapter two's, run again on ground that
has moved, and in the same tense: what they did last week, what they are doing instead, and what that
is costing them — the workaround with money in it is where the thing you can sell now is."* — Monday
1: *"…ask five of them this week — chapter two's five questions, past tense, on the changed ground;
"what would you pay to have it back" is not one of them."*

## 5. Monday 4's number is not in chapter twenty-three, and the reader cannot compute it as written

**Quote** — Monday 4: *"Read the reserve against the churned level, not the old one. Chapter
twenty-three's count: at this month's revenue, how many months does the business run before the cut
is forced? Write the date that number gives you."*

**Lens** — (e) Monday concrete enough to fail at; §4 "numbers or nothing"; continuity with ch. 23
and ch. 13.

**What's wrong** — Ch. 23's count is hours sold against hours available, per person and for the
owner. It contains no months-of-cash figure and says the money buffer is "a later chapter's." So the
reader sent to ch. 23 finds nothing to divide. The number is derivable, and every input is already
in the book: ch. 13's Monday 2 gives the cash that is actually yours (bank balance less VAT and
payroll taxes held for someone else); ch. 16's Monday 1 gives the monthly outgoings, counted from
statements; ch. 22 gives this month's takings. Months = cash that is yours ÷ (monthly outgoings −
this month's revenue). Say the formula, and it is failable — a reader who cannot fill one of the
three inputs has found the chapter she skipped. This is also the natural place to claim the handoff
ch. 23 left open; ch. 26 draws the buffer down, which is not the same as owning how large it should
be, and the sentence can say only the first.

**Rewrite** — Monday 4: *"Read the reserve against the churned level, not the old one. Take chapter
thirteen's cash — the bank balance less what is held for the tax authority — and divide it by the gap
between a month's outgoings, counted as chapter sixteen counted them, and this month's takings. The
answer is months. Write the date that number gives you. The cut, if it comes, comes on that date on
the reading, not in week one on the fear."*

## 6. The counterfactual, the hindsight and the survivorship are said four times; §6d allows one in the argument

**Quote** — Move 2, last paragraph: *"What the account does not contain is the counterfactual … and
it is written by the founder of a company that survived, three years on. The vendor to the same gyms
that did not recover has not written its account, and this chapter did not find one. So: one dated,
figure-bearing report … That is what the case shows, and it is all it shows."* — Move 3: *"The
limits are the case's own. It is one survivor's account, written with three years' hindsight, of a

above is written by the founder of the company that survived, and the vendor to the same gyms that
did not survive has left no post. The nearer debt is the survivorship itself…"* — Reading's Note:
all of it again. — Also Move 2: *"the whole fitness-tech market moved with the pandemic and not
with any one vendor's feature list."*

**Lens** — Mode 6; §6d one principal caveat; (b) an assertion dressed as fact.

**What's wrong** — Rule 1 requires the chapter to say the account shows correlation the founder did
not test, and that belongs in Move 2, once, because it changes how the reader applies the case. Rule
3 requires the survivorship to be named; the owed block is where a missing counter-account lives,
and Reading's Note is where the evidence class lives. Move 3's limits paragraph then repeats both,
and its only new content — the shock that does not lift — is the boundary the chapter actually
needs there (finding 9). "The whole fitness-tech market moved with the pandemic and not with any one
vendor's feature list" is CONTEXT's caution lifted into the chapter as a fact about a market the
chapter has no source for; the two clauses before it ("Gyms reopened whether or not their software
had a capacity limit; vaccines arrived on their own schedule") make the same point from the post.
With finding 2's re-ordering the Move 2 paragraph shrinks to what the post's own sequence does not
already show.

**Rewrite** — Move 2, last paragraph: *"The account reports the building and the recovery side by
side and does not test whether the one produced the other; gyms reopened whether or not their
software had a capacity limit, and Galperin's own reading of the sequence is stated as a belief: "I
strongly believe our ability to adapt during this period of uncertainty laid the foundation for
future success." What the case shows is a business whose entire market was closed by decree, that
lost customers to it and can say roughly when, that built for the customers it still had as they
now were, and that ended the year larger than it began it — and which of those produced which, it
cannot say."* (The "I strongly believe" quotation moves here from the paragraph above.) — Move 3,
limits paragraph: *"The boundary is the shock that does not lift. This is one account of one that
did; the road that stays closed, the regulation that stays, is a different event, and the reading
may say so…"* (continues per finding 9). — Owed: unchanged. — Reading's Note: unchanged.

## 7. "Freeze before cancel," the café and the tradesman are the book's, and "usually smaller, nearer and cheaper" has no source at all

**Quote** — Move 3: *"The customers asked "to freeze or cancel", and the difference between those
two words is the difference between a customer paused and a customer lost. The account does not say
what Gymdesk offered them; it says what they asked for, and the asking is the point."* — *"The same
move exists for a business with no software in it. The café whose office customers are at home has
customers who still eat lunch; the tradesman whose clients cannot let anyone in has clients whose
houses still break. What they now need is usually smaller, nearer and cheaper than what they needed
before, and it is on offer only to a business that asked."*

**Lens** — (c) the rule earned from the source; (d) translations marked as the book's; §4
provocations.

**What's wrong** — The freeze paragraph is honest about what the post says and does not say — and
then the rule is stated as though it followed. It does not follow; it is a proposal the book makes on
the strength of one word the customers used, and it is a good one, but it should be owned. The café
and the tradesman are the chapter's, and read as findings ("has customers who still eat lunch") when
they are guesses about businesses the book has not looked at; one clause makes them what they are.
"Usually smaller, nearer and cheaper" is a generalisation with no case behind it and a word ("usually")
that promises a population; the chapter has one company and cannot say "usually" about anything. The
useful version is the question, not the answer.

**Rewrite** — *"The account does not say what Gymdesk offered them; it says what they asked for. The
rule this book takes from that word is the book's: a customer who cannot use what you sell will leave
unless leaving is not the only exit, and a pause — dated, cheap, easy to reverse — is the exit that
keeps the relationship the record in chapter eleven was built to hold. Offer it before it is asked
for."* — *"The same move should exist for a business with no software in it, and here the book is
guessing on your behalf: the café whose office customers are at home may find they still eat lunch;
the tradesman whose clients cannot let anyone in may find their houses still break. Whether what they
now need is smaller, nearer or cheaper than before is the thing to ask, not to assume, and it is on
offer only to a business that asked."*

## 9. The shock that does not lift is handed to chapter twenty-eight over chapter sixteen's head

**Quote** — Move 3: *"a shock that does not lift — the road that stays closed, the regulation that
stays — is a different event, and the reading may say so, in which case chapter twenty-eight is the

**Lens** — continuity with ch. 16 and ch. 28 (§6i's ch. 28 entry).

**What's wrong** — §6i gives ch. 28 "the deliberate, executed decision to stop operating, and doing

## 10. Apparatus on stage and two seams

## Overall note

The rule is right, the opening is the reader's, the case is the right section of the right document,
and the Monday list is the best in Part V so far — five items, four of them failable on the day. What
needs doing is to stop borrowing. The chapter borrows ch. 23's reserve for a business that had none
and a post that never mentions cost, and then argues the timing rule from the year-end figure it had
just said it would not claim (finding 1); it borrows a causal order from re-arranging the post's
sentences, when the post's own order — stabilisation reported before the features are mentioned —
does the chapter's honest work for it (finding 2); it borrows 2022's reopening sentence for 2021
(finding 3); it borrows from ch. 2 the one question ch. 2 exists to reject (finding 4); and it sends
the reader to ch. 23 for a number that lives in ch. 13 and ch. 16 (finding 5). Put those back and the
chapter is stronger on its own thesis, because the only reserve on the page is the owner being
spent — which is what ch. 23 said would happen first. Then the usual pass: the caveat said once in
the argument instead of four times (finding 6), the book's own proposals marked as its own (finding
7), the cross-reference to ch. 19 made true (finding 8), the stopping decision routed through ch. 16
(finding 9), the apparatus off the stage (finding 10). Nothing needs a new fact; three findings add
sentences already in the post.
