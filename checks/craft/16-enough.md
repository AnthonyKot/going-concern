# Craft review — ch. 16, Enough

Reviewed 2026-08-26 against CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §6d, §6g (and its review record), and the Part III
register. Every figure and quotation is assumed correct; no rewrite below adds a fact. The narrator
stays invisible throughout.

## Does this read like a person or a machine? Mostly a person — until the case is told backwards.

The chapter has real writing in it: "It will absorb capital, hours and attention up to whatever
limit you set, and if you set none it will find the limit experimentally — usually at the point
where something in your life breaks"; "a threshold rewritten in the month it is about to be tested
is not a threshold, it is a mood"; "an owner who spent a year on something else — which is what
enough is for"; "the archive of quiet endings is not published anywhere". The generosity toward
Cegłowski is exactly right — nothing hero-shaped, nothing sneering at the sabbatical. The Monday
list is the best in Part III, and item 5 ("Finish the sentence before you look at this year's
numbers") is the chapter's rule in one line.

The damage is concentrated in two places. **Move 2 narrates the source in the order the source was
read, not the order it was lived** — 2016 first, then "two years earlier", then 2017 — so the
reader arrives at the decision (going public with the number) before the discovery that produced
it, and the one moment where a person is deciding under uncertainty is handed over as a summary of
where Part III has been "circling". And **the review round has left its fingerprints**: a heading
that says four and a sentence that says three, a refusal paragraph that announces itself, a
Boundaries opening that talks about the chapter's reader in the third person.

Sweep of the known failure modes: (1) verdict-first — yes, the central finding; (2) apparatus in
prose — five instances ("the whole of Part III has been circling", "most of the machinery for it",
"this book puts the money first and says so", "the case above", "this chapter does not claim");
(3) second-person flinch — twice, both at sharp moments (Boundaries, the run-of-periods paragraph);
(4) counting openings — one, and it miscounts; (5) summarizer seams — "an unusually honest artefact
for this genre"; (6) source accounting in the argument's path — the confounder sentence is
required by §6g and stays, but the paragraph around it is an audit, not an argument; (7) scar
tissue — the refusal paragraph and the doubled ending of the part-close.

---

## Findings, ranked by impact

### 1. The case is told in the order it was read, so the reader never stands beside him in 2014

**Quote:** "By 2016 the table read: revenue of $117K, $178K, $181K, $175K, $193K, $160K and $234K
for 2010 through 2016, against funding of zero in every one of those years. Costs, he wrote, were
"something like $17K/year to run, so you can make a good living at this artisanal SaaS stuff". Two
years earlier he had put the same point without figures — the site "makes roughly the same amount
of money (around $200K) every year", and "the number of active users stays roughly the same" — and
drew the conclusion the whole of Part III has been circling: stability was not a symptom, it was the
product."

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** The lived sequence is: five years in, he notices the line is flat and decides
that is the product (2014); two years later, no longer afraid, he publishes the number so others
can see the bar (2016). The prose gives the publication first, the discovery as a flashback, and
the discovery's conclusion as a thing *the book* has been circling — so the one moment where a
person decides under uncertainty is narrated as the book's own summary. "Read the flat line
correctly" in the next paragraph then instructs the reader in a reading the 2014 post already
performs. Also "an unusually honest artefact for this genre" in the opening paragraph is a
summarizer grading his source before showing it.

**Rewrite** (replaces the first three paragraphs of Move 2; same facts, ~40 words shorter):

> Pinboard is a bookmarking service that one person, Maciej Cegłowski, launched in July 2009 and
> still runs. Every year on the anniversary he publishes a table of statistics, and he has done so
> for eleven consecutive years, while the outcome was still unknown, with no investors to impress.
>
> At five years the surprise in the table was that there was none. The site "makes roughly the
> same amount of money (around $200K) every year", "the number of active users stays roughly the
> same", and in five years he had not received "a single email from an investor". He could have
> read that as a ceiling. He read it as the product: what a bookmarking service sells is the
> belief that it will still be there, which requires "actually earning enough money to do that".
> Stability was not a symptom. It was the offer.
>
> Two years later he put the figures in. A revenue row joined the table in 2016 — deliberately,
> because he was "no longer afraid of competitors" and wanted to encourage anyone considering their
> own "one- or zero-person business". It read $117K, $178K, $181K, $175K, $193K, $160K and $234K
> for 2010 through 2016, against funding of zero in every year, on costs of "something like
> $17K/year to run, so you can make a good living at this artisanal SaaS stuff". That is a business
> earning several times its running costs, every year, for one person, without external money —
> a specification most businesses in this book would take. The number was named, it was met, and
> it was published so that other people could see what the bar actually was.

Then "Then the same table, four years later." follows unchanged. Cut the whole "Read the flat line
correctly" paragraph; its one surviving sentence is folded in above.

### 2. The instrument's heading says four and its first sentence says three

**Quote:** "Enough is four numbers, not one … The word hides an arithmetic problem behind a feeling.
Separate it into three, in this order, because each one is answerable and the feeling is not:"

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (template seam).

**What's wrong:** The review added the second number and the sentence beneath the heading was not
updated. This sits at the head of the chapter's instrument — the reader is being asked to trust an
arithmetic and the first line miscounts it. It also opens by counting its own contents.

**Rewrite:** "The word hides an arithmetic problem behind a feeling. Take the arithmetic in this
order, because each line is answerable and the feeling is not:"

### 3. The refusal of the causal arrow is delivered as an audit, in the chapter's best paragraph

**Quote:** "Two records, one sequence, and no arrow between them. This chapter does not claim the
neglect caused the decline, and nothing in it depends on that being true. Attention was not the
only thing that changed, the interval is years long, and the source supplies its own confounder:
revenue "dropped a bit in 2015 (as I moved to an annual subscription system)" and rose again as the
first renewals came due, so this line moves on pricing mechanics as well as on demand. What the
figures establish is the sequence. What the chapter uses the sequence for is narrower and survives
the missing arrow entirely:"

**Lens:** Warmth without slack / scar tissue.

**What's wrong:** The heading sentence already makes the refusal; the next sentence makes it again
in the debt register's voice ("this chapter does not claim… nothing in it depends on"), and "what
the chapter uses the sequence for" narrates the argument's own procedure. The confounder must stay
(§6g), but "the source supplies its own confounder" is a sourcing note in the argument's path;
the fact can be stated as a fact.

**Rewrite:**

> Two records, one sequence, and no arrow between them. Attention was not the only thing that
> changed, the interval is years long, and the line was never as still as it looks: it "dropped a
> bit in 2015 (as I moved to an annual subscription system)" and rose again as the first renewals
> came due, so it moves on pricing mechanics as well as on demand. What survives without the arrow
> is narrower and is the whole point: a sufficiency number can stay fixed while the business
> underneath it changes — gradually enough to be invisible in any single year, with a flat line
> that means one thing on the way up and something else later while looking identical.

### 4. Boundaries opens by talking about the reader instead of to them

**Quote:** "This is not permission to stop selling. The most likely misreading of this chapter, by
exactly the reader it is written for, is that a named number licenses the coasting that the
previous fifteen chapters were arguing against."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** This is the §3 defence — the sharpest sentence in the chapter's ethics — and it
flinches to the third person ("the reader it is written for") and talks about "this chapter" as an
object. The reader should be caught doing it, not described doing it.

**Rewrite:** "This is not permission to stop selling. You are the person most likely to read it that
way — a named number as licence for the coasting fifteen chapters have argued against — and it is
backwards: a number with *no review date* is what licenses coasting, because nothing ever
contradicts it."

### 5. "People skip" at the paragraph's own turn

**Quote:** "Three things follow, and the third is the one people skip."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** The paragraph's point is the third thing, and the reader is handed off to
"people" one clause before it arrives. The opening also counts its contents.

**Rewrite:** "Three things follow, and the third is the one you will be tempted to skip."

### 6. The time ledger talks about "readers" and about the book

**Quote:** "Money is not the only thing the business consumes, and a chapter about sufficiency that
counts only money will be wrong for a predictable set of readers. … A business that runs out of
money stops; a business that runs out of its owner's patience usually has months of warning and
several remedies, which is why this book puts the money first and says so."

**Lens:** Practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** "a predictable set of readers" is the abstract third person at the one place a
reader with a bad year would feel seen; "which is why this book puts the money first and says so"
is the book explaining its own design. The middle of the paragraph is good and untouched.

**Rewrite:** "Money is not the only thing the business consumes, and if what is running out is
evenings rather than euros, a number that counts only euros will tell you everything is fine.
… A business that runs out of money stops; a business that runs out of its owner's patience
usually has months of warning and several remedies. That is why the money comes first."

### 7. Move 1's turn is announced through the machinery

**Quote:** "The first failure is the famous one and this book has already used most of the
machinery for it: chapter fifteen put a cost on your labour, and once that cost is in the
restatement, a business that cannot cover it stops being a lifestyle question and becomes a
viability one. But note the direction of that test."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "this book has already used most of the machinery for it" is apparatus; "But
note the direction of that test" is a throat-clear before the chapter's real distinction (labour
cost versus life cost), which is otherwise the best-argued sentence in Move 1.

**Rewrite:** "The first failure is the famous one, and chapter fifteen already answers most of it:
put a cost on your labour, and a business that cannot cover it stops being a lifestyle question and
becomes a viability one. That test runs in one direction only. It asks what the business must
carry. It says nothing about what your life costs…"

### 8. The part-close ends twice

**Quote:** "Everything in this part is input to that. / The part should leave you holding something,
not agreeing with something: one dated page … Everything from chapter eleven onwards is an input
to those five lines."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** Two consecutive paragraphs close on the same sentence with the object swapped.
The second is the stronger (it lands on the artefact, which §6g says the part closes on); the
first is scar from the review that added the five-line page.

**Rewrite:** Cut "Everything in this part is input to that." and let the chapter-four paragraph
end on "…by someone calmer than you will be on the day."

### 9. Monday 7 points at "the case above"

**Quote:** "…a decline slow enough to matter is invisible year to year, which is the entire lesson
of the case above."

**Lens:** Warmth without slack.

**What's wrong:** "the case above" is page-furniture inside an instruction, and "the entire lesson"
sells the lesson instead of naming it. Pinboard can carry it in three words.

**Rewrite:** "…a decline slow enough to matter is invisible year to year; Pinboard's took three
tables to show."

---

## Overall note

Retell Move 2 in the order it was lived — 2014, 2016, 2017, 2020 — as in finding 1. Everything the
chapter needs is already on the page; only the order is borrowed from the Reading list. Told
forwards, the reader watches one person notice a flat line, decide it is the offer, go public with
the number, take the year off that the number had earned, and then look at the same table four
years later. That is a story with a decision in it, and it is what makes "which is what enough is
for" land as generosity rather than as a caption.
