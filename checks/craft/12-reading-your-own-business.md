# Craft review — ch. 12, Reading Your Own Business

Scope: prose only. Facts assumed correct. Rules in CONTEXT.md §3, §4, §5b, §6d treated as settled.
Narrator stays invisible in every rewrite below.

Sweep of the known failure modes first: (1) verdict-first — yes, and worse than usual, because the
standfirst gives the whole chapter away in its second sentence and Move 2 then walks the statute in
statute order; (2) apparatus in prose — four instances, two of them duplicating the Reading note and
the Boundaries; (3) flinch to third person — once, in the last Monday item; (4) openings that count
their contents — twice; (5) summarizer's seams — largely clean, the figures are all glossed;
(6) source accounting in the path — none; the box and Reading hold it properly.

The chapter-specific risk — accounting primer — is mostly avoided. "Three statements, three
questions" is a primer section, but it is short, it ends on "a photograph, not a film", and it is
immediately turned against the filing. The real specific fault is different: the chapter's one
conclusion is stated four times (standfirst, end of Move 1, "the actual conclusion of this chapter",
Boundaries) and the reader never gets to discover it. See the overall note.

---

## 1. Move 2 is told in statute order, so the reader never opens the filing and finds the hole

**Quote**
> Start with who is obliged to file anything, because the answer excludes a great many readers of this book. In the Netherlands it turns on legal form, not on size: […] For those who do file, Dutch company law then sorts them by size on three tests […] What matters is what each class must make public. For a company in the small class, the statute limits the publication duty to a restricted balance sheet and the notes to it. That is the entire public filing. There is no profit-and-loss account in it. Nobody reading your filed accounts can see your revenue, your costs, your margins, or whether the year was good.

**Lens** Story craft.

**What's wrong** This is the book's first case with no company in it, so the only person who can be
in the chair is the reader — and the paragraphs are ordered as the law is ordered (who files, size
classes, publication limit) rather than as the reader would meet it (open the filing, look for the
P&L, it is not there, now why). The moment of not-knowing exists in the material — "Nobody reading
your filed accounts can see your revenue" — but arrives fourth, after the verdict has been
announced twice.

**Rewrite** (reorder only; no new facts) Open Move 2 on the discovery, then supply the law:

> Download the filed accounts of any small bv — your own, a supplier's, the competitor across town. What you get is a balance sheet, restricted, and the notes to it. That is the entire public filing. There is no profit-and-loss account in it. Nobody reading it can see revenue, costs, margins, or whether the year was good — and nor can you, of theirs.
>
> The statute is why. For a company in the small class, the publication duty is limited to a restricted balance sheet and the notes. Which class you are in turns on three tests — assets, turnover and average headcount; meet two of them in two consecutive years and you fall into micro, small, medium, or everything else. The figures are in the box.
>
> But before the class comes a prior question, and its answer excludes a great many readers of this book. Whether you file at all turns on legal form, not size: "De rechtsvorm …" [existing paragraph unchanged from here].

Then the deadline paragraph and the publication-versus-preparation paragraph as they stand.

## 2. The chapter narrates its own exception, twice

**Quote**
> This chapter is the first in the book with no company in it. The case is a filing regime, because the useful evidence here is not what one business did but what the law obliges — and, more interestingly, whom it obliges nothing of at all.

**Lens** Rhythm and music (template seams).

**What's wrong** Move 2 opens with the book explaining its own method. The Reading note already
carries this ("This is the first chapter in the book anchored on a regime rather than a company, and
the departure is deliberate") — §6d says that is where it belongs, so the prose version is a cut.
The one clause worth keeping is the good one: "whom it obliges nothing of at all."

**Rewrite** Cut the paragraph. Its surviving clause moves into the reordered opening of finding 1,
where "excludes a great many readers of this book" becomes: "and its answer, more interestingly, is
that the law obliges nothing of a great many readers of this book."

## 3. The conclusion announces itself, and two consecutive paragraphs start on "Which"

**Quote**
> Which is why the public filing is not a management report and was never shaped to be one. […]
> Which leaves the actual conclusion of this chapter, and it is unwelcome: the instrument you need to run the business does not arrive by post, is not produced by your accountant as a by-product of compliance, and will not exist unless you build it.

**Lens** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong** "The actual conclusion of this chapter, and it is unwelcome" is the book grading its
own sentence — and by this point the sentence has already been delivered in the standfirst ("the
instrument you actually need does not arrive by post") and at the end of Move 1. Two "Which …"
paragraph openers in a row make the seam audible. The triad itself is the best sentence in the
chapter and should land once, unannounced.

**Rewrite**
> So the public filing is not a management report and was never shaped to be one. […]
>
> The instrument you need to run the business does not arrive by post, is not produced by your accountant as a by-product of compliance, and will not exist unless you build it.

And cut the standfirst's third sentence ("Either way the instrument you actually need does not
arrive by post.") so the line is new when it comes. The standfirst then ends on "you publish nothing
at all", which is the better hook anyway.

## 4. The prescription's caveat is stated three times; §6d allows one

**Quote**
> The legal argument ends here. What follows is a proposed starting instrument, generated by reasoning from the chapters around it and not established by anything above.

**Lens** Warmth without slack.

**What's wrong** The same limitation appears here, in Boundaries ("The evidence supports the negative
claim, not the prescription"), and in the owed block ("the three weekly numbers are reasoning, not
evidence"). The Boundaries version is the one that changes how the reader applies the advice, and
the owed block is the honest fallback the template requires; this third copy, at the door of the
most useful section, reads as the debt register talking. "Generated by reasoning from the chapters
around it" is register vocabulary, not a sentence anyone says.

**Rewrite** One clause, then get on with it:
> The law has nothing to say about what follows. For many owner-operated businesses a useful weekly set contains one liquidity measure, one committed-demand measure, and one operating quantity that leads revenue.

## 5. Boundaries cross-references the debt register by class

**Quote**
> That is the same class of debt as chapter five's alternative resignation standard, and recorded in the same place.

**Lens** Rhythm and music (apparatus surfacing).

**What's wrong** "Same class of debt … recorded in the same place" is CONTEXT.md's filing voice, and
the sentence sends the reader to another chapter's owed block for no gain. The preceding sentence
("no business in this book is shown adopting them and faring better") already says everything the
reader needs.

**Rewrite** Cut the sentence.

## 6. The last Monday item flinches to "people" at its sharpest moment

**Quote**
> One reading is a number; six is a direction, and deciding from week one is how people talk themselves out of businesses that were fine.

**Lens** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong** This is the one line in the Monday list that is about the reader's temperament
rather than their spreadsheet, and it is exactly here that "you" becomes "people". The generosity
the line is offering — you will be tempted to panic, and the panic will be wrong — only lands in
second person.

**Rewrite**
> One reading is a number; six is a direction, and deciding from week one is how you talk yourself out of a business that was fine.

## 7. Two section openings count their contents or clear their throat

**Quote**
> Three documents get confused with each other, and separating them is most of this chapter:

> Financial statements are less mysterious than their presentation suggests. There are three, and each has one primary question it exists to answer.

**Lens** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong** The first tells the reader what the chapter is going to do; the second is a
textbook's first sentence ("less mysterious than their presentation suggests" is a reassurance
aimed at nobody in particular). In both cases the good line is the next one.

**Rewrite**
> Three documents get confused with each other:

> There are three statements, and each exists to answer one question. Learning which question belongs to which is most of the literacy, and it takes about an afternoon.

(The second rewrite also absorbs the paragraph's existing closing sentence, so nothing is added.)

## 8. Move 1's best paragraph is built on "necessary / not sufficient"

**Quote**
> Understanding the annual accounts is necessary. They are a real document, often prepared with professional help, with numbers produced under defined accounting rules. What they are not is sufficient: they are late, aggregated across twelve months in which several different things were happening, and — in their published form especially — shaped to answer somebody else's question. Some months after the year has ended you receive a careful description of a period you can no longer do anything about.

**Lens** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong** The necessary/sufficient frame is a lecturer's, and "a real document" is a
defensive concession that nobody had challenged. The paragraph's last sentence — "a careful
description of a period you can no longer do anything about" — is the one a practitioner would
say, and it is buried under the frame. Also "twelve months in which several different things were
happening" is the vague quantifier of failure mode 5.

**Rewrite**
> The annual accounts are worth understanding. They are prepared with care, often with professional help, under rules that mean the numbers can be compared. They are also late, they average a good spring with a bad autumn into one figure, and — in their published form especially — they are shaped to answer somebody else's question. Some months after the year has ended you receive a careful description of a period you can no longer do anything about.

## 9. The book talks about itself in the middle of the cash measure

**Quote**
> Why cash and profit diverge is the next chapter's whole subject; this chapter asks only that you be looking at the right figure before that chapter explains it.

**Lens** Warmth without slack.

**What's wrong** Two chapter references in one sentence, and the second half is the book explaining
its own sequencing to the reader. The measure itself ("Not the bank balance alone, which flatters —
it contains VAT you are holding") is the warm, practitioner's line; this trails after it.

**Rewrite**
> Why cash and profit diverge is the next chapter's subject. For now, look at the right figure.

## Overall note

The chapter's single conclusion — the document you can get is not the one you need — is stated in
the standfirst, at the end of Move 1, at the end of Move 3 ("the actual conclusion of this
chapter"), and again in Boundaries. Because it is announced before Move 2 begins, the case has
nothing to reveal, and Move 2 reads as a statute walk-through. The one structural change that would
most improve the chapter is to let the reader make the discovery: cut the standfirst's third
sentence, reorder Move 2 to open on the downloaded filing with the P&L missing (finding 1), cut the
method paragraph that precedes it (finding 2), and let the "does not arrive by post" triad land once,
unannounced, at the end of Move 3 (finding 3). Everything needed is already on the page; the
change is order and omission, not new material.
