# Craft review — Chapter 02, "Talking to People Who Will Lie to You"

Scope: craft only; every figure assumed correct. Judged against CONTEXT.md §3 (template)
and §4 (voice). The baseline here is high — Move 1 is the best opening move in the book so
far, and "Everpix had built an efficient machine for finding the twelve per cent and was
reading its output as evidence about everybody else" is a closer most chapters would envy —
so these findings are ranked against the chapter's own standard.

---

## 1. The case has no one in it at the only moment that matters

**Quote:** "Six and a half months later, on 5 November 2013, the company announced it was
shutting down."

**Lens:** Story craft.

**What's wrong:** The survey comes back in April saying the founding premise is false, and
the story jumps straight to November. The one decision under uncertainty the case contains —
somebody looked at 14.8% and kept going — happens between two paragraphs, and the verdict
("the research they ran and did not act on") is delivered three paragraphs later as a
sourcing note, after the story has ended. The reader is shown the numbers arriving but never
shown anyone receiving them.

**Rewrite:** "Nothing in the record suggests the answers changed anything: the company kept
building and kept pitching, and six and a half months later, on 5 November 2013, announced
it was shutting down." (Both claims are already in the chapter — the anonymised investor
rejections, the Series A that did not arrive. Adds ~13 words; finding 6's cut pays for it.)
Stronger still, if the fact pipeline confirms it from the already-cited Verge article: name
the founder who was in the room when the surveys came back — third person, one surname, no
heroics, exactly as Swinmurn and Galperin are handled elsewhere.

---

## 2. Monday's five are not the chapter's five

**Quote:** "Write down the five things you believe about your customer — who they are, what
the problem costs them, what they use instead, who signs, what they already pay."

**Lens:** The reader in the chair.

**What's wrong:** The chapter builds five named extractions, and the first — past behaviour,
the date, the "single instruction" the whole method reduces to — is missing from this list;
"who they are" has taken its seat. Monday #3's script has the correct five, so the reader
who does the work meets two different lists a paragraph apart, and the one they fill in
first omits the extraction the chapter argues is primary.

**Rewrite:** "Write down the five things you believe about your customer — when they last
hit the problem, what they do instead, what the last incident cost, who signs, what they pay
today." (Same length; now mirrors the script in #3.)

---

## 3. "Loved it" is asserted; the thing they loved is never shown

**Quote:** "Everpix was a photo company" … "And the people who used it loved it."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear (and warmth rides on it).

**What's wrong:** The case gives the sector, the funding, the line count, and the platform
list — everything except what a subscriber actually saw. A writer who had watched the
product in use would spend one clause on it; without that clause "loved it" is a
summarizer's word, and the reader has nothing to weigh $40 a year against. It also costs the
chapter its most generous move: the tragedy lands harder if the thing was genuinely good.

**What to do:** One concrete clause where "a photo company" stands — what the product did
for a subscriber, drawn from the already-cited Verge piece and passed through the fact
pipeline (this is the one finding that needs a fact the chapter does not yet hold). Pay for
it by trimming "By every engineering measure it was a real product:" to "It was a real
product:".

---

## 4. The antithesis engine runs too often

**Quote:** "One thing this case is not: it is not at your scale." — one instance of a
pattern: "That is not a prediction about your idea. It is a prediction about conversations";
"This is not vanity; it is how self-report works"; "it is not information about the market,
it is information about the room"; "That is a real limit and not a loophole"; "Keep the
notes as a ledger, not a narrative."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music.

**What's wrong:** "Not X; Y" is the chapter's best move and it fires roughly fifteen times;
by the boundaries section the reader can hear the next one loading, and the cadence starts
to belong to the template rather than the material. The quoted instance is the weakest —
two negations stacked in nine words to announce one.

**Rewrite:** "This case is not at your scale." Then let two or three of the plainer
instances elsewhere go straight (keep "information about the room" — that one has earned
its shape).

---

## 5. The method's thesis sentence trips over its own compression

**Quote:** "There are five things to extract, and you want all five — each is survivable
alone, and none is sufficient alone."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (clarity).

**What's wrong:** "Each is survivable alone" literally says each item can be survived, when
the meaning is that a *missing* one can be. The "alone … alone" parallel was chosen for
music and costs a reread at the exact sentence the method rests on.

**Rewrite:** "There are five things to extract, and you want all five — you can survive
missing one, and no single one is enough."

---

## 6. The chapter narrates its own furniture

**Quote:** "Two further limits on what it proves are in the boundaries below."

**Lens:** Rhythm and music (template seams).

**What's wrong:** This is the scaffolding showing — prose pointing at the template's own
headings. The boundaries arrive two pages later regardless, and the announcement breaks a
paragraph that had just ended well ("a venture death rather than the kind this book is
mostly about"). The chapter's other pointer ("see the boundaries below for when that is
wrong") earns its place by qualifying a claim in the same breath; this one buys nothing.

**Rewrite:** Cut the sentence. Its eleven words fund finding 1.

---

## 7. "Median response time: 7.3 seconds" is dropped, not read

**Quote:** "Of 1,268 responses: yes 12.1%, no 72.0%, with 15.9% answering \"I don't take
digital photos.\" Median response time: 7.3 seconds."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** Every other figure in the case is taught — the side-by-side paragraph
exists to show the reader how to read a pair of numbers. This one arrives unglossed and
reads like residue from the source PDF. A practitioner would say what seven seconds means.

**Rewrite:** "Median response time: 7.3 seconds — nobody needed to think about it." Pay for
the six words by cutting "of any kind" from "no commitment of any kind" in the previous
paragraph.

---

## 8. Nobody reads a press tour

**Quote:** "People who arrived under their own steam — who had read one of the press tours,
gone looking for the site, installed an uploader and waited while it ingested their library
— converted at roughly five times the rate of people who merely clicked an advertisement
about photos."

**Lens:** The practitioner's ear.

**What's wrong:** You read the coverage a press tour produces, not the tour. A small slip,
but it sits inside the sentence doing the chapter's finest work — "the enthusiasm,
measured" — where a false note costs the most.

**Rewrite:** "who had read the coverage, gone looking for the site, installed an uploader
and waited while it ingested their library" — shorter.

---

## Overall note

The chapter is called "Talking to People" and its case contains no people — only a company
and its documents. The documents-as-witness approach earns real authority (the dataset
"describes, without flinching" is exactly right), but the pivotal beat — the surveys come
back, the premise is false, and the people who commissioned them keep going for six and a
half months — is currently implied by a sourcing note rather than staged inside the story.
Findings 1 and 3 are two symptoms of that one absence. Put a human in Move 2 at the moment
the numbers arrive, named if the pipeline can confirm the name from the Verge piece already
in the reading list, and the chapter's machinery — which is working — will finally have
someone for the reader to watch it work on.
