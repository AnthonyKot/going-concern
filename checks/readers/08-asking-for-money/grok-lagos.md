**1. Gut reaction after first read**

I almost bounced at ConvertKit, MRR, MailChimp, LeanPub, Trello, and a man who can drop $50,000 then fly to Thailand. That is not my shop. I sell clothes from a phone on Instagram and WhatsApp. Then the closed-tab line hit, because I live inside silent nos: “how much?”, then they vanish. I finished angry that the story was so American, and still nodding that I have been collecting ghosts and calling it research.

**2. What landed**

“Every closed tab is a no with no reason attached” and “the only apparatus you own that converts a no into a sentence.” I do not have tabs. I have people who view the story, ask the price in DM, and go quiet. Same wound. Asking is not this month’s rent; it is making them say the sentence.

“Word of mouth is not a magic lever… It is what having customers produces, not a way of getting them.” That one is for me. Everyone here says “just post, people will share.” Sharing happens after they have bought and looked good in it, not instead of me asking.

“If your buyer definition cannot be turned into a hundred names, it is still too wide.” I sell to “anyone who likes clothes.” That is not a Monday list. “Paleo recipe blogs run by women” is a joke in Lagos, but the test is not: can I sit down and write the names.

“If you cannot fill five lines, that is the finding.” I cannot. I have likes and “I’ll get back to you.” That is the finding.

And this, which almost no American book writes: “A reader whose floor depends on this month’s invoices is running a materially different experiment.” Someone finally said I am not Nathan Barry with books paying the bills.

**3. What didn't**

The whole middle is his SaaS recovery chart. “$1,207… $50,000… 100 personal emails… 5–7 customers.” I am not emailing Udemy sellers. My buyer is not switching from MailChimp. “Is anything frustrating you with MailChimp?” does not translate to a girl in Yaba asking if the two-piece is stretchy.

“Open a Trello board today… You are not too sophisticated for it; you are avoiding it.” That is a slap. I am on a phone. Data drops. I am not avoiding a kanban; I am running the shop in WhatsApp.

“Direct sales absolutely scales and anyone who says otherwise is just too lazy” — even with the author pushing back, I still had to sit through a founder whose growth stalled on holiday. My bottleneck is light, transfer delays, and a customer who will not pay until salary week.

“The asking generalises; the cadence does not.” True, and too late. By then I had already been talked to like a bootstrapped SaaS founder who skipped sales because his time is “too valuable for $49/month accounts.” That is not my excuse. Mine is: if I spend the evening writing a hundred cold notes, I am not packing orders, and this month’s food is the orders.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

First read I thought the book was another ConvertKit sermon. Second read I saw the author is arguing *with* Barry as much as using him: the vacation, the $50k, the two things changing at once, the missing counter-case, “the base rate on asking is unknown.” The point is not “email like Nathan.” It is: silent nos teach you nothing; a reason might. I still had to mine that out of someone else’s MRR.

**5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?**

Yes, because “What to Charge” is the chapter I actually need, but if nine is another SaaS spreadsheet I am done.

**6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change**

Do not make Monday “send twenty emails and open Trello.” Make the instrument the channel the reader already uses — for me, twenty WhatsApp or Instagram DMs asking what stopped them last time, and a notes list of the last five real reasons — and put that *before* the ConvertKit numbers, not after I have already decided this book is not for people like me.
