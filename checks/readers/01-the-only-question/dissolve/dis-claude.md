**2 — Iron law, Move 3 (limit 1: execution changes the number).**

BEFORE:
> The principle underneath all of this is usually called the iron law of the market, and it says that no quality of execution will save a business selling something nobody wants. That is true. Keep it.

AFTER:
> The principle underneath all of this is usually called the iron law of the market, and it says that no quality of execution will save a business selling something nobody wants. That is true, and it is much narrower than the version you will hear, because demand does not sit in the world as a fixed quantity waiting to be discovered. Repositioning an identical product for a different buyer can turn an indifferent market into an eager one; removing friction — a form that takes two minutes instead of twenty, delivery on Thursday instead of "within three weeks" — routinely converts people who wanted the thing in principle into people who buy it; naming a category can assemble a demand that was real but had no word for itself, and therefore no way to search for you. All of that is execution, and all of it changes the number. What execution cannot do is make people value something they fundamentally do not want. Keep that.

## Where each limit now lives

- **Limit 1 (execution changes the number)** — edit 2, the iron-law sentence. The limit is a qualification of exactly this claim ("no quality of execution will save…"), so it belongs in the breath that states it; "That is true, and it is much narrower…" replaces the flat "That is true. Keep it."
- **Limit 2 (awareness vs. manufactured preference, eighteen months)** — edit 3, "It disposes of a large category of doomed ideas at no cost." The disposal only works if the line is drawn correctly; the proviso is now the condition on the benefit rather than a separate warning.
- **Limit 3 (cannot start small)** — edit 6, the fixed-to-variable instruction. This is the sentence whose advice the limit modifies, and "does not soften; it hardens" reads as a case of the same instinct, not an exception announced from outside.
- **Limit 4 (one room, twelve-month lease)** — edit 5, "Most owners could not state that number." That sentence is already about owners at every scale; the small-lease case is its most common instance, and "less margin for being early" now points back to the early-market passage (edit 4), which precedes it.
- **Limit 5 + coda (real but early; solvent when it arrives)** — edit 4, the sentence explaining what the second clause protects against. The original paragraph itself says "This is why the second clause is stated in terms of your cost structure," so the clause's own explanation is the natural host; the solvency pair closes it as the reason the whole distinction matters. The Aftermath already carries the factual part (smartphones, labour market) but not the "indistinguishable from being wrong" force, so nothing was deleted as redundant.

## What the chapter loses

The staged "Three, and they matter" cadence — the reader no longer gets the reservations gathered in one place to count, and the early-market point now arrives before the consequences rather than as the closing note of the reasoning.
