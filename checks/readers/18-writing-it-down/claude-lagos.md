**1. Gut reaction after first read**

This one is not for me yet, but I could see it coming for me. I have one girl who helps me pack and reply DMs, and every single day she still calls me to ask "aunty, this customer says the gown didn't fit, should I refund?" — so the fifteen-minutes-versus-an-hour thing hit hard. But the whole chapter is one man with ten staff and a software company, and I kept waiting for someone who looks like me.

**2. What landed**

"A support process that would 'take me a couple of minutes to go through and respond' can, for a rep who is not a developer and has no view of the development queue, 'take close to an hour, and require my participation at each one of those decision points.'" That is my life exactly. I thought I was managing badly. It's not that — the answer just lives in my head.

"I'll delegate once it's properly documented is not a plan. It is a stopping condition with no way to fail it." I do this with everything, not just documents. Website "not ready", price list "not final". That sentence caught me.

The rep writing the entry herself — "Reps input a new entry every time they encounter a ticket" — is the only part I can actually do this week. My girl types faster than me on WhatsApp anyway. She can build it, not me.

And the procedure/judgment split is real. Knowing which supplier at Balogun will actually hold my order without full payment is not something I can write down. That's face and years. Good that the book says so instead of pretending.

**3. What didn't**

"Store it where a new person would actually look, not in a document only you have ever opened." Where? He says Google Drive earlier — with data at ₦-whatever per GB and NEPA gone half the day, my staff is not opening Drive. Everything here runs in WhatsApp. Nobody in this chapter has ever considered that the document might live in a pinned chat, and it makes the advice feel written from a desk with fibre.

"leave the room" — I don't have a room. We work from my mother's parlour and I'm sitting right there. The test as written assumes an office.

Also "an assistant, a contractor, a first hire, however you got there" — that "however you got there" is doing a lot of work. My "hire" is my cousin, unpaid-ish, who may leave next month for school. Documenting for her feels different from documenting for an FTE and the chapter never touches that.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

First read I thought the chapter was weak because the man never proves the documents worked — "he does not report a rep working from those SOPs." Second read I respected that. The book says it straight instead of selling me a system, and the "what would prove this chapter wrong" part actually names the danger: someone following the written rule "off the edge of it." That's the risk with my cousin and a refund. First read I skimmed it as filler. It's the most useful paragraph.

I also caught that Monday step 4 tells me to record two numbers before I fix the document. First read I only saw "write it down."

**5. Would I keep reading?**

Yes — chapter nineteen is my actual question, whether to hire at all.

**6. One concrete thing I'd change**

Cut the dependence on the office and the Drive. Say what the handoff test looks like when you can't leave the room and the document has to live where your staff already is — a pinned WhatsApp message, a voice note transcribed, a printed sheet. "Leave the room" should become "don't answer for one hour," which is the version I can actually run.
