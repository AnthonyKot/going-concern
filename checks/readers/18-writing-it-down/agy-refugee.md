### 1. Gut reaction after first read
At first, it sounded like just another tech founder talking about scaling up when I am simply trying to start out and survive. But underneath the software examples, it is actually a cold, practical look at the difference between doing a job and building a reliable system. It didn't try to inspire me or sell me a hustle-culture dream, which I deeply appreciated.

### 2. What landed
The line "Documentation is the comfortable kind of avoidance" really hit me. When you are terrified of losing money—like I am, with my limited savings and family depending on me—it is much easier to sit at my kitchen table rewriting a checklist than to let someone else handle a task and potentially upset a customer. Also, the strict separation between procedure ("a sequence a competent stranger could follow") and judgment makes perfect sense to my professional background in Ukraine; it removes the ego and emotion from handing over work.

### 3. What didn't
I felt completely excluded when the text shifted to the scale of the example: "growing Gymdesk from a one-person operation to a company with over 10 FTE." Ten full-time employees? I am sitting here in the Netherlands hoping I can eventually afford one part-time local student to help with Dutch customer service. The talk of "self-funded B2B SaaS" and a "development queue" feels alien when my risk tolerance is basically zero. It assumes a level of stability, capital, and market confidence that someone on a temporary protection visa simply does not have.

### 4. What changed between read one and read two
On the first read, I was annoyed by the heavy focus on the software startup, thinking the advice was too corporate for my situation. On the second read, I ignored the software dressing and saw that the core method—the "Monday" checklist—is completely practical and costs nothing. The instruction to have the person doing the work write the corrections in "before you touch it" is a brilliant, low-risk way to train a helper without hovering over them or wasting my own time.

### 5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes, because chapter nineteen is about "The First Hire," which is exactly the step I am most anxious about, provided it stays grounded in reality.

### 6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Swap out the Gymdesk software example for a traditional, boring physical business—like a local accounting firm, a retail shop, or an import business—so people without tech backgrounds can actually see how "procedure vs. judgment" applies to real-world tasks.
