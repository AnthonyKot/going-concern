### 1. Gut reaction after first read
This gave me a sharp spike of anxiety, but it felt grounding rather than overwhelming. Having had my life upended once, I am deeply afraid of hidden legal traps destroying the modest savings my family has here in the Netherlands. I was intensely relieved to find zero American-style hustle cheerleading, but my immediate dread was whether I can actually afford to fix these gaps.

### 2. What landed
* **The copyright default:** *"Paying the invoice transfers nothing."* Coming from an accounting/marketing background, I assumed payment implied transfer of rights. Learning that under the Dutch *Auteurswet* (Article 2, lid 3), hiring a freelance web designer on a tight budget leaves intellectual property with them unless transferred in writing is critical. It protects me from a catastrophic mistake before spending money I cannot afford to lose.
* **The timing of terms:** *"A link in the footer of the invoice, sent after the work was ordered, is the commonest way for a business to have terms it cannot hold anyone to."* This is practical, clear, and explains the Dutch *ter hand gesteld* rule (BW 6:234) without academic fluff. 
* **The tone on relationships:** *"The instinct that keeps them unwritten is not laziness. It is that writing them down feels like the moment the business stops being a relationship..."* The author understands why people avoid contracts without mocking them.

### 3. What didn't
* **The casual cost of legal advice:** *"Buy one hour. Take items 1 to 4 and the two agreements that carry the most money to a lawyer..."* In the Netherlands, an *advocaat* or specialized jurist easily charges €250–€350 per hour. When you live under temporary protection with finite savings and family obligations, telling me to "buy an hour" as a casual Monday task feels out of touch with low-capital, self-funded micro-businesses.
* **Raw Dutch statute barriers:** Dropping dense, archaic legal Dutch like *"tenzij tusschen partijen anders is overeengekomen"* without a parallel plain-English gloss. My Dutch is improving, but navigating formal statutory language on liability and IP while evaluating business viability felt alienating.

### 4. What changed between read one and read two
On read one, I felt paralyzed by the Dutch box and assumed I needed hundreds of euros for a lawyer immediately before doing anything. On read two, I noticed the filtering logic in *When to pay someone who knows*: an hour is only owed if an agreement *"carries more than a month's revenue, lasts longer than a year, contains the words unlimited..."* That distinction actually gave me permission to rely on carefully handed-over templates for small jobs and keep my cash intact.

### 5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes, because it respects my intelligence and treats business as risk containment rather than an optimistic fantasy.

### 6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Provide a two-line plain English paraphrase alongside the Dutch statute quotes, and suggest lower-cost entry points (such as KvK standard contract formats, legal expense insurance/*rechtsbijstand*, or jurist consults) before telling a fledgling founder to simply hire a lawyer.
