### 1. Gut reaction after first read
It gave me a proper jolt of panic about my café logo and lease before hitting me with a wall of Dutch legal text. It is blunt, refreshingly free of motivational TED-talk fluff, and understands why people rely on handshakes. But halfway through, I wondered why on earth a woman opening a café in the north of England was being handed pages of Dutch statute.

### 2. What landed
- *"The instinct that keeps them unwritten is not laziness. It is that writing them down feels like the moment the business stops being a relationship..."* That is 25 years of hospitality in a nutshell; you survive on goodwill with local suppliers and regulars, so putting legal paper between you feels distrustful.
- *"Paying the invoice transfers nothing."* This stopped me dead. I paid a local lad to design my café logo, window vinyls, and menu boards; I assumed paying the bank transfer meant I owned them outright.
- *"A professional's hour is owed when the agreement carries more than a month's revenue, lasts longer than a year, contains the words unlimited, indemnify or exclusive..."* When you are watching every penny of redundancy money and a bank loan, having a clear rule for when to spend £200 on a solicitor is invaluable.

### 3. What didn't
- The raw Dutch law box: *“Het auteursrecht is het uitsluitend recht van den maker van een werk...”* Being told to "skip to Move 3" if I'm not in the Netherlands felt lazy. If I bought this book in the UK, dumping foreign statutes on me feels alienating and irrelevant.
- The consultancy jargon: Calling sections *"Move 1"*, *"Move 2"*, and *"Move 3"* feels like business-school posturing.
- The lack of fit for counter-service trade: *“Move your terms from the invoice to the quote. Attach them to whatever the customer sees before they say yes...”* In a café, people buy a toastie and a coffee at the till. We don’t issue quotes or invoices to customers, so this advice completely ignores over-the-counter retail and hospitality.

### 4. What changed between read one and read two
On the first pass, the author’s stern tone made me feel reckless and behind on my paperwork. On the second read, I spotted the admission under *Note*: *“No company case: none was found with a primary document showing a cost that followed from an unwritten or undelivered agreement.”* The author sounds completely certain of impending doom, yet admits they have no documented real-world case of a small business actually ruined by this. The "Monday" action list is solid, but the theoretical scare-mongering is heavier than the evidence.

### 5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes, because despite the Dutch detour and office-job bias, it cuts through the usual romantic nonsense and tells me straight where my money is exposed before I open the doors.

### 6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Move the foreign statutory quotes into an appendix and replace them with plain-English examples that apply to physical, cash-over-the-counter businesses rather than just digital agencies and freelancers.
