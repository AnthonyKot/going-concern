**1. Gut reaction after first read**

Halfway through I realised this whole chapter is built on one American software company's blog post from 2019, and I kept waiting for someone like me to show up. The core idea — pick three numbers, hand the reading to someone else, write the definitions down first — is genuinely useful and I can do it tomorrow. But "MRR", "expansion", "net revenue churn", "quick ratio" is a language I don't speak, and I had to translate every paragraph before I could use it.

**2. What landed**

"A figure that needs you to explain it is one more thing that only you can do." That's it exactly. My sister helps me pack orders and if I told her "we did ₦400k last week" she'd nod and it would mean nothing. That sentence named a problem I've had for a year without a word for it.

"Losing five customers is one thing if they were the five smallest and another if one of them was the account that paid for the shop's rent." I have three or four repeat buyers who order in bulk for their own resale. If one of them goes quiet I feel it in the same week. Counting customers lost and money lost as two separate numbers is obvious now and I wasn't doing it.

"A lost customer is lost on the day you wrote down in advance — thirty days without a return, sixty ... and not on a later day chosen once the number is in." This is the best line in the chapter. I would absolutely have moved that line. I've told myself "she'll order again next month" about a customer who stopped in February.

The S-1 anecdote — "A number nobody had to falsify. They only had to define it." — made the whole target/measure argument land in one sentence.

**3. What didn't**

"Each November ConvertKit pushes annual plans, sold at a discount: net revenue that month climbs 'as high as $3 million'..." Three paragraphs of a business at a million dollars a month explaining a subscription-discount calendar. I don't sell subscriptions. Nobody I know sells subscriptions. The chapter admits this late — "a distinction a subscription business needs and most of this book's readers do not" — but only after making me read all of it.

"Hand the reading to someone else ... the person should be someone the numbers do not pay." I'm one person with a sister who packs boxes and a tailor I pay per piece. Everyone near my business is paid by my business. The chapter treats this as a rule, not a difficulty, and gives me nothing when I can't meet it.

Also: not one word about inflation. My prices went up 40% this year and my "money in" grew while I sold fewer pieces. A movement number that doesn't separate price from volume will lie to me every single month, and the chapter never considers that a figure can rise for reasons that have nothing to do with the business.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

Second read I noticed the honesty — the chapter says out loud that three is *this book's* compression, that Barry describes seven, and that the evidence "reports no decision made on them and no surprise they missed." That admission bought back a lot of trust. I also saw Move 3 does give me the non-subscription version — "new customers this week against returning ones" — I'd just skimmed past it because it's one sentence buried after a wall of churn vocabulary.

**5. Would I keep reading?**

Yes — the Monday list alone is worth the next chapter.

**6. One concrete thing to change**

Give the three numbers in my language first, before ConvertKit: new buyers vs. repeat buyers, customers lost and naira lost, share of the week's money from the top three names. Then use Barry to test them. Right now I have to earn the chapter before it pays me.
