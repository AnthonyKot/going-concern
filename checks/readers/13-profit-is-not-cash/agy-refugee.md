### 1. Gut reaction after first read
The distinction between profit and cash isn't new to me—I dealt with spreadsheets back in Kyiv—but seeing it framed around survival rather than getting rich made my stomach drop. The math of how a "good month" of growth can literally bankrupt you if you don't have the cash buffer is terrifying. It made me realize just how dangerous starting a small business here could be with my limited savings.

### 2. What landed
"Which is why businesses fail while growing, and why it surprises you: every indicator you were taught to watch improves right up to the week the payroll does not clear." This hit hard. I have zero safety net here in the Netherlands; my family relies on me, and I cannot afford a liquidity crisis disguised as success. 

I also loved the warning about Dutch law: "Quoting the Burgerlijk Wetboek at a large customer's accounts department is a way to be paid once and never engaged again." I deeply appreciate this pragmatic, grounded view. It respects the actual power dynamic I would face as a foreigner with a tiny business dealing with established Dutch corporates, rather than feeding me empty hustle optimism.

### 3. What didn't
The Instacart example lost me completely. "The 2022 profit includes a '$358 million tax benefit from the release of our valuation allowance...'" Even though the author later admits the scale doesn't transfer, making me wade through hundreds of millions of dollars in deferred tax benefits for an American tech giant felt completely alien to my reality. I am trying to figure out how to float a few thousand euros for supplies without risking my family's rent, so reading an SEC filing analysis felt like an academic exercise I didn't have the luxury for. 

### 4. What changed between read one and read two
On the first read, the "Monday" checklist just looked like standard accounting homework. On the second read, looking closely at my own tight margins, I saw it as a literal survival guide for someone with my low tolerance for financial risk. The instruction to "Separate the money that is not yours" regarding VAT made me realize I would need to physically move that money to a different bank account immediately, so I never trick myself into feeling richer than I actually am.

### 5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes, because the author treats business as a dangerous machine you have to operate carefully, not a magical dream you just have to believe in.

### 6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Shrink the Instacart SEC filing breakdown significantly and replace it with a detailed micro-business example of the cash gap, so readers like me don't have to mentally translate Silicon Valley finance down to a survival scale.
