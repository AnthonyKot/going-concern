## 2. Where each limit now lives

**Limit 1** is carried by the sentence introducing the 2019 report, at the moment the reader first meets the second denominator — the right host because that is the only point where a reader could silently start reading three numbers as one line. The "like-for-like" half is restated where the 2019–2020 comparison is actually made, so the licence and its limit sit on the same claim.

**Limit 2** is carried by the "artificial whatever happened to them" sentence in the Could Not Contact section, because that section is where the chapter praises the category and is therefore where the praise needs its ceiling: the number's value lies in what it prevents, not in what it contains. The "which these reports cannot establish" caveat already existed there and was doing part of this work.

**Limit 3** splits across two hosts: the case introduction, where the outcome is first called "unusually explicit" and now says why that unusualness matters, and the opening of "What this looks like with twenty customers," where the instruction is transferred to businesses whose outcome is not a job — the point at which a reader would otherwise assume the transfer is as easy as the case made it look.

**Limit 4** is carried by the sentence describing the CIRR standard, because the self-selection problem is a property of the coalition and belongs where the coalition is introduced; "itself" and "opted into" do the self-published work, and the industry disclaimer is attached directly to the thing that would tempt a reader to generalise.

## 3. What the chapter loses

The clean, enumerable ledger of reservations — a reader who wanted to audit the chapter's modesty in one place now has to find it in four.
