**1. Gut reaction after first read**

I almost bounced. Denver, a seven-month coding school, $75,000 salaries, a “Council on Integrity in Results Reporting” — that is not my Tuesday. I sell clothes from my phone. Then the quiet-customers bit landed and I slowed down, because that is my whole WhatsApp. Still felt like I was sitting in on someone else’s lecture and stealing one useful sentence.

**2. What landed**

“They do not complain. They go quiet, and quiet reads like contentment in every system you are likely to have built.” I have no CRM. I have chats. Silence is my default “they’re fine.” “Could Not Contact” as its own line — “It costs nothing but a column, and it is the only one that gets harder to fake as things get worse.” And: “Count from the population that paid, not the population that finished. Everything else is arithmetic performed on survivors.” I count the girls who posted themselves in my pieces, not the ones who paid, collected, and vanished. “Renewal is evidence worth investigating, not a verdict” — people reorder because they already have my number, not because the last dress did what they hoped.

**3. What didn’t**

The Turing block. “Median pay $75,000”; “Hired by school in-field 4.23% 5.63%.” I glazed. Another country, another product. “By [date], this customer will be able to [observable thing]” — for a two-piece I sold last week, what? She looks expensive at a wedding on Saturday? Then what goes in the column when I never see the photos? “Add five columns to the record from chapter ten” assumes I have a record. I have Instagram and a notebook. “Ask the five most recent leavers” — I don’t have leavers. I have people who stop answering, change numbers, or lose service. And you already know: “A school is an unusually measurable business… The instruction to measure the outcome survives that; the ease of it does not.” So why did I wade through three years of CIRR tables before you said that?

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

Second time I caught 2019 versus 2020: delivery looked better, jobs collapsed. That is my year, every year. I can sew, post, deliver, and still “fail” because naira moved or her money finished. “Delivery and outcome are separate variables” is the actual chapter; I missed it under the percentages. The missing counter-case is honest. It does not help me Monday.

**5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?**

Yes — but if chapter 12 is another American institution with a PDF, I will skim.

**6. One concrete thing I’d tell the author to change**

Put “What this looks like with twenty customers” and one non-job example — a dress, a repair, a food stall — *before* Turing, not after. I needed the outcome sentence for a product that is not a career.
