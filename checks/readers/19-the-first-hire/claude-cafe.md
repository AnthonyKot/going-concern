**1. Gut reaction after first read**

Right — this one's about me, or it will be in about four months. I've hired and fired for other people for twenty-five years, but never with my own redundancy money on the line, so the bit about the wage being the small part and the obligation being the large part is exactly the thing I've been circling round at three in the morning. It's clearly written and doesn't posture. But a third of it is Dutch law, and I'm in Yorkshire.

**2. What landed**

"The test is what happens if the person is not there on Monday. If a deliverable is late, you bought a task. If the day does not run, you have a job." That's the whole chapter in three lines and I could explain it to my sister. It's the kind of test you can actually run standing in a kitchen.

"a job wearing a task's contract" — I've watched that happen. Agency cover who's been on the same shift eighteen months and knows the regulars' orders. Nobody ever decided it; it just accreted.

"the hours are worth more the longer the same person does them" — that's the honest reason a good waitress is not interchangeable with a warm body from a list, and nobody's ever put it to me that plainly.

And this, which I've genuinely never had said out loud: "A hire funded by the growth it is supposed to produce is a job bought on a task's reasoning." I've seen two owners do exactly that and blame the person afterwards.

**3. What didn't**

The chapter is built on a software company. Gymdesk hires "a content marketer and editor" to "double down on the channel that already worked, organic search." I don't have a channel. I have a Saturday. The advice to "offer the job first to the person whose task work you already buy" assumes I've been buying tasks — I've been buying nothing, because I haven't opened. So Monday item 4 and half of Move 3 don't reach me at all, and the chapter doesn't say so.

Second: "Ask your accountant for the loaded monthly cost of one employee." I don't have an accountant yet, and the sentence assumes I do without a word about what that costs or how to find one.

Third, the Dutch box. Fair enough to have it, but I got told at the top "If you are not hiring in the Netherlands, skip to Monday" — and then Monday itself is threaded with Dutch again ("on Dutch ground the box's presumption"), so I can't skip cleanly. It felt like being shown out of a room and then talked to through the door.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

First time I read the Dutch material as clutter. Second time I clocked the three-months-weekly-or-twenty-hours-a-month presumption and thought: something like that will exist here, and I don't know what it is — which is a more useful thing to have learned than the Dutch article number. Also, second time I noticed Move 1 is nearly all setup and could go; the chapter properly starts at "Hold the arithmetic."

What held up on re-read was the Monday list, apart from item 4. Item 5 in particular — "the date and the number by which you would know it was wrong" — is a discipline I've never applied to a person and probably should.

**5. Would I keep reading?**

Yes — chapter twenty is the difficult conversation and that's the part I'm worst at, so it's the one I actually want.

**6. One concrete thing I'd change**

Give me the version where I haven't opened yet and there's nobody to convert. Two paragraphs: how you run the task-or-job test on work that hasn't happened, and what you do when hiring a stranger for a job nobody's done, in a business with no trading history to check the low month against. That's most first hires, and right now the chapter's only answer is "read chapter twenty."
