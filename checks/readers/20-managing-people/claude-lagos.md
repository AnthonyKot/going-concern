# Reader report — Chapter 20, "Managing People"

## 1. Gut reaction after first read
The opening got me — "you have started arranging your week so that you do not have to say so" is exactly what I do when a girl I pay to package orders keeps mixing up sizes and I just start doing the packing myself at night. Then about a third of the way in it became a Dutch law textbook and I stopped being the reader. I have one part-time person, no contract, no lawyer, no calendar with dates in it, and by the end I felt like the book had walked out of my shop and into an office in Amsterdam.

## 2. What landed
"The month is not a plan. It is what an owner does when the decision has been made and the conversation has not, and it feels like fairness — they deserve more time — while actually being the opposite." That is me with my current girl, three times over. Nobody has ever named that thing for me.

"A date set in advance moves the decision off your willpower and onto the calendar." I have plenty of willpower for suppliers and none for people I like. Moving it to a date is something I can actually do on WhatsApp today, free.

"If you cannot write down what is not working in terms of the work — what was asked, what was done, what it cost — you do not yet have a fit problem. You have a feeling." Useful, because here I hire from church, from cousins, from people my mother knows. "Fit" in my world is 90% feeling and it is how people get cheated.

The note that the owner sees the new hire least, so ask the person who sits next to them — good, though I have nobody sitting next to anybody.

## 3. What didn't
The box. "On Dutch ground · ending an employment" — then twelve paragraphs of Dutch statute I cannot read, quoted untranslated: *"De werkgever kan de arbeidsovereenkomst niet rechtsgeldig opzeggen…"* It says skip to Monday if I am not employing in the Netherlands, and I did, but it is a third of the chapter. That is a third of a book I am reading on my phone data that is not for me. And then Monday itself keeps dragging it back — "on Dutch ground call the lawyer before you say it," "in the words they gave you." Three of the five Monday items have a Dutch tail on them.

"€ 102.000,00" severance cap. I laughed. My whole business turned over less than that since I started.

Groove is a software company with a team and a helpdesk product, paying a stranger for a "2-4 week paid trial run." Paying someone for four weeks to find out if they fit is a rich man's tool. Here, the trial *is* the job, and if I pay for four weeks and say no, that money is gone and my stock did not move.

## 4. What changed between read one and read two
Second read I saw that the transferable thing is not the trial, it is the sentence "the end of the trial acts as a forcing function." A date costs nothing. I can put a date on the person I already have without paying anything extra — the chapter even says to, four weeks out. First read I dismissed the whole Move 2 as American software money; second read I found the one part that is free.

I also noticed the "What would prove this chapter wrong" section is honest in a way I did not expect — it admits the evidence only shows one side, people kept too long, and that nobody writes up the person they let go too early. That raised my trust in the author more than any of the advice did.

## 5. Would I keep reading?
Yes — for the Move 1 and Move 3 writing, and I will scroll past the boxes the way I scroll past ads.

## 6. One concrete thing I'd change
Move the Dutch box out of the chapter body — to the back of the book or a separate page — and in its place put two or three sentences on what a founder does where there is no employment law protecting either of you, or the law exists and nobody follows it: what the date and the written notes are worth when there is no court behind them. Right now the chapter's answer to "what does ending an employment take" is entirely Dutch, and most of the people reading this on a phone are not.
