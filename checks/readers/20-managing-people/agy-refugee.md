### 1. Gut reaction after first read
As someone terrified of losing my limited savings on a bad business mistake, this chapter gave me anxiety, but also a sense of relief. It confirmed my fear that hiring people in the Netherlands is a massive financial risk if you don't know the rules. However, I appreciate that it treats management not as an American motivational speech, but as a rigid calendar problem.

### 2. What landed 
The observation that giving someone another month "feels like fairness... while actually being the opposite" hit me hard. Back in Ukraine, I managed a small marketing team and often kept underperforming people too long because I didn't want to be the bad guy. Now, with family obligations here and zero safety net, I cannot afford that. I also loved the rule to write down "what was asked, what was done, what it cost." It appeals to my professional background—feelings are useless, but a paper trail is survival. 

### 3. What didn't 
The Alex Turnbull story from "Groove" felt like the exact American tech-bubble hustle I distrust. He brags that "a trial is not a guaranteed job, but a paid consulting gig." That sounds easy for a software startup in 2015. But for me, trying to start a modest, traditional business here, treating a local worker as a "consultant" to avoid giving them a contract sounds like a fast way to get fined for fake freelancing (*schijnzelfstandigheid*). Those tech-bro anecdotes exclude people building normal businesses on tight margins where you can't just play games with labor laws.

### 4. What changed between read one and read two
On the first read, I skimmed the "On Dutch ground" box because the legal Dutch ("*De werkgever kan de arbeidsovereenkomst niet rechtsgeldig opzeggen...*") was intimidating. It reminded me of the stressful government letters I constantly have to translate. On the second read, I forced myself through it and realized that this box completely overrides the American CEO's advice. The text even admits that Groove's trial "is not this," meaning the entire inspirational case study is legally irrelevant to my actual situation in the Netherlands. The law requires a real chance to improve and strict documentation, not just a casual four-week gig ending.

### 5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes, but mostly because the author actually includes the harsh local Dutch reality instead of just leaving me with Silicon Valley fantasies.

### 6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Move the warning that the American "consulting gig" trial does not apply to Dutch employment law to the very beginning of the Groove story, so immigrant readers don't spend five paragraphs taking notes on a strategy that could get us sued.
