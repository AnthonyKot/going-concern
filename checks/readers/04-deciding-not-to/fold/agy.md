### 1. Where they should go and why

**Boundary 2 (Evidence vs. Calendar)**
* **Where:** In "Move 3 The reasoning," under "2. A date," immediately following the sentence: *"Expect to want to move it. That wanting is data."*
* **Why:** When the author imposes a strict calendar limit and asserts that wanting to push the date back is negative "data," the thoughtful reader immediately worries: "But what if I just procrastinated and didn't do the customer interviews in time? Am I supposed to kill the business?" The boundary steps in at this exact moment of friction to validate the reader's fear—clarifying that running out of time without running the test measures your calendar, not the market. 

**Boundary 1 (Business vs. Operator)**
* **Where:** In "Move 3 The reasoning," under "3. What failing it means," immediately following the sentence: *"... because the stakes of the rule become total, and nobody wants to pre-commit to demolishing something they love."*
* **Why:** The reader naturally resists a binary "stop" condition because they know a failure might just reflect their own lack of experience (e.g., poor sales skills) rather than a bad product idea. Placing the boundary here voices the reader's exact anxiety, which perfectly sets up the logic of the next paragraph: because the rule cannot distinguish the business from the operator, the "honest menu" of consequences must be wider than just stopping entirely.
