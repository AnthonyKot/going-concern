**1. Placement**

**Boundary 1 (rule can't tell business from operator)** — goes at the end of component 2, immediately after *"Expect to want to move it. That wanting is data."* This is the sentence that provokes the objection: a reader who has just been told their wish to move the date is a symptom will think, correctly, "but sometimes I would be right to move it — I have not learned to sell yet." The boundary is the honest answer to that thought, and it lands here rather than later because the failure it describes is one that happens *on the date*. It also sets up component 3, where the wider menu (cut scope, return to paid work, continue slowly) is the only practical response to a number that cannot say which thing failed.

**Boundary 2 (evidence gathered, not evidence available)** — goes at the end of "Setting the number", after *"Neither number is right; both are honest, and both can be failed."* The two invented rules are the first moment the reader holds a concrete threshold, and the developer's rule in particular invites the question "what if 30 June arrives and he simply never sent a proposal?" That is exactly the Draelos failure — a year after choosing a customer, no customer spoken to — and it is felt as soon as a real date and a real N are on the page, not before.
