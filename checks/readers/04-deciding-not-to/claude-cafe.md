**1. Gut reaction after first read**

I felt got at, which I suppose means it was working. I've told half of Hexham I'm opening this café, and the bit about "some portion of your sense of yourself as a person who finishes things" being in the room when you reassess — that's true and I didn't want it to be. But most of the chapter is about a doctor building an app in America, and I had to keep doing the translation myself.

**2. What landed**

"You are going to be a worse judge of this project later than you are today." Plain, and not a TED talk. I've watched three owners over 25 years pour good money after bad because they'd already spent the bad, and none of them was stupid.

"Enough revenue is not a number; it is a threshold he could argue himself past." That's exactly what I'd have written. "If it's doing all right by Christmas." I know what "all right" means when it's me marking my own homework.

The consequence menu — "mothball it and keep the domain… or go back to a salary and continue at reduced speed." I'd assumed the only two options were café or failure, and the Gymdesk fella going back to work four days a week and keeping the business alive on the side gave me something I could actually picture. Nobody's ever said that to me as a legitimate outcome.

The crossed-out list in Monday — "draw a line through every item that is not money from a stranger." I've already got a list like that in my head: the woman from the WI who said she'd bring the group in, the landlord saying it's a lovely spot. Lines through all of it.

**3. What didn't**

The Cydoc case is long and it's not my world. "HIPAA-compliant platform on web, Android and iOS," "EHR integration," "converted to a Delaware corporation" — I read that list and thought, this is somebody else's book. I understood the point eventually (she had loads of proof and none of it was customers paying enough), but I'd have got it from half the words.

"Money that has cleared, from customers who could comfortably have said no." Fine for an app. In a café the strangers pay £3.20 for a flat white on day one or they don't — I'm not waiting eighteen months for a stranger to pay. So what's my number? Covers per week? Takings against the loan repayment? The chapter gave me the shape of a rule and not one example that looks like a shop with a door.

"Two claims live in that sentence and they are not equally supported, so take them apart." Then it takes them apart, and then it does it again in the Gymdesk box in nearly the same words. I felt like I'd been told twice because the author was arguing with himself.

**4. What changed between reads**

First time I skimmed the Boundaries as small print. Second time it was the most useful bit: "the rule cannot distinguish" between the market not wanting it and me not yet knowing how to sell. I've never done the selling side — I've run the floor. That's the bit that frightened me properly, and it's tucked at the bottom.

I also noticed the "counter-case this chapter does not have" — the author admitting he couldn't find someone who honoured the rule and was wrong. First read I thought that was odd; second read I trusted him more for it.

**5. Would I keep reading?**

Yes, mainly because chapter 5 is "Leaving Well" and I'm about to hand in my notice.

**6. One thing to change**

Give me a second worked rule beside the doctor's — a physical business with a lease — with the number filled in, so I can see what "strangers paying" looks like when they walk through the door every day and the question is whether there are enough of them.
