**2. Where the limits now live**

**(A)** is now the second half of “Neither number is right; both are honest, and both can be failed.” That sentence is the chapter’s claim about what it means for a well-formed rule to return a miss; the limit belongs there because the conflation of market and operator is not a comment on how to set N, but on what that miss cannot be read as.

**(B)** is now the unpacking of “The date is what converts an open-ended endeavour into a bounded experiment.” That sentence is the claim that the date turns the work into a test of the market; the limit belongs there because an unrun test leaves you with a date and no market result, which is exactly the conversion the sentence is asserting.

**3. What the chapter loses**

It loses the instruction to reread the whole argument through two named constraints, and the pause that made those constraints apply to the principle as such rather than to the date and the number in the places they are specified.
