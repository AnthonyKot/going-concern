**Edits**

**1 — Delete the Boundaries section**

BEFORE:
```
Boundaries
Two, and they are serious enough that the rest of the chapter should be read through them.
A stopping rule measures the business and the operator at the same time, and cannot
tell them apart. When the date arrives and the revenue is not there, you are looking at a
single number produced by two different things: whether the market wants this, and whether you
have yet learned the parts of the job you did not know at the start — usually sales and
distribution. The rule cannot distinguish them. It will read the second as the first.
The rule is only as good as the evidence you gathered, not the evidence available.
Draelos spent an additional year after deciding who her customer was before she spoke to one:
"An entire additional year elapsed before I talked to my first real potential customers."
A threshold you fail because you never ran the test tells you nothing about the market. It tells
you about your calendar, and stopping on it would have been the wrong conclusion drawn
from the right rule.
```
AFTER: *(deleted entirely)*

**2 — "2. A date": the date bounds an experiment only if the experiment is run (carries B)**

BEFORE:
```
Fixed in advance, in the calendar, not "when I have had a proper go at it." The date is what
```

AFTER:
```
Fixed in advance, in the calendar, not "when I have had a proper go at it." The date is what
converts an open-ended endeavour into a bounded experiment — provided the experiment is actually
run in the time it bounds. Draelos decided who her customer was and then did not speak to one:
"An entire additional year elapsed before I talked to my first real potential customers." A
threshold missed because the test was never run measures the calendar, not the market, and
stopping on it would be the wrong conclusion drawn from the right rule. The date is also the
part your future self will most want to renegotiate. Expect to want to move it. That wanting is data.
```
