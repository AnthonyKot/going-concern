1. Gut reaction after first read
I almost bounced at the MD/PhD, HIPAA, Delaware, VCs, two patents. That is not my life. I sell clothes from my phone. Then the sentence about becoming a worse judge later pulled me back, because I already do that: I restock after “people said they like it” and the money is still not in my account. The chapter is long and American, but the fear it is describing is mine.

2. What landed
“You are going to be a worse judge of this project later than you are today.” That is the whole thing. By the time I have paid a tailor, posted for weeks, and told my friends I am “doing fashion,” stopping feels like I am a person who quits.

“A metric denominated in money from independent customers” and “could comfortably have said no.” My cousin buying two dresses is not a market. Instagram likes are not a market. “I’ll buy when I get paid” is not a market.

The Monday template: “if fewer than [N] strangers have paid me [amount] by [date], I will [specific action].” I can write that tonight. Also: list everything encouraging, then draw a line through anything that is not money. I already know that list would be long.

Cydoc’s arithmetic — “$30 above hosting” against “$4,000 to $6,000” per integration, “11 years per practice” — is the first time a book has shown me why “customers exist” can still mean the business is dead. “Ask them to buy it before you build it” is the line I would screenshot.

3. What didn't
The Cydoc block is too long. Patents, NC IDEA, 35 VC firms, EHR. I started skimming. You told me this book is about self-funded businesses, then spent the chapter on someone who raised $265,000.

“Return to a salary and continue slowly” and Gymdesk’s four-day week. I do not have a job that lets me keep Fridays for inventory. If this fails I am back to hustling, not a salary with benefits.

“Mothball it and keep the domain.” I do not have a domain. I have an Instagram and a WhatsApp broadcast.

“Strangers.” In Lagos a lot of real buyers find me through people they know. You later say independence is not unfamiliarity, but the template still says strangers, so I am not sure I am allowed to count the customer who came from a friend’s story.

A naira number and a date in six months is not the same number. Inflation will eat the threshold while I am “honouring” it.

“Put the date in your calendar now, with the sentence pasted into the invitation.” That is a person with a laptop calendar. I live in WhatsApp.

“If you cannot fill every slot, you do not have a rule, and the missing slot is the one you are avoiding.” True, but it sounds like a teacher.

4. What changed between read one and read two
First read I thought: this is for people who can afford to run an experiment for years. Second read I saw you already admit Cydoc took outside money, that her post-mortem is weaker evidence, and that you have no case of someone who followed the full rule, missed it, stopped, and was wrong. That honesty made me trust you more than the story did.

Second read the Boundaries section mattered more than Cydoc. The rule cannot tell whether the market is dead or I still cannot sell. That is my actual risk: I am still learning how to get people to pay, not how to sew.

I also noticed the menu is the useful part, not “stop.” Cut the line that is not moving. That I can do.

5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes — “Leaving Well” is the chapter I actually need, if it is about my kind of leaving and not another seven-year shutdown.

6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Cut Cydoc by half and put a small phone-and-inventory business next to it — someone who kept restocking on compliments, never wrote a money-and-date rule, and either stopped or cut scope — and rewrite the template so the customer test is “paid, could have said no,” not “stranger,” with the amount in a unit that still means something when prices move.
