## Recurring across chapters

- **Tech/SaaS or outsize cases exclude non-tech readers** — chapters 13, 14, 16–23, and 25–28. Refugee, café, and Lagos repeatedly have to translate Instacart, Cydoc, Pinboard, Gymdesk, ConvertKit, WP Curve, Studio Neat, Binpress, or large-company figures into local services, retail, hospitality, or phone-based trade. Dev often shares the scale/evidence objection, though software mechanisms themselves are legible to him.
- **Dutch boxes divide rather than simply localise** — chapters 13, 15, 19, 20, 24, and 28. Refugee sometimes treats Dutch rules as concrete protection or reassurance; café, Lagos, and dev repeatedly experience untranslated statutes as interruption or exclusion. Even “skip” instructions fail when the Monday list imports the Dutch rule again.
- **Stable records, currency, and infrastructure are assumed** — chapters 13b, 14–16, 19, 21–23, and 27. Lagos repeatedly says annual comparisons, fixed contribution, labour rates, cash figures, and review cadences collapse under inflation, devaluation, power failures, or seasonal revenue; café and new-business readers separately lack the requested history.
- **The caveat is both evidence and noise** — chapters 13b, 16–23, and 25–28. Readers often gain trust when the author says a case proves only a feeling, sequence, or decision; the same readers sometimes conclude that repeated qualifications leave an overlong case proving too little.
- **Office instruments exclude informal and counter-service work** — chapters 13b, 14, 17–19, and 21–24. Calendars, invoices, named customers, accountants, Google Drive, transferable business accounts, and authorised second people do not map cleanly to cafés, WhatsApp shops, family helpers, or unregistered solo firms.
- **Cross-chapter shorthand becomes a reading burden** — chapters 21, 23, and 25–28. Café and dev most consistently flag chapter-number callbacks; refugee joins in chapters 27–28, where the accumulated private vocabulary obscures the immediate test.
