**1. Gut reaction after first read**

I almost closed it at Bingo Card Creator. Another American software man with Google, AdWords, and $3,000 for freelancers in 2008, while I am selling clothes from a phone in Lagos. Then “if I stop this month, what do I still have in March?” landed, because that is Instagram versus my WhatsApp numbers — and I felt seen for a second before the chapter went back to 777,000 visits and trial signups.

**2. What landed**

“One accumulates into something you own; the other stops the moment the payment stops.” That is a boosted post versus the numbers in my phone.

“You do not have enough attempts to test five things. You have enough for two.” I do not have a budget to “test channels.” I have data, airtime, and hours.

Question two: “Can you reach them without a gatekeeper’s permission?” Instagram has switched people off. Meta is Google in this story. The correction that even ranking is “gatekept more politely” is the first time a book has said out loud what happens when the algorithm buries you.

“It costs me about 24 cents to get a trial… but only 2 cents to remind someone that their account still exists.” I do not have cents like that, but I know broadcasting to people who already bought is cheaper than hunting strangers.

“A channel that grows usually makes your ratios worse.” When I post more, more window-shoppers appear. I have treated that as failure.

The boundary that “a business selling to four hundred logistics firms in the Benelux has no long tail” at least admits bingo-for-teachers does not travel.

**3. What didn’t**

The whole McKenzie case. “Hello World attached to a random number generator,” $24.95, 546k visits, “virtual postage stamps.” I do not have AdWords, trials, or primary-school teachers typing into Google. My buyers find me on IG and close on WhatsApp.

“$3,000 of freelancer writing” is serious money here, and I am not commissioning SEO articles.

“What makes one forward something to a colleague” assumes an office. My girl forwards a dress to her friend in the group chat.

“Write the date six months out… judge whether you got better, not whether it paid.” Inflation does not wait six months. I need naira this month.

Appointment Reminder is a second software lecture. “The counter-case this chapter does not have” plus the source list felt like a researcher talking to other researchers, not to me.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

First read I heard “don’t buy ads, do SEO.” Second read I caught that they say the tactic is closed, and that of his three channels only the email list “passed question two outright.” Also that he left AdWords on autopilot instead of killing it — that is closer to how I actually live: boost when I can, live in DMs. I missed the falling conversion rate on the first pass; I would have called that a bad week.

**5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?**

Yes, but if chapter 8 is another American SaaS spreadsheet I will skim.

**6. One concrete thing I’d tell the author to change**

Put a case next to McKenzie of someone selling physical goods through a rented app and a chat list they actually own, and do the two-cent arithmetic in airtime, data, and boost budget — not AdWords.
