### 1. Gut reaction after first read
I felt a mix of relief and anxiety. Relief because the author doesn’t sell a dream; it’s just cold math about survival, which I respect after hearing too much toxic positivity about "being your own boss." But anxiety hit me hard when I saw the Dutch tax rules—it made starting a business here feel like navigating a minefield when I already have so little room for financial error. 

### 2. What landed
*"The mistake is to ask what you are worth — unanswerable, and slightly demoralising. Ask what the business would have to pay to get the work done."*
This hit home. Coming from Ukraine with a solid professional career, getting rejected for local jobs because my credentials supposedly don't translate has been deeply demoralizing. Detaching my personal worth from the market rate of the tasks feels like a practical, grounding way to approach a new business. Also, the advice to set a hard date on subsidizing the business with unpaid labor works perfectly for me. With my family obligations and temporary status, I cannot afford a "financed period" that never ends.

### 3. What didn't
Where I felt excluded was the section on Dutch tax law for incorporated companies: *"ten minste € 58.000 in 2026... There are routes to a lower figure and they run on evidence, not on assertion."*
As someone who would start as a simple *eenmanszaak* just to test the waters, reading about a mandatory €58,000 salary requirement was terrifying. Even though the author clarifies the difference between legal forms, the heavy focus on this corporate rule made me feel like this book is written for well-funded founders, not someone bootstrapping just to feed their kids. I got bogged down in the Dutch legalese and felt dismissed by the instruction to just take it to a *belastingadviseur*—those advisors cost money I simply don't have.

### 4. What changed between read one and read two
On the first read, I panicked that the Dutch government would force me to pay myself a massive salary I wouldn't have. On the second read, my old accounting background kicked in, and I absorbed what the author explicitly states: as an *eenmanszaak*, this doesn't apply to me (*"Trading in your own name... there is no salary and no minimum"*). I finally understood that the author is using the strict corporate rule to illustrate the discipline all owners should apply in their private calculations, not as a literal threat to my specific situation.

### 5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes, because the next chapter is called "Enough," and figuring out exactly what is enough for my family's survival without taking on disastrous risk is my only priority.

### 6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Move the dense Dutch tax law box out of the main flow and into an appendix; dropping it into the middle of the chapter derails the practical logic of pricing your own labor with legal jargon that will unnecessarily scare off small-scale beginners.
