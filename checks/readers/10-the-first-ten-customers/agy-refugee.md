**1. Gut reaction after first read**
At first, I braced myself for another tech-bro manifesto about "scaling" and passive income, which always feels exhausting and out of touch with my reality. But the focus on measuring actual delivery rather than dreaming about massive market size appealed to my former accounting background. It felt grounded and pragmatic, even if the case study itself felt a world away from my current life in Rotterdam.

**2. What landed**
The dismantling of the "viral growth" myth resonated deeply with my absolute allergy to American-style hustle optimism. When the author quotes, *"no virality involved. We have to work for every single customer,"* I felt a sense of relief; hard, unglamorous work is something I understand and trust. I also appreciated the shift in perspective: *"What the first customers can expose is not the market but the machinery."* For someone with very low risk tolerance and limited savings, testing if a process simply works without bankrupting or exhausting me is a much safer, more logical first step than trying to predict a whole market. 

**3. What didn't**
I felt completely excluded when the author discussed the virtue of refusing custom projects to protect standardisation: *"We have turned down tens of thousands of dollars worth of one off work."* When you have family obligations, limited savings, and temporary protection status, turning away real cash to protect a "clean process" is an unimaginable luxury. I also sighed at the admission that the founders weren't starting cold, but had *"established bloggers and podcasters who linked to them."* I am an immigrant building from absolute zero in a second language; I don't have a network of mentors waiting to launch me, making their timeline feel like a fairy tale.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**
On the first read, the WP Curve numbers (MRR, $69 subscriptions, software metrics) distracted me, making me assume this framework only applies to digital startups. On the second read, I noticed how the author strips away the founders' glamour to reveal the raw mechanics. The instruction to *"Recalculate the sale at actual delivery cost. Value the hours... add the other direct costs, and compare the result with the price"* is just rigorous, honest cost-accounting. I realized the underlying framework applies perfectly even if I start a simple local bookkeeping or consulting business. 

**5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?**
Yes, because the author promises to finally address "whether the revenue becomes enough cash, soon enough, to keep the business alive," which is practically my only real concern.

**6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change**
Replace or supplement the WP Curve story with an example of a traditional, unglamorous service business started by someone with zero pre-existing audience or safety net, to prove these principles work for normal people who can't afford to turn down "tens of thousands of dollars."
