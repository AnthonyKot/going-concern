1. Gut reaction after first read

I finished this and felt both seen and left out. The five questions are the first time a book has told me not to treat my ten Instagram buyers like “the market,” which is exactly the trap I fall into after a good week. Then it spent most of the chapter on a 2013 WordPress subscription company with MRR tables, agencies, and “tens of thousands” turned down, and I started skimming because that is not my shop.

2. What landed

“They are not a small sample of the next thousand. They are a test of the process that produced them.” That is the line. My first ten are cousins, church people, and DMs from people who already follow me. Treating them as proof I can scale to a thousand is how I over-order fabric.

“stop reading them as a sample at all” and “Ten is a review point, not an evidentiary threshold” — useful, because the usual warning is just “early customers are friends, ignore them,” which leaves me with nothing.

The five questions, especially “How much unpriced custom work appeared” and “Could you repeat the same sale and delivery next month within your available hours.” Custom work for me is altering a dress, extra photos, delivering to a compound myself. Hours are NEPA, data, and one phone.

“Asking finds the buyers who say no. Delivering finds the buyers who said yes and should not have.” I have sold to people who wanted a look I cannot restock, then spent a week hunting the same fabric.

“Repeatability is not something you discover about the first ten customers; it is something you protect by declining the eleventh when it is shaped differently.” Turning down a one-off that pays more than a repeatable order is the hard one when rent is due.

“if you are starting without [mentors and audiences], expect the same process to run slower rather than to fail.” Finally someone said the Australian blogger-with-a-list is not the default.

3. What didn't

The WP Curve ladder — May $476 through December $9,812, 178 customers, “fell $188 short” of ten thousand — is a story for people who already think in MRR. I sell wrappers and two-piece sets. Recurring $69 unlimited jobs is not my world, and I got bored in the table.

“We have turned down tens of thousands of dollars worth of one off work.” At $8,371 MRR. I cannot romanticise refusing cash. Inflation here means money today is not money next month. The author knows the cost is “paid immediately, in cash,” then still treats the refusal as the sharp lesson. Easy to write from a subscription base.

“with VAT kept separate” in the Part II checklist. We have VAT; my customers pay transfer, cash, or POS. That line is written for a UK/EU invoice.

“Do not start a fifth document. Extend the record… one row per customer… Blank cells are missing instrumentation.” I run this from WhatsApp and a notes app. Spreadsheet-as-operating-record assumes a desk, power, and that I will write hours at “replacement cost.” It started to feel like homework for a founder with a laptop.

The counter-case — “Somewhere there is a business that did this and destroyed itself; its absence from the record is the usual absence” — is honest, then the chapter still tells me to act when a pattern is visible. Thin.

4. What changed between read one and read two

First read I thought the whole chapter was “don’t scale yet, study WP Curve.” Second read the case is mostly a warning about wrong buyers (agencies, then bloggers) and that delivery, not sales calls, shows who should not have bought. The Monday list is the actual chapter; the revenue story is illustration, and a mismatched one. I also noticed they admit the first-week “seven customers” is their own division, the numbers mix products, and there is a hole in the series. The caution is better than the example.

5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?

Yes — “What Happens After the Sale” is the question I actually live with, if it stays this concrete and drops the SaaS scoreboard.

6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change

Replace or pair WP Curve with a cash, non-recurring, phone-and-chat business — a tailor, a reseller, someone who cannot decline “tens of thousands” — and write the five questions against that, or I will keep translating every number into naira and losing the plot.
