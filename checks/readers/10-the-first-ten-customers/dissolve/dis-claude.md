**Where each limit now lives**

1. *Not ten / ten not sufficient* — the sentence opening "They found out twice." That is the claim the limit qualifies most directly: the chapter's evidence for "inspect the first customers" comes from fifty conversations and 150-plus customers, so the reader meets the scale gap the moment the two discoveries are introduced, before either is argued from. The Reading note already carries the arithmetic of "about seven"; the reservation about acting on it did not exist elsewhere.

2. *Company-wide revenue* — the sentence that introduces the ladder. The ladder is the only place the reader might mistake the totals for WP Curve's, so the caveat sits on the numbers' own doorstep, and "shape is all the ladder can give" turns the limit into the reading instruction the chapter already intended. The "$476 is Informly alone" detail was already carried by Move 2's first sentence and the "If Informly held steady" division, so it is not repeated.

3. *The gap* — the sentence recording the decision to stop publishing. It is the same fact; the limit is only the inference the reader should draw from it, and putting it there means the ladder that follows is read with the hole already visible.

4. *Not starting cold* — the "effort can just as easily be reputation" sentence. That sentence lists reputation as an abstract possibility; naming the mentors and audiences makes it concrete for this case, and the "slower rather than fail" clause lands where the reader is being told what the "no virality" finding is worth.

5. *Interested party* — the sentence that introduces Norris's most self-serving quotation. "Explicit and unromantic" is exactly where a reader would extend him credit, so the reminder that he is still the seller belongs there rather than in the Reading list, where the Norris-book bullet already carries the contemporaneous-vs-retrospective distinction.
