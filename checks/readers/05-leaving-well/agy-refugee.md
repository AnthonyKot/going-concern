**1. Gut reaction after first read**
I was relieved this wasn't another American "believe in yourself and jump" motivational speech. The cold, calculated approach to risk feels very safe, which I need right now because my family depends on me and my savings are tiny. However, it also assumes a level of professional stability and comfort that I simply do not have anymore. 

**2. What landed — specific passages and why they worked for someone like me**
"The salary is not the enemy here. It is the cheapest funding you will ever have access to." This reframed everything for me. I have been resenting the work I do here in the Netherlands because it completely ignores my marketing career back in Kyiv, but seeing this job as free funding makes it tolerable. I also deeply appreciated the "On Dutch ground" section. Seeing the exact legal reality—that resigning means "you should plan on the basis that it carries no safety net" regarding WW (unemployment) benefits—is exactly the no-nonsense truth I need while living under temporary protection. 

**3. What didn't — where I felt excluded**
The opening paragraph completely excluded me: "You have a mortgage, possibly people who depend on you, and fifteen years of being good at something that pays reliably." My reliable career was erased in 2022. I rent a small apartment, and I am starting from zero, not from a comfortable fifteen-year foundation. I also rolled my eyes at the "Bingo Card Creator" example. A tech guy making $125 an hour while sleeping feels like a fantasy, completely disconnected from the reality of the small service business I am trying to build.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**
On the first read, I was so annoyed by the Japanese software guy example that I dismissed it as tech-bro privilege. On the second read, I realized I missed a crucial distinction. The author explicitly writes: "Others do not compound at all on part-time attention. A consultancy is the clearest example." I saw that the author wasn't just blindly praising tech businesses, but explaining the raw mechanics of part-time time. This made me realize I need to drastically rethink how my own service business will actually function if I can only give it evenings and weekends. 

**5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?**
Yes, because the author demands hard, written evidence over emotional bravery, which is the exact rigorous way I need to treat my limited money.

**6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change**
Rewrite "The situation" in Move 1 so it doesn't only speak to comfortable, middle-class corporate workers who are bored with their success; acknowledge that some of your readers are starting businesses out of displacement, immigration, and sheer necessity.
