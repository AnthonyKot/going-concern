**1. Gut reaction after first read**

I almost closed it. Half the chapter is Dutch company law I will never file, with untranslated quotes from a statute that does not apply to me, and I am tired of books that do this in a different accent. The other half is “stop running on the bank balance and a feeling of busy,” which is exactly how I run Instagram and WhatsApp, so I stayed, annoyed.

**2. What landed**

“The obvious failure is to read none of them and run on the bank balance and a sense of whether things feel busy.” That is me: Opay looks okay, DMs are coming, I call it a good week.

“Not the bank balance alone, which flatters — it contains VAT you are holding, payroll taxes, and customer deposits that were never yours.” Swap VAT for money I still owe the tailor or the fabric guy, and for naira that is already worth less than last month. The flattery is real.

“The instrument you need… will not exist unless you build it.” Nobody is posting me a report. If I do not put three numbers in my Notes app, they do not exist.

“Deliberately not units made — production without orders builds inventory, consumes cash, and makes the dashboard look healthier as the position gets worse.” I have ordered pieces because the page looked dead, then sat on stock.

“A spreadsheet is sufficient and a dashboard is procrastination.” I work from a phone. That line saved me from a fantasy app.

“Record six comparable weeks before reading a trend — and act at once on an absolute.” One bad week is Lagos. Empty cash before restock or data is not a trend. Handle it today.

**3. What didn't**

I glazed at “Download the filed accounts of any small bv” and the whole KVK / *eenmanszaak* / Burgerlijk Wetboek stretch, including Dutch I am expected to treat as evidence. You even say “The regime described here is Dutch… this book has checked none of them.” Then you still built the chapter on it. That is the American-advice problem with a different flag.

“Signed, not proposed, not verbally agreed.” My “committed demand” is a WhatsApp voice note. People ghost. I do not have contracts.

“Trace five lines back… with your bookkeeper.” “Your own accountant, once.” I do not have either. My books are the bank app and the chat.

“Do not try to build a balance sheet from your bank statements.” That is the only document I have.

A “recurring half hour, same day each week” assumes light, data, and a quiet Monday. Inflation is not in the three views at all. Cash that sits is a loss here.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

First read: not for me, skip. Second: the useful bit is short — three statements, three weekly numbers, annual filings are late and not yours — and it is buried under a filing regime I will never meet. The honesty that the three numbers are “reasoning, not evidence” and that a counter-case “must be common” made me trust the author more than the Dutch box did. I also noticed the next chapter is cash versus profit, which is the chapter I actually need.

**5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?**

Yes, for “Profit Is Not Cash,” not because this one earned another hour of Dutch statute.

**6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change**

Lead with the three statements and the three weekly numbers, and put Dutch law in the box as one country’s example that published accounts are not management information. Write the Monday list for someone with a phone, a bank app, and no accountant — WhatsApp orders, supplier credit, and cash that is rotting in inflation — or I will keep assuming the book is not for me.
