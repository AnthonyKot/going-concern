1. Gut reaction after first read
I sat up at the dressmaker on a phone, because that is my shop, not a side note. Then they spent a long stretch explaining why people like me leave no evidence, which felt like being let in so they could say the book was never really about me. The Monday list is the first homework I could actually do tonight on WhatsApp. I also got tired of company names I have never heard of.

2. What landed
“Your supplier is paid before your customer is, your prices move with the supplier’s” — that is my week. “A screenshot of a transfer is not money until it clears; ‘I’ll take it’ in a chat is… worth zero” is the sentence I would screenshot for every girl who says “hold it for me.” “Cash that sits is not standing still” and rebuild the floor “the day the supplier’s price moves” fit Lagos better than any dollar dashboard I have been sold. “Stock bought on a quiet page is the way this trade dies while its shelves look full” is how I burned money on a restock that looked like progress. “Take paid work of any kind and keep the stall on Saturdays” is the only stopping rule I have ever been offered that does not pretend I have a salary waiting. “The boosted post, which stops the day the money does” and airtime versus messaging someone who already paid — that is Instagram, not Silicon Valley.

3. What didn't
Opening with “None of the businesses in this book is one of them” and calling this an interlude, Chapter 13½, made me feel like leftover. Webvan, Instacart, Everpix, ConvertKit, “Galperin’s retreat,” Trello — I do not know these people and I do not care. “The seven o’clock full English you do not serve” is a British café talking over a Lekki DM. “Faces seen before, against the same week last year” is useless here: last year’s naira is a different country, and I may not even have a year. “A number that is rebuilt weekly is still a number, which is more than most owners of this kind of business can state” talked down to me. “No figure in this chapter is a claim about the world” then why should I bet my restock on it? Asking me to keep a year of sales and send it in, unpaid, while admitting my trade is “the harder test” the book did not run, felt extractive. I sell from a phone. I do not have a stall lease. I resell as much as I sew. The café and the tailor kept being the real example; I was the translation.

4. What changed between read one and read two
First read I was grateful someone finally described the chat. Second read I noticed how much of the chapter is apology and method, not instruction, and that “same week last year” and six weeks of waiting collapse under inflation. The honesty about no case impressed me more the second time — they did not invent a fake success story — but it also made the advice feel thinner. The Monday tasks looked more usable once I ignored the café.

5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes — “What a Customer Is Worth” is the question my Instagram already asks me every night — but I will skim anything that needs a filing or a salary.

6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Do not write my trade as a translation of a high-street café. Make the phone-and-transfer shop the example, drop last-year comparisons, and put the unit floor in a tiny worked week (this week’s fabric, data, generator, what I must take home) instead of telling me it “does not scale.”
