**1. Gut reaction after first read**

Well, this is the first chapter of the thing that's actually about me, and it opens by telling me the rest of the book wasn't. I appreciated the honesty and I was a bit narked at the same time — thirteen chapters in and now you tell me. But it reads like someone who's stood behind a counter, not someone who's read about one, and that's rare.

**2. What landed**

"Your supplier is paid before your customer is" — that's the whole job in nine words. I've watched three restaurants go under on exactly that and none of the owners could have said it that plainly.

"Stock bought on a quiet page is the way this trade dies while its shelves look full." I've seen the walk-in stacked with a fortnight of dairy on a wet Tuesday. That line I'd put on the wall.

The floor in units. I don't want to know I need £2,100 a week; I want to know I need to sell 340 coffees and 90 lunches. That I can count on a Saturday and feel it in my stomach by two o'clock. And "keep the old number beside the new one" — that's the bit I wouldn't have thought of.

"A screenshot of a transfer is not money until it clears" and "'I'll take it' in a chat... worth zero." Twenty-five years of people saying they'll book the function room.

The three you turn away as menu, price and hours. "The seven o'clock full English you do not serve" — I've already had that argument with my sister. Nice to have it framed as a decision that buys something rather than me being awkward.

**3. What didn't**

"Move 1 The situation / Move 2 What actually happened / Move 3 The reasoning" — the scaffolding is clanky and I'm being asked to care about the book's method more than my café. Move 2 is a page and a half explaining why there's nothing in Move 2. "This is the one chapter in the book whose case is an absence, and the absence is the finding" — that's the sort of sentence that gets said on a stage with a headset mic.

"Read against the same week last year" — I don't have a last year. Nobody opening does. The chapter's for people starting, and then it hands me an instrument that needs a year of history.

Webvan, Everpix, Cydoc, Turing, Galperin's retreat — I'm told I'm the reader now and then half the paragraph is names I'd have to go back to look up. "The version of Galperin's retreat available to someone who never had a desk" — who?

"The two-cent arithmetic is airtime" — lost me entirely.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

First time, the "no salary to return to" bit felt like it was talking about someone else. Second time I clocked it's me — redundancy money and a loan, nothing to go back to — and "run them smaller and write the stopping rule first" landed harder. I also noticed "the minimum you take home" is quietly inside the floor number, which I'd have left out and then wondered why I was skint. And on the second read the Monday list is the actual chapter; the rest is a preface to it. I'd start there next time.

**5. Would I keep reading?**

Yes, but mostly because the Monday list is short enough to actually do and I want to see if chapter fourteen is as straight.

**6. One thing I'd change**

Cut Move 2 to a paragraph and put the Monday list first. Tell me "there's no case, it's you, here's the five things" and then explain why afterwards for whoever cares. And give the "same week last year" line an alternative for someone in week one — because that's who this chapter says it's for.
