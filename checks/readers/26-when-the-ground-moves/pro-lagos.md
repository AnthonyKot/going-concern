### 1. Gut reaction after first read
It sounds like a smart American talking about a tech company with a financial cushion I can only dream of. The core idea makes sense—don't panic, figure out what my customers can still afford—but the example of a software guy taking months to adapt feels like a completely different universe. When the economy crashes here in Lagos, it crashes today, and I don't have months of cash runway to sit back and observe.

### 2. What landed
The idea of looking at what the customer is going through instead of just my own panic. *"The shock is not yours... It is something that happened to your customers, and it reaches you through them."* This hit hard. When fuel prices tripled recently, people stopped buying my two-piece sets not because my clothes got worse, but because transport ate their disposable income. The advice to ask what they *now* need—which for me meant pushing cheaper loungewear instead of expensive party dresses—is exactly how I survive inflation spikes.

### 3. What didn't
The luxury of waiting to react. I got irritated here: *"The cut, if it comes, comes on that date on the reading, not in week one on the fear."* My brother, if my dispatch rider doubles his fee on Monday because of fuel scarcity, I have to adjust my prices or cut my own costs by Monday afternoon. I don't have a *"bank balance less what is held for the tax authority"* that covers me for months. I have the working capital sitting in my OPay account. I also felt excluded by the subscription-business framing; offering a *"pause — dated, cheap, easy to reverse"* means nothing to me. You can't pause buying a dress; you just ghost my WhatsApp.

### 4. What changed between read one and read two
On the first read, I was just annoyed by the Gymdesk example because it felt entirely disconnected from hustling physical goods on IG. On the second read, I forced myself to strip away the software jargon and realized the underlying framework is actually solid. I just had to mentally translate "build a new software feature" into "source a more affordable fabric," and "pause an account" into "keep checking in on them via WhatsApp status even if they aren't buying this month to keep the relationship alive."

### 5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?
Yes, because once you translate the Silicon Valley speak, the logic underneath is cold, clear, and completely free of the usual motivational nonsense.

### 6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change
Stop relying solely on a B2B subscription software example; give me just one paragraph showing how this framework applies to a transactional, cash-based business selling physical goods, because right now it assumes every reader has recurring revenue and months of runway.
