**1. Gut reaction after first read**

I almost put my phone down in the middle of the Plausible numbers. The opening slap — a positioning line that costs nothing is not positioning — felt like it was for me, then the chapter spent itself on MRR, Hacker News, open source, and a Google Analytics fight two people in Europe picked in 2020. I kept waiting for a shop, a stall, a WhatsApp broadcast. It never came. I finished irritated and a bit talked past.

**2. What landed**

“Name the customers it turns away — and say what refusing them buys you.” That is the first sentence I could use tomorrow. I answer every DM because money is money; this made the cost of that visible.

“The cost is absent from the spreadsheet because it is not a cost in money. It is the loss of every tiebreaker you had.” I do not have a spreadsheet. I have a notes app and a POS balance. Still: when I sew for the auntie who wants asoebi *and* the girl who wants streetwear, I restock neither properly.

“Strike out every adjective… no competitor would claim the opposite of.” Quality. Affordable. Unique. That is every clothing page in Lagos. What survives might be nothing yet — that line hurt in a useful way.

“Twenty-three percent a month is not stagnation, but at $178 MRR it adds forty-one dollars, and forty-one dollars feels like nothing.” I have called a month stagnant because 80k naira does not feel like a business. The feeling versus the rate — I needed that.

“It brings that famous spike of hope after which follows the flat line of nope.” That is a celebrity story tag. That is one viral reel.

**3. What didn't**

The whole of Move 2. Uku, Indie Hackers, $64 MRR, “degoogle a website,” first-party cookies. I sell clothes from a phone. I do not have bugs, funnels, or a licensing moat.

“Which bug first. Which feature next.” You assumed a product queue. Mine is: which size to reorder before the dollar moves, and whether NEPA stays on long enough to shoot.

“Open source gives up the licensing moat and actively invites the customer not to pay… at $178 MRR, which is the point at which giving revenue away is least affordable and easiest to do.” Easy to romanticise from dollars. I cannot give revenue away. Fuel, data, restock — inflation eats the refusal.

“This is narrowing by value, not by industry. Most readers here will narrow on who the customer is — one trade, one size of company, one country. Different manoeuvre, different failure mode, not tested here.” So you knew I was the usual reader, and you still built the chapter on the untested case.

**4. What changed between read one and read two**

First read I thought you were another American book using a SaaS fairy tale. Second read I saw you warning me: the fight does not generalise, positioning was one of five changes, the source is one co-founder’s blog, “the debt stands.” That honesty is rare. I also noticed the Monday test and the incumbent question — for me the incumbent is Shein, the Computer Village woman, or just not buying. The frame held. The example still did not.

**5. Would I keep reading the next chapter?**

Yes, because you said chapter seven is where the message has to stand every Tuesday, and that is actually my problem.

**6. One concrete thing I'd tell the author to change**

Do not let the only worked example be a privacy-first open-source analytics tool. Keep the refusal test, but run it on a person with inventory, a phone, and customers who pay in cash — who I stop answering, and what that refusal is supposed to buy me this month, not at $1M ARR.
