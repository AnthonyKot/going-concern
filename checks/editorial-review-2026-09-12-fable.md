# Close editorial review: chapters 4, 19 and 24

Reviewed 12 September 2026 by Fable, at the author's request, as a companion to the same-day review of chapters 9, 16 and 28 (`editorial-review-2026-09-12.md`). Different sample on purpose: chapter 4 is the chapter the bookshelf review named as the book's best piece; chapter 19 is a Dutch chapter the reader panel hit hardest; chapter 24 is the chapter with no company case, which fell under the word floor this morning when its Dutch section stopped counting as body text.

I read all three chapters in full, the cached sources they rest on (`draelos-2026`, `galperin-2022`, `galperin-2023-covid`) against the sentences that use them, the case-ledger entries for Cydoc and Gymdesk, and the §8 history of chapter 4. The Dutch statute was not re-verified; review.sh has done that twice for chapter 24 and once for 19, and it is the lawyer's subject, not mine. Two plain defects found on the way were fixed and are logged in CONTEXT §8; everything else here is for the author.

## Main finding

The companion review's diagnosis holds in this sample too, and I can locate where it happens. The book's own §8 names the failure mode: *the chapter adding words the source lacks and reordering its sentences into causation*. In all three chapters the addition happens at one seam — the last paragraph of Move 2 and the first of Move 3, where the case has to hand over to the instrument. The claim checker cannot see this seam, because the quotations on either side of it are accurate. What changes is the connective: "then she did the arithmetic", "a hire funded by the growth it is supposed to produce", "every jurisdiction has a version of the same test". Each is the instrument speaking in the case's voice.

The good news is that the fix is local. None of these chapters needs reconstruction. Chapter 19 is the strongest of the three and close to done; chapter 4 needs two sentences changed and one omission restored; chapter 24 needs its generalisations scoped and its floor waived, not filled.

## Chapter 4: Deciding Not To

**Judgment: the reputation is earned by Move 1 and the first half of Move 3. The case has been tightened a notch past what the source says.**

### Distinctive — keep the premise and the list device

[Line 47](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:47): "You are going to be a worse judge of this project later than you are today." The whole chapter is a consequence of that sentence, and it is stated before any apparatus. [Lines 145–150](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:145) give the chapter's real idea — a stopping rule "written in a currency that the project can pay in without ever becoming a business" — and [lines 152–158](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:152) do the thing the book does best: a patent is evidence of novelty, a study of efficacy, a grant of fundability, each "correctly answering a question you did not need answered yet". The instruction at [line 124](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:124), "Now look at the list again", is the best single move in the three chapters I read. Preserve all of it.

### Unsupported — "it took seven years to ask" is not what the source shows

[Lines 135–136](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:135): "Thirty dollars a month against a four-thousand-dollar integration answered the third, and it took seven years to ask." [Line 94](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:94): "Then she did the arithmetic that the seven years of milestones had never required her to do."

The cached post gives a different shape. The $70-per-doctor hosting cost is dated: "Now that we'd added the AI scribe, the hosting and AI costs for our platform had gone up to about $70 per doctor." Before the scribe the contribution per doctor was not thirty dollars, so the arithmetic the chapter says went unasked for seven years did not have these inputs for most of them. And the arithmetic was not a decision to sit down and compute; it followed a specific event the chapter omits — an urgent-care customer walking away from what she calls "an imaginary $5,000/month contract" ("I felt punched in the gut … Even worse, I realized our business model was broken"). The source also names "a major personal health event during this period" among the causes of the shutdown. None of this contradicts the chapter's argument. It contradicts the *tempo* the chapter gives the case: a founder who never asked, rather than one whose costs changed late and whose model broke when a contract did.

Suggested repair, two sentences. At line 94: "Then a customer she had been building a custom product for walked away, and she did the arithmetic on what was left — with costs that had only recently risen to the level she quotes." At line 136, replace "and it took seven years to ask" with "and the question was not asked until the costs had already moved against her." Add the health event to the evidence paragraph at [line 117](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:117), where the chapter already concedes what makes Cydoc atypical.

### Overstructured — "exactly three components"

[Line 160](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:160): "a usable stopping rule has exactly three components, and the third is the one everybody omits." The three are good. "Exactly" and "everybody" are the provocations-as-laws pattern §4 warns against, and the chapter itself gives a fourth component two sections later — the experiment actually being run in the time the date bounds ([lines 186–191](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:186)), which is what saves the developer at line 226 from failing a rule he never tested. Either fold that into the list as the condition on the date, or drop "exactly".

### The Gymdesk limit is slightly unfair to its subject

[Line 249](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:249): "Enough revenue is not a number; it is a threshold he could argue himself past." His sentence was "reach enough revenue within the 6 months runway I had built to continue working on it without taking on outside work." That is a definable quantity — the revenue that replaces outside work — and it was met by neither reading. The chapter's point survives (no number was written, no consequence was), but "not a number" overstates the vagueness in order to make the case fit "about one and a half of three". Say "not written as a number".

### Repetitive — the cases are made to certify the rule twice

The Gymdesk block ([lines 259–263](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:259)) and the owed block ([lines 269–272](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/04-deciding-not-to.html:269)) both explain what Gymdesk does and does not prove. The §8 history shows this block went through four passes; the residue is two paragraphs of boundary. Keep the first, which is precise ("Writing it earlier would not have made him righter, only made the rule easier to honour"), and let the owed block state only what is owed.

**Revision direction:** two sentences at the Move 2/Move 3 seam, the health event restored, "exactly" dropped, one Gymdesk boundary paragraph instead of two. Nothing structural.

## Chapter 19: The First Hire

**Judgment: the best-built of the three. One duplicated quotation (fixed), one insinuation at the seam, one reading of the law that is a gloss and should say so.**

### Distinctive — the test and the obligation

[Lines 127–131](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:127): "The test is not the hours, the contract, or the invoice, all of which can be arranged to say what you would prefer. The test is what happens if the person is not there on Monday." [Lines 143–145](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:143): "a job wearing a task's contract … most of a job's dependence without any of its commitment." [Lines 162–164](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:162): "The wage is the small part of the cost. The obligation is the large part." These are the chapter, and the case supports them directly — Galperin's own sentence about being "responsible to make sure they are paid every month" is exactly the obligation the chapter names, and the chapter says so without adding to it.

Monday item 3 ([line 202](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:202)) is the model Monday item in this sample: a figure to obtain, what to set it against, and what to do if it fails — with the escape ("a reserve you are choosing to spend, borrowing, a smaller role, or continuing to buy the task") and a date so the threshold "does not become a reason to wait indefinitely". The companion review asked chapter 9 for exactly this and it is already here.

### Fixed — a sentence quoted twice in a row

[Lines 89–92](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:89) quoted "were never going to understand our audience like I did" in full and then again in the next sentence. The second instance is now "His own reason is about the audience, not the output", and the argument that follows is unchanged. Logged in §8.

### Unsupported — the seam insinuates that Gymdesk hired on hope

[Lines 169–172](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:169): "Gymdesk made its first hire in the middle of 2021, on revenue that had ended the previous year near $19.5k a month after churning through a shutdown of its whole market. A hire funded by the growth it is supposed to produce is a job bought on a task's reasoning."

Nothing in the post says the June 2021 hire was funded by expected growth. The surrounding sentences in the source are "we were doing pretty well", "no real need for the capital", and an owner who was "pretty optimistic" at $19.5k a month for a one-person business. The chapter makes no false claim; it places a warning directly after a fact so that the fact appears to illustrate it. Either say plainly that the record does not show what the hire was funded from and that the chapter's low-month test was not run on it, or move the warning away from the case. The first is better, because it is true and costs one clause.

### The law is characterised, and the section says it is only quoted

[Lines 250–252](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:250): "A presumption is not a reclassification — it can be rebutted — but it moves the burden: past that threshold, whoever wants the arrangement to be something other than employment is the one who has to show it." That is a correct description of a rebuttable presumption, and it is the most useful sentence in the section. It is also not a quotation, and the checked line says "The statute and the enforcement statement are quoted, not paraphrased." §8 already recorded the ch. 15 lesson — quoting correctly is not characterising correctly, and the connective tissue in a Dutch section is unsourced prose wearing the statute's credibility. Mark this sentence as the book's gloss, or cite the source that says it.

### Overstructured — the owed block's asymmetry is asserted

[Lines 219–220](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/19-the-first-hire.html:219): "this chapter's test is cheap to run and its errors are visible within a quarter". Chapter 20, next door, says a trial can judge fit but not speed, and that a competent hire is slower for a while by design. A wrong first hire is not reliably visible in a quarter, and the chapter has no case in which it was. Cut the clause or qualify it; the asymmetry does not need it.

**Revision direction:** one clause at the seam, one gloss labelled, one assertion cut. The chapter is otherwise ready.

## Chapter 24: What You Signed

**Judgment: an honest chapter with no case, which says so. Its problem is not length but reach — it generalises from the one jurisdiction it checked. Waive the floor; scope the claims.**

### Distinctive — the situation and the reversal

[Line 52](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:52): "Nothing has gone wrong. That is the situation, and it is the only time this chapter is cheap." [Lines 119–120](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:119): "Writing the agreement down does not make the relationship a contract. It was one already; writing it down makes it yours." [Lines 96–97](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:96): "every one of them was drafted by a professional, and that the professional was not yours." These earn their compression. The four-agreements map is a real contribution — most readers will not have thought of the fourth — and Monday items 3 and 4 (terms from the invoice to the quote; the largest-loss sentence with a number) are concrete enough to fail at.

The owed block ([lines 164–171](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:164)) is exemplary and should be the model for the book's other debts: it names both the case and the counter-case it lacks, in the shape each would have to take.

### Unsupported — "every jurisdiction", "everywhere this book knows of", "commonest"

[Line 80](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:80): "Everywhere this book knows of, the default starts with the person who made the work." [Lines 86–88](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:86): "every jurisdiction has a version of the same test: did the customer have a chance to see them before the deal, or only after?" [Line 95](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:95): "every one is written in the other side's favour, because they had a lawyer and you had a form." [Lines 88–90](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:88): "the commonest way for a business to have terms it cannot hold anyone to." [Lines 145–147](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:145): "What does not differ is the order … the world last, because it is the least likely and the largest."

The Reading note and the Dutch section both say the book checked one jurisdiction. Each of these sentences claims more. "Everywhere this book knows of" is honest only if the book says where that is; "every jurisdiction" and "commonest" are empirical claims with no source; "least likely" is a frequency the chapter has no data for; and the order is the author's proposal, presented as invariant. This is the same reach the companion review found in chapter 28's "the only line that changes is whose money it was" — which was my sentence, written this morning, and which I have now corrected for the same reason.

Suggested scope, in place: "In the one jurisdiction this book has checked, and in the others the author knows, the default starts with the maker"; "jurisdictions this book knows of ask a version of the same question"; "a common way"; "the order this book proposes: … the world last, because it is the largest." Drop "every one is written in the other side's favour" or make it "drafted for the other side", which is what the next sentence actually supports.

### The template fights the chapter

"Move 2 — What actually happened" heads a section in which, by its own first sentence, nothing happened. The chapter handles this as well as the template allows, by opening with the admission. But the reader who has learned the four moves arrives expecting a company and a date and gets a taxonomy. Two options: keep the heading and add a kicker line under it, as the case blocks have ("The default, in place of a case"); or let this chapter, chapter 12 and chapter 15 — the three regime-anchored chapters — share a variant heading, recorded in CONTEXT §3. The second is the more honest, since the template's own note says "if the case is thin the chapter is thin", and a chapter with no case should not pretend to the heading.

### The word floor

At 1,843 body words without its Dutch section, the chapter is under the 2,000 floor. Do not fill it. Every candidate for expansion is a generalisation of the kind the section above asks to remove, and §6f already set the precedent for the riskiest chapters: say less, quote more. Waive the floor by decision in §9, with the reason that the chapter has no case and should not act as if it had. If a documented case of an unwritten agreement costing a small business money is ever found, the floor takes care of itself.

### Minor — rules of thumb stated as owed

[Lines 133–137](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/24-what-you-signed.html:133): "A professional's hour is owed when the agreement carries more than a month's revenue, lasts longer than a year, contains the words *unlimited*, *indemnify* or *exclusive*…" The Reading note calls these rules of thumb. The prose says "is owed". One phrase in place — "as a rule of thumb" — closes the gap without a second note.

**Revision direction:** scope five sentences; decide the Move 2 heading for the three regime chapters; waive the floor with a reason; leave the argument alone.

## Where I agree with the companion review, and where I would add

I checked its two evidential claims that mattered most. On chapter 28, "the timeline now is Sept. 2014" is a marker in a retrospective, not a deadline the founders set; "Binpress had the date in the record and did not use it" reads as the second and should say the first. On chapter 16, a search of the four cached Pinboard posts for any stated sufficiency target or goal finds none; I did not read the posts end to end, but the burden is on the chapter, and "the number was named" needs a sentence in the source or it needs to go.

What I would add to its "why this pattern recurs": the checks are aimed at quotations and the failure is at seams. `verify.sh` proves each quoted string is in its source; `review.sh` reads against a checklist of known failure modes; neither is asked the question a close reader asks, which is whether the sentence *between* two accurate quotations is the source's or the author's. A cheap addition to the review checklist: for every chapter, list the connective sentences at the Move 2/Move 3 boundary and ask of each, "which sentence in the source licenses this?" The three findings above that matter — Cydoc's tempo, Gymdesk's funding, the jurisdictions — would all have been caught by that question and by nothing the pipeline currently asks.

For the first revision in this sample, choose chapter 4. It is the chapter people will be sent first, its repairs are two sentences and a restored omission, and the difference between "a founder who never asked" and "a founder whose costs moved late" is exactly the kind of accuracy the book claims as its reason to exist.
