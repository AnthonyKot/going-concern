# Close editorial review: chapters 9, 16 and 28

Reviewed 12 September 2026. Manuscript review only; chapter files are unchanged.

The sample contrasts a practical commercial decision (9, *What to Charge*), a personal definition of success (16, *Enough*), and the book's concluding decision (28, *Knowing When to Stop*). I read all three chapters, the relevant editorial rules in CONTEXT.md, the four cached Pinboard posts cited by chapter 16, and the cached Binpress postmortem cited by chapter 28. This is an editorial and evidential reading, not a fresh verification of every business figure or the legal sections. Findings about the book as a whole are hypotheses supported by this sample, not the result of reading all chapters.

## Main finding

The strongest source material contains uncertainty, conflicting ambitions and people making imperfect decisions. The chapters repeatedly compress that material into the book's preferred instrument: a number, a date and a consequence. That instrument can be useful. The problem is that it sometimes determines what the case is allowed to mean.

The prose has a recognisable voice already: sceptical of entrepreneurial theatre, attentive to money and unpaid work, concerned about an owner's life. Its artificial quality comes chiefly from repeatedly settling an argument in a balanced, emphatic sentence before the evidence has earned that certainty. Cosmetic informality would leave this intact.

One correction to our starting assumptions: the current Monday lists are not all five items. Chapter 16 has seven; chapters 12, 15 and 17 have six. The directory contains 29 chapter files, including 13b. Repeated structure is a real issue, but exact numerical uniformity is not the diagnosis.

## Chapter 9: What to Charge

**Judgment: strongest of the three as an editorial model. Keep its basic argument; revise selected conclusions.**

### Distinctive — preserve the opening and the actual pricing problem

[Chapter 9, line 35](/home/diablo/book4/chapters/09-what-to-charge.html:35): “Studio Neat sold every one of the Cosmonaut's first three thousand slots and was still short of the number required to make it.”

This opens on a specific contradiction. The reader has a reason to continue before being introduced to the apparatus. The short follow-up, “Demand had answered. Price had not,” earns its compression because the preceding sentence gives it content. This is a useful distinction when editing: short, polished sentences are not inherently the problem.

The refusal to pretend that value is “a number waiting behind the sofa” (lines 64–68) also sounds like a writer taking a position. The section on hourly work (201–211) complicates the early criticism of effort-based pricing instead of merely restating it. Preserve both.

### Overstructured — the case supports less than the full procedure

Lines 114–122 explicitly explain that the campaign is not evidence for value pricing or a retail demand curve. That is good reasoning. But the chapter then prescribes ten comparable asks, a stable proposal and a diagnostic record (183–190). The case establishes the problem of selling below a funding requirement; it does not establish that particular testing procedure.

Keep the procedure as the author's proposed way to learn, with ten identified as a starting batch rather than an evidential threshold. A brief distinction at the transition would do more than another limitations paragraph at the end. The case and recommendation need not have identical scope, but the reader should see where the author's judgment begins.

### Unsupported — Monday turns uncertainty into a lookup table

Line 224: “expensive with a cheaper alternative named is a price problem; expensive with none named is a trust problem”.

This is the clearest local example of the artificial certainty. Earlier, lines 192–199 allow several meanings of “expensive,” including urgency and approval. By Monday, those possibilities have disappeared. A buyer's failure to name a cheaper alternative does not establish mistrust. A named cheaper alternative does not establish that lowering the price is the right response.

Suggested replacement, as an editorial example:

> After ten comparable quotes, read the reasons alongside the outcomes. A cheaper alternative gives you something to investigate. If a buyer only says “too expensive,” record that the reason is still unclear. Compare delivery effort with your estimate before changing the next batch of quotes.

The instruction remains actionable without diagnosing motives the record cannot reveal.

### Repetitive — evidence boundaries are explained three times

The case limits at 114–122, the missing counter-case at 231–239, and the Reading boundary at 249 revisit much of the same territory. Each serves a slightly different purpose, but together they make the reader watch the chapter certify its own restraint.

Retain the concrete limitations beside the case. Shorten the later repetition. Likewise, the claim that a quoted range “merely announces” that the buyer should pick its bottom (178–181) is more absolute than the useful recommendation to quote a defined unit requires.

**Revision direction:** retain the opening, campaign arithmetic and hourly-work exception; present the quoting exercise as a practical proposal; replace the deterministic diagnosis in Monday. This chapter needs selective editing, not reconstruction.

## Chapter 16: Enough

**Judgment: largest gap between the chapter's promise and its treatment. Best candidate for the first substantial revision.**

### Distinctive — the distinction that deserves the chapter

Lines 68–77 separate the replacement cost of the owner's work from the needs of their household. The contrasting €70,000/€45,000 and €30,000/€60,000 examples make that distinction legible. Lines 279–283 add the more demanding judgment: “A business whose numbers work is not therefore worth your forties”.

Those passages open a substantial question: when a business is viable, what would make it worth continuing for its owner? The chapter repeatedly returns from that question to compliance with a review procedure.

### Unsupported — a published revenue number becomes a personal target

Lines 101–102: “The number was named, it was met”. Lines 228–233 say the case shows an enough number “left undefended.” Lines 256–258 say it was “not re-decided for several years.”

The four cited Pinboard posts, available in `.cache/pb-5.raw`, `pb-7.raw`, `pb-8.raw` and `pb-11.raw`, report revenue, approximate running costs, an intention to keep the service stable, and changes in the owner's work. They do not establish a household sufficiency target or whether he privately reconsidered one. Publishing actual earnings is not the same act as declaring a target before earning them. The absence of a published review is not evidence of an absent review.

This matters beyond fact-checking. The language supplies the missing behaviour that makes the person fit the chapter's procedure. Remove those assertions unless additional evidence supports them. The case can still show a small business making a living while its maintenance demands and the owner's interests change.

### Distinctive material suppressed — burnout is central in the source

The 2014 post explicitly calls burnout the business's biggest remaining risk and discusses responsibility for other people's saved material. The 2017 post describes political organising, neglected support and a return to maintenance. The 2020 post describes overdue technical upgrades. Together they offer a complicated account of what keeping a small, established service alive asks of its owner.

Chapter 16 gives time one paragraph (201–206), ending with the claim that an owner running out of patience usually has warning and remedies, which justifies putting money first. That ordering is a position the author can argue for, but it should face the case's strongest challenge. Presently the source's tension has been subordinated to the predetermined financial sequence.

Do not import the blogger's jokes to manufacture personality. Restore the substantive conflict: sufficient income does not settle what continued responsibility costs the owner, and choosing other work does not remove responsibilities to existing customers.

### Overstructured — the personal question becomes a compliance exercise

“Enough is four numbers, not one” (132), “a threshold ... is a mood” (179–180), and “the date is the whole mechanism” (185) repeatedly promise to settle ambiguity by formalising it. The imperative voice continues through seven Monday items and a part close that explains the five-line artefact again (285–288).

The problem is not having a worksheet. It is making the worksheet seem sufficient to resolve the subject. “The word hides an arithmetic problem behind a feeling” (134) dismisses part of the question the title invited. Feeling may include conflicting preferences, obligations or satisfaction that the money calculation cannot decide.

### Unsupported precision — a suggested cadence is stated as a law

Line 195: “Three years is the shortest window in which a slow decline is visible at all.” Line 245 permits an incomplete-evidence verdict once but treats a second as avoidance. Neither rule follows from the Pinboard account. Annual tables constrain what this particular record shows; they do not establish a universal observation period.

Choose a concrete review practice, explain why it is useful, and identify it as a default. This preserves decisiveness without implying that a different cadence is self-deception.

### Generic reader diagnosis — the text claims private knowledge

“You are the person most likely to read it that way” (210–214), “Most readers have something here” (148), and “every reader ... has one of those situations” (232–233) presume more than the text knows. They give the narrator the authority of an intimate adviser without establishing that relationship. Use conditional situations the reader can recognise instead.

**Revision direction:** build around the difference between an adequate income and a life the owner wants to continue. Keep the household/replacement-cost distinction. Let the Pinboard account complicate the argument; put the financial worksheet in a supporting role; remove unsupported claims about the owner's private target. Combine repeated instructions and shorten the part close.

## Chapter 28: Knowing When to Stop

**Judgment: strong emotional material, weakened by compulsory synthesis and an overly general procedure.**

### Distinctive — the situation understands why stopping is hard

Lines 52–59 begin, “It has not failed. That is what makes it hard.” Customers still pay and people still come to work. The paragraph describes a difficult intermediate state rather than relying on a spectacular collapse. Preserve it.

The Binpress founders repeatedly asking what else they could do, instead of discussing closure, is the relevant human conflict. The detail is specific enough to carry the chapter without extensive reminders of the previous framework.

### Overstructured — the source becomes a quotation procession

Lines 72–115 move through revenue, hires, experiments, a pivot, avoidance and dissolution. Transitions such as “Then the position,” “Then the months,” and “And then the stop itself” leave much of the narrative work to stitched quotations.

Compress the early growth history and hire list. Spend more space on the choice to pivot and what the founders thought made it plausible. The cached postmortem includes encouraging responses from existing publishers and the founder's later uncertainty about the causes of failure. These details would help readers understand the decision from inside it, rather than only in hindsight.

### Unsupported — a retrospective date becomes an unused decision rule

Lines 123–129: “Binpress had the date in the record and did not use it: September 2014”. The paragraph then condemns choosing the pivot without a date.

September 2014 dates the financial position in the retrospective. It is not, on that evidence, a stopping deadline the founders had set and ignored. The source establishes delayed discussion after the pivot; it does not establish the absence of every prior deadline. Separate the founder's account of avoidance from the author's recommendation to set a review date.

### Repetitive — callbacks replace an explanation at the point of need

Lines 131–151 depend on chapter twenty's forcing function, sixteen's sentence and throw-off, twenty-two's figures and twenty-six's shock. The first Monday item (173) does the same. These references ask readers to reconstruct the system during the chapter's most consequential decision.

State the decision in ordinary language first: the change being attempted, the result it must produce, the remaining room to try it, and the point at which the owners must decide again. Link earlier worksheets after that. Anchors would ease navigation but leave the conceptual dependence intact.

### Unsupported generalisation — the translation erases material differences

Line 153 moves from Binpress to a café and concludes: “The only line that changes is whose money it was.” The next section adopts Binpress's order as general (157–166).

The chapter's own final section distinguishes legal forms, assets, outstanding contracts and creditor obligations. Those differences make the café claim untenable on the chapter's own terms. A sequence from one retrospective cannot by itself justify a general winding-up procedure. This is an internal scope problem; this review does not supply replacement legal advice.

Keep the account of what Binpress did. Rework the general passage so it does not imply that swapping investors for staff, landlords and suppliers leaves everything else unchanged.

### Overstructured — the missing counter-case ends by vindicating the rule

Lines 184–195 admit that the desired counter-case has not been found, then conclude that a dated rule can be wrong once while its absence is “wrong every month”. That final symmetry overwhelms the admission. A premature stop can have substantial consequences; counting occasions of error does not compare their cost.

The useful uncertainty is whether a deadline should hold after new information arrives. The chapter permits one extension (177), but a count alone does not explain why that extension is justified. Discuss what would count as changed evidence and what would merely repeat the original hope.

**Revision direction:** centre the avoided conversation; distinguish the source's timeline from the proposed stopping rule; reduce callbacks; separate the case's ending from jurisdiction-dependent procedure; leave the reader with a substantive uncertainty rather than one final proof that the apparatus was right.

## Why this pattern recurs

CONTEXT.md requires fixed moves and headings (43–53), boundaries inside principles (297–307), and expiry dates for every evidence standard (265–295). It also explicitly separates precision in claims from confidence in instructions. My inference is that this combination helps explain the recurring shift: a careful case, multiple caveats, then an instruction more categorical than either.

These rules have protected the book from several kinds of carelessness. They also encourage every subject to culminate in the same device. Revisit the constraints when revising the sample chapter; otherwise a later consistency pass may restore the patterns removed during editing.

For the first revision, choose chapter 16. Its strongest distinction is already present, its sources have unused material directly relevant to its title, and the gap between its promise and procedure is clear. Success would mean that the reader can explain what the case complicates, distinguish evidence from the author's proposed practice, and describe a decision about their own life without needing the book's coined terms.
