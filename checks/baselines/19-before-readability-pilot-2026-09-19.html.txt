  <p class="kicker">Part IV · Can it run without you? · Chapter 19</p>
  <h1>Deciding to Add Help</h1>
  <p class="lede">You can describe the work that needs to move, but nobody has the time to take
  it. Before offering a role, establish what help would change, what it would require from you,
  and how the business would meet the commitment if the hoped-for growth did not arrive.</p>

  <div class="decision">
    <span class="tag">The decision</span>
    Whether to add help now, change the work or defer the commitment, using a clear requirement,
    a realistic support plan and a dated funding scenario.
  </div>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>Bring the unfilled requirement</h2>
    <p>The handover review may have found a suitable recipient, or it may have ended with nobody
    available. Start with that result. Name the work that waits, what a satisfactory result looks
    like and when it is needed. “I need an assistant” names a possible solution before explaining
    the problem.</p>
    <p>Adding someone can serve different purposes: meeting an existing commitment, relieving
    an owner, bringing a missing skill or pursuing new demand. State which applies. A growth role
    may be funded deliberately before its work produces revenue, but the funding and the limit
    on that experiment need to be visible. Future sales cannot pay a bill due before they arrive.</p>
    <p>If you have no record of the work, reconstruct a recent period from requests, deliveries
    and your calendar. Separate time doing it from time waiting for information or approval.
    An estimate is a useful starting point when labelled; it is not proof of a permanent workload.
    Where demand varies, compare a busy period with a quieter one before choosing the hours.</p>

    <h3>Noor needs cover beyond the first handover</h3>
    <p><strong>This continues chapter seventeen's invented consultancy, under a new proposed
    arrangement.</strong> Ivo's limited Tuesday test has not established cover for other mornings.
    In this scenario he cannot take on additional sessions. Noor is considering two four-hour
    mornings a week of support and report checking. The aim is to cover existing promises and
    protect time for her own delivery work, not to assume that free time automatically creates
    another customer.</p>
    <p>Her brief names the work: check incoming requests during the covered mornings, use the
    approved recovery steps, check report output and send an accurate response within the agreed
    time. It also names what stays with Noor: new integration quotes and changes outside the
    recovery limits. She needs somebody with relevant troubleshooting skills who can learn the
    customer context; she has not shown that every request fits a checklist.</p>
    <p>She plans to spend four hours on preparation and training before the start, then two hours
    each week reviewing work and handling exceptions during the first four weeks. That is twelve
    owner hours, including preparation. It is a hypothetical allowance to test. She must put it
    in her calendar before promising that hiring will free all eight covered hours each week.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>Compare arrangements for the work</h2>
    <p>Noor first asks whether the requirement can be reduced. Clearer input instructions might
    prevent some requests; a different service promise might change the hours that need cover.
    Those are proposals, not changes she can silently impose on customers who already bought a
    particular response. Existing commitments need covering while any new arrangement is agreed.</p>
    <p>She could investigate a service provider responsible for an agreed support result, a
    suitably scoped addition to an existing arrangement, or a new employee role. For each, ask
    who directs the work, when capacity is available, what context must be learned, who handles
    exceptions and what happens if that person or service is unavailable. Compare what is actually
    supplied, rather than treating every quoted hour as equivalent.</p>
    <p>A fixed deliverable may still leave support, correction or other obligations. A recurring
    service may have notice periods or minimum charges. A role may need equipment, administration
    and substantial owner time. Find those commitments in the proposed arrangement; neither
    an invoice nor a short description establishes how cheaply you could change it later.</p>

    <h3>Operational need does not determine legal status</h3>
    <p>Whether the business stops without someone tells you about dependence. It does not, by
    itself, classify the working relationship. Describe the actual arrangement before choosing
    its paperwork. On Dutch ground, the Belastingdienst's assessment guidance considers all the
    facts and circumstances together. The dated <a href="#ground">legal section below</a>
    explains a separate statutory presumption; it is not a task-versus-job scoring rule.</p>
    <p>Take the proposed work, direction, hours, responsibilities and payment arrangements to
    the appropriate adviser where classification is uncertain. Do the same for a proposed trial
    or work sample. Calling something a trial does not settle its terms. This chapter's cost
    example cannot establish which arrangement would be lawful in your circumstances.</p>

    <h3>What Gymdesk's first hires establish</h3>
    <p>In his May 2023 account, Eran Galperin described unsuccessful experiments with growth and
    advertising agencies before deciding to build an in-house team. His first full-time hire,
    in June 2021, had previously written for Gymdesk and had an SEO background. The role was
    content marketing and editing, supporting the organic acquisition channel he already valued.</p>
    <p>Later that year he hired a full-time customer-service person after earlier contractor
    arrangements had not worked as he wanted. He reported that the new representative removed
    a large part of his daily distractions and said he regretted not hiring sooner. His account
    also emphasised the responsibility of ensuring an employee was paid each month. By the end
    of 2022 he reported eight employees and four contractors.</p>
    <p>These are the founder's retrospective observations. They do not show that agencies could
    not have worked under a different brief, that prior writing proved suitability for every
    part of the new role, or that customer support should always be the second hire. The account
    does not disclose the costs and funding schedule needed to reproduce the decision. Its useful
    question is what responsibility the business needed someone to hold, and what accepting that
    responsibility required from the owner.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>Cost the arrangement and the work you retain</h2>
    <p>For a proposed employee, obtain an estimate of the total business cost for the actual role
    and hours, including applicable employer charges, leave, pension and administration. For a
    service, check included work, exclusions, minimum charges, notice and payment dates. In both
    cases include recruitment, equipment, access, training and the work still left with you.
    Record what an estimate includes so that you neither omit nor count the same item twice.</p>
    <p>Keep three views beside each other. The economic review asks whether the business can
    support the work at its full cost. The cash forecast asks when actual payments fall due.
    The calendar asks whether you and the other person can do the work when required. The
    <a href="12-reading-your-own-business.html">period review</a> and
    <a href="13-profit-is-not-cash.html">cash forecast</a> provide the first two records;
    extend them rather than inventing a salary-only affordability test.</p>
    <p>When another person takes hours you previously supplied, replace the corresponding owner
    allowance in the management comparison with the cost of that person's work. Keep an allowance
    for the training, decisions and supervision you still do. Do not subtract both a full worker
    cost and your old allowance for the same transferred hours. Equally, an hour released from
    your calendar is not automatically an extra hour sold.</p>

    <h3>A first-period forecast can rule out a proposed start</h3>
    <p>Noor considers an employee arrangement for the two mornings. All figures below are
    invented budget amounts, not wage rates, quotations or statutory calculations. For the
    four-week window, assume €2,400 of total staffing cash outflows in two €1,200 instalments,
    plus €600 of setup purchases. The staffing total includes the associated costs assumed due
    in this window; a real estimate must identify each item and its actual date, including
    obligations arising now but paid later.</p>
    <p>Her opening business cash is €5,000. Other payments are €1,800 on day 2 and €800 on day 21;
    for this simplified scenario those include the other business, tax and household outflows
    due within the window. A completed job is expected to bring €2,800 on day 18. There are no
    other movements in this illustration. Noor chooses a €1,500 minimum balance for comparison;
    it is a planning choice, not a universal reserve.</p>
    <p>Before reading the balances, calculate whether the proposal stays above that minimum.
    Then move the customer's receipt to day 32, beyond the two staffing payments. Keep the
    payments unchanged: the work would still have been supplied.</p>

    <p>The on-time version ends with €2,200 but falls to €1,400 on day 14, €100 below Noor's
    chosen floor. The delayed version reaches −€600 on day 28. That is an unfunded proposal,
    not an assumption that a bank allows the payment. The later €2,800 receipt would restore
    €2,200 on day 32 only if no other payments occurred; extending the real forecast requires
    including everything else due through that date.</p>
    <p>The first version needs a change even under its expected receipt date. The second needs
    €600 to avoid a negative balance at its lowest point, or €2,100 to preserve the chosen floor,
    before other obligations. Those are different funding questions. Noor cannot count hoped-for
    new sales from her released time as arranged funding.</p>
    <p>This window is enough to expose a problem, but cannot approve the longer commitment.
    Four weeks is a forecast boundary, not the end of an employment obligation. Extend the
    schedule through further pay dates, delayed charges, quieter demand and whatever notice or
    other commitments the actual arrangement entails. Check the economics over an appropriate
    operating period as well; cash from earlier work can conceal an ongoing loss.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>Decide what you can commit to now</h2>
    <p>In the example, Noor has not offered the role or ordered the setup. She defers that start
    while obtaining a costed alternative and checking whether a different receipt or start date
    could support it. Until an alternative is agreed, she keeps covering existing promises and
    declines extra work that depends on unavailable help. That has a cost in her own time and
    possible sales; it is still a decision she can explain from the present record.</p>
    <p>Your result may be different. You might deliberately fund a ramp-up from savings or
    arranged borrowing, buy a narrower service, recruit for a revised role or choose a smaller
    business. State how much money and time you will commit, what must be in place before the
    start and when you will reconsider. A review date does not cancel obligations already made.</p>

    <h3>Evaluate the actual role, including with someone you know</h3>
    <p>Prior work is useful evidence. Ask what it demonstrates and what the new arrangement
    adds: sustained availability, responsibility for exceptions, communication with customers,
    or coordination with someone else. Ask whether the person wants those responsibilities.
    An excellent occasional writer may have neither the interest nor the availability to run
    an editorial schedule.</p>
    <p>Where a work sample would answer an important uncertainty, define a relevant, bounded
    piece of work, agreed payment and terms, and what you will assess. Avoid creating uncosted
    customer obligations as an audition. A sample can reveal how somebody approaches that task;
    it cannot establish performance across every part of an ongoing role.</p>
    <p>Before the start, agree the initial outcome, quality standard, access, authority and help
    available. Reserve your own support time. Set a review around work the person will actually
    have an opportunity to do, and collect their account alongside yours. A delayed result may
    expose your instructions or workload as well as their performance.</p>
    <p>The <a href="17-you-are-the-bottleneck.html#handover">handover test</a> helps you discover
    those gaps. <a href="20-managing-people.html">Chapter twenty</a> takes up agreed work, feedback and the
    harder decision when an arrangement is not working. Keep a management review separate from
    the legal process for changing or ending employment, covered in its dated end section.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="monday">
    <h2>Monday · one requirement, one funded next step</h2>
    <p>Use a short role or service brief beside your existing calendar, business review and
    <a href="../resources/cash-forecast-blank.csv" download>dated cash forecast</a>.</p>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Describe the missing work.</strong> State its result, timing, skill, authority
      and supervision needs, with the observations behind your estimate. Include what stays with you.</li>
      <li><strong>Compare feasible arrangements.</strong> Include changing or deferring the work.
      Record availability, included service, limits and continuing obligations. Resolve uncertain
      classification from the actual arrangement before relying on its proposed terms.</li>
      <li><strong>Cost and date the commitment.</strong> Add setup, total worker costs and your
      support time once each. Test expected receipts and a relevant setback; extend the horizon
      through the obligations the decision creates. Name actual funding for any gap.</li>
      <li><strong>Choose and prepare the next step.</strong> Hire, obtain a missing quote or
      assessment, revise the role, or defer. Record the limit, preconditions, owner and review date.
      If proceeding, agree the first assignment and the support needed to try it.</li>
    </ol>
    <p>You have finished when you can explain what help would change, what it would cost and
    require from you, and why the next commitment fits the evidence and funding. An unresolved
    quote or classification is a named next action, not a guessed input that makes the forecast pass.</p>
  </section>

  <div class="owed">
    <p class="case-head"><span class="who">What remains uncertain</span></p>
    <p>Gymdesk's account does not validate Noor's budget or this decision routine. A funded role
    can still fail to deliver its intended result; deferring it can prolong overload or lose an
    opportunity. A documented counter-case of an owner making this careful, funded commitment
    and becoming worse off because of it remains owed. The worked example checks relationships
    and arithmetic. The actual arrangement needs its own evidence and review.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="ground" id="ground">
    <h3>On Dutch ground · who counts as an employee</h3>

    <p>This section is for readers hiring in the Netherlands. Employment status is a legal
    assessment of the working relationship, separate from the operational decision about what
    help the business needs.</p>

    <p><strong>The definition.</strong> Article 7:610 of the Civil Code says what an employment
    contract is: <em lang="nl">"De arbeidsovereenkomst is de overeenkomst waarbij de ene partij, de
    werknemer, zich verbindt in dienst van de andere partij, de werkgever, tegen loon gedurende
    zekere tijd arbeid te verrichten."</em> Three elements — work performed <span
    lang="nl">in dienst van</span> the other party, for pay, over a period of time. The statute
    defines the relationship by those elements, not by the heading on the contract.</p>

    <p><strong>The presumption.</strong>  Article 7:610a goes further: <em lang="nl">"Hij die ten behoeve van een ander tegen beloning door die ander
    gedurende drie opeenvolgende maanden, wekelijks dan wel gedurende ten minste twintig uren per
    maand arbeid verricht, wordt vermoed deze arbeid te verrichten krachtens
    arbeidsovereenkomst."</em> Someone who works for you, for pay, for three consecutive months,
    weekly or at least twenty hours a month, is <em>presumed</em> to be doing so under an
    employment contract.</p>

    <p><strong>What the presumption means.</strong> Rijksoverheid's
    <a href="https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/arbeidsovereenkomst-en-cao/wat-staat-er-in-een-arbeidsovereenkomst">explanation of employment contracts</a>
    says that an employer who disputes the presumed employment relationship must prove that
    there is no employment contract. This is an English summary of that guidance; the
    presumption can be rebutted, and what would rebut it in your arrangement needs legal advice.</p>

    <p><strong>Enforcement.</strong> The government's own page on <span
    lang="nl">schijnzelfstandigheid</span> — self-employment in name only — states that
    <em lang="nl">"De Belastingdienst controleert sinds 1 januari 2025 weer volledig op
    schijnzelfstandigheid."</em> The consequence for the business that got it wrong is that it
    <em lang="nl">"betaalt de werkgever alsnog loonbelasting, premies werknemersverzekeringen,
    bijdragen Zorgverzekeringswet en soms pensioenpremies"</em>, and the same page notes that
    responsibility is shared: <em lang="nl">"Bedrijven en zzp'ers zijn samen verantwoordelijk om
    schijnzelfstandigheid te voorkomen."</em></p>

    <p>None of this is advice. This box quotes the definition, the presumption and the fact of
    enforcement, and summarises the government's explanation of the presumption; it does not say how the assessment goes in your case, what the criteria weigh, or
    what a rebuttal would need. Before the first contract of either kind, pay an <span
    lang="nl">arbeidsrechtadvocaat</span> for an hour with the actual working arrangement  in front of them.</p>
