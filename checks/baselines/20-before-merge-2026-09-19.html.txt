  <p class="kicker">Part IV · Can it run without you? · Chapter 20</p>
  <h1>Helping Someone Succeed</h1>
  <p class="lede">You agreed what someone would take on. Now a result is late, or needs correcting,
  and you have to decide what to do. Start with the work both of you can inspect: the promise,
  the attempt, the help available and the next opportunity to get it right.</p>

  <div class="decision">
    <span class="tag">The decision</span>
    What to continue or change in one person's assignment and support, based on the work
    delivered and both people's account of what happened.
  </div>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>Start with an assignment both people understand</h2>
    <p><a href="19-the-first-hire.html">Chapter nineteen</a> ends with a decision about adding help.
    Noor bought one covered morning from a provider and postponed the hire. She has no employee to manage. If you also deferred,
    prepare the assignment below for a future start; leave the observations and review result
    blank. If someone already does the work, use their current assignment.</p>
    <p>This chapter proposes a management routine, illustrated by an invented business. It is
    guidance to try and review, not a method whose effectiveness the case evidence establishes.
    Chapter seventeen's <a href="17-you-are-the-bottleneck.html#handover">handover record</a> supplies
    the instructions, permissions and limits; here you use that preparation to support ongoing work.</p>

    <h3>A report that should have been ready</h3>
    <p><strong>Maya and Sam are a separate, fictional team.</strong> Maya runs a small reporting
    consultancy; Sam is already employed there. For this example, assume the role's funding,
    hours and terms have been settled. Sam prepares customer reports; Maya approves changes
    to how the reports are calculated.</p>
    <p>For Tuesday, they agree that Sam will place a checked draft in the shared folder by 11:00.
    It must cover the requested dates, reconcile its totals to the supplied export and identify
    missing inputs. Sam can correct formatting and rerun the approved process. A change to a
    calculation requires Maya's approval. The draft stays internal until it passes review.</p>
    <p>They reserve 09:00–11:00 for the task and fifteen minutes afterwards for review. Maya
    provides a completed example, folder access and the written checks. Sam explains the task
    back: “A reconciled draft by eleven; if the input is incomplete, flag it rather than fill
    the gap myself.” This checks understanding of the result, not whether Sam remembers Maya's
    exact words.</p>
    <p>There is already a weakness in that agreement: they have not said when Sam must flag a
    block, or how Maya will respond. Stating a deadline does not by itself make the route to
    that deadline workable.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>Inspect the attempt before deciding its cause</h2>
    <p>At 11:00 the draft is incomplete. Maya first thinks Sam needs more spreadsheet practice.
    She asks to see the attempt and hears Sam's account. The export contains a new transaction
    type that the instructions do not explain. At 09:20 Sam left a question in the report
    comments. Maya did not see it until the review. Sam completed the unaffected sections and
    did not invent a calculation.</p>
    <p>Several explanations were possible before they inspected the work: missing skill, an
    unrealistic workload, incomplete instructions or a blocked decision. In this attempt, the
    new transaction and unanswered question support the last two. The missing draft does not
    establish that Sam lacks the skill to prepare the rest of the report.</p>
    <p>Maya's feedback can now be specific: “The date checks and existing totals are right, and
    you correctly left the unknown calculation alone. I needed to know the draft was blocked
    before eleven. We left the escalation route unclear.” Sam explains that a comment seemed
    sufficient because it was where Maya had asked for review notes. They record both accounts.</p>
    <p>Maya supplies the missing calculation rule and adds a worked row to the instructions.
    For the next attempt, Sam will mark any unresolved calculation as blocked and send Maya a
    direct message as soon as it is found. Maya reserves a 09:30 check and agrees to reply by
    10:00. If that reply does not arrive, Sam will flag that the 11:00 draft is at risk and
    continue the unaffected work. Maya owns any change to the customer promise.</p>
    <p>Notice the division of responsibility. Sam owns checking and signalling. Maya owns the
    missing rule and the decision she reserved. Asking Sam to “be more proactive” would not
    supply either of them.</p>

    <h3>A shared record, with a result still to come</h3>
    <p>Their record fits in the task notes: <em>Tuesday's draft missed 11:00; new transaction
    rule missing; question posted 09:20 but unseen. Maya adds rule and example. Sam uses direct
    escalation for unresolved calculations. Maya checks at 09:30 and replies by 10:00. Review
    Thursday at 11:00 after the next comparable report.</em> Neither signs away a disagreement;
    if their accounts differ, the difference belongs in the record.</p>
    <p>On Thursday, Sam completes the comparable draft at 10:45. Maya checks the dates and
    reconciliation and finds no correction needed. The added example covered the transaction;
    no escalation was needed. They continue the assignment and keep the new route available.
    This supports keeping the instructions. It does not yet show that the escalation route
    works when Maya is busy.</p>
    <p>If the task had not occurred, they would have no new performance result to review.
    If it had remained blocked, they would inspect the unresolved dependency. If a comparable
    error recurred with the needed information, time and support available, they would address
    that specific gap. A calendar date organises the review; it does not manufacture evidence.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>What Groove's experience supports</h2>
    <p>In his January 2015 account, Groove founder Alex Turnbull reported turnover of nearly
    40% of the team the company launched with. He described the people he had hired as qualified
    and attributed delayed milestones, slower growth and lost personal productivity to poor
    collaboration and his own reluctance to act. These are his assessments, not measured effects
    of a management intervention.</p>
    <p>Groove used a paid trial lasting two to four weeks, which Turnbull described as a consulting
    engagement. Its end forced a decision he had previously found easy to postpone. He also
    acknowledged that insisting on this arrangement could exclude talented candidates.</p>
    <p>The part most useful here happens before that decision. Turnbull wrote that he had
    initially waited until the trial ended to give feedback. He changed to frequent feedback,
    sometimes daily, and kept notes to distinguish patterns from isolated events. He sought
    the views of colleagues who worked closely with the person and asked the person how the
    arrangement felt to them.</p>
    <p>The account supports attributing those practices to Groove. It does not test this
    chapter's shared assignment record, establish the right review interval for your work or
    classify a similar trial in your jurisdiction. Keeping a record accessible to both people
    is this book's proposal; Turnbull described keeping notes for himself.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="move">
    <h2>Make the review useful when work succeeds or struggles</h2>
    <p>When the agreed result is sound, say what made it sound and continue. Do not create an
    improvement task merely to fill the record. You may agree greater independence, but only
    for work the evidence covers: Sam's correct draft does not grant authority to change
    calculations.</p>
    <p>When a result falls short, describe the gap and its consequence. Hear the person's
    account before choosing a response. An example or practice may address a skill gap;
    access may remove a block; fewer simultaneous assignments may make the workload feasible.
    Name who will supply the change and when there will be comparable work to review. If the
    owner cannot supply the promised support, revise the plan explicitly.</p>
    <p>Repeated difficulty also has a cost: customer delays, correction work and time spent
    supporting the assignment. You need not repeat the same intervention indefinitely.
    Record what has been tried, what happened and what remains unresolved. “Poor fit” adds
    little unless you can explain it in terms of the work; resemblance to your own style
    is not a result standard.</p>
    <p>Where a persistent problem leads you to consider changing or ending employment, the
    applicable process is a separate question; readers employing in the Netherlands should
    read the dated <a href="#ground">legal section below</a> before acting.</p>
    <p>A shared assignment can still depend on somebody else's decision. The next chapter,
    <a href="21-meetings-that-arent-theater.html">Coordinating Work and Acting on Signals</a>,
    picks up Maya and Sam's escalation route and asks how the team gets a response in time.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="monday">
    <h2>Monday</h2>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Choose one current assignment.</strong> Write its result, quality checks, date,
      authority, dependencies, available time and support. Reuse the handover instructions.
      If you have not brought anyone in, prepare this brief and leave the remaining steps pending.</li>
      <li><strong>Agree it with the person doing the work.</strong> Ask them to explain the result
      and flag missing access, examples or time. Name the route for a block, who responds and when.</li>
      <li><strong>Inspect one real attempt together.</strong> Compare requested and delivered work.
      Record the person's account alongside yours, including what worked and the consequence of
      any gap. Mark observations you do not yet have as unknown.</li>
      <li><strong>Record the next action and support.</strong> Continue successful work without
      forcing a change. For a gap, agree a specific response with an owner and date. Keep the
      record accessible to both people.</li>
      <li><strong>Set and complete a review after a relevant opportunity.</strong> Check whether
      the result improved, stayed sound or remains unresolved. Continue, revise or address a
      persistent problem through the applicable process. If there was no opportunity to do the
      work, record that and set the next review.</li>
    </ol>
    <p>Your output is one agreed assignment and a shared review record. A prepared brief is
    enough for a reader without a recipient; a completed management review requires actual work.</p>
  </section>

  <div class="owed">
    <p class="case-head"><span class="who">What would challenge this advice</span></p>
    <p>The counter-case is still owed: a small business that used clear assignments, adequate
    support and timely shared reviews, yet found the routine itself caused more harm than it
    prevented—for example, by consuming scarce working time or narrowing capable people's
    judgment to the written checks. A comparison would need to show what happened to delivery
    and initiative as well as time spent reviewing. Groove's account does not supply that
    evidence. A persistent performance problem alone would not refute this routine; dealing
    with one is an outcome the routine already allows.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="ground" id="ground">
    <h3>On Dutch ground · ending an employment</h3>

    <p>This section is for readers employing in the Netherlands. Read it before the conversation, not after it: a review in this chapter decides what
    to try next with the work, not whether someone stays, and here the two are different
    acts.</p>

    <p><strong>There is no dismissal at will.</strong> Article 7:671 of the Civil Code: <em
    lang="nl">"De werkgever kan de arbeidsovereenkomst niet rechtsgeldig opzeggen zonder
    schriftelijke instemming van de werknemer, tenzij"</em> — and the exceptions that follow include termination during the trial period and permission
    <em lang="nl">"als bedoeld in artikel 671a"</em>, which that article confines to grounds a and
    b of article 669: <em lang="nl">"op grond van artikel 669, lid 3, onderdeel a of b, verzoekt
    hiervoor schriftelijk toestemming aan het Uitvoeringsinstituut werknemersverzekeringen"</em>.
    For the grounds listed next, the door is the court, article 7:671b: <em
    lang="nl">"De kantonrechter kan op verzoek van de werkgever de arbeidsovereenkomst ontbinden:
    a. op grond van artikel 669, lid 3, onderdelen c tot en met i"</em>. Outside those, the
    employee's written agreement is what article 671 names. Article 7:669 sets
    what the employer must have first: <em lang="nl">"De werkgever kan de arbeidsovereenkomst
    opzeggen indien daar een redelijke grond voor is en herplaatsing van de werknemer binnen een
    redelijke termijn, al dan niet met behulp van scholing, in een andere passende functie niet
    mogelijk is of niet in de rede ligt."</em> A reasonable ground, <em>and</em> no reasonable
    redeployment.</p>

    <p><strong>Unsuitability is a ground with conditions.</strong> Among the grounds, lid 3
    onderdeel d concerns unsuitability for the agreed work, other than
    through illness — <em lang="nl">"mits de werkgever de werknemer hiervan tijdig in kennis heeft gesteld en hem in
    voldoende mate in de gelegenheid heeft gesteld zijn functioneren te verbeteren en de
    ongeschiktheid niet het gevolg is van onvoldoende zorg van de werkgever voor scholing van de
    werknemer of voor de arbeidsomstandigheden van de werknemer"</em>. Told in good time, given a
    real chance to improve, and not left untrained. Feedback given as it arises, and a record that
    it was given, are here close to a precondition of the ground existing; notes kept only for
    yourself are not notice to the person.</p>

    <p><strong>The trial period is statutory and short.</strong> Article 7:652: <em
    lang="nl">"De proeftijd wordt schriftelijk overeengekomen"</em>; on an indefinite contract
    <em lang="nl">"een proeftijd worden overeengekomen van ten hoogste twee maanden"</em>; and
    none at all where the contract <em lang="nl">"is aangegaan voor ten hoogste zes maanden"</em>.
    Turnbull calls Groove's arrangement a consulting engagement. That description does not
    establish how a similar arrangement would be classified here. The Belastingdienst's assessment
    guidance considers all facts and circumstances of the actual relationship; see also
    <a href="19-the-first-hire.html#ground">chapter nineteen's Dutch-ground section</a>.</p>

    <p><strong>Notice and severance.</strong> Article 7:672 lid 2: <em lang="nl">"De door de
    werkgever in acht te nemen termijn van opzegging bedraagt bij een arbeidsovereenkomst die op
    de dag van opzegging: a. korter dan vijf jaar heeft geduurd: één maand"</em>, rising with
    service. Article 7:673 lid 2: <em lang="nl">"De transitievergoeding is voor elk jaar dat de
    arbeidsovereenkomst heeft geduurd gelijk aan een derde van het loon per maand"</em>, pro rata
    for part-years, and <em lang="nl">"bedraagt maximaal € 102.000,00 of een bedrag gelijk aan ten
    hoogste het loon over twaalf maanden indien dat loon hoger is dan dat bedrag"</em>. The cap
    <em lang="nl">"wordt telkens met ingang van 1 januari"</em> adjusted, so in a later year the
    figure above is stale by construction. The transitievergoeding is owed on a dismissal the
    employer initiates on a valid ground; it is not a fine for doing it wrong, and paying it does
    not supply the ground.</p>

    <p>None of this is advice. The box covers an employer ending an indefinite contract; a
    fixed-term contract's expiry, a termination by agreement, illness, and every other ground in
    article 669 are outside it. Before the first conversation that could be heard as a dismissal,
    pay an <span lang="nl">arbeidsrechtadvocaat</span> for an hour with the contract and your
    notes in front of them.</p>

    <span class="checked">Checked 28 August 2026 against Burgerlijk Wetboek Boek 7 articles 652, 669, 671, 671a, 671b, 672
    and 673, consolidated text at 1 January 2026. Statute quoted, not paraphrased;
    how the UWV and the courts assess a ground in practice is not covered here.
    Trial-classification explanation corrected 19 September 2026 against the Belastingdienst
    guidance linked above; this does not refresh the earlier statutory check.</span>
