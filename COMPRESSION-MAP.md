   contains chapter 6's lesson (anyone → authors → bloggers). Maya and Sam's one blocked report

   measure, ConvertKit's dashboard definitions, targets) is old chapter 22 and does not belong
   to the story around it.

- **23 + 24.** Both short now and joined by a cliffhanger. A merge buys nothing.
- **12 + 13, or anything in 10–14.** Lea's carried figures hold them together; they need
  shortening, not joining.

## 5. Risks

- Every merge removes a page that could be shared alone. Everpix, Cydoc and Plausible are
  three of the most shareable stories in the book. Mitigation: each keeps its own `id`, its own
  heading and its old URL as a redirect to that anchor.
- 2+3 carries three cases. It works only if each is cut to its turn (the survey pair; the
  pledge; the refund arithmetic). If the draft passes 3,500 body words, split it again.
- 6+8 demotes Plausible to second case. If the author remembers Plausible better than
  ConvertKit, lead with it instead.
- Mechanical cost: `claims.tsv` chapter keys, `test_consolidation.py` expectations, nav, index.
  All Codex work, none of it judgment.

## 7. Amendments after review (2026-09-19) — these override anything above that disagrees

1. **Basic customer access stays early.** "No use before customers" was too strong: a reader
   needs somewhere to find the first prospects. 6+8 carries a small practical step (a few named
   prospective buyers and one plausible way to reach each). Only the deeper comparison, owned
   against rented and which two channels get the hours, moves beside 14. The dead SEO tactic
   does not make the distribution question obsolete.
2. **The hundred-name test is dropped as the beginner's test.** It rebuilds the workload barrier
   removed from 6–8 this week. Start with a few identifiable buyers and one way to reach them.
   A hundred names is an optional prospecting exercise, and failing to list them does not by
   itself show the audience is too broad.
3. **4+5 is the riskiest merge.** Continuing an experiment and giving up a salary are related
   and different. The merged chapter gets one question: *what evidence would justify my next
   commitment?* Leaving employment is one application of it, with its own costs and
   constraints (floor, benefits, contract clauses, no unemployment cover). If that reads as two
   chapters stapled together, they stay two and both shorten.
4. **27 optional, exit preparation not.** 28 stands alone on continuing against closing,
   including obligations and the time leaving takes, and keeps a short statement of the
   practice/asset distinction. 27 holds the detailed transferability discussion.
5. **Word budget, defined.** Measured by `python3 checks/mainpath.py`: *visible* words are what
   a reader meets with every fold closed, excluding fold-outs, dated legal sections and Reading
   lists. Today: 58,900 visible across all 24 pages (folds 960, legal 5,200, Reading 4,600);
   51,300 on the proposed main path (without 7, 13½, 27). **Budget: 38,000 visible words on the
   17-chapter main path**, about 2,200 a chapter, 3,500 as the review trigger for a merged
   chapter. Optional chapters, folds, legal and Reading are reported beside it, never inside it.
   Two reworked chapters are still over their share: 1 (3,175) and 10 (3,392).
6. **A shared section must stand alone.** A redirect is not enough. Each named story section
   (Everpix, Glif, Coolest Cooler, Cydoc, Plausible…) keeps its own heading and `id`, enough
   setup to be read cold, and its consequence.
7. **Order.** Pilot 2+3 (say → pay → deliver). At 3,500 visible words, first cut repetition and
   fold optional depth; split only if that fails. Then draft 4+5 and read 1 → 2+3 → 4+5
   continuously. The test is whether the reader reaches a useful commitment decision sooner,
   not whether the contents page is shorter.
