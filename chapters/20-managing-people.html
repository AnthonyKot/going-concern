Part IV · Can it run without you? · Chapter 20

When Work Stops Between Two People

Three years in, Alex Turnbull wrote that his company, Groove, had "turned over nearly 40% of the team we launched with". He did not blame the people: "every single person I’ve hired at Groove has been more than qualified to do the job they were hired for". The cause he named was his own waiting. When someone's work is late or wrong, how do you find out what actually stopped it, and get the decision it needs made in time?

The decision What to change in one person's assignment, support and escalation route, based on the work delivered and both people's account of it, and whether the routine earns the time it takes.

Groove: what waiting cost

Groove — Alex Turnbull, 2015

Turnbull's list of what "my own hesitance to spot poor fits and wait too long has resulted in" is specific: "Our team taking longer to hit milestones that we should have because we weren’t collaborating as well as we could have. Our growth slowing because unhappy employees weren’t working at optimal productivity. My own productivity dropping because I was preoccupied with trying to force a fit where there was none."

What he changed came before any decision about a person. "I used to wait until the trial period was over to give feedback. This helped nobody"; he moved to feedback as it arose, sometimes daily. He kept notes: "After two weeks, you won’t remember every small thing you said to someone. But if you took notes, you’ll be able to easily spot the trends in your feedback, and separate the true strengths and weaknesses from the anomalies." He asked the people working beside the new hire, "I trust my team’s (who works much more closely with the new employee) judgment even more than my own", and he asked the person how the arrangement felt to them.

Say it while it can still change something, write it down, and hear the other account. The rest of this chapter runs those three through one invented week of work, because the commonest reason a result is late is not the person. It is a decision that someone else was holding.

Tuesday, 11:00: a draft that should have been ready

Maya and Sam are invented, and separate from chapter nineteen's Noor, whose help so far is a provider's covered morning and Ivo's few hours, not an employee to manage. If that is you too, read this for the brief you would write, and use it when someone starts. Maya runs a small reporting consultancy. Sam already works there; assume his hours, pay and terms are settled.

For Tuesday they agree that Sam will put a checked draft in the shared folder by 11:00. It must cover the requested dates, reconcile to the supplied export and flag missing inputs. Sam may fix formatting and rerun the approved process; a change to a calculation needs Maya. They reserve 09:00–11:00 for it, Maya supplies a finished example and the written checks, and Sam says the task back in his own words: “A reconciled draft by eleven; if the input is incomplete, flag it rather than fill the gap myself.” One thing is missing, and neither notices: when Sam must flag a block, and how Maya will answer.

At 11:00 the draft is incomplete. Maya's first thought is that Sam needs more spreadsheet practice. Before saying so she asks to see the attempt. The export contains a transaction type the instructions do not cover. At 09:20 Sam left a question in the report comments, where Maya had asked for review notes; she did not see it. He finished every unaffected section and did not invent a calculation.

Four explanations were possible before they looked: a missing skill, an unrealistic workload, incomplete instructions, a blocked decision. The attempt supports the last two. So Maya's feedback can be exact: “The date checks and existing totals are right, and you correctly left the unknown calculation alone. I needed to know before eleven that the draft was blocked. We left the escalation route unclear.” Asking Sam to “be more proactive” would have supplied neither the missing rule nor the decision she had reserved.

They fix both. Maya adds the rule and a worked row to the instructions. Sam will mark any unresolved calculation as blocked and message Maya directly as soon as he finds it. Maya will check at 09:30 and answer by 10:00; with no answer by then, Sam flags the draft as at risk and carries on with the rest. It goes in the task notes, with both accounts. On Thursday Sam delivers a comparable draft at 10:45 and Maya finds nothing to correct. The instructions worked. The new route was not needed, so it is still untested.

The next Tuesday: the route worked, and Maya did not

Sam meets another unfamiliar calculation at 09:20 and sends the direct message. Maya has booked a customer call across her own 09:30 check. At 10:00, with no answer, Sam flags the draft as at risk and continues. Maya sees the message at 11:15. The internal deadline has passed, and this customer expects the checked report at noon.

Sam did everything agreed. More status updates from him would not have given him the authority Maya kept. She works out that the remaining check will not fit, phones the customer, and agrees 13:00. She supplies the rule, checks the finished report, and it goes out at 12:40. They record both facts: delivered inside the revised promise, and the original noon missed. Quietly replacing noon with 13:00 would hide what the block cost.

The repair is Maya's. She protects 09:30–10:00 before accepting calls. If she cannot, she must name someone capable and authorised to decide in her place, or change the work and the customer's promise in advance. A calendar invitation is not cover. Note the two roles: Sam keeps the record and notices the block; Maya owns the decision. Noticing a block does not give anyone the authority to remove it.

How the question travels should follow how long the answer can wait. A written note is enough if the decider will see it in time; a short conversation when several people must trade something off; an agreed urgent route when delay does harm. Maya and Sam add no meeting at all; they keep a written note before each report session and the protected half hour.

What it cost, counted

To find out whether the routine earns its time, they count three things for the next five reports after that Tuesday, with the protected half hour in place, and rebuild the same three for the previous five from their records:

  • Delivery: checked reports sent by the original customer deadline, out of all reports due. A revised deadline does not erase the first one, and an unknown time stays unknown.
  • Quality: reports needing a correction within seven days.
  • Coordination time: minutes spent on updates, calls and reviews, summed across both people. An eight-minute call between two people is sixteen minutes.

The result is undramatic, and that is the point of counting. The routine costs twenty more minutes across five reports. Four were on time, but the earlier window might also have had four, since one timestamp is unknown. Corrections did not move. Nothing here shows the routine caused better delivery. It does tell them where to look: the one late report. Maya had answered on time; the customer's input arrived late. So they keep the half hour for another five reports and add an earlier input check with that customer.

Two cautions. Once a count becomes a target, ask how it could improve without the work improving: Sam could mark a draft complete before reconciling it, Maya could rewrite a deadline. Keep the original promise and the corrections beside the count. And five reports are not a verdict on a person.

One event, three readings: ConvertKit's public metrics

In January 2019 Nathan Barry explained his company's public dashboard. Monthly recurring revenue is a view of subscription value; the dashboard's “net revenue” is money that came in during the month. Each November the company sold discounted annual plans. He reported net revenue reaching "as high as $3 million" during the push, while the discount lowered MRR, and revenue churn rose because it includes contraction, without the same change in customers leaving. Reading that churn figure as lost customers would be the wrong diagnosis. He also reported "6,994 customers (33% of the total) generating $811,117 in MRR (68% of the total)": a revenue share means little without the group behind it. Before judging a number, write down what it counts, over what period, and from which record.

When the work succeeds, and when it keeps struggling

When a result is sound, say what made it sound and carry on; do not invent an improvement to fill the record. More independence follows the evidence: Sam's clean draft does not grant authority over calculations. When a result falls short, describe the gap and what it caused, hear the person's account, and then choose: an example for a skill gap, access for a block, fewer simultaneous tasks for an impossible workload. Name who supplies the change and when comparable work will show whether it helped.

Repeated difficulty has a cost too: late customers, correction work, your time. You need not repeat the same fix indefinitely. Record what was tried and what remains, in terms of the work; “poor fit” says little, and resemblance to your own style is not a standard. Where a persistent problem makes you consider changing or ending someone's employment, the process is a separate question from this review; readers employing in the Netherlands should read the dated section at the end of this chapter first.

Maya and Sam's route now works, on one condition: Maya is there to answer. Chapter twenty-three asks what happens on the morning she is not.

Monday · one assignment, with its route for a block

Do this one thing. Pick one piece of work someone else does for you this week: an employee's task, a contractor's job, or, if you work alone, a handoff to a supplier or a customer who must send you something. Write five lines: the result, the deadline, what they may decide themselves, what needs you, and the route for a block: how they tell you, and by when you will answer. Send it to them and ask them to say it back in their own words. You now hold one agreed assignment and a promise of your own to keep.

Nobody works with or for you yet? Write the five lines for the first task you would hand over, using chapter seventeen's page, and leave the rest pending. Do not know by when an answer stops being useful? Ask the person doing the work; they usually know, and asking counts as finishing.

If you have another hour:

  1. Look at one real attempt together before deciding its cause. Record their account beside yours, and mark what you do not know as unknown.
  2. Count the last five deliveries against the original promise, with corrections within seven days.
  3. Add up a week's coordination minutes across everyone involved, and ask which of them changed a decision.
What this chapter rests on, and what would prove it wrong

Both Groove accounts are Turnbull's own. The 2015 post reports turnover, his view of its costs, and his practices; it contains no comparison showing the practices worked. Groove also used a two-to-four-week paid trial whose end forced a decision, which he acknowledged could exclude talented candidates, and which says nothing about how such an arrangement would be classified where you are. He kept notes for himself; a record both people can see is this book's proposal. The 2016 post gives meeting formats and his judgment that the team was working more efficiently after a couple of months, with no before-and-after delivery figures. ConvertKit's dashboard was not audited for this book. Maya, Sam, their deadlines and both windows of figures are invented; the trial shows how to inspect a routine, not that this one is worthwhile.

Different work needs different signals: a stock seller needs quantities on hand and confirmed arrival dates; a consultancy considering a booking needs usable hours by skill. Use chapter twelve's period review for the economics and chapter thirteen's forecast for dated payments; do not rebuild the accounts in a meeting.

Still owed: a small business that used clear assignments, timely shared reviews, named decision owners and response times, counted the cost honestly, and delivered worse, because the routine consumed the attention the work needed or narrowed capable people to the written checks. Neither account measures that. The debt stands.

Closing Part IV · Can it run without you?

Three chapters, three tests of the same question. One task handed over and tried, with what still comes back to you written down. Help you can afford, tested against a late receipt. A decision that someone else needs, made in time. None of them makes the business independent of you. Each makes one dependency visible and smaller.

The last part asks what happens when something goes wrong that nobody planned: you are away, a document you rely on does not say what you assumed, or the ground moves under your customers.

On Dutch ground · ending an employment

This section is for readers employing in the Netherlands. Read it before the conversation, not after it: a review in this chapter decides what to try next with the work, not whether someone stays, and here the two are different acts.

There is no dismissal at will. Article 7:671 of the Civil Code: "De werkgever kan de arbeidsovereenkomst niet rechtsgeldig opzeggen zonder schriftelijke instemming van de werknemer, tenzij" — and the exceptions that follow include termination during the trial period and permission "als bedoeld in artikel 671a", which that article confines to grounds a and b of article 669: "op grond van artikel 669, lid 3, onderdeel a of b, verzoekt hiervoor schriftelijk toestemming aan het Uitvoeringsinstituut werknemersverzekeringen". For the grounds listed next, the door is the court, article 7:671b: "De kantonrechter kan op verzoek van de werkgever de arbeidsovereenkomst ontbinden: a. op grond van artikel 669, lid 3, onderdelen c tot en met i". Outside those, the employee's written agreement is what article 671 names. Article 7:669 sets what the employer must have first: "De werkgever kan de arbeidsovereenkomst opzeggen indien daar een redelijke grond voor is en herplaatsing van de werknemer binnen een redelijke termijn, al dan niet met behulp van scholing, in een andere passende functie niet mogelijk is of niet in de rede ligt." A reasonable ground, and no reasonable redeployment.

Unsuitability is a ground with conditions. Among the grounds, lid 3 onderdeel d concerns unsuitability for the agreed work, other than through illness — "mits de werkgever de werknemer hiervan tijdig in kennis heeft gesteld en hem in voldoende mate in de gelegenheid heeft gesteld zijn functioneren te verbeteren en de ongeschiktheid niet het gevolg is van onvoldoende zorg van de werkgever voor scholing van de werknemer of voor de arbeidsomstandigheden van de werknemer". Told in good time, given a real chance to improve, and not left untrained. Feedback given as it arises, and a record that it was given, are here close to a precondition of the ground existing; notes kept only for yourself are not notice to the person.

The trial period is statutory and short. Article 7:652: "De proeftijd wordt schriftelijk overeengekomen"; on an indefinite contract "een proeftijd worden overeengekomen van ten hoogste twee maanden"; and none at all where the contract "is aangegaan voor ten hoogste zes maanden". Turnbull calls Groove's arrangement a consulting engagement. That description does not establish how a similar arrangement would be classified here. The Belastingdienst's assessment guidance considers all facts and circumstances of the actual relationship; see also chapter nineteen's Dutch-ground section.

Notice and severance. Article 7:672 lid 2: "De door de werkgever in acht te nemen termijn van opzegging bedraagt bij een arbeidsovereenkomst die op de dag van opzegging: a. korter dan vijf jaar heeft geduurd: één maand", rising with service. Article 7:673 lid 2: "De transitievergoeding is voor elk jaar dat de arbeidsovereenkomst heeft geduurd gelijk aan een derde van het loon per maand", pro rata for part-years, and "bedraagt maximaal € 102.000,00 of een bedrag gelijk aan ten hoogste het loon over twaalf maanden indien dat loon hoger is dan dat bedrag". The cap "wordt telkens met ingang van 1 januari" adjusted, so in a later year the figure above is stale by construction. The transitievergoeding is owed on a dismissal the employer initiates on a valid ground; it is not a fine for doing it wrong, and paying it does not supply the ground.

None of this is advice. The box covers an employer ending an indefinite contract; a fixed-term contract's expiry, a termination by agreement, illness, and every other ground in article 669 are outside it. Before the first conversation that could be heard as a dismissal, pay an arbeidsrechtadvocaat for an hour with the contract and your notes in front of them.
