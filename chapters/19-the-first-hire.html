Part IV · Can it run without you? · Chapter 19

Deciding to Add Help

Eran Galperin ran Gymdesk alone for years. Of the person he finally hired to answer customers he wrote, "I still regret not doing this sooner." Of the hire before that one he wrote that he was now "responsible to make sure they are paid every month." Both sentences are true of the same decision. How do you tell, with your own numbers, which one you will be writing a year from now?

The decision Whether to add help now, change the work or defer the commitment, using a clear requirement, a realistic support plan and a dated funding scenario.

The hire he wished he had made sooner

Gymdesk, the gym software from chapter four, was, until the middle of 2021, in its founder's words, "a one-man, owner run business": freelancers and contractors came and went, and nobody was on the payroll. When he wanted more growth than one person could produce, he bought it from outside first. It did not take. "After a few failed experiments engaging “growth” and advertising agencies, I came to the conclusion that outside consultants and agencies were never going to understand our audience like I did."

So in June 2021 he made his first full-time hire, and he chose someone he had already seen work: a person who "used to write for us", with a background in SEO, to run content for what he called his strongest acquisition channel, organic traffic. He did not find it a small step. A freelancer's invoice ends when the job ends. An employee has to be paid every month, whatever that month's sales turn out to be, and he says plainly that this weighed on him.

Later the same year he hired a full-time customer-service person. He had tried contractors for that work through Upwork, "and it just wasn’t working out." The new hire, by his account, removed a large part of the daily interruptions that had been landing on him, and that is the hire he regrets not making earlier. By the end of 2022 Gymdesk had eight employees and four contractors, and it "passed $1M in ARR in mid 2022".

Read the order again, because it is the useful part. The hire he regrets delaying was not the ambitious one. It was the one that took over work already arriving every day, that he already knew how to do, and that was already costing him the hours he needed for everything else. The growth hire came with a bet attached. The support hire came with a requirement attached. This chapter starts from the requirement.

Name the work before you name the person

Bring what the handover review left you with: the work that waits for you, what a good result looks like and when it is needed. “I need an assistant” is a solution. “Someone must answer failed-report requests before noon on the mornings I am delivering” is a requirement, and only a requirement can be priced.

Say which purpose the help serves: keeping a promise you have already made, relieving you, bringing a skill you lack, or chasing new demand. They are funded differently. Help that keeps an existing promise is paid for by customers you already have. Help that chases demand is an experiment, paid for before the demand exists, and it needs a limit. Future sales cannot pay a bill that falls due before they arrive.

If you have no record of the work, rebuild a recent fortnight from requests, deliveries and your calendar, and separate time doing the work from time waiting on someone. Compare a busy stretch with a quiet one before you choose the hours.

Noor's requirement

Noor and her consultancy are invented, continued from chapter seventeen. Ivo handled two real Tuesday requests, the second without her, but he has no more time to give. Noor wants two four-hour mornings a week covered: check incoming requests, use the approved recovery steps, check the report output, answer the customer within the agreed time. New integration quotes and anything outside the recovery limits stay with her.

She also writes down what the help will cost her: four hours of preparation and training before the start, then two hours a week reviewing work and handling exceptions for the first four weeks. Twelve hours. They go in her calendar before she tells herself that hiring frees eight hours a week.

Three ways to get the work done

First ask whether the requirement can shrink: clearer instructions to customers, or a different response promise agreed with them. Then compare what could supply the rest: a provider responsible for an agreed result, more hours from someone you already work with, or an employee. For each, ask who directs the work, when it is actually available, who handles exceptions and what happens when that person is ill, and get the minimum charge, notice and payment dates. A quoted hour from one is not the same thing as a quoted hour from another.

One boundary changes what you do next. How much the business depends on someone tells you nothing about whether the law treats them as an employee. On Dutch ground the Belastingdienst weighs all the facts of the actual relationship together, and a label such as “freelance” or “trial” does not settle it. Describe the real arrangement — work, direction, hours, payment — and take it to an adviser before you sign, including for a paid work sample. The dated legal section quotes the statute.

Put the payments on dates

For an employee, the cost is the whole role: employer charges, leave, pension, administration. For a service, it is the included work and its payment dates. For both, add setup, access, training and the work still left with you, and put the payments into the dated cash forecast. A salary divided by twelve does not say whether you can pay on the day.

When someone takes over hours you used to supply, replace your allowance for those hours with their cost, and keep an allowance for the supervision you still do. Count each hour once. An hour released from your calendar is not yet an hour sold.

The employee version fails on day 14

All figures are invented budget amounts, not wage rates or quotations. For an employee on two mornings, Noor assumes €2,400 of total staffing cash in the first four weeks, in two €1,200 instalments, plus €600 of setup. She opens with €5,000. Other payments — the rest of the business, tax and her own household transfer — are €1,800 on day 2 and €800 on day 21. A finished job should bring €2,800 on day 18. She will not let the balance go below €1,500; that floor is her choice, not a rule.

Four weeks is also only where the table stops. An employment does not stop there. A real version runs on through the next pay dates, the charges that arrive later, a quiet month and whatever notice the arrangement carries.

What Noor did, and what four weeks showed

Noor does not offer the job. She also does not stop, because doing nothing has a price she can already see: every covered morning she keeps for herself is a delivery morning she does not have. She goes back to the three arrangements and asks a support provider to quote for one covered morning a week against the same brief. The invented quote is €160 a morning, paid weekly, with a €200 paid handover session first and two weeks' notice either way. She puts the actual arrangement in front of her adviser before agreeing to it.

With the late payment, the lowest point is €1,560 on day 28: €60 above her floor. That is thin, and she writes down what would break it — any unplanned payment over €60 before the customer pays. But it holds in the bad version, which the employee plan did not do in the good one. She starts.

Four weeks later she reads what happened, not what she planned. The customer paid on day 25, so the balance never went below €1,720. All four covered mornings met the noon response. One request, in week three, was a new integration question and came to her, as the brief said it should. Her own time was the surprise: four hours of preparation, then two and a half, one, one and a half, and half an hour across the four weeks. Nine and a half hours against sixteen hours covered. The first month cost €840 and returned six and a half hours.

That is a poor month and a reasonable trend: by the last three weeks a €160 morning was costing her about an hour and returning three. She keeps the one morning for another eight weeks. The second morning waits until a two-month forecast clears the floor with a late payment in it. The employee role stays deferred, with a number attached to it now instead of a feeling. None of this is on Galperin's scale, and it is the same order he wishes he had followed: the help that takes over work already arriving, bought in the smallest form that keeps the promise, before the hire that comes with a bet.

Your answer may be different. You might fund a ramp-up deliberately from savings, recruit for a revised role, or decide the business should be smaller. Whichever it is, say how much money and time you are committing, what must be true before the start, and the date you will read the result. Deferring counts only if it has those three things too.

If the person is someone you already know

Galperin's first hire had written for him before. That is real evidence and it has an edge: it shows how someone does a task, not how they carry a role. Ask what the new arrangement adds — sustained availability, responsibility for exceptions, talking to customers — and ask whether the person wants those things. A very good occasional writer may have no wish to run an editorial schedule.

Where one uncertainty matters most, test it with a bounded, paid piece of real work on agreed terms. It shows how they approach that task and nothing more. Before any start, agree the first result, its standard, the access and authority that go with it, and the help available, and reserve your own support hours. A late first result may be telling you about your instructions.

Once someone is doing the work, the question changes from whether to add help to whether the help is succeeding. Chapter twenty starts there: one agreed assignment, one missed deadline, and what to look at before deciding whose problem it is.

Monday · one requirement, on dates

Do this one thing. Write the requirement in two sentences, the work and when it must be done, and put a price beside the smallest arrangement that would meet it. You now hold a requirement and a number you can test.

No price yet? Send the two sentences to one provider and ask for a price by a date. For an employee, a job advertisement for similar work gives you a salary to start from; your tax authority publishes what an employer pays on top. The request sent counts as finishing.

If you have another hour:

  1. Test it on dates. Put the arrangement and your own support hours into your dated cash forecast, move your largest uncertain receipt two weeks later, and find the lowest balance. If it clears your floor, you have a start date; if not, the amount that has to change.
  2. Price a second arrangement against the same two sentences, including its minimum charge, notice and what it leaves with you. If you cannot tell whether an arrangement would count as employment, find out before signing; the Dutch section below covers the Netherlands.
  3. Set the review. Write the date, what you will count — promises kept, money paid, your hours spent — and what result would make you stop, keep or extend.
What this chapter rests on, and what it cannot show

The Gymdesk account is one founder writing about his own company two years later. It gives the order of his hires, his reasons and his regret. It does not give their cost or how they were funded, and it cannot show that agencies would have failed under a different brief, that having written for him made the first hire right for the whole role, or that support should be anyone's second hire. The reading of his sequence offered above — requirement before bet — is this book's, not his.

Noor, her provider, every euro and every hour are invented to show the arithmetic and the review. Her four weeks are an illustration, not a result. No case here tests this routine.

Still owed: a documented owner who made a careful, funded commitment of this kind and was worse off for it. A funded role can still fail; a deferral can still cost an opportunity that does not return.

On Dutch ground · who counts as an employee

This section is for readers hiring in the Netherlands. Employment status is a legal assessment of the working relationship, separate from the operational decision about what help the business needs.

The definition. Article 7:610 of the Civil Code says what an employment contract is: "De arbeidsovereenkomst is de overeenkomst waarbij de ene partij, de werknemer, zich verbindt in dienst van de andere partij, de werkgever, tegen loon gedurende zekere tijd arbeid te verrichten." Three elements — work performed in dienst van the other party, for pay, over a period of time. The statute defines the relationship by those elements, not by the heading on the contract.

The presumption. Article 7:610a goes further: "Hij die ten behoeve van een ander tegen beloning door die ander gedurende drie opeenvolgende maanden, wekelijks dan wel gedurende ten minste twintig uren per maand arbeid verricht, wordt vermoed deze arbeid te verrichten krachtens arbeidsovereenkomst." Someone who works for you, for pay, for three consecutive months, weekly or at least twenty hours a month, is presumed to be doing so under an employment contract.

What the presumption means. Rijksoverheid's explanation of employment contracts says that an employer who disputes the presumed employment relationship must prove that there is no employment contract. This is an English summary of that guidance; the presumption can be rebutted, and what would rebut it in your arrangement needs legal advice.

Enforcement. The government's own page on schijnzelfstandigheid — self-employment in name only — states that "De Belastingdienst controleert sinds 1 januari 2025 weer volledig op schijnzelfstandigheid." The consequence for the business that got it wrong is that it "betaalt de werkgever alsnog loonbelasting, premies werknemersverzekeringen, bijdragen Zorgverzekeringswet en soms pensioenpremies", and the same page notes that responsibility is shared: "Bedrijven en zzp'ers zijn samen verantwoordelijk om schijnzelfstandigheid te voorkomen."

None of this is advice. This box quotes the definition, the presumption and the fact of enforcement, and summarises the government's explanation of the presumption; it does not say how the assessment goes in your case, what the criteria weigh, or what a rebuttal would need. Before the first contract of either kind, pay an arbeidsrechtadvocaat for an hour with the actual working arrangement in front of them.
